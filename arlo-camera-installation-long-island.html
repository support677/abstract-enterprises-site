The Neighborhood

Why Long Island Properties Need Arlo Camera Installation

Long Island is a place of separate houses on their own lots, and a wireless camera system has to cover every one of those separate pieces: the front step, the driveway, the side gate, the back slider and the garage. The U.S. Census Bureau's 2023 American Community Survey counts 477,860 housing units in Nassau County, and 364,529 of them are one-unit detached houses. In Suffolk the figures are 579,790 units and 463,095 detached houses. Put another way, roughly three out of every four homes on the island stand alone. That is very different from a city apartment, where one doorbell camera on one door covers most of what a resident cares about. A Long Island house has a perimeter, and the people who live in it usually want to see all of it from a phone.

The age of that housing matters for an Arlo job almost as much as its shape. The same Census tables put the median year a Nassau home was built at 1955 and the median Suffolk home at 1970. Levitt and Sons built 17,447 houses in Levittown between 1947 and 1951, Cape Cods first and then ranches, many of them on concrete slabs with no basement. A Newsday analysis of nearly 12,000 home sales in 2026 found that more than 5,000 of the houses sold were built in the 1950s and 1960s. Those decades produced homes with the router usually in a corner of the house where the cable company first brought the line in, plaster or early drywall walls, aluminum or later vinyl siding, and garages that were often added or rebuilt later. Wi-Fi was never part of the plan. When an Arlo camera is screwed to the corner of a detached garage forty feet from that router, the wall, the siding, the car and the distance all take a bite out of the signal.

Then there is what people are actually worried about. In January 2024, ABC7 New York reported on a multi-jurisdictional task force of Nassau and Suffolk police and district attorneys, the NYPD, State Police and the FBI, formed to fight a run of residential burglaries and car thefts. Police told ABC7 they believed the burglars were attracted by cars in people's driveways, and that break-ins while people were at home had gone up. In November 2025, CBS New York reported that the Suffolk District Attorney had charged 14 men in a package-theft ring that worked across 31 communities, including Brentwood, Central Islip, Shirley and Deer Park, between October 2023 and February 2025, allegedly learning when phone carriers were delivering devices and taking more than 200 phones and tablets. Closer to the Nassau Hub, Patch's Garden City crime reports in 2026 have included packages taken from a front porch, an attempted snowblower theft that a homeowner caught because home video surveillance alerted them, and a rear sliding door broken during a string of burglaries on Brook Street. The pattern in the reporting is the driveway, the porch and the back door, which are the three places a Long Island Arlo layout almost always starts.

The phrase "Long Island hub" also has a local meaning worth naming, because many of our calls come from there. The Nassau Hub is the stretch of East Garden City and Uniondale around Nassau Veterans Memorial Coliseum, in the Town of Hempstead. Wikipedia describes East Garden City, also known as the Nassau Hub, as a hamlet within Uniondale on the Hempstead Plains, and Homes.com describes the area as split by Hempstead Turnpike, with the busy commercial section to the north and mostly residential streets to the south. North of the turnpike are condos, townhouses and apartment complexes such as Avalon at Garden City and the Meadowbrook Pointe community for residents 48 and older. South of it are Cape Cods and minimal traditional houses, often with detached garages and private driveways. NeighborhoodScout puts single-family detached homes at about 70 percent of Uniondale's housing, most of it built from the 1940s through the 1960s, with about 62 percent of households owning and 38 percent renting. That mix of owners, renters, HOA residents and office tenants is a small version of the whole island's Arlo work.

There is a reason Arlo comes up so often on Long Island in particular. A battery or solar Arlo camera can go on a house with no new wiring at all, which suits owners who do not want holes in a finished basement, renters who cannot drill much, and HOA residents who need the smallest possible change to a shared exterior. The trade-off is that a wireless camera depends entirely on its connection and its battery. When Tropical Storm Isaias came through in August 2020, ABC7 reported that almost half of PSEG Long Island's 1.1 million customers were in the dark at the peak. A camera with a full battery is no use if the router, modem and SmartHub it talks to have no power. Planning for that, with the right storage and a battery backup on the network gear where it makes sense, is part of doing the job properly here.

We also want to be clear about what our research did not find. We looked for Reddit threads, Yelp complaints and local news stories specifically about Arlo installations going wrong in Nassau or Suffolk and found none we could cite. We could not capture Google's People Also Ask boxes, Bing or DuckDuckGo results for this page. So where this page talks about why a garage camera keeps dropping offline or why a doorbell will not power up, it draws on Arlo's own support material, published reviews, and what we see in houses of these types generally, not on a survey of Long Island Arlo owners. Your own property is the only one that counts, which is why the first thing we do on site is measure it.

1955 Nassau, 1970 Suffolk: How the Age and Spread of a Long Island House Changes an Arlo Layout

Driving east from the Queens line to the North Fork, the houses get newer and the lots get wider, and both changes show up in an Arlo job. A Nassau house is more likely to be a postwar Cape or ranch on a compact lot, with the garage close to the house and the neighbors close on both sides. A Suffolk house is more likely to be a larger two-story home from the 1970s or later, with a longer driveway and a garage that may be across the yard. The postwar Capes and slab ranches

The Levitt houses and their neighbors across Nassau were small when they were built, and most have since been extended with dormers, rear additions and rebuilt garages, sometimes, as one Levittown remodeler notes, without permits. For an Arlo system that history is mostly good news, because the distances are short. The difficulty is the number of walls a signal has to pass through, and what those walls are made of now. An addition built on the back of a 750-square-foot Cape may put two exterior walls between the router in the front room and a camera watching the back yard. Radiant heat in the slab, which many original Levitt homes had, is another reason we never drill into the floor for anything. When a camera needs a wired connection or a wired doorbell needs a new run, the route is through the attic or along the outside of the house, never through the slab.

The split-levels and hi-ranches

A split-level puts the family room half a floor below the kitchen and the bedrooms half a floor above it, and the router often ends up in whatever room the cable installer reached first. Arlo cameras on the outside of a split-level are spread across those levels too: a doorbell at the mid-level entry, a camera over the garage on the lower level, one on the back deck off the kitchen. Each half-floor of framing, ductwork and flooring between the router and a camera costs signal. We often move the SmartHub or base station, or add a mesh point on the level that faces the weakest camera, rather than trying to push signal through two floors.

The larger Suffolk houses and long driveways

In a 1970s or 1980s colonial on a wider lot, the camera that matters most is often the one that watches the end of the driveway or the side of a detached garage, and it can be sixty or eighty feet from the router. A battery Arlo will connect at that distance in open air with a clear line, but a garage door, a parked car and a few trees can make the difference between a camera that wakes instantly on motion and one that misses the first seconds of every clip. Arlo's own reviewers at TechRadar noted years ago that, depending on the shape of a house, the cameras may not lie in range of the base station. That is why we measure first. If a camera spot is weak, the choices are a different spot, an access point or extender closer to it, or a short Ethernet run to an access point in the garage itself.

The North Shore estates and waterfront homes

The Gold Coast that ran from Great Neck east to Huntington once held more than 500 mansions, and while many were subdivided after the Second World War, large houses, stone walls, carriage houses and long gated drives remain across that part of the island. Arlo can work on properties like these, but the honest answer is that a purely wireless system across several buildings and hundreds of feet of drive is the wrong tool for the whole job. We usually put Arlo where it is strongest, close to the main house and its doors, and recommend wired networking to any outbuilding that needs coverage, so the cameras there have a strong access point right next to them. On the South Shore, where Sandy caused about 150 million dollars of damage in Long Beach alone according to Wikipedia's account of the storm, we also keep hubs and network gear off basement floors.

Router to Driveway: What to Check at Your Long Island Property Before an Arlo Visit

Most of the time lost on an Arlo job is lost on things nobody knew in advance: cameras that need a SmartHub the owner does not have, a router locked by the internet provider, a doorbell transformer too weak for the new doorbell, or an HOA that has not approved anything on the exterior. Ten minutes with a phone camera before the visit prevents nearly all of it. Please do not take the existing doorbell apart or reset the router yourself; just look and send us what you find.

Walk the property with your phone

Stand at each place you want a camera, open a Wi-Fi speed test or simply your phone's Wi-Fi settings, and note how many bars you have on your home network. Do it at the end of the driveway, beside the garage, at the back slider and by the side gate. It is not a precise measurement, and we bring proper tools, but it tells us immediately whether the job is a simple mount or a network job first. If the bars drop to one or none at the garage, tell us when you book so we bring an access point or extender.

Think about who will change the batteries

Battery Arlo cameras need recharging, and the right height for a camera is a balance between being out of reach of a passer-by and being within reach of you. SafeWise suggests around six to seven feet above the ground for an Arlo camera, which most people can reach from a short step ladder. A camera mounted at the peak of a two-story colonial sees a lot of yard, but somebody has to climb up to it every few months. If nobody in the house is comfortable on a tall ladder, tell us, and we will plan positions, solar panels or spare batteries around that.

Arlo Camera Installation for Long Island's Architecture

Long Island's housing covers postwar tract houses, mid-century split-levels, large suburban colonials, North Shore estates, South Shore waterfront homes, garden apartments, gated communities and office towers. Each one changes three things about an Arlo job: what the mount is fixed into, how far the signal has to travel, and who has to agree before anything goes on the outside wall.

Postwar Capes and Ranches

The Levittown model repeated across Nassau: small footprints grown by additions, slabs with no basement in many original houses, attics for any wiring, and siding over the original walls. Short distances, many walls.

Split-Levels and Hi-Ranches

Common around Hofstra and the Coliseum. Several half-floors between the router and the cameras, garages on the lower level, decks on the upper. Hub and mesh placement does most of the work.

Suffolk Colonials and Newer Homes

Larger two-story houses on wider lots, long driveways, rear decks and pools. More cameras, more distance, and more ladder height under the eaves.

North Shore Estates and Outbuildings

Stone and brick walls, carriage houses and long drives. Arlo near the main house, wired network to the outbuildings, and village historic review checked where it applies.

Condos, Townhouses and Gated Communities

Developments like those north of Hempstead Turnpike in East Garden City, including a 48-and-older gated community. Shared exteriors, HOA approval and crowded Wi-Fi.

Rentals and Multifamily Houses

First-floor and basement apartments in multifamily houses, common in Uniondale listings. Renters need the owner's agreement, so no-drill mounts and interior cameras come first.

On vinyl and aluminum siding. A great many Long Island houses wear vinyl siding over older wood or asbestos-cement shingles, and an Arlo mount screwed straight into the vinyl alone will rattle in the wind and eventually crack it. We fix mounts through the siding into the sheathing or framing behind it, use a mounting block where the siding profile is uneven so the mount sits flat, and seal every penetration so water does not track behind the siding. One Uniondale restoration contractor warns that postwar Capes and ranches in that area frequently contain asbestos materials. If we see old cement-board shingles or other materials that could contain asbestos where a mount would need to go, we stop, show you, and choose a spot on trim or a newer surface instead of drilling into them.

On block and brick. The same contractor describes many local Capes and ranches as sitting on concrete-block foundations, and brick facing is common on the lower level of split-levels. A camera aimed low across a driveway is sometimes best placed on that masonry. We drill with a hammer drill, set plastic or sleeve anchors sized for a light camera mount, and place them in the block or brick face rather than the mortar joint wherever the mount pattern allows. Arlo cameras are light, so the challenge with masonry is not weight; it is keeping a clean, sealed hole that will still be tight after several freeze-thaw winters.

On HOA and condo exteriors. In a townhouse or condo community, the outside walls, the trim and often the front door belong to the association, not the owner. Many associations allow a doorbell camera or a small camera under an eave with written approval, and some limit color, size or position. We ask you to get that approval before the visit, and we install exactly what was approved. Interior cameras looking through a window are a tempting workaround, but as one Arlo buyer complained publicly, an Arlo camera cannot detect motion through glass, so they rarely do what people expect. We will tell you plainly if the approved position cannot see what you hoped.

Where Arlo fits in a business, and where it does not

A dentist's waiting room on the Hempstead Plains, a two-person insurance office in Melville or a boutique on a village main street can be well served by three or four Arlo cameras: one at the front door, one at the back door, one over the register or reception desk, one in the stockroom. The owner sees everything on a phone and can share access with a manager. A warehouse along Route 110, a larger store or an office floor that needs continuous recording for months is a different matter. Battery cameras are designed to record when motion triggers them, and Arlo's own knowledge base notes that local storage saves only automatically triggered recordings. If a business needs every minute of every day kept, or needs plate capture at a lot entrance, we will say that Arlo is the wrong system and quote a wired one instead. That honesty saves money later.

Rules and practical limits for business cameras on Long Island

  • State licensing. New York's General Business Law Article 6-D and 19 NYCRR 195.2, as summarized by a New York law firm, require a security and fire alarm installer license for installing or servicing a closed-circuit television system used to detect or monitor intrusion, theft or movement. AESS holds NYS license #12000287431.
  • County home improvement licensing. Nassau County says it is against the law to operate a home improvement business there without a license, and notes that contractors working only on commercial buildings do not need one. Suffolk has its own consumer affairs licensing. We tell you which applies to your address.
  • Footage requests. In May 2026 News 12 reported a proposed Suffolk County bill that would require a business where a felony occurs to preserve footage and give it to police within one day of a request, after a fatal stabbing at a CVS in Lindenhurst. Reporting later said the sponsor agreed to pursue amendments. We could not confirm its final status. We set up business Arlo accounts so the owner can export clips quickly either way.
  • Police camera registries. The Suffolk County Police run a Camera Registry Program, and the Garden City Police Department runs a voluntary private camera registration program that, in its own words, does not give police access to privately owned systems. Registration simply lets detectives know whom to ask for video.
  • Audio. New York's eavesdropping law is usually described as one-party consent, but sources disagree on how it applies to cameras that record people who are not part of a conversation. For offices and stores we leave audio recording off unless the owner has taken legal advice and wants it.
  • Office building rules. Towers like those at the Nassau Hub have management offices, freight elevators and work-hour rules. We ask you to get building management's requirements to us before the visit, including any insurance certificate they want on file.
Field Notes

Field Notes: The Signal Walk Before Any Arlo Goes Up

We do not publish stories about a particular customer's house unless we can document them, so this is not a tale about one address. It is the routine our technicians follow on the kind of Long Island property we expect most often: a detached house from the 1950s or 1960s, the router in a front room, a driveway down one side and a garage at the back.

We leave the cameras in their boxes and start with the router. We look at its make, whether the internet provider has merged the 2.4 and 5 GHz bands under one name, and how many neighboring networks are crowding the same channels. Then we walk the property with a signal meter, stopping at every spot you have asked for a camera: the front step, the driveway corner, the side gate, the back slider, the far side of the garage. At each spot we note the signal and look at what stands between it and the router: the garage door, the kitchen, the addition built in the 1980s. We look up at the eaves to see where a camera can sit at a height you can still reach, and we look at the sky over each solar panel position. Only when every spot shows a signal we are happy with, or we have agreed with you how to fix the weak ones, does the first camera go on the wall. In our experience that walk is the difference between a system that works for years and one that sends "camera offline" alerts every week.

Your Questions Answered

Arlo Camera Installation in Long Island: Questions Answered

Hubs, base stations and signal

Some Arlo boxes mention a SmartHub and some don't. Which cameras on a Long Island house actually need one?

Arlo's knowledge base says the Arlo Pro 3, Ultra and Ultra 2 require an Arlo SmartHub or base station; many newer models can connect to your Wi-Fi directly. The hub plugs into your router and talks to the cameras on its own link, which can help on a property where the router's Wi-Fi is weak at the edges, and some hubs also take a USB drive for local recording. Arlo also says to keep a camera within three to six feet of the SmartHub while pairing it, so we pair everything first and then carry each camera to its spot and check the connection there. If you have a mix of older and newer cameras, we set up both on the same account and tell you which ones depend on the hub, so nobody unplugs it thinking it is spare equipment.

The camera on our detached garage keeps showing "offline." Is the camera faulty, or is it the distance from the house?

Far more often it is the connection, not the camera. A detached garage in Uniondale or a Suffolk colonial's garage across the yard can be forty to eighty feet from the router, with the house's own walls, a garage door, cars and trees in between. TechRadar's Arlo review noted that, depending on the shape of a house, cameras may not lie in range of the base station. We measure signal at the camera's position, check the battery and firmware, and look at the router's 2.4 GHz settings. The fix is usually one of three: move the hub or router to the side of the house facing the garage, add a mesh point or access point closer to it, or run one Ethernet cable to an access point inside the garage. We will tell you which one your property needs and why.

Our internet provider's router only shows one network name. Why does the Arlo app keep insisting on 2.4 GHz?

Because almost every Arlo camera needs a 2.4 GHz connection to set up, as SafeHome's setup guide notes, and many provider routers now merge the 2.4 and 5 GHz bands under a single network name. A phone on the 5 GHz band can then fail to hand the camera the right network during pairing. We check whether your router lets us separate the bands or create a 2.4 GHz-only network for the cameras, and if the provider has locked those settings, we put your own router or mesh system behind theirs. The 2.4 GHz band also travels farther through walls than 5 GHz, which is one reason it suits cameras spread around a Long Island yard.

Recording and storage

If we never sign up for Arlo Secure, does anything get recorded at all?

It can. Arlo's knowledge base says a subscription is not required to use local storage, and that only automatically triggered recordings are saved locally to a connected USB device. In practice that means a compatible SmartHub or base station with a USB drive plugged in, and recordings that start when a camera detects motion, not continuous video. Several reviewers also point out that recent Arlo cameras ship with no free cloud storage, so if you buy new cameras and skip both a subscription and a hub with a USB drive, you may get live view and notifications but no saved clips. We confirm what your models support before the visit and set up whichever storage you choose.

How long do clips stay, and can we keep a recording the police ask for?

How long clips stay depends on the Arlo Secure plan you choose for cloud storage, or on the size of the USB drive for local storage, which fills up and overwrites older clips over time. Either way, a clip you need to keep should be downloaded from the app to your phone or computer as soon as you know it matters. That is the practical point behind the police camera registries on Long Island: the Suffolk County Police say they may ask registered participants to check video for a specific date and time, and Garden City police say detectives may ask to inspect video for a finite period. At handover we show you exactly how to find and download a clip, so that request never catches you out.

Mounting, power and placement

Can an Arlo sit inside the front window looking out at the driveway, so nothing goes on the outside of the house?

It will show you a picture, but it is unlikely to work the way you want. Battery Arlo cameras detect motion with a heat-sensing sensor, and as one buyer complained in a widely read forum thread, the camera cannot detect motion behind a glass window. At night, the camera's infrared light also reflects off the glass and washes out the image. For renters and HOA residents who cannot mount outside, we look first at an approved exterior spot under an eave or on the door frame, then at a doorbell camera, and only then at an interior position aimed at the inside of a door, where it will do its job properly.

Our side yard is shaded most of the day. Will an Arlo solar panel keep the camera charged there?

Probably not on its own. Arlo's own guidance for solar models is to confirm regular direct sunlight and avoid deep shade, covered porches and eaves that block the panel. Many Long Island side yards are narrow passages between two houses that see direct sun for only an hour or two, and winter sun on the island is low. The panel does not have to sit beside the camera, though. Its cable lets us place it higher, on a fascia board or a sunnier wall, while the camera stays where it sees best. If there is no sunny spot within reach, we plan for spare batteries instead and tell you honestly how often you can expect to charge them.

We bought a wired Arlo doorbell for our 1950s Cape. Will the old doorbell wiring power it?

Sometimes. A wired video doorbell needs a doorbell transformer of the right voltage, and buyer reviews of Arlo's wired doorbell warn that without standard 16-volt doorbell wiring in place, you will have to add it. In older houses the transformer may be original, undersized, or hidden in a basement ceiling near the electrical panel, and the chime may be mechanical or long since disconnected. We find the transformer, measure it, and check the chime before we connect anything. If the existing supply is not right, we tell you before work starts; where a transformer change involves line-voltage wiring, that part is for a licensed electrician. A battery doorbell is the other option and needs no wiring at all.

Arlo's own site says it won't install the Wired Floodlight. Can you?

We can do the camera side, but not the circuit. Forbes reports that Arlo does not offer installation for the Wired Floodlight Camera and says it requires a licensed electrician. That is right: replacing an existing outdoor light fixture or adding a new outdoor circuit is electrical work. Our work is low-voltage and mounting. So for a wired floodlight we coordinate with a licensed electrician who connects the power, and we handle the mounting position, aiming, Wi-Fi and app setup. Battery floodlight models avoid the electrician entirely, and we install those ourselves. The quote lists exactly who does what.

Rules, neighbors and approvals

Does putting a few Arlo cameras on a house in the Town of Hempstead or a Suffolk village need a permit?

For a standard residential camera install, sources we found generally say no permit is required in Nassau or Suffolk, and we did not find a town or village rule that requires one for battery cameras. Long Island is a patchwork, though: ny.gov lists 64 incorporated villages in Nassau alone, each able to set its own rules, and historic districts in places such as the Town of Oyster Bay, Huntington and Sea Cliff review exterior changes. We found nothing saying whether a small camera counts, so if your house is in a historic district, we suggest asking the village or town before the visit, and we choose mounts that are easy to remove if they object. A wired floodlight or new outlet is a different case, because electrical work is inspected.

Our driveway camera can see straight into the neighbor's kitchen window. How do you set it so we stay out of trouble?

On close Nassau lots this comes up often. We start with the mounting angle: a camera aimed down the driveway from the front corner of the house usually sees less of the neighbor than one on the garage looking back toward the street. Then we use Arlo's activity zones so motion on the neighbor's side does not trigger recordings, and privacy masking where your model offers it, to black out their window in the picture. We also leave audio recording off on cameras that face a neighbor's property, because sources disagree about how New York's recording law applies to conversations a camera overhears. A camera that only watches your own driveway is the easiest one to defend if a neighbor ever asks.

We live in a gated 55-and-over community off the Meadowbrook. What do we need from the HOA before you come?

In a gated or age-restricted community like the one in East Garden City, the exterior walls, doors and trim are usually controlled by the association, so we ask for written approval before the visit. It helps if the approval says which devices, in which positions, and in what color, because many HOAs limit what can face a common walkway. Send us the approval and any rules on work hours or vehicle access at the gate, and we will install exactly what was approved and nothing more. If the HOA allows only a doorbell camera, we will set that up well and add interior cameras for anything else you want watched.

I rent a first-floor apartment in a Uniondale house. How much can you put up without the landlord objecting?

Inside your apartment, a camera on a shelf or a no-drill mount is generally your own business, and it is the first thing we suggest. Anything on the outside of the building, even a battery doorbell held on with adhesive, is on the landlord's property, so we ask you to get the owner's agreement, ideally in writing, before we mount it. Many landlords are happy to allow a doorbell or a small camera at your own entrance, especially if it uses existing screw holes or a mount that comes off cleanly. We avoid aiming anything at the upstairs tenant's door or windows. When you move out, the cameras come with you, and we can come back to take them down.

What AI Gets Wrong About Arlo Camera Installation in Long Island

What follows are claims we see repeated in quick online answers about Arlo and about camera installation on Long Island, set against Arlo's own documentation, published reporting and our field practice. Marketplaces such as Angi, HomeAdvisor and Fixr publish national cost and how-to guides that feed many of those answers; they are useful for a rough idea, but they are not a plan for a house in Massapequa or Smithtown.

"Arlo is wire-free, so installation takes five minutes."

Screwing a magnetic mount to a fascia board does take five minutes. Getting a camera to stay connected, wake quickly on motion, record where you expect and last a season on one charge does not, and that is the part quick answers leave out.

On Long Island the time goes into the network: provider routers that merge Wi-Fi bands, hubs that some models require, and detached garages at the edge of range. Arlo's own knowledge base asks for cameras to be within three to six feet of the SmartHub during setup, which already tells you pairing and mounting are separate steps.

Our routine is to measure signal at each spot, fix the network where it is weak, pair, then mount. The mount is the short part. The rest is what keeps the system from sending "offline" alerts for the next three years.

"Arlo records everything to the cloud for free."

This was closer to true years ago. Reviewers now note that recent Arlo cameras ship without free cloud storage, and the Arlo app is built around the Arlo Secure subscription plans.

The free path is local storage. Arlo says a subscription is not required for it, but it works only with a compatible SmartHub or base station and a USB drive, and only motion-triggered recordings are saved, not continuous video.

So the honest answer for a Long Island buyer is: decide on storage before you buy. If you want no monthly plan, choose models and a hub that support local storage. We confirm compatibility before the visit and test a clip from every camera before we leave.

"A security camera on Long Island needs a permit."

Some answers borrow New York City rules, such as Department of Buildings permits or Landmarks Preservation Commission review, and apply them to the whole state. Nassau and Suffolk are outside the city, and those agencies do not cover them.

The sources we found say standard residential camera installs in Nassau and Suffolk generally need no local permit. What does apply is licensing for the installer, under New York's General Business Law Article 6-D, and county home improvement licensing in Nassau and Suffolk.

The exceptions are electrical work, such as a new circuit for a wired floodlight, and village or town historic districts that review exterior changes. Long Island's dozens of villages can each set their own rules, so we check your address rather than give one answer for the whole island.

"New York is a two-party consent state, so turn the microphone off."

This one appears even on installer websites, including some of ours in older versions. New York's eavesdropping statute is generally described as one-party consent: a person who is part of a conversation can record it.

The real question for a camera is different. A doorbell or driveway camera records conversations between other people, such as two delivery drivers or neighbors talking on the sidewalk, where the camera owner is not part of the conversation at all. Sources we read disagree on how the law treats that, and some say it is unsettled.

Our practice is simple: two-way talk on a doorbell is fine, because you are part of that conversation. Continuous audio recording on cameras that face sidewalks, neighbors or a business's customers stays off unless the owner has taken legal advice. That avoids the question rather than gambling on it.

"Wireless cameras are just as hard to defeat as wired ones."

Wi-Fi cameras have real advantages, but in 2022 CrimeOnline reported on a serial burglar in Queens and Long Island seen carrying a device investigators believed jammed Wi-Fi to knock cameras offline. The camera that captured him did not need Wi-Fi to record. News 12 Long Island has also relayed police advice, from a New Jersey case, to hardwire security systems rather than rely on Wi-Fi.

That does not make Arlo useless. It means a Wi-Fi camera system has a known weakness, and the buyer should know about it. Local storage on a hub still holds clips recorded before a signal is lost, and a strong, well-placed signal is harder to disrupt than a weak one at the edge of range.

For homes where that risk matters, we often suggest Arlo at the doors and driveway for convenience, with a wired camera or two at the most important point. We will lay out the trade-off for your property rather than pretend it does not exist.

"Arlo's installation partner covers Long Island, so a local installer isn't needed."

Forbes and NerdWallet both report that Arlo offers professional installation through partners such as OnTech and HelloTech, and Forbes says that covers all 50 states. That is a real option, and for a simple job it may be all you need.

The same Forbes review notes the limits: Arlo's partner installation does not include the Wired Floodlight Camera, which needs a licensed electrician. National booking services also tend to quote the device in the box, not the network around it.

Where a local installer earns its keep is the property-specific work: the access point for the detached garage, the doorbell transformer in a 1950s basement, the HOA approval, the battery backup after the last outage, and coming back if something changes. Choose whichever fits the job; we simply want you to know what each one covers.

"Mount every camera as high as possible so nobody can reach it."

Height protects a camera from tampering, but a camera at the peak of a two-story Suffolk colonial sees the tops of heads, struggles to recognize faces, and needs a tall ladder every time its battery runs low.

SafeWise's Arlo guidance suggests around six to seven feet above the ground. That height sees faces under a hat brim, keeps the camera out of easy reach, and lets most owners swap batteries from a step stool.

On a ranch or Cape, the eave is often about that height anyway. On taller houses we use the first-floor soffit, a porch ceiling or a garage fascia rather than the roofline, and we put solar panels higher than the camera where the sun allows, so the camera can sit where it sees best.

Why do some people complain about Arlo?
+

The complaints we see in reviews and forums tend to fall into three groups: subscription costs for cloud storage, batteries that need charging more often than expected, and cameras dropping offline at the edge of Wi-Fi range. What happens to our Arlo cameras if the power or internet goes out?
+

Battery cameras keep their charge, but they need the router and, where used, the SmartHub to reach the app. If the power or internet is down, you lose live view and notifications. A small battery backup on the modem, router and hub keeps things running through short outages, and local storage on a compatible hub can keep recording motion clips while the internet is down.
