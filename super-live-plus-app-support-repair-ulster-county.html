Why Ulster County Properties Need Super Live Plus App Support & Repair

Ulster has become one of the places New York goes for a weekend. The Ulster County Comptroller’s office, working from AirDNA data, found that the monthly average number of short-term rentals in the county rose from 942 in 2015 to 3,013 in 2024, an increase of 220 percent, peaking at 3,310 in October 2023. In May 2025 the count stood at 2,827 housing units, or 3.28 percent of every home in the county, and 98 percent of those listings were entire houses rather than spare rooms. Four towns — Shandaken, Woodstock, Gardiner and Denning — had more than seven percent of their housing in the rental pool, and Shandaken topped the county at 9.76 percent: 261 rentals among 2,675 homes. The same report found that 1,008 of the rentals send their owner’s mail outside Ulster, 603 of them to New York City.

Put those numbers next to a camera system and the job changes. A recorder in a Phoenicia cabin or a Woodstock cottage is not only watching an empty house for its owner; for most of the year it is pointed at a place where strangers have paid to feel private. The hosting platforms have written that tension into their rules. Airbnb announced on March 11, 2024 that security cameras are “not allowed inside listings, regardless of their location, purpose or prior disclosure,” effective April 30 of that year, and that outdoor cameras must be disclosed with their general location before guests book and may not monitor indoor spaces or areas such as an enclosed outdoor shower or sauna. Vrbo’s surveillance policy goes further on angles: a camera placed outside but aimed in through a window counts as indoor surveillance, and any camera that can see part of a pool or hot tub must be disclosed both in the listing and at the property itself.

Most of the camera systems we meet in Ulster rentals were not designed with any of that in mind. They were bought as a four- or eight-camera kit, often a white-label recorder built on TVT’s platform, and the owner watches it through Super Live Plus — the free app the App Store lists under the seller name Peng AnTai with a support link to TVT, rated 3.9 stars from 15,849 U.S. ratings. The cameras went up wherever the cable reached: one over the kitchen island to watch the dog, one in the mudroom, one on the porch looking at the front windows, one on the deck that happens to catch the hot tub. When the house became a rental, the app kept showing every one of them. That is the system an Ulster host needs to reshape — channel by channel, inside the recorder rather than just inside the app — before the next booking.

Ulster’s own patchwork of local rules adds a second layer. Each town writes its own rental law, and the differences matter for cameras. Hurley allows rentals only in the owner’s primary residence with the owner present for each stay, so a family’s own indoor cameras share the house with guests. The Village of New Paltz limits rentals in residential districts to the host’s own home. Esopus requires a host within roughly fifteen driving miles of a non-owner-occupied rental, Woodstock’s investment rentals must name a local contact, and Kingston has capped whole-home permits at about 106. Separately, every hotel, motel and short-term rental operator must register with the Ulster County Department of Finance within three days of starting business, and the state’s December 2024 law adds a registry framework on top. None of these laws tells you where to aim a camera — but each one decides who lives in the house, who answers at night and how long a rental can last, and those are exactly the questions a camera set-up has to answer.

Rental Cameras Dark Before a Check-In? Same-Day Ulster Repair

The most common emergency call from an Ulster host is not about privacy at all: the cameras disappeared from Super Live Plus a day or two before guests arrive, and the owner is in Brooklyn. We triage by phone first, then send a technician into the gap before check-in. Most systems are back on the owner’s phone within one to two hours of arrival. These are the checks we run, in order, and the ones a cleaner or neighbour can help with while you stay on the line.

  1. Is the recorder on? A dark front panel after a storm usually means the outlet, a tripped breaker or a power strip someone switched off while cleaning. Rentals lose power on purpose more than you would think — a guest unplugs the “humming box” in the closet to charge a phone.
  2. Is the router the same router? Guests sometimes press the reset button on the internet box when the Wi-Fi is slow. A factory-reset router hands out new addresses and loses any port forwards, and the recorder drops out of the app until it is re-homed.
  3. What colour is the device in the app? The SuperLive Plus manual describes the server list: green means connected, grey means the connection failed, and a twinkling icon means it is still trying. Grey on every phone points at the house; grey on one phone points at that phone.
  4. Did the internet change? Ulster towns are mid-way through fiber builds. A new provider means a new gateway, and a recorder set up years ago with a fixed address from the old router can end up on a network it cannot reach.

What We Install

Super Live Plus App Support & Repair Services in Ulster County

Every Ulster job starts from one question: who is in the house, and who is watching it? The services below are the pieces we combine to answer it, on the recorder, the network and every phone that opens Super Live Plus.

Indoor channels retired properly

An indoor camera that is merely unplugged still shows as a channel and still sits in the house. We remove the camera, delete or disable the channel on the recorder, take it off every phone’s view, and record what was removed and when.

Privacy masks on outdoor views

Windows, a neighbour’s yard, an outdoor shower or a hot tub at the edge of a driveway shot can be blanked inside the recorder. The TVT NVR manual allows up to four mask areas per camera; we draw them, then check the result on the phone, not just on the monitor.

Audio switched off where it should be

Many cameras carry microphones. On a rental porch or patio we switch audio recording off on the recorder and the cameras, so guests’ conversations are never captured and two-way talk cannot be opened by accident.

Alerts aimed at the responder

Push alerts from the recorder can run on a schedule. We set motion areas on the driveway and gate, decide which events reach the owner, the local host or the cleaner, and stop the all-night pinging from deer, headlights and swaying branches.

Checkout and arrival clips

We show the host how to pull a clip by time from the app and how to export a clean copy from the recorder to USB, so evidence for a damage claim or a neighbour’s complaint is saved before the drive overwrites it.

Recorder kept out of guests’ reach

The recorder, router and modem go into an owner’s closet or locked cabinet with power protection, the guest Wi-Fi is separated from the camera network, and the router is set so a guest reset does not strand the system.

Offline recorders brought back

When the app shows grey, we trace the break through power, router, internet line and the recorder’s NAT link or forwarded ports, re-add the recorder by serial or QR code, and test from outside the property on cellular.

Logins cleaned up after many hands

Rental houses collect phones: past managers, a former partner, the handyman. We list every account on the recorder, retire the old ones, change the administrator password and leave the owner with the only copy.

Firmware and security checks

TVT’s NVMS-9000 firmware before version 1.3.4 carries a published flaw that can leak administrator passwords from an exposed port. We check the version, close forwarded ports that are no longer needed and update where the maker offers it.

Ulster’s housing is old, spread out and increasingly shared with visitors. The Census Bureau’s American Community Survey counts about 86,200 homes in the county, a quarter of them built before 1940, and 6,545 held for seasonal or occasional use — 70 percent of the homes in Hardenburgh, 55 percent in Denning and a third in Shandaken. Each kind of building puts the recorder, the cameras and the guests in a different relationship.

Catskills cabin rented by the weekend

One open room, a sleeping loft and a deck, often with the recorder in a utility closet off the kitchen. Indoor coverage has to go entirely; the deck camera usually needs a mask where it catches the hot tub, and the driveway camera becomes the main view.

Woodstock or Saugerties cottage

Older frame houses on small lots close to neighbours. Porch cameras built for package watching tend to look straight at the front windows and the next house; we re-aim or mask so the view stops at the property line and the glass.

Owner-occupied house with a guest room

The family’s own cameras — nursery monitor, back hall, garage — share the building with paying guests. Here the recorder needs a clear split between channels that stay with the family and channels that can never see the guest side.

Kingston two-family or row house

Stockade and Rondout buildings with a rental unit upstairs and a shared entry. Hallway and stair cameras are the hard case: a common area in a multi-unit building is treated differently by Vrbo from the inside of a single home, and the rules for each unit have to be clear.

New Paltz student house

Shared houses near the SUNY campus with a landlord who owns the cameras and tenants who change every year. Driveway, porch and trash-area views are common; anything pointed at bedrooms, bathrooms or common rooms where tenants live is not something we set up.

Farm stay, orchard cottage or barn

A guest cottage on a working farm off Route 32 or Route 209, with barn and equipment cameras on the same recorder. The farm channels keep running; the cottage channels are cut back to the parking area and the path.

Old walls, short cables and the angle a guest sees

On an old Ulster house the cable usually decides where a camera went, not the owner. Coax or network runs come up through a wall cavity, along a joist or through a window frame, and the camera sits at the end of whatever length was in the box. When we retire an indoor camera we pull or terminate that cable rather than leave a live run behind a guest-room wall, and we label the end in the recorder closet so the next person does not plug a camera back in without knowing what it was for.

Outdoors, the aim of each camera is set by what it must not see as much as by what it should. On a cottage with the porch two steps from the living-room window, a slight tilt down and a mask across the glass is usually enough. On a cabin where the deck wraps around a hot tub, we often move the camera to the corner of the building so the hot tub falls out of frame entirely, because a mask can be switched off by anyone with administrator rights, while a different mounting angle cannot. Where a property sits inside a Kingston historic district, we keep to existing mounting points and cable paths, since the city’s preservation commission reviews material changes visible from the street and ordinary maintenance is exempt.

Rules that touch rental cameras in Ulster

  • Airbnb: no indoor security cameras in any listing since April 30, 2024, whatever their location or disclosure; outdoor cameras and noise decibel monitors must be disclosed before booking; outdoor cameras may not monitor indoor spaces or private outdoor areas such as an enclosed shower or sauna.
  • Vrbo: cameras and audio recorders are not allowed inside a vacation rental or pointed in from outside; outdoor camera location and coverage must be described in the listing (a photo of the camera is not enough); a camera that sees a pool or hot tub must also be disclosed at the property.
  • New York Penal Law §250.45: unlawful surveillance in the second degree, a class E felony, covers recording a person without their knowledge or consent in places such as bedrooms, bathrooms, changing rooms and rooms assigned to guests, where they have a reasonable expectation of privacy.
  • New York Penal Law §250.05: eavesdropping — recording a conversation without the consent of at least one party — is a felony, which is why audio stays off on rental cameras.
  • Ulster County: hotel, motel and short-term rental operators register with the Department of Finance within three days of starting business, at no cost, and receive a Certificate of Authority to collect the four percent county occupancy tax.
  • Towns and villages: rental permits, inspections, caps, owner-presence and local-contact rules differ by town — check the town or village code office for the specific address.
  • Ulster County Chapter 185: electrical work, including wiring for signalling and data, is regulated; a new outlet or circuit for a recorder closet is a job for a licensed electrician.

This is general information about published rules, not legal advice. For a rental question that turns on a specific permit, ask the town and, where needed, a lawyer. Rental hosts who also run a video doorbell at an Ulster front door should apply the same disclosure to it.

These are the questions Ulster hosts, owner-occupiers, landlords and venue owners bring us about Super Live Plus. They are grouped by the decision behind them — what the recorder may see, who it alerts, what it proves and how it survives the people passing through the house — and answered for the towns, rules and houses found here.

Making a TVT Recorder Fit the Booking Platforms

Our Woodstock cottage had a camera over the kitchen island before Airbnb’s 2024 ban — is unplugging it enough, or does it have to come out of Super Live Plus and the recorder too?

Unplugging is not enough. Airbnb’s announcement says indoor security cameras are not allowed inside listings regardless of location, purpose or prior disclosure, and rental-industry guides read the rule as covering a camera that is merely switched off: a guest who sees a lens on the wall has no way of knowing it is dead. The camera itself should come down. If it was cabled, the run should be terminated in the recorder closet or pulled, and the wall plate patched so nothing that looks like a camera mount remains in the room.

Then deal with the recorder and the app. On a TVT-platform recorder the channel can be deleted or disabled so it no longer appears in live view or records; once it is gone from the recorder, it disappears from Super Live Plus on every phone the next time the device list refreshes. If the house is also your own home for part of the year, decide now whether that camera ever goes back up — reinstalling it between bookings is exactly the kind of habit that leads to a forgotten camera during a stay. We write the change down with the date, which is useful if a guest or the platform ever asks.

The driveway camera at our Shandaken rental also catches the hot tub on the deck — what do we change on the recorder, and what do we tell guests?

You have three choices: move the camera so the tub falls out of frame, mask the tub inside the recorder, or keep the view and disclose it. Airbnb bars outdoor cameras from areas with a greater expectation of privacy and names examples such as an enclosed outdoor shower or sauna; Vrbo is more specific, saying that if an outdoor camera can see any part of a pool or hot tub, the host must state that in the listing and at a visible place at the property, such as a welcome binder or a sign near the area. Most Shandaken hosts would rather the camera simply not see the tub.

Moving the camera is the strongest fix, because nobody can undo a mounting angle from a phone. Where that is not practical, the TVT NVR manual describes privacy masks under Settings → Camera → Image → Mask Settings, with up to four areas per camera. A masked area is blanked in live view and recording. We draw the mask with a margin around the tub, then check it from Super Live Plus on a phone, because a mask drawn on the recorder’s monitor at one resolution can sit slightly differently on a sub-stream. Whatever you choose, the listing should describe the camera as it actually is.

A Vrbo guest complained that our porch camera looks through the living-room window — can a mask fix that without moving the camera?

Usually, yes. Vrbo’s policy says cameras must not be placed outside and used to capture the inside of the property, with a camera pointed through a window as its example. A mask over the window pane in that camera’s image removes the inside of the room from both live view and recording. The porch, the steps and the path to the door stay visible, which is what a host needs to see arrivals and deliveries.

Two cautions. First, glass reflects and night lighting changes what shows through it, so we check the mask in daylight and after dark with the interior lights on. Second, a guest who has already complained will judge by the camera, not the settings; if the lens still visibly faces the window, consider a small re-aim as well, and reply to the guest with what was changed. Keep the old disclosure wording in your records and update the listing so the description matches the new coverage.

Does a camera with a built-in microphone on the porch of a Saugerties rental break New York law if guests talk near it?

It can, if it records their conversation. New York’s eavesdropping law, Penal Law §250.05, makes it a felony to record a conversation without the consent of at least one party to it, and a host who is not in the conversation is not a party. Many outdoor cameras sold in kits have a microphone and record audio by default. On a rental, we switch audio off — on the camera and in the recorder’s recording settings — and confirm in Super Live Plus that playback is silent.

Two-way talk is a separate feature. Some hosts like being able to speak through a camera to a delivery driver or a lost guest. That is speaking, not recording, but it does mean the microphone is live while the session is open. If you keep two-way talk on a doorbell, say so in the house information, and do not use it on cameras near seating areas or hot tubs. Airbnb’s policy separately allows noise decibel monitors in common spaces precisely because they measure volume without recording sound — a different device from a camera with a microphone.

How should the Airbnb listing describe each camera so the words match what Super Live Plus actually records?

Write it from the channel list, not from memory. Open Super Live Plus, go through each live tile and describe it in plain terms: where the camera is mounted, what the frame covers, whether it records continuously or on motion, and whether audio is off. “Camera on the garage corner covering the driveway and parking area, video only” is the kind of line both platforms ask for; Vrbo says a photo of the camera in the listing does not count as disclosure.

The common mistake is a disclosure written when the system was installed and never revisited. A camera gets nudged by a ladder, a mask is lost in a firmware update, a new camera goes up on the shed. We leave each Ulster rental owner with a one-page table — channel number, name, location, coverage, audio, recording mode — plus a screenshot of each view as of the visit. When anything changes on the recorder, the table and the listing change the same day.

When the Owner Lives Under the Same Roof

Hurley lets us host only while we’re home — how do we keep the family’s indoor cameras from recording the guest side of the house?

Hurley’s law, as summarised in a 2026 review of Ulster town rules, allows short-term rentals only in the owner’s primary residence with the owner present for each stay. That puts your family’s own cameras — a baby monitor, a back-hall camera, a garage view — in the same building as paying guests. Airbnb’s ban on indoor cameras applies to the listing, and for a rented room inside your home the safe reading is that no indoor camera should be able to see any part of the guest’s space or the routes they use.

So the first step is a map: which rooms, stairs and halls guests use, and which stay private to the family. Indoor cameras that can see any guest area come down. Cameras that remain in genuinely private family rooms — a nursery the guests never enter, for example — should be on a separate recorder or on channels that the guest side can never be pointed at, and they should not be described as security cameras for the listing. Many owner-hosts find it simpler to keep indoor cameras off the TVT recorder entirely during the rental season and rely on outdoor views and a doorbell.

Can the recorder stop recording our hallway camera automatically on nights a room is booked, and pick it up again when we’re alone?

Technically a TVT recorder can record on a schedule, and push alerts can follow a schedule too — the NVR manual shows schedule settings under recording and under Event Notification → Push. But a schedule is the wrong tool for a camera that guests could see. A schedule does not move the lens, does not tell a guest the camera is off, and a booking that changes at the last minute will not change the schedule with it. Airbnb’s ban is about cameras inside the listing, not about when they record.

Where schedules do help is outdoors. A driveway camera can record continuously every day but only send alerts during the hours you are asleep; a gate camera at a farm stay can alert during the night and stay quiet while the farm is working. For a hallway inside a home you share with guests, we recommend removing the camera for the season rather than relying on timing.

In a New Paltz student house, can the landlord’s porch and driveway cameras stay in Super Live Plus without seeing into tenants’ rooms?

Yes, with the same discipline as a rental: outdoor views of the parking area, the porch and the trash area, masks over any window that the frame catches, and no audio. Student houses near the SUNY New Paltz campus — which enrolls more than 6,000 undergraduates — change tenants almost every year, so the system should be set up once in a way that does not depend on each year’s group agreeing. Unlawful surveillance under Penal Law §250.45 includes recording people without consent in bedrooms, bathrooms and similar rooms where they have a reasonable expectation of privacy.

Shared living rooms and kitchens inside a student house are the tenants’ home. We do not install cameras there for a landlord. For the outdoor channels, a written note to tenants listing each camera and what it covers avoids disputes, and keeping the recorder in a locked space the landlord controls keeps tenants from unplugging the system after a noisy weekend.

Alerts for the People Who Have to Respond

Esopus expects a host within about fifteen driving miles of a non-owner-occupied rental — which camera events should ring that person’s phone at 1 a.m.?

Very few. The point of a nearby host is to respond to something real: a car that should not be there, a gate left open, a guest locked out. Every alert that is not one of those teaches the host to ignore the phone. On a TVT recorder the alert rules start with the motion areas you draw on each camera — the manual allows more than one area per camera — and the push setting, which can be tied to a schedule. We draw areas on the driveway entrance and the parking pad, leave out the road, the trees and the neighbour’s lights, and set night-only push for those channels.

Then we decide who gets what. The owner in the city may want every alert; the local host in Port Ewen or West Park wants only the driveway at night. Because alerts in Super Live Plus follow the phone and account that receives them, the local host’s phone should be set up with only the channels and alert types that matter to them, and tested with a real car pulling in before the visit ends.

Our Route 212 rental is booked for four guests — how would we know from the city that a dozen cars have turned up?

The cameras will not count cars for you on most budget recorders, but the motion area can be drawn so that only the parking pad triggers it, and the recording that follows gives you the count in a few seconds of playback. Headlights sweeping across a frame from the road are the classic false trigger on a Catskills driveway; the fix is a motion area that stops short of the road edge and, where the recorder offers it, raising sensitivity thresholds on that channel.

Some newer TVT-platform recorders and cameras offer vehicle or human detection as a separate event type; where yours does, use vehicle events on the parking channel rather than plain motion. Either way, the result should be one alert when cars arrive, which the host can open to see how many there are, rather than a stream of notifications through the evening.

Can an alert from the gate or porch camera tell our turnover cleaner that the guests have actually left?

It can tell her that a car left and the porch is quiet, which is usually what she needs to decide whether to drive over. The simplest version is a motion alert on the parking channel during checkout hours sent to a phone the cleaner uses for your property. The cleaner does not need to see live video of the house during a stay for this, and generally should not.

Clips That Hold Up After a Stay

Airbnb gives hosts 14 days after checkout to file a damage claim — how do we pull the arrival and departure clips before the recorder writes over them?

Airbnb’s help centre says a host files a reimbursement request in the Resolution Center within 14 days of the responsible guest’s checkout. Most budget recorders overwrite the oldest footage when the drive fills, and a small drive on a busy driveway may hold well under two weeks. So the clips should be saved the day you notice a problem, not when the claim is drafted.

In Super Live Plus, open playback for the parking or porch channel, pick the date, and record the relevant minutes on the phone; the manual describes playback by date with forward, rewind and single-frame controls. For a cleaner copy, export from the recorder to a USB stick by time range, which keeps the original resolution and the recorder’s time stamp. Check the recorder’s clock before you rely on the time — after a power cut it can drift — and save arrival and departure for the whole stay, not just the moment of damage.

What can outdoor footage actually prove in a damage dispute, and what can’t it?

It can show who arrived and when, how many people and vehicles came and went, what was carried in or out, and whether something visible outdoors — a railing, a grill, a fence — was damaged during the stay. It cannot show what happened inside, because there should be no indoor camera, and it cannot show anything hidden behind a mask. That is the trade the platforms have chosen, and it is why the rest of your evidence matters: dated photos of each room at check-in, receipts and the cleaner’s notes.

Footage helps most when it shows a timeline. A clip of eight people arriving for a four-guest booking, followed by a broken deck chair the next morning, says more than either alone. We set channels so the arrival point is clear and well lit at night, and we make sure the time stamp on the image is legible on a phone screen, because that is often where the clip will be watched.

A neighbour on our Olive road says a renter’s car clipped his mailbox — can we share that one clip without handing over our login?

Yes, and you should never hand over the login. Find the moment in playback, save a short clip to your phone or export it from the recorder, and send the file. The Super Live Plus manual describes a press-and-hold option that shares a device by QR code; that shares access to the recorder itself, which is far more than a neighbour needs and hard to take back. A clip is a file; a shared device is an open door.

Before you send it, watch the whole clip to be sure it shows only the road and your driveway, not your guests’ faces at the door or anything else you would not want shared. If the neighbour intends to report the damage to police, keep your original export untouched and send a copy. The recorder’s time stamp and a note of the channel and date make the clip easy for anyone else to rely on.

Keeping the System Out of Guests’ Hands

Guests use the same Wi-Fi as our recorder — could one of them find it on the network or open its login page?

On a flat home network, yes. Any device on the same network can usually see the recorder’s address, and the recorder’s web page or client port answers anyone who asks. The SuperLive Plus manual lists the recorder’s factory login as admin with the password 123456; a recorder that still has it is open to the first curious guest. TVT’s NVMS-9000 firmware before version 1.3.4 is also the subject of CVE-2024-14007, a flaw that can disclose administrator usernames and passwords to anyone who reaches its control port.

The fix is separation. Most current routers can run a guest network that cannot see the main one; the recorder and any owner devices stay on the main network, and guests get the guest network’s password. We also change the administrator password, retire old accounts, check the firmware version and remove forwarded ports the remote viewing no longer needs. With that done, a guest on the Wi-Fi sees the internet and nothing else.

A guest pressed the reset button on the router in our Phoenicia rental and the cameras vanished from the app — how do we make the system survive that?

A factory-reset router forgets everything: guest network, reserved addresses, any port forwards. If the recorder relied on a forward, remote viewing breaks until it is rebuilt; if it relied on a fixed address the router no longer recognises, it may lose its connection entirely. Phoenicia is also in one of the ZIP codes Hudson Valley One listed among Ulster’s worst for cell coverage, so you may not even be able to call a guest to talk them through it.

Three changes make the system resilient. Put the router and modem where guests cannot reach them, in the owner’s closet with the recorder. Use the recorder’s NAT link, which reaches TVT’s servers outbound and does not depend on forwarded ports, as the remote path. And set the recorder to take its address automatically with a reservation on the router, so that if the router is reset the recorder still gets an address and the app comes back once the internet does. We leave a printed card in the closet with the router’s settings in case a reset happens anyway.

Ulster Rules and Paperwork

Kingston caps whole-home rental permits at about 106 — if we lose ours and go back to a long-term tenant, what changes on the cameras?

The camera rules change from a platform’s policy to ordinary landlord-and-tenant privacy. A long-term tenant’s apartment is their home; cameras inside it are out of the question, and outdoor cameras that see into their windows deserve the same masks as before. What usually changes is who watches. With a tenant in place, the owner rarely needs live alerts from the porch, and the tenant may reasonably ask what the cameras cover.

In practice we tidy the system for its new use: remove alert recipients who belonged to the rental operation, such as cleaners and co-hosts; decide with the tenant whether they get their own login to an entrance camera; and make sure the recorder is not on the tenant’s internet account. Does registering the rental with the Ulster County Department of Finance, or the state’s new registry, involve the cameras at all?

Not directly. The county registration gives you a Certificate of Authority to collect the four percent occupancy tax and must be displayed where occupants will see it; it says nothing about security equipment. The state law signed in December 2024 and amended in February 2025 lets counties create registries that record addresses, lengths of stay and guest numbers. Neither asks you to list cameras.

Your town’s rental permit is where cameras can come up, because some towns inspect each year and an inspector may look at the house as guests would. A camera in a bedroom or bathroom would be a problem for any reasonable inspector; a disclosed driveway camera is not. Keep your one-page camera table with the permit papers so you can show it if asked.

AI Overview Reality Check

What AI Gets Wrong About Super Live Plus App Support & Repair in Ulster County

We could not read Google’s AI Overview answers directly from our build environment, so this section tests seven claims that recur in hosting forums, rental-camera blogs, help-desk answers and summary sites, including the cost pages run by Angi, HomeAdvisor and Fixr. Each is checked against the platforms’ own policies, New York law and the TVT documentation.

“Indoor cameras are fine on Airbnb as long as you disclose them”

That was the rule until 2024. Airbnb’s own newsroom post explains that it previously allowed indoor cameras in common areas such as hallways and living rooms if they were disclosed, clearly visible and kept out of sleeping areas and bathrooms. The March 2024 update replaced that with a flat ban, effective April 30, 2024, “regardless of their location, purpose or prior disclosure.”

Plenty of articles written before 2024 still circulate, and summaries built from them repeat the old rule. Many Ulster rentals were set up during the boom the county comptroller describes, when the old rule applied, which is why we still find disclosed living-room cameras in the field.

The correction is simple: if a camera is inside the rental, it goes. Outdoor cameras stay, disclosed, pointed away from the inside.

“A camera that’s switched off doesn’t count”

From the guest’s side, an unplugged camera looks exactly like a working one. Rental-industry guides reading Airbnb’s policy say the indoor ban applies even when a device is switched off, and Vrbo’s policy talks about devices that are not allowed inside at all, not devices that are turned off.

There is also a practical problem. A switched-off camera is one cable and one click away from being switched on again by a well-meaning helper, and a recorder channel that is merely disabled can be re-enabled by anyone with administrator rights.

So we remove indoor cameras physically, retire their channels, and note it in writing. That is the only state a guest, a platform or an inspector can verify.

“Outdoor cameras can point anywhere outside the house”

Both platforms limit outdoor views. Airbnb excludes outdoor areas with a greater expectation of privacy, such as an enclosed outdoor shower or sauna, and bars outdoor cameras from monitoring indoor spaces. Vrbo forbids outdoor cameras that capture the inside through a window and requires extra disclosure for any camera that sees a pool or hot tub.

New York law adds its own limit. Penal Law §250.45 is about recording people where they have a reasonable expectation of privacy without their knowledge, and a camera on the outside wall that looks into a bedroom window can do exactly that.

The correction is to review each outdoor frame for windows, hot tubs, outdoor showers and neighbours’ property, and use aim and masks so the camera sees only what it is for.

“Audio is fine if the camera is on your property”

Ownership of the camera has nothing to do with consent to record a conversation. New York’s eavesdropping statute, Penal Law §250.05, requires the consent of at least one party to the conversation, and the host is not a party to guests chatting on the porch.

Many kit cameras ship with audio recording on, and app reviews and forums show owners discovering it only when they play back a clip. Vrbo’s policy names audio recorders alongside cameras in its indoor ban.

On every rental we switch audio off at the camera and the recorder, then confirm playback in Super Live Plus is silent.

“Footage on your phone is all the proof a damage claim needs”

Footage helps, but it is one piece. Airbnb’s host damage protection asks hosts to file within 14 days of checkout and support the request, and outdoor cameras by design cannot show what happened inside the house.

Phone screen recordings also lose quality and can show the phone’s time rather than the recorder’s. A clip exported from the recorder keeps its resolution and time stamp, which matters when someone else reviews it.

Our advice is to export arrival and departure clips from the recorder the day a problem appears, and pair them with dated room photos and receipts.

“If guests aren’t given the app, they can’t get into the cameras”

Guests do not need the app to reach a recorder that sits on the same network they do. The SuperLive Plus manual gives the factory login as admin and 123456, and the NVD record for CVE-2024-14007 describes NVMS-9000 firmware before 1.3.4 disclosing administrator credentials to anyone who reaches its control port.

GreyNoise reported a surge of exploitation attempts against TVT NVMS9000 DVRs in spring 2025, peaking at more than 2,500 unique addresses on April 3, which shows these recorders are actively hunted when exposed.

Separating the guest network, changing the factory password, retiring old accounts and closing unnecessary ports is the correction — the app is not the only door.

“Super Live Plus is a camera brand, so replacement parts come from Super Live Plus”

The App Store lists SuperLive Plus as a free app from the seller Peng AnTai with a support link to TVT’s site, and Google Play describes it as client software for DVRs and IP cameras of compatible models. Retailers sell recorders labelled “SuperLive Plus” because that is the app they open, not because the app maker builds them.

For a host, that means there is no Super Live Plus parts counter. A failed TVT-platform recorder is replaced with a compatible recorder, and cameras are matched to the recorder’s type — analog HD over coax or network cameras over PoE.

We identify the recorder from its label and menus before quoting, so the replacement keeps working with Super Live Plus or moves the house to an app the owner prefers.

Can you give us a written list of every camera and what it covers, to copy into the listing?

Yes, every rental visit ends with one. Both Airbnb and Vrbo ask hosts to describe outdoor cameras before booking, and Vrbo says a photo of the camera alone is not enough, so the table is written to be pasted straight in.

If we disable an indoor camera’s channel, does the footage it already recorded disappear?
+

Not straight away. Disabling or deleting a channel stops new recording; what is already on the hard drive stays until the recorder overwrites it in the normal cycle. If that older footage shows the inside of a house where guests stayed, it is better not to keep it. We can show you how to let it age out, or clear it, before the drive goes to anyone else.

Is the video doorbell at the front door of an Ulster rental still allowed under Airbnb’s rules?
+

Yes, when it is outside and disclosed. Airbnb’s 2024 announcement names doorbell cameras among the devices that remain permitted, provided guests are told about outdoor cameras and their general location before they book, and that no outdoor camera monitors the inside of the listing. A doorbell that looks through a glass storm door into the hall is the exception to check; a mask over the glass usually solves it.

Should a noise monitor go into Super Live Plus alongside the cameras?
+

No. Airbnb describes noise decibel monitors as devices that measure sound level only, without recording or transmitting conversations, allowed in common spaces when disclosed. They run in their own apps. Super Live Plus shows cameras and doorbells on a TVT-platform recorder; a camera microphone in the app is a different thing and should stay off on a rental.

During a stay, should outdoor cameras record all the time or only on motion?
+

For the driveway and front door we usually set continuous recording, so there is no gap when someone walks in slowly or a motion trigger is missed at night, and use motion only to decide when alerts go out. On a small drive, continuous recording shortens how many days are kept, so we check the drive size against your typical gap between stays before choosing.

Our keypad lock has its own small camera — does it belong in the same app as the recorder?
+

Only if it is built to work with Super Live Plus, and most smart locks are not; they live in their own app. That is fine. What matters is that the lock camera is counted in your disclosure like any other outdoor camera, that its audio settings are checked, and that guest codes are changed for every stay. We list it in the camera table even when it sits in a different app.

Is it enough to mention the cameras in the house manual instead of the listing?
+

No. Airbnb requires outdoor cameras to be disclosed before guests book, and Vrbo requires the location and coverage to be clearly disclosed in the property listing. The house manual is a good second place — and for Vrbo, a pool or hot tub that a camera can see must also be disclosed at the property — but it does not replace the listing.

Can you confirm in writing which channels were removed or disabled, in case a guest or the town asks?
+

Yes. The camera table we leave records which cameras were taken down, which channels were deleted or disabled on the recorder and the date of the visit. Keep it with your town permit papers. It is a record of what we changed, not a legal opinion; if a dispute turns on the law, take it to a lawyer.

A guest has asked for the footage of their own stay — should we send it?
+

Talk to the platform first, and do not send anything that shows other people. Outdoor footage of a stay can include neighbours, cleaners and other guests.
