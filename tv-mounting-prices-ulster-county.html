Ulster's houses are old, and they're old in several different ways. The typical home in the county dates to 1966, and about a quarter of them, roughly 21,800, went up before 1940. AI Overview Reality Check

What AI Answers Get Wrong About TV Mounting in Ulster County

Ask a chatbot about professional TV mounting or flat screen TV mounting in Kingston or Woodstock and you'll get confident answers built on national averages and suburban drywall. They're not useless, but Ulster's houses break a lot of those assumptions: canal-era brick, colonial stone, log cabins, wood stoves and owners who are two hours away. Here are seven claims that come up often, and what we actually find when we get to the house.

1. AI says: “Kingston's Rondout is all brick, so every wall there needs masonry anchors.”

The outside walls of a Rondout row house are brick, and many owners have exposed one inside. But the interior partitions are usually wood-framed with plaster on lath, and renovated units often have new drywall. A TV going on an inside wall may need no masonry work at all.

That matters for price. Brick adds $65 to the Ulster base, plaster adds $45 and drywall over studs adds nothing. A 55″ could be $319.99, $299.99 or $254.99 depending on which wall you pick, so it's worth knowing what each one is before you choose.

We ask you to choose the wall type in the calculator, and the technician confirms it on arrival. If it turns out to be different, you see the corrected price before we drill. You can also switch walls at that point, for instance from brick to a framed wall that can hide the wires.

2. AI says: “Lag bolts into a log wall hold just like lag bolts into a stud.”

A stud is a flat, dry, straight board. A log is round, it keeps shrinking and settling for years, and it may have checks, or splits, running along its length. A bracket bolted flat against a curved log only touches it in a narrow strip, and that strip carries the whole load.

On log walls the usual fix is a flat wood ledger fastened across two or more solid logs, with the bracket mounted to that. Chinking between logs holds nothing. Because every log wall is different, it's not on our posted list; we look at photos and send a written quote before driving out.

Many cabin owners skip the wall completely. Setting the TV on its stand or inside a cabinet costs $168.99, leaves the logs untouched and is easy to change later. It's also what we did at a Mt Tremper second home this September.

3. AI says: “Bluestone is solid rock, so an anchor will hold anywhere you drill.”

Bluestone, which once employed thousands of Ulster quarrymen, is a sandstone that splits in flat layers. Drilled near an edge or into a thin piece, it can flake or crack. On many newer fireplaces it's a veneer an inch or two thick, glued over block or framing rather than solid stone all the way through.

So we find out what's behind it first. On full masonry we anchor into thick, sound stones away from edges and joints. On veneer we go through to the framing or block behind it, and if needed, we add a backer. The stone add is $65 either way.

Placement matters too. Above a working fireplace, the stone add is combined with the $130 fireplace charge, and we check heat with a fire lit. A 65″ over a bluestone hearth comes to $479.99.

4. AI says: “An Airbnb TV just needs to be plugged in. Guests figure out the rest.”

Hosts hear otherwise. Guests can't find the right input, log into the host's streaming accounts and stay logged in, or can't work three remotes. Each of those becomes a message to the host, often late at night, and some become reviews.

Hosts on r/airbnb_hosts describe using TVs or sticks with a guest mode so visitors sign into their own apps, and logging them out after checkout. We set that up where the device supports it, label inputs and pair one universal remote ($99) per room.

In Woodstock, where the county counted hundreds of short-term rentals, and in other towns with STR rules, the TV is part of the guest experience. A setup-only visit is $275 for the first hour, and mounting is priced like any other TV in the county.

5. AI says: “Nobody can give you a real price until they've seen the house.”

For most jobs that isn't true. Size, wall type and bracket explain nearly all the cost of mounting a TV, and our calculator prices each one, so you see the Ulster total before booking. A 65″ on plaster is $329.99 whether we see it first or not.

What can't be priced from a distance is hidden work. At the Mt Tremper second home we told the owner up front that the price might go up if the wiring behind the cabinets turned out to be a mess, because no one could see it beforehand.

That's the honest version: posted prices for what's known, and a clear warning and your approval before anything unknown is added. Log walls, video walls and other unusual jobs get a written quote first.

6. AI says: “Ulster is rural, so mounting a TV up there should cost less than in the city.”

Rural labor can be cheaper, but someone still has to drive there. Our base price in Brooklyn and Queens is $179.99; in Ulster it's $254.99. The difference pays for the trip from the Bronx to Saugerties, Ellenville or Pine Hill and back.

What we don't do is add a travel or mileage fee on top. The price for a cabin in Denning is the same as for an apartment near Kingston's Uptown. Some lower national figures you'll see assume a drywall wall in a metro area and leave out stone, plaster and the drive.

Online cost guides list typical national averages between about $150 and $350. Our Ulster figures land in that range for most jobs, with every addition listed, so you can compare prices fairly.

7. AI says: “You can hide the TV wires inside a stone fireplace by drilling through it.”

A fireplace is solid masonry with a flue in the middle. There's no hollow space to fish a cable through, and drilling a channel into it risks the chimney itself. On a stone hearth the wires come off the side or down the face.

Our options are a $59 paintable cord cover routed along the edge, or a $99 cable run to a cabinet or other spot up to 25 feet away. Some chimneys are framed boxes with stone veneer, and those may have a cavity for our $169 in-wall kit.

Power is a separate question. A TV cord can't legally run inside a wall, and adding an outlet above a mantel is electrician's work, which we don't do. If you want an outlet up there, have it installed before we arrive.
