## Three adjacent neighbourhoods, three genuinely different trades

This was the hardest differentiation problem in the silo so far — three areas sharing a name and a border. The theses are not stylistic variations, they describe different work:

**Pelham Bay** — two doors, two entirely separate systems. The two-family brick stock almost always has a front entrance and a side or basement entrance, and those were usually installed years apart with **separate transformers and no electrical relationship**. A working front door proves nothing about the side. This is the exact inverse of Norwood, where several street doors share one board.

**Pelham Gardens** — this is a doorbell neighbourhood, not a buzzer one. Detached single-family housing where what people call a buzzer is a transformer, a button and a chime with **no electric release at all**. Different equipment, different faults, different diagnosis. The commonest fault is a silent transformer nobody can locate; the commonest complaint is that the household cannot hear the bell rather than that it does not ring.

**Pelham Parkway** — large prewar buildings with hundred-line panels where the directory drifted for the *opposite* reason to Morris Park's. Not turnover — long tenancy. Changes are so rare that nobody has a process, and a handful of unverified relabels across thirty years leaves a panel nobody trusts. Also covers, factually and without overstatement, why a failed release matters more in a building where many residents depend on entry access for home care and deliveries.
