The Neighborhood

Why Long Island Properties Need eufy Camera Installation

Long Island is a place of driveways, side doors and back yards, and that shapes what a eufy system has to do here. The US Census Bureau's American Community Survey for 2023 counts 477,860 housing units in Nassau County, 364,529 of them one-unit detached houses, and 579,790 in Suffolk, 463,095 of them detached. In other words, most Long Islanders are not watching a single apartment door off a hallway. They are watching a front step, a driveway with one or two cars on it, a side gate, a back slider and, in a lot of cases, a garage that stands on its own at the far end of the lot. That is exactly the kind of property eufy sells into: wireless cameras that can go up without running cable through finished walls, battery models that can sit on a garage corner with no outlet, doorbells that use the wires already at the door, and a HomeBase that stores video in the house instead of in a monthly cloud plan. The catch is that every one of those conveniences depends on a radio signal, a power source and a setting in an app, and Long Island houses are hard on all three in their own ways.

Start with age. The same Census survey puts the median year built at 1955 in Nassau and 1970 in Suffolk. A Newsday analysis of nearly 12,000 home sales in 2026, quoted in a Newsday social post, found that more than 5,000 of the houses sold were built in the 1950s and 1960s. Levittown is the extreme case and the best known. Levitt & Sons put up 17,447 homes there between 1947 and 1951 on old potato fields, and the ranches were built on concrete slabs with radiant heating pipes cast into them and an unfinished expansion attic overhead. There is no basement in that house. When an installer wants to get a wire from the router to the front door, or from the kitchen to the garage, there is no cellar ceiling to run it along. The route is the attic, the eave or the outside of the house, and the slab is off limits because of the heating pipes. In Uniondale, NeighborhoodScout describes the housing as predominantly capes and ranches built from the 1940s through the 1960s, with 55.45 percent of the housing from that era, and Homes.com describes the blocks south of Hempstead Turnpike as single-family capes and minimal traditionals, often with detached garages and private driveways. For eufy that means a lot of homes where the router sits in a back bedroom or a finished upstairs, and the camera that matters most is out at the garage, past several walls and an open driveway.

Then look at what Long Islanders have actually been dealing with. In January 2024, ABC7 reported that Nassau and Suffolk police, the district attorneys, the NYPD, the State Police and the FBI had formed a task force on burglaries and car thefts, and that police believed burglars were attracted by cars parked in people's driveways. In June 2026 the LI Herald reported four men sentenced for a key-fob burglary spree through Woodmere, Lawrence, Hewlett Harbor and Franklin Square, with the district attorney urging residents to consider cameras and alarms. In November 2025 CBS New York reported that the Suffolk DA had charged 14 men in a package theft ring that worked 31 communities, including Brentwood, Central Islip, Shirley and Deer Park, and allegedly took more than 200 phones and tablets from deliveries. Closer to the Nassau Hub, Patch's Garden City crime log reported a resident's stolen package in March 2026 and packages taken from a front porch in September 2026, and Garden City News reported police responding to a Pell Terrace home after a man approached the front door and may have been recording video. A doorbell camera and a driveway camera are the obvious tools for that list, and the New York Attorney General's own package-theft advice recommends exactly that.

But two Long Island stories also show where a wireless system can let people down, and we would rather say so here than after you have bought one. In May 2026 Daily Voice reported that burglars in North Valley Stream repositioned a homeowner's cameras and covered the lenses with tape while the family was away. And CBS New York reported a Long Island man who believes thieves jammed his home cameras to steal a personal watercraft from his driveway, with a security expert's advice summed up as "hardline it." News 12 Long Island has carried the same police advice: hardwire your system rather than rely on Wi-Fi alone. eufy's battery cameras and HomeBase depend on wireless links. That does not make them a bad choice; it makes the mounting height, the placement of the HomeBase, the offline alerts and the choice of which cameras should be wired the parts of the job that decide whether the system holds up on the night it matters. Those are the parts we spend our time on.

In 2025 Attorney General Letitia James announced a settlement of four hundred fifty thousand dollars with three companies that distribute eufy cameras, after her office found that, in certain situations, video sent over the internet from eufy products was not protected by end-to-end encryption and that part of the connection had no encryption at all. The companies agreed to implement proper encryption, run regular penetration testing and keep a vulnerability management program. On March 2, 2026, National Today reported that Representative Elise Stefanik and Senator Rick Scott had asked the FCC and the Commerce Department for a formal investigation into Anker, eufy's parent company. The Trust Question

eufy After the New York Settlement: What Changed and What We Switch On in Every Long Island Install

It is unusual for a camera installer to open a service page by discussing a regulator's findings against the brand it installs. On Long Island we think it is the only honest way to do it, because a large share of the people who call us about eufy have already read about the New York case, and some of them have been told by a neighbor or a relative to stay away. Here is what the public record says, and how it changes the way we set a system up.

What the Attorney General found

The New York Attorney General's office announced in 2025 that it had secured a settlement of four hundred fifty thousand dollars from three companies that distribute eufy home security cameras. Its investigation found that, in certain situations, video sent over the internet from eufy products was not protected by end-to-end encryption, and that at least part of the connection used no encryption of any kind. The settlement required the distributors to implement proper encryption, conduct regular penetration testing and maintain a vulnerability management program. One trade outlet, ChannelNews, reported a different figure of six hundred forty thousand dollars; the reports drawn directly from the Attorney General's announcement say four hundred fifty thousand, and that is the figure we rely on. Either way, the substance is the same: New York decided that a camera sold to protect a home had not protected the video of that home well enough, and required changes.

The setting that is not on by default

The Mozilla Foundation's privacy review of the eufy S350 indoor camera notes that the camera does offer end-to-end encryption for video uploaded to the cloud, but that it is not switched on by default. That single detail is why our handover includes an encryption check on every device that supports it. A homeowner who unboxes a camera, pairs it and mounts it will usually have a working camera with the default settings, and the default settings are not always the protective ones. We do not assume that every eufy model has the same menu or the same option, because product lines and firmware change. We look for the encryption setting on each device you own, turn it on where it exists, and tell you plainly which devices have it and which do not.

The 2026 federal scrutiny

On March 2, 2026, National Today reported that Representative Elise Stefanik and Senator Rick Scott sent a letter to the FCC and the Commerce Department asking for a formal investigation into Anker, eufy's parent company, citing spying fears. A request for an investigation is not a finding, and as of the date we reviewed this page we had not found any federal ruling that bars eufy cameras for home use. We mention it because it is real, because it is in the news on Long Island, and because some customers, particularly those whose work involves government contracts or sensitive information, may decide that this is a reason to choose another brand. That is a reasonable decision, and we install other brands too.

What we do on every Long Island eufy job because of it

  • Encryption on where offered: we look for the end-to-end encryption option on each camera and doorbell, switch it on, and confirm live view and playback still work afterwards.
  • Two-step login on the account: the eufy account is the key to every camera; we set up two-step verification on the owner's phone, not ours, and we never keep your password.
  • Local storage first: where the system records to a HomeBase or onboard storage, we set it to do so, so your everyday footage lives in your house rather than depending on a cloud plan.
  • Indoor cameras with a plan: indoor cameras get privacy modes or schedules so they are not watching the family when everyone is home, if you want that.
  • Firmware updated on day one: we update every device and the HomeBase before handover, since the settlement's vulnerability program only helps if the fixes reach your devices.
  • Shared access done properly: family members get their own logins through the app's sharing feature rather than everyone using one password.

When we would steer you to another brand

We install eufy because people want it: it is affordable, it can go up on a Long Island house without opening walls, and many models record locally without a monthly fee. But we would suggest something else if a wired system would serve the property far better, for example a large house with a detached garage, a pool house and a long driveway where wireless links would be stretched thin, or a homeowner worried about the Wi-Fi jamming that CBS and News 12 have reported on Long Island. We would also suggest something else for a business that needs a recorder its staff can manage, many cameras on one screen, or a platform its insurer or IT person specifies. The research on our other Long Island pages shows that our crews most often install wired NVR systems from brands such as Hikvision, Dahua and Lorex, with eufy as the option when a client asks for it. That is still how we work. If eufy is the right answer for your house, we set it up properly. If it is not, we say so before you buy.

Nassau and Suffolk Rules

Low-Voltage, Plug-In or Hardwired: Which eufy Jobs Need an Electrician in Nassau and Suffolk

Most eufy devices never touch a house's 120-volt wiring. A battery camera has its own battery. A solar panel charges that battery. A doorbell runs on the low-voltage wires that already go to the button. A plug-in floodlight plugs into an outlet. A window camera sticks to the glass. None of those needs an electrician, and in most cases no permit. The exception is the hardwired floodlight, and on Long Island it is a real exception, because Nassau's rules on who may touch a house's electrical system are stricter than many people expect. What the Nassau sources actually say

Town of Hempstead code, published on eCode360, says no electrical wiring or fixtures may be put into use until a final certificate of compliance is issued by an inspection agency approved by the town's Examining Board of Electricians. Long Island Electrical Inspectors' FAQ, answering a Nassau homeowner who asks whether they can do their own work, says that in Nassau County recent work must be completed by a licensed electrician approved in the town. E. H. Haines Electric's guide to Nassau permits says virtually all towns and incorporated villages in the county require permits for electrical work, naming the Town of Hempstead, the Town of North Hempstead and the Town of Oyster Bay, and villages including Garden City, Mineola and Long Beach, while noting that a one-for-one swap of a switch or outlet typically does not need a permit. A Long Island electrical contractor's guide gives typical turnaround for a residential electrical permit as three to seven business days and says most Nassau towns use the New York Board of Fire Underwriters for inspections. Those last figures are a contractor's, not the county's, so treat them as a guide.

What changes in Suffolk

The same inspector FAQ says Suffolk is different: if you live in Suffolk, you are allowed to make repairs yourself. Suffolk also licenses many trades at the county level through its Office of Consumer Affairs, under Chapter 563 of the county code, though that licensing does not apply in towns and villages that run their own. In practice, for a eufy job, the low-voltage and plug-in work is the same in both counties. The difference shows up only if your plan includes new wiring for a floodlight or an outlet, and even then the safe route in both counties is the same: a licensed electrician does the line work, the inspection happens where required, and we mount and set up the camera on the approved box.

Why we work this way

We hold a New York State security and alarm installer license, number 12000287431, and the state's rule at 19 NYCRR 195.2 requires that license for installing a camera system used to monitor for intrusion or theft. Our license covers the camera work. It does not make us electricians, and we do not pretend it does. A floodlight camera is a light fixture as much as a camera, and on a Nassau house a wrong connection on the 120-volt side is both a hazard and a problem the day you sell the house, when an inspector or buyer's engineer asks for the certificate. Splitting the job keeps everyone inside their license and keeps your paperwork clean.

eufy Camera Installation for Long Island's Architecture

A eufy system is wireless, but the house it sits on is not neutral. Siding, stucco, cedar shingle, brick, slabs, attics, split floors and detached garages all change three things: what we can fix a camera to, how far the signal travels, and where power can come from. These are the house types the Census figures, local real estate data and Long Island histories describe, and how each one changes the work.

Levitt-Era Slab Ranches

Levittown's 1947 to 1951 houses and the many like them: one floor, a concrete slab with radiant heat pipes, an expansion attic often finished long ago. No basement route, so any wire runs through the attic or along the eave.

Capes and Minimal Traditionals

The Uniondale and central Nassau type from the 1940s to 1960s: a steep roof, dormers upstairs, a concrete-block foundation, and very often a detached garage at the end of a private driveway.

Split-Levels and Hi-Ranches

The 1950s and 1960s houses around Hofstra and the Coliseum and across Suffolk: half-floors stacked at different heights, so the router on one level and a camera on another can be separated by a floor, a stair and an exterior wall.

Two-Story Colonials

Common in Suffolk, where the median home dates to 1970: tall back walls, second-floor eaves that need an extension ladder, and back yards and pools far from the front of the house.

Garden City's Older Homes

Garden City began with A. T. Stewart's 1869 purchase of about 7,000 acres of the Hempstead Plains; 44 residences from 1871 to 1878 are in a national historic district. Mounts on these houses are about trim, slate and wood.

Condos, Townhouses and HOAs

East Garden City's condos and townhouses, the gated 48-and-older Meadowbrook Pointe and similar communities, where the board usually controls what goes on an outside wall and where it may be aimed.

Multifamily Houses and Rentals

First-floor and basement apartments in multifamily houses near the Hub and elsewhere, where the tenant needs the owner's written agreement for anything drilled, and no-drill eufy options do most of the work.

Waterfront and Seasonal Homes

South Shore houses that went through Sandy, and East End homes that sit empty in winter, where salt air, wind and long absences decide which cameras go where and how they are powered.

Slab ranches: the attic is the only highway

In a slab ranch the instinct to drill down and run a wire under the floor has to be suppressed completely. The radiant heat pipes Levitt cast into the slab are why. Most eufy cameras do not need a wire run at all, which is one reason the brand suits these houses, but the doorbell and any wired accessory still need a path, and the HomeBase still needs to sit somewhere central. In these houses we usually place the HomeBase near the middle of the single floor, where it reaches both the front door and the back yard through interior walls rather than across the length of the house. If a doorbell transformer has to be found, it is often in the attic or near the heating equipment rather than in a cellar. Many of the original houses were expanded over the decades, and one Levittown remodeling firm warns that some expansions were done without permits or proper support. An addition on the back may have a different wall build and a different signal path, so we test it separately rather than assume the house is one uniform box.

Capes and their detached garages

The cape is the classic eufy house on Long Island, and the detached garage is the classic eufy problem. The garage is where the bikes, the tools and the snowblower live, it is often the first thing a thief tries, and in March 2026 Patch reported a Garden City homeowner alerted to an attempted snowblower theft by their home video. It is also usually forty or fifty feet from the house across an open driveway, with the router tucked in an upstairs dormer room or a back bedroom. We measure the signal at the garage before we commit. If it is strong, a battery camera on the garage fascia aimed back up the driveway covers both the garage door and the cars. If it is weak, the options are moving the HomeBase toward that side of the house, adding a Wi-Fi extension point in a window facing the garage, or, where the garage already has power and the owner wants the most dependable result, choosing a wired option for that one camera. A restoration contractor's guide to Uniondale warns that postwar capes and ranches there often contain asbestos materials, so any hole through an original interior surface is discussed with you first, and we avoid disturbing old ceilings, flooring or siding that might contain it.

Split-levels: count the floors between router and camera

A split-level or hi-ranch looks compact from the street but behaves like a much bigger house to a radio signal. The router might sit in a lower family room, the HomeBase beside it, and the front door camera half a level up, behind a stair, a floor and a framed wall. The camera over the back slider might be a full level up again. The smarteufy blog, a third-party guide rather than eufy itself, notes that HomeBase range can drop significantly in homes with several floors or lots of electronics. That matches what any installer sees in multi-level houses. We put the HomeBase on the level and side that sees the most cameras, and we check each camera's connection in the app before and after it is mounted. The lower level's outdoor outlet, common on these houses, is often the right place for a plug-in floodlight over the garage.

Colonials: height, distance and winter sun

Suffolk's 1970 median home is more likely to be a two-story colonial than a ranch, and the work changes with it. Second-floor soffits give cameras a better view and put them out of reach, which matters after the North Valley Stream case where burglars taped lenses. They also mean extension ladders and, for battery cameras, a battery swap that the homeowner cannot easily do. That is where solar panels earn their place. On Long Island the low winter sun matters more than the summer sun, so we place panels on the walls that face south and test that trees and the roofline do not shade them in December. Back yards and pools on colonial lots are often the farthest point from the router. We decide with you whether that camera is worth the signal effort, or whether an indoor camera aimed out a back window does the job.

Older Garden City houses and historic districts

Garden City's 1978 national historic district includes 50 original buildings from the A. T. Stewart era, 44 of them residences built between 1871 and 1878. Being on the National Register does not by itself require a permit for a camera, and we found no Long Island source that spells out whether exterior cameras need local design review in Garden City, so we treat it as a question for the village when it applies. Other towns do run review boards: the Town of Oyster Bay's landmarks commission reviews exterior alterations in its historic districts, Huntington requires a certificate of approval for exterior structural changes in a historic district, and Sea Cliff has its own commission. On any older house, historic or not, we prefer to fix cameras into painted trim boards or soffits, where a hole is easy to fill later, rather than into slate, original clapboard or masonry. Battery models help here too, because they avoid opening original walls for wire.

Condos, HOAs and rentals: permission first

In a condo or HOA, the outside wall usually belongs to the association. In a rental, it belongs to the landlord. Either way, the first step is written permission for anything drilled, and the second is a clear plan for where each camera looks. eufy's own guidance for apartment buyers is that a wired doorbell needs low-voltage doorbell wiring from a transformer, not a building intercom or access-control line, and that it does not work with intercom systems. For renters who cannot drill, eufy recommends a window camera mounted inside against the glass, working best on a first- or second-floor window. On Long Island that suits the first-floor and basement apartments in multifamily houses that are common around the Hub.

When eufy fits a business, and when it does not

Rules and practical limits for business cameras

  • State license for the installer: New York requires a security and alarm installer license for installing camera systems used to monitor for intrusion or theft, under 19 NYCRR 195.2. Ours is NYS license #12000287431, and an industry association warns that unlicensed installers can face fines.
  • Nassau home improvement license: Nassau County says it is against the law to operate a home improvement business there without a license, and its Office of Consumer Affairs lets residents check a contractor. The county also says work done only on commercial buildings does not need that license, which covers the office towers but not a home-based business.
  • Notice of cameras: New York Penal Law section 250.65, as summarized by a legal guide, exempts security systems from certain criminal liability when a written notice is posted conspicuously or the cameras are clearly visible. We recommend visible cameras and a posted sign in any public-facing business.
  • Audio stays off unless you decide otherwise: recording conversations raises legal questions an installer cannot settle. We leave microphones off on business cameras and suggest asking your own lawyer before turning them on.
  • Building and landlord rules: office towers such as the Omni and RXR Plaza have their own access and alteration rules, and landlords and villages may ask for a certificate of insurance before work begins. Send us the request with the booking.
  • Alarm permits are a separate matter: Nassau and Suffolk police require alarm permits for monitored systems with police dispatch. A self-monitored eufy camera system is not a police-dispatch alarm, but if you add professional monitoring later, check the county's alarm rules then.

Your Questions Answered

eufy Camera Installation on Long Island: Questions Answered

Permits, electricians and the 120-volt line

Will putting eufy cameras on my Long Island house need a permit from the town or village?

For most eufy jobs, no. Battery cameras, solar panels, doorbells on existing doorbell wiring, plug-in floodlights, window cameras and indoor cameras are low-voltage or plug-in work, and installer guidance for Nassau and Suffolk says standard residential camera installs typically do not need a municipal permit. The exceptions come from the electrical side and from local design rules. Any new 120-volt wiring, such as a new box for a hardwired floodlight or a new outdoor outlet, falls under town and village electrical permits, and the Town of Hempstead requires a final certificate of compliance before such wiring is used. Historic districts with review boards, such as those in Oyster Bay and Huntington, and condo or HOA boards can also control what goes on an outside wall. We check the address against those before we book.

I live in Nassau and I'm handy. Am I allowed to connect a hardwired eufy floodlight myself?

Based on the Nassau sources we found, we would not advise it. Long Island Electrical Inspectors' FAQ tells Nassau homeowners that electrical work there must be completed by a licensed electrician approved in the town, and Hempstead's code does not allow wiring or fixtures to be put in use without an approved inspection. Suffolk is more lenient for repairs, according to the same FAQ. A hardwired floodlight is a light fixture connected to line voltage, so even replacing an existing fixture touches that rule, although a straight one-for-one swap often does not need a permit. The simplest route is to have a licensed electrician make the line connection and have us mount, pair and set up the camera. If you want to avoid the electrician altogether, a plug-in model such as the Floodlight E340 uses an existing outdoor outlet.

What is the weatherproof junction box the floodlight instructions keep mentioning, and how do I know if I have one?

It is the sealed electrical box behind an outdoor light fixture, where the house wiring is joined to the fixture's wires. A hardwired eufy floodlight needs one with 120-volt AC power behind it, and as a buyer on Slickdeals was told, if there is no existing fixture, the box and wire must be installed by a qualified electrician. If you already have an outdoor light over the garage or back door, there is almost certainly a box behind it. Whether it is the right type, properly mounted, sealed and sound enough to carry a heavier floodlight camera is a separate question. Send us a photo of the existing light and, if you can do it safely with the power off, the base where it meets the wall. We can usually tell from that whether an electrician needs to replace the box first.

Doorbells on older houses

Can a eufy wired doorbell use the doorbell wires already at my 1950s front door?

Often it can, but the old transformer decides. eufy says its wired doorbell needs 16 to 24 volts AC at 30VA or more. Many older houses have the original doorbell transformer, and some of those were never meant to power a camera. We measure the voltage at the button and read the label on the transformer, which on Long Island ranches and capes is usually near the heating equipment, in the attic or in a garage. If it meets eufy's figure, the doorbell goes on the existing wires and the chime is checked. If it does not, the transformer needs replacing, and because that touches the 120-volt side, in Nassau we arrange a licensed electrician for that part. Another choice is a battery doorbell, which skips the transformer question.

My house never had a doorbell, or the old wires are dead. Can I still get a eufy doorbell?

Yes. The battery-powered eufy doorbells, such as the E340 that buyers on Slickdeals asked about, run on their own battery and connect over Wi-Fi, so they need no doorbell wiring at all. You charge or replace the battery periodically, and in the cold months on Long Island the battery may need attention more often. If you would rather have a wired doorbell where none existed, a new low-voltage run and transformer can be added, with the transformer's line connection done by an electrician in Nassau. For many capes and ranches with a covered front step, a battery doorbell is the simpler choice.

Signal, the HomeBase and the detached garage

Will a camera on our detached garage stay connected to the HomeBase inside the house?

It depends on the distance, what is in the way and where the HomeBase sits, and the only reliable way to know is to measure it on site. Detached garages are common on the cape and minimal traditional blocks south of Hempstead Turnpike, and the signal has to leave the house through an exterior wall, cross the driveway and reach a camera on the far side of the garage. We test the signal at the garage before mounting. If it is strong, the camera goes up as planned. If it is marginal, moving the HomeBase toward that side of the house often fixes it. If it is poor, a Wi-Fi extension point near a window facing the garage, or a different camera choice for that one location, is the answer, and we tell you before you pay for something that will drop out.

If the internet goes down during a storm, do eufy cameras keep recording?

The answer depends on the model and how it stores video, which is why buyers on Slickdeals keep asking. Systems that record to a HomeBase or to storage on the camera itself are designed to keep footage locally rather than relying on the cloud, but you will not get alerts or remote viewing while the internet is out, and if the power is also out, the router and any plug-in devices stop unless they are on battery backup. Long Island has seen big outages: ABC7 reported almost half of PSEG's 1.1 million customers out at the peak of Isaias. At handover we unplug your router and show you what your specific setup still records, and we can put the router and HomeBase on a battery backup so a short outage does not interrupt them.

Trust and the 2025 settlement

After the New York case about eufy encryption, is it actually safe to put these cameras on my house?

The New York Attorney General found that eufy video had, in some situations, travelled without full encryption, and the 2025 settlement required the distributors to fix that, test regularly and manage vulnerabilities. That does not make any brand risk-free, and in 2026 two members of Congress asked federal agencies to investigate eufy's parent company. What you can control is the setup. Mozilla notes that end-to-end encryption on at least one eufy model is not on by default, so we switch it on where it exists, set up two-step login, update firmware, keep recording local where possible, and set indoor privacy modes. If, after reading the record, you would rather not use eufy, we install other brands and will quote one instead.

FAQ

Long Island eufy Camera Installation FAQ

For a Long Island house, is eufy or Ring the more dependable choice?
+

Both work well when the signal and power are right, and both struggle when they are not, so on a Long Island house placement matters more than the logo. The bigger difference is how you pay to keep video: many eufy models store footage locally on a HomeBase or the device, while Ring generally relies on a cloud plan for saved recordings. Buyer forums carry complaints about both, including eufy doorbells that go offline and will not reconnect. If you already have Ring devices, staying with Ring keeps everything in one app. If you want no required monthly fee, eufy is the usual pick, with the privacy settings switched on.

Should I buy eufy gear from Amazon, a big-box store or eufy's own website?
+

Any authorized seller is fine for installation purposes. What matters to us is that the products are new, sealed, compatible with each other and the right type for your house, for example a plug-in floodlight if you have no outdoor junction box. Buy from a seller with a clear return policy, because if our pre-visit check finds a mismatch you will want to exchange it. Send us the model names before you buy and we will tell you whether they suit your property.

Is a battery eufy camera or a wired one better for my driveway?
+

A battery camera is easier to place anywhere on the house and needs no outlet, but it needs charging or a solar panel and depends on a wireless link. A camera with continuous power, such as a floodlight camera on an outdoor outlet or box, never runs flat and can record more consistently. On Long Island driveways, where police have linked parked cars to burglaries and CBS has reported a Wi-Fi jamming case, we usually suggest the most dependable power you can get for the driveway camera, and battery models for the corners that are hard to reach.

Do eufy Dual doorbells or other eufy cameras require a monthly subscription?
+

Many eufy devices are sold to record locally, on a HomeBase or on the device's own storage, without a required plan, and eufy offers optional cloud plans on top. What a specific model stores locally, and for how long, depends on the model and its storage, so we check yours and set it up to record locally wherever it can. If you choose a cloud plan, we switch on end-to-end encryption where the model offers it, since Mozilla found it was not on by default on at least one camera.

How long does a typical eufy installation take on a Long Island house?
+

A single doorbell with a transformer that passes the voltage check is a short visit. A doorbell, HomeBase and several outdoor cameras on a one-story ranch is usually a few hours. A two-story colonial with second-floor soffits, solar panels, a detached garage that needs signal work and an electrician's floodlight connection can take most of a day and sometimes a second visit for the electrician. The walk and signal test at the start adds a little time and removes most of the risk of a return trip.

How many eufy cameras does a typical Long Island home actually need?
+

Count your entries and the places you park, rather than starting from a kit size. Most Long Island houses have a front door, a driveway, a side or back door and a back yard, and many have a detached garage. A doorbell covers the front step; one camera covers the driveway and garage door if it is placed well; one covers the back door or slider. Add more for a pool, a side gate or a second driveway. We would rather install three cameras that cover what matters than six that overlap and strain the signal.

Do you install eufy systems in both Nassau and Suffolk, including the East End?
+

Yes. We cover both counties from the Nassau line out to the East End. Nassau addresses are priced on the Nassau list at 1.15 times our Brooklyn base, and Suffolk addresses on the Suffolk list at 1.20 times, mostly because of travel time on longer routes. For seasonal homes we can schedule the install before you close the house for winter, and set up the cameras and offline alerts so you know if something stops working while you are away.

Will a eufy floodlight camera work with the HomeBase I already own?
+

It depends on the exact floodlight model and HomeBase generation, and buyers on Slickdeals have asked this about several models with mixed answers. Some eufy floodlights connect straight to Wi-Fi and store video on the device, while other cameras pair through a HomeBase. eufy publishes compatibility information for each device. Send us both model names before you buy, and we will check that the pair works the way you expect, including where the floodlight's recordings will be stored.

The eufy app shows me ads before I can see my cameras. Can you change that?
+

A Slickdeals user complained about exactly that, having to dismiss promotions for other products before reaching the live feed. We cannot change how the app is built, but at setup we turn off the promotional notifications the app allows you to turn off, set up the home screen so your cameras are the first thing you see, and add shortcuts or widgets where your phone supports them. That usually cuts the steps to your front door camera down to one or two taps.

My eufy doorbell keeps going offline and will not reconnect to Wi-Fi. What usually causes it?
+

A Tom's Guide forum post noted many users reporting the same problem. On Long Island houses the usual causes are a weak signal at the front door because the router is at the back or on another level, a low-power transformer on a wired doorbell, a new router or internet provider with a changed network name or password, or a crowded Wi-Fi channel. We test each of those in turn, starting with signal strength at the door and the transformer voltage, and fix the actual cause rather than just re-pairing the doorbell until it drops again.

Should I register my eufy cameras with the police, and does that give them access?
+

You can if you live in an area with a program, and it does not give anyone live access. Garden City Police run a voluntary Private Camera Registration Program and state that it does not give them access to privately owned systems; detectives may ask you to check footage for a set time. The Suffolk County Police Camera Registry works the same way, with video retrieved at the owner's convenience. We found no county-wide Nassau police registry in our research. If you register, keep your clip-download steps handy, which we show you at handover.

Is it legal for my eufy cameras to record sound or point toward my neighbor's yard?
+

Pointing cameras at your own property, including your driveway and the street in front of it, is the normal setup, and New York law gives security systems protection when cameras are clearly visible or a notice is posted. Aiming into a neighbor's windows or private yard invites disputes, so we set privacy masks to block those areas. Audio is a separate legal question that we are not qualified to answer for you, so we leave recording audio off by default and suggest asking a lawyer before turning it on, especially on cameras near a sidewalk or a shared fence.
