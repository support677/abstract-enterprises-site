Why winter decides the hardware

A reader on a heated lobby door in White Plains lives an easy life. A reader on a barn door in Ulster County, a gate post on a Dutchess County driveway or a side entrance on an unheated garage in Orange County does not. Cold slows the batteries in wireless locks, thickens the grease in electric strikes and makes doors shrink and bind against their frames. Frost heave moves gate posts a little every year, so a gate operator that lined up perfectly in August can fight its own latch by February.

That is why we start every Hudson Valley survey outdoors, not at the panel. We look at which doors face north, which ones sit in unheated space and which ones get snow drifted against them. Those doors get hardware rated for the cold, weather-sealed readers and strikes sized for a door that will not sit perfectly square all year.

Fail-safe or fail-secure: decide it door by door

When power is lost, an electric lock either releases (fail-safe) or stays locked (fail-secure). Neither is right everywhere:

  • Main egress doors in a business usually need to let people out freely, so the free-egress side stays mechanical no matter what the lock does.
  • Storage rooms, server closets and equipment barns usually stay fail-secure, so an outage does not leave them open.
  • Driveway gates need a manual release that someone can actually reach in a snowstorm, plus a plan for deliveries and emergency vehicles.
  • Weekend houses need a mechanical key override the owner can find, because the owner may be two hours away when the grid fails.

Backup power that lasts a real outage

Ice storms and nor'easters regularly knock out lines across Central Hudson, NYSEG and Orange & Rockland territory, and rural repairs can take longer than in town. A controller with a small battery covers a blink. It does not cover a weekend. For properties where the outage is the main worry, we size the battery for the number of doors and the expected hours, put the internet router and the access controller on the same backup, and add a cellular path so you can still lock, unlock and see entry logs from your phone when the cable modem is dark.

What a Hudson Valley install day looks like

  1. Walk every door and gate, outside first, and mark each one fail-safe or fail-secure.
  2. Check door alignment and gaps, since a binding door defeats any electric strike.
  3. Run cable, mount readers and controllers, and seal outdoor penetrations.
  4. Set up users, schedules and phone access, then pull the power on purpose to test every door's outage behavior with you watching.

Questions to ask before you sign

  • What happens at each door if power is out for 24 hours?
  • Is the outdoor hardware rated for sub-freezing operation?
  • Who can unlock the gate remotely if the internet is down?
  • Can I add a door or a second property later without replacing the system?
