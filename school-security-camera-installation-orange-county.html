The Vocabulary on an Orange County Camera Quote

NVR / DVR

The two recorder generations — network recorders take digital IP streams; DVRs digitize analog coax. Your closet’s current occupant decides between migration and fresh build.

PoE

Power over Ethernet: one Cat6 run feeding both electricity and video, which is how a camera reaches a gate post or a barn eave without trenching power across a field.

WDR

Wide dynamic range — the processing that reads a face in a dark entry while sun blasts the lot behind it. Open-country light swings make it non-negotiable here.

Person / Vehicle Classification

Alerts that fire for people and cars and stay silent for the deer that cross nightly and the bears that visit routinely. In Orange, this setting is the system.

Retention

Days of recorded history before overwrite. County builds open at a thirty-day floor and stretch to sixty or ninety as drives allow.

NDAA §889

The federal exclusion: when NPSE, nonprofit-security, or other federal money funds the buy, certain manufacturers are barred. Our default list clears it in advance.

EoC

Ethernet-over-coax converters carrying modern IP down cable a 2010 installer left behind. Clean copper keeps ceilings closed and quotes smaller.

IK10 / IP66

Impact and weather ratings. Reachable mounts and gyms take the first; everything facing a Highlands winter takes the second with stainless hardware.

LPR

License-plate reading at drives, gates, lots, and yards — every arriving vehicle becomes a timestamped, searchable record before a door opens.

New York School Camera Law, Read for Orange County

Facial Recognition: Closed to Every School

The State Education Department’s September 2023 determination reaches every school in New York — district, charter, Catholic, yeshiva, independent, and religious without distinction: facial recognition may not be purchased or operated. A UTA building in Kiryas Joel and the district school in Goshen sit under the same sentence. Ordinary video was never touched — cameras, recording, and motion analytics remain fully lawful — and our installs ship with face-matching disabled in firmware, the setting documented in the commissioning file that retires the question permanently.

Lawful Ground and Felony Ground

Recording in bathrooms, mikvah and changing areas, dormitory living quarters, or any space where privacy is reasonably expected is a felony under Penal Law 250.45 — and schools receive no carve-out. Everything else on the property is lawful ground: entries, corridors, stairs, lunchrooms, gyms, lots, fields, drives, yards, rooftops, mechanical rooms, perimeter. Classroom interiors we route to your board, hanhala, or counsel rather than defaulting into — our standing county drawing watches each classroom doorway from the corridor, and deliberate exceptions get initialed on the plan. On boarding campuses, coverage stops at residential thresholds, drawn precisely and documented, and heads near farm neighbors or public roads are composed onto school ground with the framing photographed at survey.

When Footage Becomes a Student Record

FERPA can fold identifiable video into a student’s education record once it drives a disciplinary decision — bringing parental viewing rights and disclosure limits — and New York’s Ed Law 2-d attaches binding contract terms to any outside vendor holding student-identifiable data. We default to the architecture that keeps this simple: recorder inside your building, drives on your inventory, a written review-and-release procedure at handover, and off-site copies only under a 2-d-conforming agreement. The eventual difficult parent conversation then opens a binder, not a debate.

District buildings draw on the state’s $2 billion Smart Schools bond, which covers camera projects so long as facial recognition never enters scope. Orange’s nonpublic schools — and Kiryas Joel files at a scale few counties anywhere match — use NPSE reimbursement, with federal nonprofit-security and school-safety grants stacking above and carrying the §889 hardware exclusions along. Field Notes: Twenty-Six Cameras, a River-City Parish School, and the 3 A.M. Ladder Crew

Twenty-six cameras at a parish school in one of the county’s river cities, installed across three summer weeks while the building stood empty. The school occupied a handsome old masonry building — thick walls, high ceilings, a flat roof crowded with HVAC units — and it had been hit twice in eight months by the same regional plague: copper crews working the rooftop mechanicals at night, cutting line sets and condenser coils for scrap weight, leaving five-figure damage each visit for a few hundred dollars of metal. The pastor’s brief was blunt. The parish had already paid two deductibles, the carrier had put non-renewal in writing, and the third strike could not happen. Beyond the roof, the standard needs: a verified entrance for September, corridor and lunchroom coverage, the lot where dismissal knotted up, and a recorder that would actually be recording when someone finally asked.

The masonry set the method. Drilling century-old brick walls for every run made no sense, so we worked the bones the building offered — existing riser paths, an attic crawl that ran the full length of the main corridor, surface raceway painted to the trim where the plaster was too good to cut. The entrance got the vestibule treatment: eye-level lens outside, trap camera inside, live monitor at the school office, release on recognition. The roof got the package the whole job existed for: one head positioned over the mechanical cluster with a clear view of every unit, a second covering the ladder approach and hatch, both on person-classified after-hours alerting routed to the facilities director’s phone. Exteriors went in winter-grade — sealed glands, stainless, low-temp housings — because river-valley weather audits shortcuts annually. Commissioning ended with the export drill at the office desk: find, clip, save, log, two minutes flat, rehearsed until the secretary could do it with a phone ringing.

Nineteen nights after handoff, 3:07 a.m., the ladder-approach camera fired. Two figures, an extension ladder off a pickup parked in the alley shadow, headlamps taped down to slits — a crew that had clearly done this before. The facilities director had live view open by 3:09 and the city police department’s desk on the line by 3:10; the pair made the roofline, met the second camera face-on, and were gone inside four minutes without touching a coil. The export went in that morning — faces, the pickup, a partial plate — and detectives matched it against a string of church and school rooftop jobs running the river towns. The carrier renewed with the clips in the file. The units have not been touched since, and the pastor’s renewal letter now reads differently than his last one did — which was the entire assignment.

From the land inward: vehicle entries and drives first, then building doors, then corridors and gathering spaces, then field edges, rooftops, and yards. Is facial recognition allowed in any Orange County school?

In none. SED’s September 2023 order closed facial recognition to every New York school — district, charter, Catholic, yeshiva, independent — without exception. Conventional video was never restricted: cameras, recording, and motion analytics remain fully lawful. Our systems ship with face-matching switched off in firmware, and the commissioning file records that state in writing.

Can cameras go inside classrooms?

Often lawful, never our default. That call routes to your board, hanhala, or counsel; our standing county drawing covers each classroom doorway from the corridor — every entry and exit logged, no lens inside the learning space — and any deliberate exception gets initialed on the plan before a bracket goes up.

Which areas can never be recorded?

Bathrooms, changing and mikvah areas, dormitory living spaces, and anywhere privacy is reasonably expected — Penal Law 250.45 treats recording there as a felony and gives schools no exemption. Lawful ground covers the rest: entrances, corridors, stairs, lunchrooms, gyms, lots, drives, fields, rooftops, mechanical rooms, and perimeter, with boarding-campus residential thresholds drawn precisely on the plan.

We won’t commission under thirty days — school matters surface on a delay — and sixty or ninety is available whenever the drive budget stretches.
