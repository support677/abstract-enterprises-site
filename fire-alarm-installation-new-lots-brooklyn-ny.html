The Neighborhood

Why New Lots Fire Alarm Work Starts With Who Holds the Keys and What the Walls Are Made Of

New Lots fire alarm work starts with two questions most neighborhoods never have to ask: who is responsible for the building this year, and what is it built of. Both answers have changed recently here. New Lots is the old heart of what is now called East New York; Wikipedia's article on East New York says the area "was founded as the Town of New Lots in the 1650s," was ceded from Flatbush in 1852 and was annexed to the City of Brooklyn as its 26th Ward in 1886, and it describes New Lots today as "a sub-section of East New York." The neighborhood sits in Brooklyn Community District 5. City Neighborhoods NYC draws it between New Lots and Dumont Avenues to the north, Linden Boulevard to the south, Pennsylvania Avenue to the west and Fountain Avenue to the east, while New York YIMBY has described blocks just south of Linden Boulevard near the L tracks as part of the same New Lots subsection. This page covers the blocks around New Lots Avenue and both sides of Linden Boulevard; neighboring areas have their own pages.

City Neighborhoods NYC says Dutch farmers called this tract "Nieuw Lots" by 1670 and that their homesteads lined the ridge that became New Lots Avenue. The 1922 IRT extension to New Lots Avenue brought the brick two-family houses and small apartment buildings of the 1920s and 1930s, and after the war public housing filled big blocks near Linden Boulevard.

The biggest change of the last five years is in the towers. On December 29, 2021, NYCHA announced that Linden Houses and the Pennsylvania Avenue-Wortman Avenue Houses, 1,922 apartments in 22 buildings, had closed a renovation deal under its PACT program, which brings in private partners through the federal Rental Assistance Demonstration. New York YIMBY counts 21 Linden buildings with 1,586 apartments from the 1950s, across from Linden Park, and three 1970s Penn-Wortman towers with 336 apartments at 851 to 895 Pennsylvania Avenue, run by C&C Apartment Management for a joint venture called Stanley Avenue Preservation. The same week, NYREJ reported that Boulevard Houses, with Fiorentino Plaza and the Belmont-Sutter Area elsewhere in East New York, closed its own PACT financing: 1,673 apartments in 29 buildings, managed by Lisa Management and PRC Management.

Why does a change of manager matter to a fire alarm? Because its duties stay with the building: keeping the system maintained, the central station connection live, the inspection records kept and every FDNY Notice of Defect answered. When Brownstoner covered the first weeks of private management at Linden Houses, residents described losing NYCHA's 707 hotline, and resident association president Carol Barnes said, "This building is full of seniors and disabled people who are now stuck in the house," after an elevator outage. Fire alarm gaps from a handover tend to surface at the first inspection afterward.

The other change is in the materials. The New Lots Reformed Church at 630 New Lots Avenue, now the New Lots Community Church, was raised by volunteers in May 1823 from hurricane-felled oaks, its timber frame notched and joined with wooden pegs, Brownstoner reports; it became a city landmark in 1966. Next door, the Brooklyn Public Library is replacing the 1950s concrete New Lots branch at 665 New Lots Avenue with a building of about 25,000 square feet framed in mass timber, due to open in 2028. One is wood because that is what farmers had; the other is wood by choice.

So what is a commercial fire alarm system, in New Lots terms? A panel with power supplies and standby batteries; detectors and pull stations that sense or report a fire; horns, strobes or speakers that warn people; relays that recall elevators, stop fans and release doors; and a communicator that sends every signal to an FDNY-approved central station. How does a commercial fire alarm system work? The panel supervises its own wiring, so a broken conductor shows as trouble and a detector or pull station as an alarm, and then it runs the sequence the engineer designed. When does a building need a fire alarm system? The city's Building Code answers by occupancy and size; NY Engineers' summary of section 907 lists every school, every institution, assembly spaces with 300 or more occupants, and apartment buildings with apartments three or more stories above the lowest exit discharge or 16 or more units. Nearly every tower, school and larger mixed-use building in New Lots falls inside those lines, which is the frame for NFPA 72 fire alarm installation here.

Fire Alarm Repair in New Lots: Towers Under Renovation and Store Buildings Under Apartments

Fire alarm repair in New Lots mostly comes from towers under renovation, where crews opening ceilings and rebuilding lobbies disturb devices and cable every week, and from mixed-use buildings where a renovated store sits under apartments. On a fire alarm trouble emergency service call we read the panel, say plainly what is still watched and whether a fire watch may be needed, and fix the cause rather than silence the sound.

Why is my fire alarm panel showing trouble? Because the panel has found a fault in its own wiring, power or devices. Trouble is not a fire, but it can mean a floor or a stair is no longer watched. In a tower under renovation the usual causes are a detector unplugged so a ceiling could be replaced, a dust cover left on after the crew moved on, or a cable nicked when a new lobby wall went up. Whether the call comes in as a fire alarm panel beeping repair, a fire alarm panel trouble light, a fire alarm trouble signal repair or a fire alarm system trouble condition, we start with the event history and the circuit it names, then compare it with the renovation schedule.

What causes a fire alarm ground fault? A circuit leaking current into conduit, a metal box or a damp surface. New roofs and windows help a tower in the long run, but while they go in, rain gets into top-floor junction boxes and roof bulkheads. Fire alarm ground fault troubleshooting isolates the leak by splitting the circuit and watching the panel after each split, and fire alarm ground fault repair fixes the run it lands on. The same splitting method works for fire alarm SLC troubleshooting, fire alarm signaling line circuit repair, fire alarm short circuit troubleshooting and fire alarm open circuit repair.

Fires leave alarm damage behind, too. The New Year's Eve 2023 courtyard fire at Boulevard Houses and the June 2026 fire behind a restaurant on New Lots Avenue, both covered by News 12, sent water and smoke well past the flames. After fires like these a fire alarm device fault repair, a fire alarm wiring repair or a fire alarm notification circuit repair follows, with every device tested before the building is called normal again.

Losing the link to help is the most serious trouble. An alarm that cannot reach the central station may go nowhere, so fire alarm communication failure repair starts by asking the central station what it last received, then checking the radio, antenna and network path; in a converted tower we also confirm the account moved to the new manager. Anything that leaves floors unwatched, whether logged as a fire alarm system not communicating, a fire alarm communication failure emergency repair, a fire alarm system down repair, a fire alarm panel failure repair or a fire alarm system malfunction repair, goes first in our queue.

Fire alarm intermittent trouble tends to come from a loose connection that opens when a heavy door slams or a compactor chute shakes, so a fire alarm fault diagnosis looks for a pattern in the history before swapping parts. Batteries are easier: fire alarm battery trouble repair is fire alarm battery replacement plus fire alarm battery load testing to prove the new set. In a renovated tower a fire alarm supervisory trouble often means a sprinkler valve the plumbers left partly closed, and fire alarm system diagnostics confirm it before anyone climbs a ladder.

False alarms are their own problem. The Mayor's Management Report for fiscal 2025 counted 21,966 malicious false alarms citywide, apart from the far larger number caused by cooking, steam, dust and construction. In a tower under renovation, fire alarm false alarm troubleshooting usually ends at dust from sanding or drywall reaching a corridor smoke detector; in a building with a restaurant downstairs it ends at a kitchen door. Smoke detector circuit troubleshooting and fire alarm circuit troubleshooting confirm the devices and wiring are sound before anyone moves a detector or changes a sensitivity setting.

Who repairs fire alarm control panels? In this city, a company holding the FDNY's Fire Alarm Company Certificate, with at least one principal carrying the S-97; anyone servicing more than standalone smoke detectors needs it. Fire alarm control panel repair, fire alarm control panel troubleshooting and FACP repair all happen under that certificate, and filed work brings in a licensed electrician. Our fire alarm service technician works alongside the certified company and leaves a written record of what failed and what changed. A fire alarm repair company or fire alarm repair contractor that leaves nothing in writing slows every later visit, and after a management change it can stall them.

The calls that come in late at night use every phrase there is. Someone types 24 hour fire alarm repair, another after hours fire alarm service or same day fire alarm repair; a manager wants urgent fire alarm repair or emergency fire alarm repair, an emergency fire alarm technician, emergency fire alarm troubleshooting, emergency fire alarm service, fire alarm system emergency service or commercial fire alarm emergency repair. Every one of them is really asking whether the people asleep upstairs are covered tonight. Converted public housing towers and campuses

The towers along Linden Boulevard, Stanley Avenue, Wortman Avenue and Pennsylvania Avenue are the largest buildings in New Lots. Pincus's property record for one Linden parcel lists eight 14-story fireproof buildings from 1955 with 756 apartments. The renovation scope described by New York YIMBY and NYREJ includes enlarged lobbies with parcel lockers, community rooms, key-fob entry, new elevators, roofs, windows and building system upgrades, and almost every item touches the fire alarm: lobby devices and the annunciator move, doors must release, elevators must recall and roof equipment must shut down. A volunteer-built timber church from 1823

Where detection goes in, it has to be fastened without splitting original timber, routed without cutting the frame and chosen for spaces that may be unheated.

The new New Lots Library is the opposite case: a modern wood building with exposed beams on purpose. Compliance callouts for New Lots owners and managers

  • The design professional files. FDNY guidance says a New York State registered design professional, a PE or RA, prepares and certifies the fire alarm plans and files the TM-1 through FDNY Business.
  • Do not start before authorization. The FDNY authorizes the project once plans are approved; NYC Business lists the application fee as 420 dollars for a new filing, plus 525 dollars where a complex technical analysis applies.
  • Ask for two credentials. The company needs the FDNY Fire Alarm Company Certificate for any work beyond standalone smoke detectors, and a principal must hold the S-97, which requires NICET Level II or above or a New York City master or special electrician license.
  • Move the accounts with the keys. When management changes, the central station contract, the call list and the FDNY correspondence have to move too; the FDNY's 2012 bulletin requires reinspection when a central station or dialer changes.
  • Plan in months. The FDNY's average from plan submission to approval is 20 to 24 weeks, with up to eight for the first review, and the central station link has to be live before an inspection can be booked.
From the Field

Field Notes: The Binder Nobody Handed Over

These notes are written in our company voice and describe the kind of review we do when a building changes hands, not a particular job at a particular address. Picture a campus like the ones along Linden Boulevard on the first Monday after a new manager takes over: a dozen or more towers, a basement panel in each, a central station that has been calling the same phone number for years, and a renovation contractor who wants to start opening lobby ceilings next week.

The first thing we ask for is the paper: a Letter of Approval for each building, the approved and as-built drawings, the central station contract and its call list, open Notices of Defect, and the dates of the last annual inspection, battery test and sensitivity check. In a handover, half of those answers sit in an office that no longer manages the building, and without them nobody can tell which devices were approved and which were never filed.

The second step is walking every floor with the renovation schedule. An enlarged lobby means the annunciator, smoke detection and door holders move, so a modification is filed before walls come down; new elevators mean recall is retested; new roofs mean duct detectors need covers and a plan for every night the roof is open. Get the records first, design the changes second, and keep the old system working until the new work is accepted.

Your Questions Answered

Fire Alarm Questions From New Lots Managers, Congregations, Library Planners and Store Owners, Answered

Below are questions that come up when we first sit down about a New Lots building. Treat the answers as general guidance; your design professional applies the code to your address.

Our Linden Boulevard campus changed from NYCHA management to a PACT partner. What fire alarm records should the new manager have on day one?

At least five things for each building: the FDNY Letter of Approval, the approved and as-built drawings, the central station contract with its current call list, the last annual inspection and test reports, and any open Notice of Defect or violation. Request anything missing in writing before the redesign starts.

Contractors are rebuilding the lobbies and compactor rooms in our tower while residents stay home. What keeps the fire alarm working during that work?

A plan agreed before the first wall comes down. Only the devices in the work area should be covered or bypassed, only during working hours, with the central station told when and for how long, and with a fire watch where the code or the FDNY requires one. Each evening the covers come off and someone checks the panel before the crew leaves.

The new key-fob entry doors at our campus lock automatically. Do any of them have to unlock when the fire alarm sounds?

Some do. Doors on the path out of the building must always let people leave, and electrically locked doors that the code ties to the fire alarm must release on alarm. Which doors those are is set by the design professional and the building code, and the connection is drawn on the fire alarm plans and tested at acceptance.

After the courtyard fire at Boulevard Houses, should construction materials stored outside a building be covered by the fire alarm?

A building fire alarm watches the inside of the building, not a courtyard or a sidewalk. Outdoor staging falls under site safety rules and the contractor's fire prevention plan. What the fire alarm can do is make sure smoke that enters through windows or doors is detected in corridors and stairs, so keep those devices in service and uncovered next to any staging area.

The New Lots Reformed Church frame is joined with wooden pegs. Can fire alarm devices be fastened to the original timber?

They can be, but the better plan usually avoids it. Detector bases and notification appliances can often mount on later surfaces, such as plaster, ceiling boards or new backer plates, and cable can run in small surface raceway along existing trim rather than through drilled beams. The designated landmark also brings the Landmarks Preservation Commission into any exterior work, so keep conduit and devices off the outside of the building unless the design truly needs them.

While the sanctuary was repaired in 1990, services moved to the community center next door. If a congregation does that today, does the fire alarm follow it?

The fire alarm requirement follows the use. A community room that suddenly holds Sunday services may hold far more people than it was designed for, and the occupant load, exits and alarm needs should be checked before the move, not after.

The New Lots Library is being rebuilt in mass timber. Does a wooden structure call for a different kind of fire alarm than a concrete one?

Not a different kind of system. A library's fire alarm is set by its use as assembly and education space, and the same NFPA 72 rules and FDNY filing apply. What the timber changes is the detail: deep beams and radial members create ceiling pockets that affect detector spacing, and cable routes have to be planned with the structural and fire-resistance design, since exposed wood leaves fewer places to hide them.

The new library's meeting room is planned for about 150 people. Is a room that size enough on its own to require a fire alarm?

On its own, usually not; NY Engineers' summary of the code sets the assembly trigger at 300 or more occupants. But the building is judged as a whole, adding every floor, program room and the learning center, and libraries are public buildings that carry their own owner standards. Expect a full addressable system, and let the design professional confirm the trigger in writing.

The Penn-Wortman community center runs programs at the base of a residential tower. Is that space on the tower's fire alarm or its own?

It depends on how the building was approved. Community spaces built into a residential tower are often covered by the tower's panel, with their own devices on its circuits, while a separate building or a space with separate exits may have its own system. Check the drawings, and tell the program operator who to call about trouble in their room.

Our PACT renovation added parcel lockers and a bigger lobby. Who checks that the new layout still leaves the fire alarm devices where the approved drawings put them?

The design professional, through a filed modification, and the certified company at the retest. Lockers and new walls can block a pull station or a strobe. The right order is to show the lockers on the fire alarm drawings first, adjust the devices, and test before the lobby reopens.

A restaurant on New Lots Avenue reopened after a renovation. What should a building owner confirm about the fire alarm when a ground-floor tenant renovates?

Four things: that devices in the space were moved or added under a filed modification, that none were painted, covered or left disconnected, that any hood suppression link still reports to the panel, and that the work was tested and documented before opening.

Our campus has many identical towers. How does the FDNY find the right building and floor quickly when the alarm goes off?

Through clear labels at three levels: the central station's account for each building, the panel's device descriptions naming building, floor and room, and the annunciator or floor map in each lobby. Addressable systems let each device be named in plain words.

+

Many small ones do not. NY Engineers' summary of the code ties mercantile fire alarms to size, such as a fire area over 12,000 square feet, more than three stories or a large sales floor below grade, so a small one-story store usually falls outside. Add apartments above, a place of assembly, a day care or a school and the answer can change, so the design professional should review the whole building.

What fire alarm paperwork should a New Lots nonprofit ask for before signing a lease in a building with a system?
+

The FDNY Letter of Approval, the latest inspection and test reports, the name of the central station and who holds that contract, any open defects, and a copy of the device layout for the space you are leasing. Ask the landlord in writing who pays for any devices your layout needs, and whether your planned use changes the occupancy.
