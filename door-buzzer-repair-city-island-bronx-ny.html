The City Island Repair Glossary

Village doors, standard names: entry buzzer, apartment entry system, building entrance system, visitor entry system, door release system, building access system — on the island those labels run from a cottage’s front vestibule buzzer to a walk-up’s lobby entrance buzzer to a club’s gate call box, and beneath each sits the same chain: a door release button, the electric strike it commands, the buzzer transformer and buzzer power supply behind the circuit, the entry system wiring crossing kitchen wall or captain’s-house stair, and on modernized doors a remote door release riding a phone.

Service-hours repair. The row’s scheduling promise: diagnosed and fixed around the shift, because the line cannot stop for a door.

Delivery buzzer. The back-door call point that must beat a working kitchen — specified for volume and placement, not just function.

Pre-season check. The spring visit that catches what winter killed quietly — booked by the island’s smartest operators like the fire inspection.

Winter kill. A failure that happened in the closed months and surfaces at reopening. Diagnosed fast; prevented cheaper.

Cottage rig. A frame house’s bell-and-release assembly, often generations old — one small system, diagnosed whole, repaired within the fabric.

Marine-aware hardware. Enclosures, contacts and terminations chosen for doors that live in sound-side air — the waterfront’s baseline, not its upgrade.

Gate box. A club or yard’s street-end call point — outdoor hardware with outdoor failure modes, serviced accordingly.

Release path. Button to strike, end to end, on any island door. Heard but never admitted means the fault lives here and nowhere else.

Split circuit. A mixed-use building’s honest wiring: shop buzzer and apartment system separate, so neither’s fault or bill lands on the other.

Conversion pair. A captain’s house divided into two households — one panel, two stations, and the crossed-pair fault that defines its repairs.

Station. The in-unit wall unit, any generation. Fails alone, replaced alone — the rest keeps ringing around it.

Platform vs. part. The whole repair-or-replace question in four words: parts fail cheap and often, platforms rare and dear. Diagnosis keeps them separate — galley door or garden gate.
