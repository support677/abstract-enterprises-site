The Neighborhood

Why Boerum Hill Fire Alarms Are Decided at Two Edges: the Landmarked Storefront and the New Tower

Boerum Hill is a small neighborhood with an unusually wide spread of buildings, and that spread is what makes its commercial fire alarm work different from the blocks around it. The core is a landmarked grid of three-story row houses built between roughly 1840 and 1870, and the Landmarks Preservation Commission protects about 538 of those buildings across the original 1973 district and the 2018 extension. Along Atlantic Avenue and Smith Street, many of those same buildings have shops, bars and restaurants on the ground floor and people living upstairs. A few blocks north or east, the stock jumps to glass and concrete: a 44-story rental tower at 505 State Street, a 19-story condominium at 265 State Street, and the steel frame of a 15-story borough-based jail at 275 Atlantic Avenue.

The fire that best explains the neighborhood's risk happened on its western edge. On March 14, 2022, a fire that started on the second floor of the gym at 96 Boerum Place, near Pacific Street, grew to five alarms, drew more than 200 firefighters and injured 12 people, nine of them firefighters. The Brooklyn Eagle reported that the fire got into the space between the roof and the top floor, under a rooftop tennis court. "The roof was some kind of tennis court that had foam insulation," FDNY Acting Chief of Department John Hodgens said. "Once that starts burning, it's hard to extinguish." For anyone planning commercial fire alarm work in Boerum Hill, that is the lesson in one sentence: the hidden spaces of a converted or mixed-use building, the voids above ceilings and below roofs, are where a fire can grow before anyone in the occupied rooms knows.

Residential fires have hit the neighborhood's larger buildings too. On February 4, 2024, a fire that began in a back bedroom on the first floor of a six-story building at 178 Bond Street, at the Gowanus Houses, left one man dead and three people injured, including a two-year-old, according to News 12 Brooklyn; firefighters later handed out more than 50 free smoke detectors across the street. In June 2017, a fire in a third-floor air conditioner at 272 Wyckoff Street, a 21-story Wyckoff Gardens building, pushed smoke into homes on lower floors. "I got out of the building when I smelled the smoke," a fifth-floor resident told Patch. Both developments are NYCHA's, which runs its own fire safety work; we cite them for context only.

So what is a commercial fire alarm system, in the language of a Boerum Hill block? It is the control panel in the cellar or lobby, the smoke and heat detectors, the manual pull stations by the exits, the horns, strobes and speakers that warn people, the modules that shut down fans, recall elevators and release doors, and the communicator that sends signals to an FDNY-approved central station, all designed for one building and approved by the FDNY. How does a commercial fire alarm system work? Each detector and pull station reports to the panel over supervised wiring; the panel decides whether it is seeing a fire, a wiring fault or an off-normal sprinkler valve, sounds the right appliances, runs the programmed outputs and reports out. When does a building need a fire alarm system? The NYC Building Code and Fire Code answer that by occupancy group, occupant load, height and fire area size, and a change of use, a big renovation or a new building can trigger the requirement. The design professional reads those sections for your building before any equipment is ordered.

Need Fire Alarm Repair in Boerum Hill? Photograph the Screen, Then Call

Pressing reset or silence can clear the active event from the screen, and the words on that screen are what a fire alarm service technician needs most. In New York City, hands-on fire alarm repair, whether the request is for fire alarm system repair, commercial fire alarm repair or ongoing commercial fire alarm service, is carried out by a fire alarm company holding an FDNY Fire Alarm Company Certificate, together with a licensed electrician where the work calls for one, so the next question after "what does it say" is "who services this panel now."

The question we hear most on these calls is why is my fire alarm panel showing trouble when nothing is burning. A trouble is the panel reporting a problem with itself or its wiring: an open or shorted circuit, a ground, a device that has gone missing, a weak battery, a loss of building power, or a broken phone or cellular path to the central station. A lit fire alarm panel trouble light means a fire alarm system trouble condition is active right now, and proper fire alarm trouble signal repair removes the cause instead of muting the buzzer. A fire alarm supervisory trouble is different again: something the panel watches, often a sprinkler control valve or a duct detector, is out of its normal position, and the building may be less protected than its owner believes.

Boerum Hill's building stock produces its own repair patterns. What causes a fire alarm ground fault here? In the row-house-scale buildings of the historic district, the usual culprits are cable run decades ago along damp cellar walls, an old splice behind a storefront's dropped ceiling, or a device box near a leaking rear yard door. Fire alarm ground fault repair and fire alarm ground fault troubleshooting are done with a meter, by splitting the circuit until the fault has nowhere left to hide, not by swapping detectors at random. In the newer towers, troubles more often trace to construction: a cut cable or a detector bagged for dust and never unbagged. A fire alarm intermittent trouble that comes and goes with rain points to water; one that comes and goes with deliveries points to a rear door. Fire alarm battery trouble repair is the single most common fix behind fire alarm panel beeping repair calls in small storefronts, because batteries are easy to forget until the panel complains.

Building Specialists

Commercial Fire Alarm Systems for Boerum Hill's Building Types

Six kinds of buildings account for nearly all commercial fire alarm systems in Boerum Hill, and each fails in its own way, so commercial building fire alarm installation here begins with identifying which kind you own.

Landmarked row-house-scale buildings with storefronts

The 2018 extension of the historic district reached the block-and-a-half of Atlantic Avenue between Hoyt and Nevins Streets, Pacific Street between Hoyt and Bond, and parts of Dean, Bergen and Wyckoff. The LPC described Greek Revival and Italianate buildings from the 1850s to 1870s, some with historic storefronts and neo-Grec apartment buildings mixed in. When one of those buildings needs a fire alarm, usually because the ground floor became a restaurant, a bar or a larger store, the interior is where the work happens: cable up a rear stair or a chase, devices placed to the drawings, and nothing new on the front facade unless the LPC has approved it.

Restaurants and bars on Smith Street and Atlantic Avenue

Smith Street is the neighborhood's best-known dining strip, with long-running places such as Clover Club at 210 Smith Street, and Atlantic Avenue mixes bars and cafes with a large halal shopping district run by Middle Eastern and North African merchants. Kitchens get heat rather than smoke detection near the line, hood suppression reports to the panel where one exists, and larger dining rooms can trip assembly requirements. A commercial kitchen fire detection installation is planned with the hood contractor during design, never bolted on after the hood is hung.

Mid-rise rentals and condos

Between the historic blocks and the towers sits a band of six- to fourteen-story buildings built in the last twenty years: The Bergen at 316 Bergen Street (eight stories, 84 rentals, 2014), The Hendrik at 509 Pacific Street (six stories, 33 condos), 211 Schermerhorn Street (14 stories, 48 condos), as listed by 6sqft. Built with systems from day one, they mostly need tenant changes, communicator updates and deficiency corrections.

High-rise towers on the Flatbush and Schermerhorn edge

At the northeast corner of the neighborhood, Alloy Development's first tower at 505 State Street rises 44 stories with 441 apartments and was finished by April 2024; a school building at 80 Flatbush Avenue on the same site is planned to house an expanded Khalil Gibran International Academy and a new 350-seat elementary school. The Boerum at 265 State Street is a 19-story condominium. High rise fire alarm installation in buildings like these means voice evacuation, a fire command center, elevator recall, smoke control interfaces and multiple tenant fit-outs that all connect back to the base building system. Base building work on a new tower is normally carried by the developer's own team; our role in such buildings is usually tenant spaces, modifications and service once the building is occupied. Public school space inside such projects is built through the city's school construction agencies, and we do not claim that work.

Converted factories, galleries and gyms

The Invisible Dog Art Center at 51 Bergen Street opened in 2009 in a converted factory of about 30,000 square feet, and the neighborhood has other studios, performance rooms and fitness spaces in older industrial or commercial shells. The 2022 fire at 96 Boerum Place began in a gym. Open ceilings, roof voids and event-night occupant loads drive the design.

Schools, libraries and institutions

P.S. 261 Zipporiah Mills at 314 Pacific Street serves about 500 children in pre-K through fifth grade, Brooklyn High School of the Arts sits near Dean Street and Third Avenue, and the Pacific branch of the Brooklyn Public Library at 25 Fourth Avenue opened in 1905 as Brooklyn's oldest Carnegie library. Public schools and public library buildings are maintained through city agencies, so their fire alarm work is not ours to claim. Private schools, day cares and nonprofits hire their own teams, and school fire alarm installation for them follows the FDNY path.

Healthcare, hotels, storage and data rooms

Boerum Hill has no hospital of its own; hospital fire alarm installation belongs to large health systems with their own engineers. What we see here is the smaller end: medical and therapy suites in mixed-use buildings, and occasional nursing home fire alarm installation or assisted living fire alarm installation inquiries from operators of senior housing, such as the senior building planned on Third Avenue between Bergen and Wyckoff Streets, where residents may need help to evacuate and the design reflects it. Hotel fire alarm installation comes up in small and condo-hotel buildings. Storage facility fire alarm installation and parking garage fire alarm installation apply to below-grade garages under the newer buildings and the few storage rooms left on the Third Avenue edge. Where an office keeps servers, data center fire detection installation usually means very early warning detection in one room rather than a full data hall. Warehouse fire alarm installation, warehouse smoke detection installation, industrial building fire alarm installation, manufacturing facility fire alarm installation and industrial fire alarm installation are rare inside Boerum Hill itself; industrial fire alarm systems are much more common in Gowanus to the south. When a former loft or workshop does come up, industrial fire detection system installation is chosen for the dust, heat and ceiling heights actually present.

Business Fire Alarm Installation Along Boerum Hill's Corridors

Business fire alarm installation in Boerum Hill follows its streets, because each corridor has a different building stock and a different kind of tenant. Business fire detection systems here range from a single conventional panel behind a cafe counter to an addressable network serving a tower's retail base, and the hardest part is usually getting to the floors above the business without disturbing the people who live there.

Atlantic Avenue

Atlantic Avenue is the neighborhood's commercial spine and its northern edge. The business district along it covers 1.2 miles from the harbor side through Cobble Hill and Boerum Hill, with more than 300 small businesses. "We are a massive halal shopping destination in New York City," executive director Kelly Carroll told the Brooklyn Eagle in 2024. Between Hoyt and Nevins Streets the storefronts sit inside the 2018 historic district extension, which brings LPC review to exterior changes. Farther east, the block at 275 Atlantic Avenue is the construction site of the borough-based jail, a city project with its own design-build team, and residents have been vocal about its effects. "It's demolition material that we shouldn't be breathing," one Atlantic Avenue resident told BK Reader during the 2024 demolition. For neighbors, lane closures and crane picks can affect when a crew can stage equipment.

Smith Street

The northern end of Smith Street, between Atlantic Avenue and Bergen Street, is the Boerum Hill stretch of one of Brooklyn's busiest dining streets. It was also once the center of the city's Mohawk ironworker community, the area known as "Little Caughnawaga." Today the business mix is restaurants, bars, boutiques and national stores in two- to four-story buildings, most with apartments above. A restaurant opening here almost always means a fire alarm question at lease signing: whether the building has a panel, whether it has capacity, and whether the change of use makes a new system necessary.

Court Street and Boerum Place on the west edge

Along Boerum Place and the streets toward Court, the buildings get larger and more institutional, and it was here that the 96 Boerum Place fire showed how a fire in a converted commercial building can hide above the ceiling.

State, Schermerhorn and Flatbush on the north and east edge

This is where the neighborhood has grown upward. Towers at 505 State Street and 265 State Street, a 52,000-square-foot Chelsea Piers Fitness gym that 6sqft reported was planned for 265 Schermerhorn Street at the base of the 33 Bond Street rental, and the New York Transit Museum in a 1936 subway station at 99 Schermerhorn Street make this corridor a mix of high-rise residential, large-floor fitness and assembly, and institutional space. Each tenant space in a building like that is a modification to a base building system rather than a stand-alone alarm.

Third Avenue, Fourth Avenue and Nevins Street on the east

The eastern edge mixes new mid-rise housing, schools, the Pacific library branch on Fourth Avenue and the planned senior building on Third Avenue. New construction fire alarm installation is more common here than anywhere else in the neighborhood.

What the code and the FDNY expect on every corridor

Whichever corridor the building is on, the FDNY sequence does not bend. Per the FDNY's tip sheet, plans are prepared and filed by a New York State Professional Engineer or Registered Architect; the FDNY examines them under a TM-1; installation is by a NYC licensed electrician or a fire alarm vendor with an FDNY Fire Alarm Company Certificate; and the Fire Alarm Inspection Unit, which works out of 9 MetroTech Center a few blocks north, witnesses the acceptance test and issues the Letter of Approval. Strobe placement in public areas follows accessibility rules, and the historic district adds one Boerum Hill question: will anything show on the outside?

Field Notes

Field Notes: The Void Above the Top Floor

This describes a typical survey, not one specific job. An owner calls about a two- or three-story building on one of the avenues: a business on the ground floor, a second-floor tenant, a flat roof that someone once used for something. Before we look at the panel, we ask to see the top of the building, because after the 2022 Boerum Place fire the space between the top-floor ceiling and the roof deck is the first place we want eyes on. We look for a hatch or a removable ceiling tile, check what the insulation is, note whether the void runs across party walls into the next building, and photograph any old detection or wiring up there. Then we go to the cellar and do the same thing at the bottom. In landmarked buildings, we also note where the original cornice and front wall make any exterior path impossible, so the design professional knows from day one that every cable has to stay inside. That does not stall the project; it tells the engineer what must be confirmed before drawings are sealed, and it tells the owner why our first proposal separates "find out what is here" from "build what is needed."

Your Questions Answered

Fire Alarm Installation in Boerum Hill: Questions Answered

Fourteen questions from Boerum Hill first calls, sorted into the six things people worry about: money and paperwork, trust, who is allowed to touch the system, technical puzzles, landlord versus tenant, and complaints.

Cost and paperwork

We want to turn a landmarked Atlantic Avenue shop between Hoyt and Nevins into a wine bar. Does the LPC need to see the fire alarm plans?

The FDNY reviews the fire alarm drawings; the LPC cares about what changes on the outside of the building. If every device, cable and panel stays inside, LPC review of the fire alarm itself is usually not the issue, though other parts of the fit-out, like signage or a new storefront, may need it. Anything on the facade or roof should go to the LPC early.

The broker says the building on Smith Street "has a fire alarm." What paperwork proves that before we sign?

Ask the landlord for the FDNY Letter of Approval, the approved drawings and as-builts, the most recent inspection and test reports, the name of the servicing company and the central station contract. A panel without that paperwork may never have been approved for your use.

Quality and trust

Our Bergen Street condo board got three proposals that look nothing alike. How do we compare them fairly?

Lay them side by side by stage: design and sealed drawings, FDNY filing, installation, programming, pre-test and acceptance test. Check that each names the certified fire alarm company and the electrician who will appear on the filing, and that each says who handles FDNY objections. The cheapest proposal is often the one that leaves a stage out.

How can a gallery owner on Bergen Street check that the company servicing the panel is legitimate?

Ask for the company's FDNY Fire Alarm Company Certificate and the name of the principal who holds the S-97 certificate of fitness, then make sure the same company name is on your inspection reports and any FDNY filing. A legitimate company answers that request quickly and without fuss.

Who can do the work

Our general contractor on a Dean Street restaurant build-out wants his own electrician to wire the fire alarm. Is that allowed?

The FDNY requires the installer to be a NYC licensed electrician or a company holding an FDNY Fire Alarm Company Certificate, and the system has to be installed to drawings sealed by a design professional and approved by the FDNY. Ask your contractor who will be on the paperwork.

Can the super of a Pacific Street rental replace a hallway smoke detector that is chirping?

If it is part of the building's code-required fire alarm system, no. Components of that system, other than single-station smoke alarms inside apartments, are serviced by an FDNY-certified fire alarm company, because a replacement detector has to be the listed type, programmed to the right address and tested. A system detector that "chirps" is usually reporting a problem the panel already knows about.

Technical

Our 1850s building in the historic district has no hung ceilings. Where does the new fire alarm cable go?

Usually up an existing chase, a rear stair, a dumbwaiter shaft or a closet stack, then along corridors in surface raceway or small conduit painted to match the walls. The facade stays untouched, and every route appears on the drawings.

Could vibration from the jail construction on Atlantic Avenue be causing our panel's troubles?

It is possible but not the first thing to assume. Residents reported buildings shaking during the 2024 demolition. Vibration can loosen an old splice or a marginal termination, and a trouble that started around the same time deserves a careful look at terminations and device bases. Most troubles still come from batteries, water and construction inside the building itself, so the panel's event log decides it.

The roof deck over our two-story Boerum Place building is used for events. Does the space under it need detection?

Is a new strobe on the outside of a landmarked Boerum Hill storefront allowed?
+

Only with Landmarks Preservation Commission approval for the exterior change, and only if the design actually calls for it. Most fire alarm devices go inside, and the design professional can usually lay out the system so nothing new appears on a historic facade.

Does a Smith Street restaurant need its own fire alarm panel, or can it join the building system?
+

If the building already has an approved system with capacity, the restaurant usually adds devices and modules to it under a filed modification. If the building has no system and the change of use requires one, the new system serves the whole building, not only the restaurant.

Will the jail construction at 275 Atlantic Avenue delay our fire alarm project next door?
+

It should not affect the FDNY filing, but street closures, crane work and delivery limits around the site can affect when crews and materials reach a neighboring building. Build some slack into the installation schedule if your building shares the block.

Do we need a fire alarm for a fitness studio going into a former shop on Atlantic Avenue?
+

Possibly. A change from retail to an assembly-type use can change the code requirements, depending on the occupant load and the building. The design professional checks the code for the new use before the lease is final, which is the cheapest time to find out.

Who holds the fire alarm records when a Boerum Hill storefront changes tenants every few years?
+

The building owner. Keep the FDNY Letter of Approval, approved drawings, as-builts, inspection reports and the servicing company's contact at the building, not with a tenant who may leave and take the binder along.

Can a Boerum Hill condo board schedule the FDNY acceptance test around residents' work hours?
+

The FDNY sets the inspection appointment, so the board has limited control over the date and time. What the board can do is post notices well ahead, explain that alarms will sound and elevators may be recalled for a few hours, and name a contact for questions on the day.

Is the space above a top-floor ceiling in an older Boerum Hill building something the survey should check?
+

Yes. Concealed spaces under roofs and above ceilings can hold fire unseen, as the 2022 Boerum Place fire showed. The survey opens hatches or ceiling tiles where it can so the design professional knows what is there before sealing the drawings.

Does a gallery in a converted Bergen Street factory need different detectors than an office?
+

Often, yes. High open ceilings, skylights and exposed structure can call for beam or air sampling detection instead of ordinary spot detectors, and event use can raise the occupant load. The engineer chooses detection for the actual space.

What happens to the old panel when a Boerum Hill building replaces its fire alarm?
+

It stays in service until the new system is installed, tested and accepted by the FDNY, so the building is never without protection. After acceptance, the old panel and any abandoned devices are removed or labeled as the approved drawings direct.

How early should a Boerum Hill business start on the fire alarm before opening?
+

At lease signing. The FDNY puts plan submission to approval at about 20 to 24 weeks, design has to come before that, and landmarked buildings may need LPC sign-off for exterior work. A late start is the most common reason an opening slips.

How fast can a technician get to a Boerum Hill building after a call?
+

The visit is booked within 2 hours of your request. Installation itself cannot be same-day, since it waits on sealed drawings and FDNY approval, but a technician can read the panel quickly and tell you whether the building is still protected.
