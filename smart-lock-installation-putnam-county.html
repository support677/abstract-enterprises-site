The Neighborhood

Why Putnam County Properties Need Smart Lock Installation Fitted to Lake, Highland and Commuter Life

Putnam is small on the map and long on the drive. The county's own hazard mitigation plan describes nine municipalities — the towns of Carmel, Kent, Patterson, Philipstown, Putnam Valley and Southeast, and the villages of Brewster, Cold Spring and Nelsonville — spread over a little more than 240 square miles, with the most rugged ground in Philipstown, Putnam Valley and western Kent, where many peaks rise above 1,000 feet. The Census Bureau counts about 99,000 residents in roughly 38,700 housing units, and 83.8 percent of occupied homes are lived in by the people who own them. That combination — owner-occupied houses scattered across hills, lakes and reservoir land — is what shapes a smart lock job here. The owner decides everything, the doors are many and varied, and the nearest help can be a long way off.

The lake communities are the first thing that sets Putnam apart. According to the Town of Patterson's history site, New York City developers Warren and Arthur Smadbeck, working with McGolrick Realty and the New York Daily Mirror, began building three recreational communities around man-made lakes in 1928: Lake Carmel in Kent, Lake Peekskill in Putnam Valley and Putnam Lake in Patterson. They were sold to city families as summer bungalow colonies. At Putnam Lake, the same history records, building lots were laid out at 20 by 100 feet and sold for $50, with room for little more than a bungalow and an outhouse, and the community was largely deserted after Labor Day. Nearly a century later many of those cottages are year-round homes, winterized and extended one project at a time. Their doors tell the story: thin, light slabs intended for July, re-hung in frames that have been patched and re-trimmed, and side or lake-facing doors that have become the main entrance. A modern motorized deadbolt has to be matched to that door, not the other way round.

The second is the water supply. Much of Putnam lies within the Croton watershed, part of the New York City water system. West Branch Reservoir in Kent and Carmel, in service since 1895, holds 8 billion gallons in two basins separated by Route 301; Croton Falls Reservoir in Carmel and Southeast holds 5.2 billion; Boyds Corner sits in Kent, and Lake Gleneida beside Route 52 and Kirk Lake in Kent are controlled lakes that feed the city. A great deal of land along the county's roads is regulated with that water in mind, and wetlands alone covered about six percent of the county in the plan's 2011 land-use figures. For a lock installer that matters at the bottom of the driveway rather than at the front door: a keypad on a gate a few hundred feet from the house may mean a trench, and a trench near a stream, a wetland or a reservoir is not a weekend decision.

The third is the commute. The Census Bureau puts the mean travel time to work for Putnam residents at 39.3 minutes, and the county plan lists seven Metro-North stops on the Harlem Line in the east and the Hudson Line in the west, with Brewster, Southeast, Cold Spring and Garrison the busiest. In practice that means houses that are empty from before seven until after seven, children who come home from the school bus before their parents are off the train, and service visits — oil, propane, well pumps, septic — that happen while nobody is there to open the door. A smart lock in Putnam is often less about convenience at the front step and more about letting the right person in, on the right day, while the owners are fifty miles away.

And there is distance from help. Putnam is protected by volunteer fire departments and ambulance corps — Mahopac, Carmel, Lake Carmel, Kent, Patterson, Putnam Lake, Brewster, Putnam Valley, Continental Village, Garrison and North Highlands among them. The Kent Volunteer Fire Department, for one, reports 359 incidents in 2024, 250 of them EMS calls, and uses the Knox Box system so crews can open a property without forcing a door, with the code to release a key sent by the Putnam County 911 dispatch center. With 21.3 percent of county residents aged 65 or over, a lock plan that ignores how responders get in is incomplete. Every plan we write for a Putnam house answers that question alongside the everyday ones.

Door and hardware details we check on every Putnam job

Thickness and core. Smart deadbolts list the door thicknesses they fit, and the common minimum is 1-3/8 inches; Allegion's preparation sheet for one current Schlage model, for example, lists 1-3/8 to 2 inches with a 2-3/8 or 2-3/4 inch backset. Plenty of converted lake-cottage doors meet the number but are hollow or lightly cored, which means the lock's through-bolts have little to clamp and the bolt hole has little wood around it. We check by weight, by tapping and, where needed, by looking inside the existing bore before recommending anything. A hollow door gets a solid replacement or a reinforcing kit first; a lock on a door that can be kicked through is a false economy.

Frames that move with the seasons. Bungalow frames sit on old sills and post foundations close to the water table, and many have been shimmed, re-trimmed and re-hung more than once. They move between a dry January and a humid August. A motorized bolt is less forgiving than a hand-thrown one: if it scrapes the strike, the motor strains, the batteries drain and the lock reports a jam. We test the bolt with the door pushed shut by hand and by its own weight, deepen and realign the strike so the bolt throws clear in both seasons, and run long screws through the strike into the framing rather than the trim.

Exposure on the lake side. A door that faces open water takes wind-driven rain, low winter sun and summer humidity that an inland door never sees. We choose exterior keypads that their makers rate for outdoor use, keep the lock body and any bridge on the inside, and favour finishes and seals that will tolerate the conditions on the specific door rather than the one in the brochure.

One turn to get out — always. Every lock we put on a Putnam house door opens from inside with a thumb-turn or lever, no key, code or phone required. That matters even more where a door is the only route from a bedroom wing or a walk-out basement, and where help can be twenty minutes away. On outbuildings and gates, we make sure a person can never be shut in by the hardware we fitted.

Compliance callouts for Putnam business doors

These are the questions Putnam owners, lake associations, congregations and businesses put to us before a smart lock goes on a door or a gate. Lake cottages and lake-side doors

Our Lake Carmel cottage began as a 1930s summer bungalow and its front door is thinner than modern doors — can it take a smart deadbolt at all?

Sometimes, and only after we have looked inside the door. Most smart deadbolts list a minimum door thickness of 1-3/8 inches, and many bungalow doors in Lake Carmel, Putnam Lake and Lake Peekskill do reach that number. The trouble is what is inside. Doors bought for a summer colony in the late 1920s and 1930s, or replaced cheaply in the decades since, are often hollow or lightly cored, sometimes with a veneer face that has delaminated near the knob. A deadbolt clamped through that kind of door has little to grip, and the bolt hole has almost no wood around it.

We check three things: the actual thickness at the lock edge, what the existing bore shows of the core, and how the door hangs in a frame that may have been re-trimmed several times. If the door is solid enough, we fit the lock with a reinforcing plate around the bore and a strengthened strike. If it is hollow, the honest answer is a new solid or steel-skinned door first. That door can be ordered pre-bored for the lock you choose, which saves time and makes the finished job neater.

Very thin interior-grade doors pressed into exterior service — and we still find them on porches and side entrances around the lakes — are below every smart lock's listed range. Those doors should be replaced whether or not the lock changes, because they are a weak point on their own.

Neighbours on our Putnam Lake street keep spare keys for each other — is a code better than the key under the planter?

Usually, yes. Lake communities built on narrow cottage lots are close-knit, and the habit of a spare key under a planter, on a nail in the shed or with the neighbour two doors down runs deep. The trouble with a hidden key is that anyone who watches long enough can find it, and a key handed to a neighbour years ago may since have been lent to a contractor or a nephew. Nobody knows how many copies exist.

A personal code for the neighbour who checks on the house does the same job without those weaknesses. It can be changed or deleted at any time, it shows in the log when it is used, and it doesn't vanish when the planter is moved. If the neighbour also needs to get in during a power cut or with a dead lock battery, the mechanical key can stay with them, but the hidden one should go.

For cottages that are empty for part of the year, we suggest one neighbour code, one family code per adult, and nothing hidden outside. If the lock is connected, the family gets a notification each time the neighbour's code is used, which tends to be reassuring rather than intrusive.

Our lake association wants a keypad on the beach gate or clubhouse — how should members' codes be handed out and taken back each season?

Plan the codes around the membership list and the calendar, not around the lock. Putnam's lake communities are run in different ways: the Putnam Lake Park District, for example, is a special district of the Town of Patterson created after a 2011 community petition, with its beaches and facilities reserved for residents who own or rent homes within the district, and boats allowed on parkland only between March 1 and December 1. Other lakes are run by private associations or clubs. Whoever administers the gate needs a code policy that matches its own rules.

The practical choices are individual codes per household, which can be deleted one at a time when a house is sold or a renter leaves, or a seasonal code changed every year, which is simpler but impossible to take back from one household mid-season. For a clubhouse or boat-storage gate with more than a few dozen users, a fob or card system with expiry dates is usually easier to administer than codes, and leaves a log the board can review. We also check that anyone inside a fenced beach or storage area can always get out without a code.

Power and weather decide the hardware. A clubhouse door with electricity can take a keypad and electric strike; a remote beach gate is often better served by a battery or solar keypad lock built for outdoor gates. Ask us to walk the site with a board member before the association votes on anything.

Service visits, emergencies and wildlife

We already have a fire-department key box at our Kent house — what has to change when the front door gets a smart lock?

Mostly, the key inside the box. Several Putnam departments use the Knox system. The Kent Volunteer Fire Department, for example, explains on its website that the key to a property is kept in a Knox Box mounted at the building; the department's responding vehicle carries a secured key safe that the Putnam County 911 dispatch center unlocks with a code sent wirelessly only during an emergency, and each access is recorded with the date, time and who asked for it. That arrangement only works if the key in your box still opens your door.

A new smart deadbolt arrives with its own keys. Either we re-key its cylinder to the key already in the box, where the lock's cylinder allows it, or the department is asked to place the new key in the box — the box belongs to the fire department's system, so the owner arranges that with them rather than opening it. Either way, we test the key from the box position before we leave. We also make sure the smart lock has no setting, such as an inside privacy lockout, that would stop the key from opening the door.

Remote unlocking by a family member through the app is a useful second route when crews arrive, but never the only one; cellular signal and Wi-Fi fail at the worst moments. Avoid extra chains and slide bolts on that door; they stop a crew as surely as they stop a burglar.

The oil delivery driver and the well-pump technician both need our basement — can the outside bulkhead door go keyless?

Usually the better answer is a keyless door at the bottom of the bulkhead stairs rather than on the hatch itself. Steel bulkhead doors, the kind many Putnam farmhouses and older capes have over their cellar steps, are normally secured by a slide bar on the inside. Bilco, the best-known maker, sells a keyed lock kit for its Classic Series doors so the hatch can be locked and unlocked from outside with a key. That suits an owner who wants to carry a key, but it doesn't give a delivery driver a code, and the hatch itself is exposed to snow, ice and standing water.

So we look at the stairwell door at the foot of the steps, or the basement door from the garage. A keypad deadbolt there, with a separate scheduled code for the oil or propane company and another for the well or septic contractor, lets each one in on the days you expect them and tells you when they arrive and leave. The bulkhead stays secured with its inside bar or keyed kit, and the family's own codes never have to be shared.

Two cautions. If anyone sleeps or works in the basement, the stairwell door is an escape route, so it opens from inside with one turn and never needs a code to leave. And many oil companies fill from an outside pipe and never need the house at all — check before creating a code nobody uses.

Bears around our Putnam Valley house have learned to try door handles — does a smart lock change anything?

It helps in one specific way: a door that locks itself. Black bears are regularly reported in Putnam — the Carmel Police Department has posted sighting warnings for residents, and Lohud reported a bear in Mahopac in 2017 — and Connecticut's Department of Energy and Environmental Protection, which borders Patterson and Southeast, warns that open or unlocked doors and windows let bears get into homes, that locking screen doors does little because a bear can break through a screen, and that bears can easily open most unlocked car doors. Wildlife agencies in western bear country go further and advise replacing exterior lever handles with round knobs that a bear can't push down.

A smart deadbolt with auto-lock throws the bolt a set time after the door closes, so a door left unlocked by a child or a guest doesn't stay that way. A bolted door can't be opened by pressure on the handle, lever or not. On doors where bears have already tried the handle, we often pair the lock with a knob-style or fixed pull handle outside, and keep the lever inside where it helps people get out quickly.

The lock won't help with a screened porch, a flimsy garage panel door or food left near the house; those are matters of storage and housekeeping. But for the kitchen and mudroom doors that face the woods, auto-lock removes the human error that bears take advantage of.

Commuter households

If my phone dies on the late train home, how do I get into the house?

The same way you did before the phone was involved — which is why the phone should never be your only credential. We set up every Putnam lock so that each adult has at least two ways in that don't depend on one another: a code or fingerprint at the keypad, and either a fob or a mechanical key kept on the keyring or in the car. The phone app is for administration and remote access, not for being the only way through your own front door.

Commuters who park at Brewster, Southeast, Croton Falls or Cold Spring all day sometimes rely on the phone's Bluetooth unlock as they walk up from the driveway. That works well until the battery is at two percent after a delayed train. A keypad you can use in the dark with gloves on, or a fingerprint reader, removes the problem entirely.

It's also worth keeping one spare mechanical key with a neighbour or relative nearby. A dead lock battery, a jammed motor after a hard freeze or a lost phone are rare, but a key never needs a charge.

Gates, long driveways and the watershed

We want a keypad at the foot of a 600-foot driveway — do reservoir and wetland rules affect running a cable there?

They can, which is why we look at the route before we price a wired gate keypad. Much of Putnam drains to New York City reservoirs in the Croton system, and the city's Department of Environmental Protection has watershed regulations covering stormwater, stream protection and similar matters for communities in Putnam, Westchester, Orange and Ulster. Towns also regulate work in and near wetlands and watercourses. A trench that crosses a stream, a wet swale or the edge of a wetland may need permission from the town, the state or the city before anyone digs, and some routes are simply not worth it.

That leaves three practical options. Where an existing conduit or a power feed already runs to the gate, we use it. Where a trench is reasonable and stays out of regulated areas, we lay conduit and run low-voltage cable for the keypad and its controller, with the dig marked through 811 first. Where neither works, a battery or solar-powered keypad lock built for gates, or a wireless link from the house, avoids digging entirely, at the cost of more maintenance.

Whatever powers the keypad, the gate must have a manual release that anyone inside the property can use without a code, and a way for emergency crews to get through. Ask your fire department whether it uses a key switch or key box at gates in your district.

Lightning on our hilltop took out a doorbell last summer — does a wired gate keypad need surge protection?

Yes. A long cable run from the house to a gate on an exposed Putnam ridge behaves like an antenna during a nearby strike, and the surge it picks up arrives at both ends — at the keypad and at the controller or network switch in the house. We put surge protection on the low-voltage lines at the building entry, bond the protectors to a proper ground, and keep the gate electronics on their own circuit so a hit at the gate doesn't travel into the rest of the system.

Fibre between the house and the gate removes the conductive path entirely and is worth considering on long, exposed runs, especially where a camera or intercom shares the trench. Where the run is short or already buried, protectors and grounding are usually enough.

Battery and solar gate keypads aren't immune — a direct or very close strike damages anything — but with no long wire back to the house they don't carry the surge indoors. Whatever we fit, we leave you with the part numbers so a damaged keypad can be replaced quickly.

Old farmhouses and village houses

Our Garrison farmhouse still has an antique rim lock on the front door — can a smart lock go on without taking it off?

Often, yes. A rim lock is the box lock mounted on the inside face of the door rather than set into its edge, common on nineteenth-century houses in Philipstown and across the county. Many owners want to keep it for the look and the history, and the door around it is frequently thick and solid. The usual approach is to leave the rim lock working as a latch and add a separate smart deadbolt a short distance above or below it, bored through the door where the wood is sound and clear of the old hardware's screw holes.

Before drilling we check the door's thickness against the lock's listed range, the condition of the stile where the new bore would go, and how the new bolt meets the frame — old frames are often out of square and need a new strike set into fresh wood. Where the door is thicker than standard locks allow, we look at models listed for thicker doors or at a retrofit unit over a new deadbolt.

The old lock's key, if you still have it, usually stops mattering once the deadbolt is in. We leave the rim lock exactly as we found it, and if the house is in Cold Spring's historic district we keep the outside of the new lock as plain and low-profile as possible.

Churches and community halls

The youth group, the food pantry and an evening meeting all use our Brewster church's side door — can one lock give each group its own nights?

Yes, and that is one of the clearest wins for a congregation. A keypad deadbolt or a fob reader on the side door can carry a separate credential for each group and each leader, each with its own schedule: the youth leaders on Tuesday evenings, pantry volunteers on Thursday and Saturday mornings, the evening meeting's organiser on its night only. Outside those windows the door stays locked to them, and the church office keeps full access.

Because every credential is individual, a leader who steps down is removed in seconds without touching anyone else's access, and the log shows which group opened the building before the boiler was found running all weekend — without anyone being accused. It also lets the church re-key the old mechanical locks once and keep the new keys to two or three people, instead of chasing copies handed out over the years.

Exit rules apply as for any assembly space: every door that is a way out opens from inside without a code. Groups that use the kitchen or a storage room get those doors added to their schedule only if they need them.

Our community hall in Putnam Valley is rented for parties most weekends — how do renters get in without being handed a key?

Give each booking its own code that starts and ends with the rental. Halls run by lodges, veterans' posts, fire companies and civic groups across the county are often rented out for birthdays, showers and meetings, and the traditional method — a key collected from a volunteer on Friday and returned on Monday, or not — is exactly how keys multiply. A keypad deadbolt or a keypad with an electric strike on the hall entrance lets the booking secretary create a code valid from, say, noon Saturday to 1 a.m. Sunday, send it to the renter, and know it will stop working on its own.

The log shows when the renter actually arrived and left, which settles disputes about the deposit and the lights left on. Kitchen, bar storage and the office stay on separate locks or separate credentials, so a renter's code opens the hall and the restrooms and nothing else. Volunteers who set up and clean keep their own permanent codes.

Assembly rules still apply: every exit door opens from inside without a code, panic hardware stays exactly as the fire marshal approved it, and nothing we fit changes the way out. Where the hall has no reliable internet, we choose a lock that can generate time-limited codes offline.

AI Overview Reality Check

What AI Gets Wrong About Smart Lock Installation in Putnam County

A note on sources: the tools used to research this page could not open Google's AI Overview, so the seven headings below are not its words. They are claims that turn up again and again in lock marketing, cost guides from Angi, HomeAdvisor and Fixr, and how-to articles, which answer engines then repeat. Each is followed by what we see on real Putnam doors and gates.

“Any exterior door can take a smart deadbolt in about thirty minutes”

Review sites like to say a smart lock goes on with a screwdriver in half an hour, and on a modern steel door in a square frame that is roughly true. It assumes a solid door of standard thickness, an existing bore at the right backset, and a strike that already lines up.

Around Putnam's lakes, a surprising number of doors fail one of those assumptions. Bungalow doors from a summer colony are often hollow or thin, frames have been shimmed and re-trimmed, and strikes sit in soft wood that has been drilled three times before.

The installation itself still doesn't take long. What takes time is making the door worth locking, and on a converted cottage that is where most of the visit goes.

“Smart lock installation costs $250 to $400”

Fixr's national cost guide, updated in January 2025, puts a typical smart lock installation at $250 to $400, and Angi and HomeAdvisor publish similar national figures. Those numbers describe one lock on one ordinary door with the hardware included.

A Putnam job is rarely one ordinary door. It may be a slider, a stairwell door below a bulkhead, a gate a few hundred feet from the house, or a door that needs replacing before any lock goes on. A gate keypad with a cable run is a different job from a deadbolt swap.

That is why our Putnam house work is quoted flat after a walkthrough, and our published Putnam figures cover the entrance and gate work that can be priced in advance.

“Unlock the door from your phone and you never need a key again”

Remote unlocking is a real convenience, and for a family on the Harlem Line it is often the reason to buy a connected lock. But it depends on the lock's radio, a bridge or hub, home Wi-Fi, the internet connection and a charged phone, all working at once.

The crews who matter most in an emergency — volunteer firefighters and ambulance members — work with physical keys. Kent's fire department describes a Knox system in which the property key is released from a secured safe through Putnam County 911 dispatch, not an app.

So every lock we fit keeps a working key cylinder, and every adult has two credentials that don't depend on each other.

“A keyless keypad on the gate is a simple add-on”

Gate-opener marketing makes a keypad on a driveway gate look like a quick upgrade. The keypad is simple; the cable to it, or the power for it, often isn't.

In Putnam the route from the house to the gate can cross ground near streams, wetlands or reservoir land that the town, the state or New York City's watershed rules care about, and lightning on exposed ridges makes a long copper run a liability without surge protection.

We price a gate only after walking the route, and we offer battery or solar options when a trench isn't worth the approvals or the risk.

“A smart lock makes a house secure”

A locked deadbolt is excellent protection for the door it is on, and auto-lock fixes the most common failure, which is the door nobody locked. That is genuinely valuable: after two burglaries in Philipstown in September 2024, The Highlands Current reported, the Putnam County Sheriff's Office reminded residents to keep doors and windows locked and not to post on social media when they would be away.

But a lock protects only its door. A lake-side slider without a bar, a bulkhead with its slide bar left open, a garage side door and ground-floor windows are separate problems.

That is why our Putnam plans list every opening, and why we often pair locks with door contacts and a camera at the most-used entrance.

“Smart locks work the same on every door in the house”

Product pages show a smart deadbolt on a front door and leave the impression that the same lock suits the back door, the slider and the cellar. Sliding doors have no bore for a deadbolt, bulkhead hatches are secured from inside, and an old farmhouse door may carry a rim lock nobody wants removed.

Each of those openings needs its own hardware, and some need none at all — a mechanical lock and a contact can be the right answer on a door that is opened a few times a year.

We plan door by door, and the cheapest good plan often puts one or two smart locks on the doors that matter and leaves the rest mechanical.

“Anyone handy can install one, so licensing doesn't matter”

Plenty of owners fit their own deadbolts, and there is nothing wrong with that on their own door. Paid work is different. Putnam County's code says no one may run a home improvement business in the county without registering under Chapter 135, and Cold Spring asks permit applicants to list their contractors' Putnam County licence numbers.

Wired work — electric strikes, gate keypads, controllers — adds low-voltage wiring and, sometimes, electrical and fire-code questions. AESS holds NYS Low-Voltage License #12000287431.

Ask any installer for their county registration and insurance before they start, ours included.

Lake Cottage Checklist

A Door Checklist for Putnam's Converted Lake Bungalows

A bungalow that started life as a summer cottage deserves a few checks before it gets a motorized deadbolt. This is what we go through at Lake Carmel, Putnam Lake, Lake Peekskill and the smaller lake colonies.

  • Which door is really the front door? On many cottages the street door is rarely used and the side or lake-facing door is the everyday entrance. The smart lock goes where the traffic is.
  • Is the door solid? Weight, a tap test and a look inside the existing bore tell us whether the door has a core worth bolting. Hollow doors are replaced or reinforced first.
  • Is it thick enough? Most smart deadbolts list a 1-3/8 inch minimum. Interior-grade doors pressed into outside service are below every listed range.
  • Does the frame hold a strike? Old sills, shims and re-trimmed jambs often leave the strike in thin trim. We anchor it into the framing with long screws.
  • Is there a slider or a porch door? These are often the weakest openings on a lake house and get bars, contacts or dedicated sliding-door locks.
  • Is there signal at the door? Many bungalows have the router at the opposite end of the house or in a converted attic; a bridge or a small mesh node usually fixes it.
  • Who else comes in? Neighbours who check on the house, family who visit at weekends, and contractors finishing yet another winterizing project each get their own code.

Every cottage we work on ends with the same test: the door pulled shut by its own weight, the bolt thrown by the motor, and the door opened from inside in the dark by someone who has never seen it before.

Rim lockAn old box lock mounted on the inside face of a door. Common on farmhouses; it can usually stay while a separate deadbolt is added.
Hollow-core doorA light door with a cardboard or honeycomb interior. Found on some converted lake cottages; too weak to anchor a deadbolt without reinforcement or replacement.
Key boxA high-security box at the door holding a key that only the local fire department can open in an emergency. Where your district uses one, your smart lock must keep a working key.
Auto-lockThe lock re-bolts itself a chosen number of seconds or minutes after the door shuts — the cure for the mudroom door a teenager leaves open on the way to the bus.
Surge protectorA device on the low-voltage line that diverts lightning-induced surges to ground before they reach the keypad or the equipment indoors.
Fail-safe / fail-secureWhether an electrified lock releases or stays locked when power is lost. Chosen per door, depending on whether it is an exit.

Field Notes on Putnam County Doors and Gates

How a walkthrough runs on a Putnam lot — our standing routine, described in general terms rather than as one customer's story

We start at the bottom of the driveway, not the front door. If there is a gate, we look at how it opens by hand, where power could come from, and what the ground looks like between the gate and the house: a stream, a wet dip, a stone wall, an old conduit stub. That decides whether a gate keypad is a wiring job, a battery job or not worth doing at all.

Then the door the family actually uses. On a lake house that is often the side door or the slider; on a farmhouse it is the mudroom. We lift the door, tap it, look inside the bore and pull it shut by its own weight to see whether the bolt would meet the strike.

Then the cellar. We open the bulkhead, check its inside bar, walk down to the stairwell door and open it from inside with the lights off. If anyone could ever be down there in an emergency, that door stays one turn from open.

Last, the people: who comes home first, who services the house while it is empty, which fire department covers the road, and who will hold the admin account. The lock gets chosen after that conversation, not before it.

What does a keyless business entrance or gate keypad cost in Putnam County?

Will a smart lock keypad work when we're wearing winter gloves?
+

Physical push-button keypads work with most thin and medium gloves; touchscreen keypads and fingerprint readers generally need a bare finger. If the household spends winter mornings scraping cars and carrying firewood, we suggest a keypad with raised buttons, or a fob that can be held against the reader through a glove.

Can a smart deadbolt go on a door that swings outward onto a deck or porch?
+

Often, yes, but it needs checking first. On an outswing door the hinges face outside, so we fit security hinge pins or studs to stop the door being lifted off, confirm the lock is listed for the door's handing, and make sure the deadbolt throws into a strike that sits in the frame rather than the trim. Some outswing patio doors use multipoint locks, which need a compatible smart handle instead.

Will the walkthrough cover the gate, the garage and the outbuildings, or only the house?
+

Everything you want considered. On Putnam properties we usually walk the gate and the route to it, any detached garage, shed or boathouse, the bulkhead and the house doors in one visit, so the plan can tell you which openings deserve a smart lock, which are better with a mechanical lock and a contact, and which can be left as they are.

We're ordering new exterior doors — should they come pre-bored for a particular smart lock?
+

Choose the lock first, then order the doors bored to its specification, usually a standard cross bore and edge bore at a 2-3/8 or 2-3/4 inch backset. Ask for a solid or reinforced lock area and a strike prep in the frame. If the door is going in before the lock is chosen, standard deadbolt prep keeps your options open.

Do you cover Philipstown and Putnam Valley as well as Carmel, Brewster and Patterson?
+

All of it. The six towns and three villages are one service area for us, Nelsonville and Garrison as much as Mahopac and Brewster, and the Connecticut-line streets of Putnam Lake as much as the river villages. Visits are offered every day in 2-hour arrival windows from 8 AM to 8 PM.

Will a smart lock lower our homeowners insurance premium?
+

That is up to your insurer, and practice varies. Some insurers offer discounts for monitored alarms or certain protective devices; a lock on its own is less commonly listed. Ask your agent before you buy, and we will give you an itemised invoice describing what was installed in case it helps.

Can the lock's alerts also go to a neighbour or caretaker who checks on the house?
+

Yes. Most connected locks let the owner add a second user with notification rights but no administrator powers, so a neighbour or caretaker can be told when a door is unlocked or left open without being able to add codes. We set it up that way, and show you how to remove them later.

Can a gate keypad go on a post beside the drive instead of on the gate itself?
+

Usually that is the better place. A keypad on a separate post at window height, a few feet before the gate, can be reached from the driver's seat, stays out of the gate's swing and doesn't flex with the gate leaf. We set the post's height and distance at the walkthrough, and run the cable to it in conduit.
