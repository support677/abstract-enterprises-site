AI Detection

Ubiquiti UniFi AI Camera Installation

Ubiquiti Questions NYC Owners Ask

Cost

How much does Ubiquiti camera installation cost in NYC?

A typical installed Ubiquiti system runs about $400–$700 per camera including the NVR, PoE cabling, configuration, and the UniFi Protect app setup, with the per-camera cost dropping on larger jobs. A small 4-camera residential system often lands around $1,800–$3,000; a mid-size commercial build runs higher with PTZ, LPR, or high-channel NVRs. Final pricing follows a free site visit. Call (800) 486-0943.

Is Ubiquiti cheaper than other camera brands?

Generally yes — Ubiquiti delivers 4K, AI detection, and full-color night vision for noticeably less than premium brands, which is its main appeal. The hardware cost is lower; professional installation labor is similar to any brand. For most private commercial and residential NYC properties, that makes it a strong value.

Quality / Trust

Are Ubiquiti cameras good?

For image quality and features per dollar, they’re among the best on the market — Ubiquiti is the world’s largest surveillance manufacturer, and its UniFi G5 and UniFi AI technology is genuinely excellent. The hardware is reliable and well-supported. The main caveat is regulatory, not performance — see the government-use note below.

Can Ubiquiti cameras be viewed on a phone?

Yes — the the UniFi Protect app app gives live view, playback, and push alerts from anywhere. We set it up on your phone at install and show you how to add other users. Local NVR recording needs no monthly fee; cloud storage is optional.

DIY vs Pro

Can I install Ubiquiti cameras myself?

You can mount a small kit, but the value of a pro install is in the parts that go wrong solo: running PoE cable cleanly through NYC walls and ceilings, sizing the NVR storage correctly, aiming cameras to avoid glare and cover blind spots, configuring UniFi AI zones, and securing the system against the remote-access exploits Ubiquiti gear is targeted for. We also handle building access and any commercial notification requirements.

Which Ubiquiti camera is best for a warehouse?

Usually a mix: a PTZ speed dome with auto-tracking to patrol the open floor and yard, fixed UniFi AI bullets covering aisles and dock doors, and LPR at the gates. We size a high-channel NVR to record it all. We design the layout on a free site walk.

Technical

Why is my Ubiquiti camera offline or not recording?

The usual causes are a PoE/power fault on the cable run, a full or failed NVR hard drive, an IP or network conflict, or firmware that needs updating. “Offline” is often the camera losing its link to the NVR; “not recording” is usually storage. We diagnose which it is and fix it — often in one visit, on systems we didn’t install.

I forgot my Ubiquiti password — can it be reset?

Yes. Ubiquiti devices reset through a secure code procedure tied to the device and date, or via the the UniFi Protect app account that owns it. We recover access, then set you up with proper credentials and remote viewing so it doesn’t happen again.

Why does my night footage look blurry or washed out?

Often it’s infrared glare, a dirty or spider-webbed lens, poor camera placement, or an older non-UniFi G5 model. Upgrading to UniFi G5 gives true full-color night video; sometimes it’s just re-aiming and cleaning. We assess and tell you which.

Commercial / Compliance

Is Ubiquiti banned in the US?

Not for private use. US restrictions (the NDAA and related FCC rules) bar Ubiquiti from federal government and certain federally-funded or critical-infrastructure projects. For private NYC apartment buildings, retail, offices, and homes, it’s legal to buy and install. If your property is government, school-district, or federally-funded, tell us — we’ll spec a compliant alternative brand instead.

Do I need to notify people they’re being recorded?

For commercial properties in NYC, posted notice of surveillance is generally expected, and audio recording has stricter consent rules than video. We help you place required signage and configure audio appropriately so your system is set up correctly from day one.

What the Internet Tells You About Ubiquiti in NYC — and What’s Actually True

Search “Ubiquiti camera installation NYC” and you’ll get aggregator estimates, brand spec sheets, and a lot of fear-mongering headlines. Here’s the honest read.

The Cost Estimators Miss What Actually Drives Price

Angi, HomeAdvisor, and Fixr will hand you a national per-camera average that ignores the things that actually move an NYC quote: how far the PoE cable has to run through a finished pre-war building, how many channels the NVR needs, and whether you want PTZ or LPR. A 4-camera home and a 4-camera warehouse aren’t the same job.

Ubiquiti’s hardware is genuinely cheaper than the premium brands, so the camera line item is lower — but the labor to install it properly in a NYC building is the same skilled work regardless of brand. An estimator that quotes only hardware undersells the real number; one that walks your property quotes accurately.

That’s why we quote after a free site visit, not over a form. The brand is the easy part; your building is the variable.

“Is Ubiquiti Banned?” — The Honest Answer

This is the headline that scares people, and the truth is more specific than the scare. US law — the NDAA and related FCC rules — restricts Ubiquiti from federal government use and certain federally-funded and critical-infrastructure projects. It is not banned for private commercial or residential use, and millions of the cameras are installed legally across the country.

For a private NYC apartment building, store, office, or home, Ubiquiti is legal to buy and install. The real question isn’t legality — it’s fit. If your property is a public school, a government facility, or anything federally funded, we won’t install Ubiquiti; we’ll spec a compliant brand instead. A contractor who tells you that up front is more useful than a spec sheet that hides it.

We’d rather lose a Ubiquiti sale than put you in a non-compliant position. That’s the difference between an installer and a box-mover.

Security of the Cameras Themselves Matters

Because Ubiquiti is the most widely deployed brand, it’s also the most-targeted — the internet is full of stories of cameras left on default passwords getting accessed remotely. That’s not a reason to avoid the brand; it’s a reason not to install it carelessly.

A proper install changes the default credentials, isolates the cameras on their own network segment, disables unused remote ports, and keeps firmware current. Most of the horror stories trace back to a plug-and-play job that skipped all of that. We don’t.

The hardware is only as secure as the hands that configure it. That’s the part a DIY kit or a cheap installer skips and we don’t.

The Bottom Line: Right Brand, Right Property, Right Install

Ubiquiti is excellent value with class-leading AI and night vision, legal and appropriate for the vast majority of private NYC properties, and a poor choice for the specific government and federally-funded jobs the law carves out. The headlines flatten all of that into “banned” or “spyware,” and neither is the useful answer.

The useful answer is a contractor who knows when Ubiquiti is the right call, installs it securely, and tells you honestly when a different brand fits your property better. That’s the service we provide — the brand is just one tool in it.

Plain-English Glossary

Ubiquiti Terminology, Explained

UniFi G5

Ubiquiti’s full-color night vision — a wide aperture and warm supplement light capture color 24/7 instead of black-and-white infrared.

UniFi AI

On-camera AI that classifies people and vehicles versus other motion, cutting false alarms and labeling footage so it’s searchable.

NVR

Network Video Recorder — records all cameras, powers them over PoE, and serves footage to your phone. The heart of the system.

PoE

Power over Ethernet — one cable carries both power and data to each camera, so no separate electrician or outlet is needed at the camera.

PTZ

Pan-Tilt-Zoom — a motorized dome that moves and zooms, with auto-tracking to follow a subject across a large area.

DeepinView / AI 360

Ubiquiti’s higher-end lines — DeepinView for deep-learning analytics, AI 360 combining PTZ and panoramic in one unit.

LPR / ANPR

License Plate / Automatic Number Plate Recognition — reads and logs plates with timestamps for a searchable vehicle record.

the UniFi Protect app

The app for live view, playback, and alerts on your phone — no monthly fee for local NVR recording.

Quick Answers: Ubiquiti in NYC

How many Ubiquiti cameras do I need?

It depends on entrances, blind spots, and what you need to see. A small home is often 4–6; a building covers each entrance, lobby, and common area. We map coverage on a free walk-through and recommend the fewest cameras that leave no gap.

How long does Ubiquiti footage stay recorded?

As long as the NVR storage allows — we size drives to your camera count and resolution so you get the retention you want, commonly 2–4 weeks and often more. UniFi AI labeling makes finding an event fast.

Can Ubiquiti cameras record audio?

Many models can, but audio recording carries stricter consent rules than video. We configure audio only where appropriate and help with required notice so your system stays compliant.

What internet speed do Ubiquiti cameras need?

Recording is local to the NVR and needs no internet. You only need a modest connection for remote viewing on the UniFi Protect app — and we configure the stream quality so it doesn’t bog down your network.
