Why Suffolk Homes and Businesses Hire Out Their TV Mounting

Quick answer: In Suffolk County, TV mounting costs $229.99 for a 32–55″ TV on a fixed or tilting mount over drywall and wood studs. Suffolk is big. The Census Bureau puts it at 911 square miles of land and about 1.55 million people, and 82 percent of homes are owner-occupied. That makes most of our Suffolk work house work: chimney breasts, finished basements, vaulted family rooms and back decks, not co-op boards and doormen. It also explains the price. A Suffolk visit starts at $229.99, twenty dollars above our Nassau base, and that difference is the whole distance charge. We don't add mileage for Mattituck, Montauk or Orient on top of it.

The houses change as you drive east. Babylon, West Islip and Islip are full of postwar capes and ranches with drywall over wood studs, the easiest walls we see. Brookhaven has thousands of split-levels and high ranches whose dens sit on a slab and often wear paneling or beadboard. The East End plays by second-home rules. The do-it-yourself crowd is loud too. In a r/longisland thread asking for a TV mounting referral, the top advice was to buy a $40 kit, a stud finder, a drill and a socket wrench. East End second homes

Hamptons shingle houses and North Fork farmhouses mix painted drywall, shiplap and tongue-and-groove with stone hearths and outdoor living space. Shiplap over studs is priced as framing. Outdoor screens add $90, and we coordinate access with a caretaker when you're not out east.

🏘️

Condos, HOAs and active-adult communities

Suffolk has many townhouse and 55-plus communities with shared walls and association rules. Interior walls are usually drywall on wood or steel studs; steel adds $30. Patio TVs on common elements may need board approval first, and we send the certificate of insurance at no charge.

Drywall over wood studs ($0 add)

We locate two studs with a finder, confirm each with a thin probe, and drive 5/16″ lag bolts at least 2½″ into solid wood. Wider brackets get four lags across two studs. That's the standard Babylon ranch or Commack colonial wall, and it carries no wall surcharge at all.

Brick and stone chimneys ($65 add)

We hammer-drill into solid brick or stone, not into a thin veneer cap, and set sleeve or wedge anchors sized to the bracket. Before drilling we check whether the chimney is full masonry or a framed chase with veneer, because that changes the anchor and sometimes the height.

Block and poured concrete ($75 add)

Basement block has hollow cores, so we map the solid webs and place anchors there, or use grout-filled cells if they're present. Poured foundation walls take concrete screw anchors or wedge anchors. Damp walls get noted first, since moisture behind a TV is your problem to fix, not ours to hide.

Steel studs in offices and condos ($30 add)

Light-gauge steel is thin, so a lag bolt has nothing to bite. Hauppauge suites and many newer condos use light-gauge steel. We use steel-rated toggle anchors through the stud face, and for heavy full-motion arms we recommend an $85 plywood backer spanning two studs so the load spreads across the wall instead of two small points.

Plaster in older North Shore houses ($45 add)

Plaster cracks if you hammer near it, so we drill slowly with a sharp bit, find the lath and framing behind, and lag into the studs. Where the plaster sits on masonry, we switch to masonry anchors and price the brick rate instead.

Tile backsplashes and stone surrounds ($95 tile, $65 stone)

Pool-house bars and kitchens often have porcelain or marble tile. We score with a diamond bit, drill without hammer action, and anchor into the framing or masonry behind. Even careful drilling can crack tile or marble, and booking means you accept that risk.

New outlets need a Suffolk-licensed electrician

Do I need an electrician to install an outlet behind my TV? When it's a brand-new outlet, yes. Suffolk County licenses master electricians through its Department of Consumer Affairs under Article XI of the County Code. We don't add or move outlets. Our in-wall kit carries power from your existing receptacle through a listed, code-compliant cable assembly; if you want a brand-new outlet behind the TV, a licensed electrician installs it first.

Poolside screens need a proper wet-location outlet

Under National Electrical Code 406.9(B)(1), a 15- or 20-amp receptacle in a wet location must stay weatherproof even with a plug inserted, which is why outdoor outlets carry bubble-style in-use covers. We plug outdoor TVs only into an existing compliant outlet. If yours lacks one, an electrician should fix that before your appointment.

Wall screens in tasting rooms, offices and clinics

The 2010 ADA Standards (section 307.2) mean a wall object whose front edge sits 27 to 80 inches up can project at most 4 inches into a walkway. In a narrow hallway or waiting room, that can mean mounting higher, choosing a slim fixed bracket, or setting the screen in a spot outside the walking path.

HOA and condo approvals

Townhouse and 55-plus communities across Suffolk often require approval for anything attached to exterior walls or common elements, such as a patio TV. Get that sign-off before booking. If the association asks for our insurance certificate, request it at checkout and we'll send it free.

AI Overview Reality Check

What AI Answers Get Wrong About TV Mounting in Suffolk County

Ask a chatbot what TV mounting costs on Long Island and you'll get a national average and a few general tips. Suffolk is different enough that some of that advice costs people money or leaves a bracket in the wrong wall. Below are seven claims we hear from customers who checked an AI answer first, with what we actually find in Suffolk houses, bars and pool houses.

1. AI says: “Expect a travel surcharge for anything out in the Hamptons.”

Some installers based in the city do price by distance from Manhattan, and they say so plainly on their own service pages. AI tools pick that up and apply it to every Suffolk job, from Amityville to Amagansett. It isn't how our price list works at all.

Our Suffolk base is $229.99, and it already accounts for the drive across a county that stretches well over a hundred miles from the Queens line to Montauk Point. A Water Mill job and a Deer Park job use identical numbers for size, wall and extras.

So the honest comparison is total against total. Add up any quote's base, travel charge, job minimum and wall fees, then set it beside our calculator total for the same TV on the same wall. That's the figure you will actually pay on the day.

2. AI says: “Salt air only matters if your house is right on the ocean.”

Salt carries farther than people expect. Houses on the Great South Bay, Moriches Bay, Peconic Bay and Long Island Sound all see corrosion on outdoor hardware, light fixtures and grills, not only the homes sitting directly behind the dunes in Westhampton or Montauk.

For an outdoor TV, that means choosing a screen built for outdoor use, or at least a deeply covered spot, and a bracket with a corrosion-resistant finish and hardware. A cheap indoor arm bolted to a bay-front deck can rust through in a season or two.

We price outdoor work at a $90 add and plug only into an existing weatherproof outlet. If you'd rather not leave the set out through winter storms, a take-down is $85, and rehanging it in spring is simply booked as a regular outdoor install.

3. AI says: “A stud finder will show you the studs behind any wall.”

Electronic stud finders read density changes behind a thin, uniform surface like drywall. Suffolk split-level dens often have wood paneling over furring strips instead, which fools them into showing neat lines every 16 inches that are really just strips of lumber.

Behind those strips there may be wood studs or a block foundation wall. The bracket has to land in one or the other, never only in a three-quarter-inch furring strip nailed into who knows what, because that strip will pull loose under load.

We confirm with a magnet that finds nail lines, then a small test hole in a spot the TV will cover. That single hole decides whether the job is priced as wood framing with no add or as block at the $75 masonry rate.

4. AI says: “In a vaulted room, put the TV high on the tall wall.”

Vaulted great rooms in newer Mount Sinai, Miller Place and Manorville homes make the tall wall look like the obvious spot. AI answers often agree, simply because the wall is big, the TV fits, and nothing else in the room competes for it.

But seated eye level doesn't rise with the ceiling. A screen centered seven feet up means a tilted head for every movie, and the r/TVTooHigh community exists largely because so many people regret that choice after the bracket is already in.

We'll mount high if you want, with a $75 add above about 8 feet, but we first show you the height with painter's tape. A tilting bracket, or a pull-down mount over a fireplace, often solves the problem better than height alone.

5. AI says: “Long Island chimneys are solid brick, so any masonry anchor works.”

Many older North Shore chimneys in Huntington, Northport and Centerport are solid brick or stone. Plenty of newer Suffolk houses, though, have a framed chase with a thin stone or brick veneer on the face, and prefab gas or wood fireplaces are boxed in with lumber.

Masonry anchors in a one-inch veneer hold almost nothing, and they can crack the veneer right off its backer board. A framed chase actually wants lag bolts into the wood behind, which is easier, cleaner and often cheaper than drilling masonry.

So we check before quoting a height. Solid masonry gets the $65 add and sleeve or wedge anchors. A framed chase over a gas insert is priced as a wood wall plus the $130 fireplace placement, once we confirm the insert's heat clearance.

6. AI says: “Basement block walls will hold an anchor anywhere you drill.”

Concrete block is hollow inside, with solid webs between the cores. An anchor set into a hollow core can spin or pull out, especially under a full-motion arm that levers against it every single time someone swings the screen toward the bar or the couch.

Block foundations are common across South Shore and mid-Island basements, from Lindenhurst to Ronkonkoma, and they need a map before drilling. We locate the solid webs or grout-filled cells, place anchors there, and confirm each one bites before the bracket goes on.

Damp block is its own problem. White efflorescence or water staining means moisture is moving through the wall. We note it and recommend fixing that first, because a TV pressed against a wet foundation wall tends to be a short-lived TV.

7. AI says: “Second-home owners can't get TV work done unless they're out east.”

Plenty of Hamptons and North Fork homeowners live in Manhattan or Nassau until summer. AI answers tend to assume you must be home for every visit, which pushes everyone's TV work into the same crowded June weekends right when the roads are worst.

You can book and pay online, choose a 2-hour arrival window and leave access details for a caretaker, neighbor or lockbox. Send photos of the wall in advance and we'll flag brick, tile, paneling or outdoor issues before the trip out.

Two things still matter. The TV and bracket must be on site, and someone must confirm access, or the $150 return-trip fee applies. Spring visits before Memorial Day are usually much easier to schedule than anything in July or August.
