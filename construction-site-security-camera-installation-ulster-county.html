Element 4 · Why Ulster County Jobsites Get Hit

The Exposure Starts Before the Job Does

Ulster's development story is dominated by big properties waiting on their second life. The former IBM Kingston plant in the Town of Ulster — roughly 200 acres and more than a million square feet of buildings along Enterprise Drive, just off Thruway Exit 19 — has been the subject of a long redevelopment effort whose master plan has included warehouse and commercial space plus workforce housing, and which has moved in fits and starts, with the county advancing foreclosure on two West Campus parcels in early 2026. Meanwhile a $87 million, 164-unit affordable and supportive development was built on the 20-acre site of the former county jail in Kingston, and over 200 affordable apartments across two dozen buildings in Kingston, Saugerties and the Town of Ulster have gone through rehabilitation.

What that mix produces is a security problem most jobsite camera advice never addresses: the dormant period. A building empty for a decade still holds copper, switchgear, mechanical and piping, and nobody is on site to notice a truck at 2 a.m. By the time construction starts, the loss may already have happened. Power tools remain the most commonly reported theft from active American jobsites and recovery on any single tool is poor, but on a stalled campus the metal in the walls is the exposure — and federal estimates put national copper theft in the range of a billion dollars a year.

Dormancy

Vacant is not secure

Phased and stalled redevelopment means buildings sit empty between approvals. Coverage during that window is different work than covering an active crew — fewer people, more entries, and nobody to call anything in.

Scale

Campus-sized perimeters

A 200-acre property with multiple buildings and several access roads cannot be covered like a fenced lot. Entrances and the buildings actually holding value come first; everything else follows the phases.

Jurisdiction

Sheriff, city, towns and DEP

The Ulster County Sheriff's Office, Kingston city police, town and village departments across the county, State Police out of Troop F, and New York City DEP Police on watershed land around the Ashokan and Rondout reservoirs.

Element 5 · Systems & Configurations

Four Ways to Cover an Ulster County Site

The configuration follows the site — what it has, what phase it is in, and how long coverage has to last.

Configuration 1

Battery + cellular unit

Self-contained camera with its own battery and LTE radio, mounted to fencing, a container, a pole or a building parapet. This is what covers a vacant structure or a pre-construction parcel, because it needs no service and no crew on site.

Configuration 2

Solar-assisted unit

Same head with a panel sized to the site. Open parcels along the Route 9W and Route 209 corridors carry solar well. Shaded Catskill sites and north-facing slopes in Woodstock, Olive and Shandaken get larger battery banks instead.

Configuration 3

Hardwired PoE

Cameras on Cat6 back to a recorder in a locked field office once temporary power is set. On campus redevelopment this often goes in building by building, becoming the permanent system as each structure is brought back online.

Configuration 4

Mobile surveillance trailer

For acreage, laydown yards and access roads with nothing to mount on — the practical answer on a large campus before any building is secured. See solar surveillance trailers for Ulster County.

Element 5.5 · Terminology

What the Quote Actually Says

PoE

Power over Ethernet — one Cat6 run carries video and power, so a hardwired system needs a cable path rather than an outlet at every camera.

LTE / cellular

The unit has its own SIM and data plan, independent of any site network. Data budget is why clip length and alert sensitivity get tuned during commissioning.

AI detection

On-camera classification separating people and vehicles from branches, weather and wildlife — essential on a large rural campus where movement is constant.

Talk-down

A speaker that lets an operator or the account owner address someone on site live rather than reviewing a clip the next morning.

Dormancy coverage

Camera coverage of vacant buildings and parcels between approvals and construction, when there is no crew and no service but the metal is still in the walls.

Non-penetrating mount

Fence, container, ballasted-pole or trailer mounting that requires no excavation — the standard approach on watershed-regulated parcels.

Element 5.7 · System Selection

Four Questions That Settle the Spec

1. Is anyone on site yet?

A vacant property needs coverage aimed at entries and the buildings holding metal. An active site needs coverage aimed at the work front, storage and staging. Most Ulster campus projects need both, in sequence.

2. Is there power?

No service means battery or solar. Once a service is energized and lockable, hardwired becomes available and gets cheaper per camera across a long build.

3. How many access roads?

On a campus, entrance count drives the plan far more than acreage. Every road in is a separate point that has to be covered at plate height.

4. Who needs access?

An owner watching one house is a different account than a developer, a GC, a public funder's inspector and a property manager all needing separate logins.

Element 5.8 · Placement Planning

Where the Cameras Actually Go

Placement comes off the drawings and the walk. On an Ulster site the priority order usually runs like this.

  1. Every access road and gate. On a campus that is two or three points, each needing a head at plate height and angle. On a village lot it is the single curb cut.
  2. Buildings still holding metal. On a vacant or phased property, the structures with switchgear, mechanical and copper still in place are the target — not the empty ones.
  3. The container row and gang boxes. Once a crew is on site, tools leave from where tools are stored.
  4. Material laydown and equipment parking. Machines, fuel, wire and mechanical staged for lift, which on a campus can sit a long way from any occupied area.
  5. Street frontage. On Kingston, New Paltz and Saugerties infill this doubles as documentation of the sidewalk and adjacent property before your equipment arrived.

Element 22 · Build-Phase Security Map

What to Watch, Phase by Phase

Phase 0

Dormancy & pre-construction

The phase most plans skip. Vacant buildings still hold copper, switchgear and mechanical, and there is no crew to notice anything. Access roads and the buildings holding value get covered before the first permit is pulled.

Phase 1

Abatement & demolition

Doors open all day, dumpsters cycling, and the existing metal being removed by people who are supposed to be removing it. Entry coverage and clear documentation of what left and when.

Phase 2

Structure & rough-in

New wire, plumbing and mechanical arrive. Peak copper exposure, and the phase where a loss costs weeks rather than dollars.

Phase 3

Finish & closeout

Appliances, fixtures and a dozen subs moving through propped doors. Access-driven losses overtake perimeter losses, and the camera record becomes how disputes get settled.

Element 9 · Questions Ulster Builders Actually Ask

Fourteen Real Questions, Answered Straight

The property is vacant and we won't start for a year. Is a camera premature?

Usually the opposite. A vacant building with copper and switchgear still in it, no crew and no service is the most exposed the property will ever be. Coverage during dormancy is cheaper than replacing what disappears before the job starts.

There's no power on the campus.

Battery and cellular covers it. Units need a mount and a signal, not a service, which is why they can go up on a property that has been dark for years.

We have three access roads onto the property. How does that work?

One head per road at plate height and angle, because those are the only points every vehicle must use. Everything after that follows the buildings that actually hold value.

Half the buildings are empty shells. Do they need coverage?

Not equally. The ones with mechanical, switchgear and piping still in place are the targets. Empty shells can usually be covered by the access-road heads rather than individually.

Do we have to sign a year?

Rentals are month-to-month, which matters on a phased project where a stall can last a season. You pay for coverage while you need it rather than for a term.

Will wildlife set off alerts all night out here?

On a rural Ulster parcel, wildlife is the leading cause of nuisance alerts. AI classification and zone drawing handle it, and the tuning happens at commissioning rather than as a later service call.

Who owns the video?

The account owner. Access is granted per user, retention is configurable, and clip export is available to the owner on request.

We're renovating in the Kingston Stockade District.

Temporary units go on fencing, containers and poles rather than on the protected facade, which generally keeps design review focused on the building. The permanent system is a separate conversation at turnover with the architect involved.

Does this do anything for our insurance?

Recorded footage may assist with incident and insurance documentation. Coverage requirements and any possible policy impacts must be confirmed directly with your insurer — we make no claims about your policy.

Our parcel is in the Ashokan watershed. Can you mount anything?

Yes, using non-penetrating methods. Fence, container, ballasted-pole and trailer mounts avoid ground disturbance entirely, which keeps the camera outside the permitting conversation.

What if somebody takes the camera?

Units alert on tamper and removal, and footage lives in the cloud as well as on board, so pulling a unit does not erase what it already captured.

Our project is state and county funded. Does that change the equipment?

It usually does. Publicly financed work generally expects NDAA-compliant equipment with documentation in the submittal and documented retention at closeout, so compliant lines are specified by default.

Cell coverage is bad up in the mountains.

It varies sharply with terrain in the western towns. Carrier selection and a signal check at the actual mounting height are part of the walk, and on-board recording carries the site through dropouts until the link returns.

What does removal cost at the end?

Nothing additional on a rental. Closeout removal is included, which matters when the last two weeks of a job are already a scramble.

Element 23 · Delivery-Week Protocol

The Weeks That Carry the Most Risk

On most Ulster builds the highest-value window is when wire, mechanical, appliances or fixtures land and sit before installation — and on a campus that staging area may be a long way from anyone. The protocol is mostly scheduling rather than hardware.

  1. Tell us the delivery date so coverage is re-aimed at the staging area before the truck arrives rather than after.
  2. Motion zones tighten around the material for the duration, so alerts mean something during the window that matters.
  3. Overnight monitoring can be added for the delivery week alone on rental accounts and dropped afterward.
  4. Signage goes up at each access road — visible equipment is part of the deterrent value.
  5. When the material is installed, zones return to normal so the site is not generating noise for the rest of the job.

Licensed installation on record · dormancy and active-phase coverage planned together · equipment matched to canopy, signal and power · non-penetrating mounts for watershed parcels · cloud retention independent of the device · optional live operators · a three-year warranty on AESS-supplied products.

Element 21 · Field Notes

Notes From Ulster Installs

Practical observations from working in this county, offered as installer guidance rather than as customer accounts:

On large properties the useful question is not how many cameras but which buildings still hold metal. A dozen structures on a campus are rarely equal targets — the ones with intact switchgear, mechanical rooms and copper risers are, and covering those plus the access roads gets more protection per dollar than spreading heads evenly across the site.

Dormancy coverage has different power math than active-site coverage. There is no crew to swap a battery or notice an offline unit, so banks get sized longer, low-battery alerts get routed to someone who will actually act, and solar is favored wherever the sky allows it. A unit that quietly dies in February on an empty property is worse than no unit, because everyone assumed it was working.

Cellular coverage in the western towns changes with terrain rather than distance. A site in Shandaken or Denning can have usable signal at the top of a rise and nothing three hundred feet down the road. Checking at the actual mounting position during the walk — and confirming carrier — prevents the return trip.

Element 26 · Weather & Winter Operations

Catskill Winters, Canopy and December Sun

Three weather realities drive equipment choices here, and they hit harder in Ulster than downstate. Cold reduces usable battery capacity, and the western towns run colder and longer than the river corridor, so banks get sized for a real Catskill February. Short days cut solar harvest exactly when the site is dark for sixteen hours, which is why panel angle is set for the low winter sun rather than for June. And wind across a ridge or an open campus loads a mast-mounted camera hard enough that mounting hardware gets specified for the exposure.

Winter storms add the other problem — multi-day power and network outages, and access roads that may not be plowed on a dormant property. A cellular unit with on-board recording keeps working through an outage that takes a wired system down, and uploads what it captured once the link returns.

"The property sits empty between phases."

Dormancy coverage with longer battery autonomy, alert routing to someone who will act, and heads on the buildings that still hold metal.

Plate-height heads on each access road, because those are the points every vehicle must use regardless of where they end up.

Signal checked at the actual mounting height, with carrier selection and on-board recording covering the gaps.

NDAA-compliant equipment, documented retention and access records assembled at commissioning rather than reconstructed at closeout.

Catskill and western-town sites get on-site deterrence — visible equipment, lighting and optional talk-down — because passive visibility from the road is not doing the work there.
