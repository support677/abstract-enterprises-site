TERMINOLOGY

The Allerton Repair Handbook

Shared names keep courtyard work honest: entry buzzer, apartment entry system, building entrance system, visitor entry system, door release system, building access system — in Allerton those labels run from a rowhouse’s front vestibule buzzer to a quadrangle wing’s lobby entrance buzzer, and beneath each sits the same chain: a door release button, the electric strike it commands, the buzzer transformer and buzzer power supply behind the circuit, the entry system wiring threading court and riser alike, and on modernized entrances a remote door release riding a phone.

Court topology. The map of a complex’s entrances, gates, trunks and stages — the first thing drawn on any courtyard call, and the reason faults get found instead of chased.

Two-stage entry. Street gate first, building door second. Each stage carries its own strike, wiring and timing, and each can fail alone.

Wing. One entrance’s slice of a complex — its panel, its branch, its stations. Dead wing with healthy neighbors means wing-level fault.

Shared trunk. The spine feeding multiple wings. When several entrances degrade together, the trunk is the suspect — not the apartments.

Gate circuit. The court gate’s own loop: call point, release, wiring. Serviced in sequence with the lobby stage, never confused with it.

Coops-era stock. The 1920s cooperative buildings and their garden cousins — preserved hardware, matched parts, boards that document everything.

Stage test. Proving gate and lobby circuits separately before blaming either — the two-stage entry’s version of testing in order.

Branch fault. A failure in one wing’s feed off the trunk. Repaired at the branch; the rest of the complex never notices.

Release path. Button to strike, end to end — per stage. Visitors heard but never admitted means the fault lives here, at one stage or the other.

Station. The in-unit wall unit, any generation installed here. Fails alone, replaced alone — the wing keeps ringing around it.

Minute book. Where a co-op’s findings land: photographed, itemized, priced both ways when a decision is pending.

Platform vs. part. The whole repair-or-replace question in four words: parts fail cheap and often, platforms rare and dear. Diagnosis keeps them separate — per wing and per complex.
