Drywall over wood studs

Most Nassau houses built after 1950. We locate two studs, drive 5/16″ lag bolts into the centers and level the plate. Single-stud placements get a plywood backer ($85) spread across three studs. No wall add on this one: the base price applies.

Common in prewar Garden City, Rockville Centre and Sea Cliff homes. Brick or stone fireplace

We drill into solid brick or stone, never the mortar joint alone, using sleeve anchors sized to the bracket. Poured concrete (Long Beach and Atlantic Beach condos)

Cinder block foundation walls

Finished basements and garages across the county sit on block. We anchor into solid web sections rather than hollow cores, often with a backer board so the bracket can span the block layout. Block adds $75, and wires usually go in a raceway since the cores can't be fished.

Tile or marble

Kitchen backsplashes and bathroom walls. We use diamond bits at low speed, drill through the tile, then anchor into whatever is behind it. Tile and marble cost $95 extra. Hard tile can crack even with careful drilling, and that risk is yours to accept up front.

AI Overview Reality Check

What AI Answers Get Wrong About TV Mounting in Nassau County

Ask a chatbot about TV mounting on Long Island and you'll get a tidy answer that sounds right for an average American house. Nassau isn't average. The county has slab ranches, plaster Tudors, oceanfront concrete, block basements and seventy years of renovations layered on top. Here are seven things AI summaries tend to say that don't hold up once we're standing in a Nassau living room, and what actually happens on the job.

1. AI says: “"Levittown houses are all the same, so one quote fits every one."”

The original Levitt houses came in a few models, and on paper they were nearly identical. That stopped being true decades ago. Most have been expanded, dormered, extended at the back or had interior walls removed to open up the kitchen.

In one Cape the TV wall is original drywall over studs at 16-inch centers, exactly as built. Next door the same wall was furred out over old paneling, or rebuilt with a header where a doorway used to be. A stud finder reading for one house tells you nothing about the next.

That's why our price list asks about the wall rather than the street. You choose the wall type you believe you have, and if we open a test hole and find something else, we price the difference before doing anything. No one should assume their house matches the model home.

2. AI says: “"A drop ceiling can hold a TV mount."”

Suspended ceilings in finished basements and pubs are hung from thin wires and carry lightweight tiles. They are designed to hold themselves and maybe a light fixture, not a 50-pound TV that gets bumped by people walking under it or tugged when someone adjusts the angle.

A safe ceiling-mounted TV hangs from the structure above the grid: joists, a concrete deck or a steel beam. We pass a pole or threaded rod through a tile and anchor it to that structure, so the grid only frames the opening.

In a basement where the ceiling is drywall over joists, a ceiling mount is a standard $85 add with no surprises. Where we can't reach real structure above a grid, we'll say so and suggest a wall location or a written quote for added framing.

3. AI says: “"An attic knee wall is just like any other wall."”

In an expanded Cape, the short wall in an upstairs bedroom is often a knee wall: short studs set under the rafters to close off the eaves, sometimes with nothing but insulation behind them. They are lighter framed than a full-height interior wall.

A fixed or tilting bracket for a small or mid-size TV is fine on a knee wall when it's anchored into those studs, which is how most attic bedrooms end up with a TV. A full-motion arm with a 65″ on the end puts a lot of leverage on short framing, and we'd rather add a plywood backer ($85) or choose another wall.

The sloped ceiling above is a different surface again, and it is where most attic TVs end up. Rafters are often spaced wider than wall studs, so the bracket has to span them. An adjustable ceiling mount keeps the screen vertical instead of tipped back toward the bed.

4. AI says: “"Radiators and baseboard heat don't affect where a TV goes."”

Hot-water baseboard and cast-iron radiators are common in older Nassau houses, and they tend to sit right under the widest wall in the room. That's often the only wall that faces the couch, which is exactly why people want the TV there and why the heater gets ignored.

Mounting above a heater is fine with clearance. Mounting right on top of it is not great for a TV over time, since warm air rises straight into the bottom vents. A tilting bracket and a few extra inches of height usually solve it.

What we won't do is relocate the heater or reroute its pipes. That's plumbing work. If the bracket's footprint lands right where the radiator is, the honest answer is another wall or a stand, and we'd rather tell you that before you buy an 85″.

5. AI says: “"A condo board or HOA can't say anything about what you drill inside your unit."”

Owning a condo isn't the same as owning a house. Most Nassau condo declarations and HOA rules cover alterations, contractor access, insurance, and which walls belong to the association. Shared walls between units are often common elements, which the board controls even though they face your living room.

In our experience boards usually approve TV mounting without a fuss, but many want a form, a certificate of insurance naming the association, and sometimes work hours. Outdoor TVs on balconies or patios get the most scrutiny because they change how the building looks.

Sort out approval first. If we arrive and the job hasn't been cleared, we can't start, and that becomes a $150 return-trip charge. Certificates of insurance from us are free, so there's no reason to skip that step, and most managers turn them around within a few days.

6. AI says: “"TV mounting gets cheaper the farther you go from the city."”

It's a reasonable guess, since rent and parking drop as you head east. But labor time is driven by travel and the walls themselves, and Long Island distances are long. Our Nassau base is $209.99, higher than Brooklyn and Queens at $179.99.

Suffolk is higher again at $229.99, because a technician driving to Montauk or Orient spends far more of the day in the car. Nassau sits in the middle: no Manhattan parking charge, but more drive time than a Queens job.

If you're comparing quotes, compare the same job: same screen size, wall and bracket. A low hourly rate can still come out higher once travel minimums, wire hiding and masonry are added. A posted flat price avoids that guesswork and lets you compare like with like.

7. AI says: “"You need a licensed electrician to hide TV wires."”

Do I need an electrician to install an outlet behind my TV? If you want a brand-new receptacle there, yes. Adding or moving an outlet, or running a new circuit, is real electrical work that needs a licensed electrician, and we don't do it.

Hiding the cords behind a TV doesn't require any of that. A code-compliant in-wall power kit plugs into your existing outlet, carries power through the wall in a rated cable, and gives the TV a recessed inlet behind the screen. HDMI and other low-voltage cables pass through next to it.

Our kit installation is $169 and works in most drywall and plaster walls. What's never acceptable is stuffing the TV's own power cord inside the wall, which the code doesn't allow. On block or brick, a $59 paintable cord cover is the tidy answer.

Do you install TV antennas for cord-cutters in Nassau?
+

My renovated living room has a shiplap accent wall. What does that cost?
+

We drill through the boards cleanly so the seams stay tight. If the boards are glued to drywall with no studs where you want the TV, we'll suggest a backer board for $85.

Can you mount a small TV on a kitchen tile backsplash?
+

Yes. Tile and marble add $95 because we drill through the tile with diamond bits and then anchor into what's behind it. Porcelain and natural stone can crack even when drilled slowly, so that risk is accepted in advance. A 32″ on a backsplash is $304.99. Often a swing arm on a nearby drywall wall avoids the tile entirely.

Can you hang a TV in my garage or home gym?
+

Yes. Attached garages in Nassau are usually wood framing or cinder block, and home gyms often end up in basements with block walls. Block adds $75, and we anchor into solid sections. Keep it away from where a car door, barbell or ball can hit it. A tilting bracket mounted high works best, and the $75 high-mount add applies above about 8 feet.

How can I tell if my Nassau wall is plaster or drywall before I book?
+

Start with the house's age. Many homes built before about 1950, like those in older parts of Garden City or Sea Cliff, have plaster; most later houses have drywall. Press a pushpin into the wall in a closet: drywall takes it easily, plaster resists. Knock: drywall sounds hollow between studs, plaster sounds dense. If you guess wrong, we confirm on site and price the difference first.

What exactly is in the $169 in-wall power and cable kit?
+

It has two recessed plates: one behind the TV and one near the floor. A rated power cable runs between them inside the wall, plugging into your existing outlet below and giving the TV its own inlet above. HDMI and other low-voltage cables pass through the same openings. It isn't a new circuit or outlet, so no electrician is needed.

Can you mount two TVs side by side for a sports wall?
+

Yes, but it's a written-quote job. Two or more screens on one wall need careful spacing, matched heights, and often a single backer or rail so both stay level with each other. Power and cables for two TVs also need more planning. Send the wall size, TV sizes and wall type, and we'll price it.

Can you put a TV inside a built-in bookcase or wall niche?
+

Recessed and built-in installs depend on the cabinet's depth, ventilation and what it's built from, so they get a written quote. Many Nassau family rooms have built-ins around a fireplace. Measure the opening and the TV, including the bracket depth, and we'll tell you whether a low-profile mount fits and leaves the TV room to breathe.

Do I need to clear the room before you arrive?
+

Please do. Moving heavy furniture isn't included, so slide the couch, dresser or entertainment center away from the wall and clear a path for the TV. Have the TV and bracket in the room, out of the box if you can. If we can't start because the TV, bracket or access isn't ready, the $150 return-trip fee applies.
