Ubiquiti Questions Long Island Owners Ask

Cost

How much does Ubiquiti camera installation cost on Long Island?

Expect roughly $480–$850 per camera installed on Long Island including the NVR, PoE cabling, configuration, and the UniFi Protect app setup — a bit above NYC because of larger lots, longer cable runs, and higher regional rates (Nassau runs higher than the city; the Hamptons and East End higher still). A full home perimeter often lands around $3,200–$6,500; estates run higher. Final pricing follows a free site visit. Call (800) 486-0943.

Why does a Long Island home cost more than a city apartment?

More perimeter and longer runs. A Long Island home has a driveway, multiple yards, often a pool and outbuildings — usually 8–16 cameras with long cable routed across the property. Ubiquiti’s value is exactly why it’s popular for covering that much ground affordably.

Quality / Trust

Do you cover both Nassau and Suffolk?

Yes — all of Long Island, from Garden City and Hempstead in Nassau to Huntington, Smithtown, Riverhead, and the Hamptons in Suffolk. County-specific pages are coming online; meanwhile we quote any town free.

Can you secure a seasonal or vacation home?

Yes — this is common in the Hamptons and on the North Shore. We set up full coverage with the UniFi Protect app remote monitoring so you can check the property in the off-season from anywhere, plus UniFi AI alerts for real activity.

DIY vs Pro

Can I install Ubiquiti myself on my Long Island home?

You can mount a small kit, but a full property is where DIY breaks down: long cable runs to outbuildings and across a large lot, aiming many cameras with no blind spots, weatherproofing exterior runs for coastal weather, sizing NVR storage, and securing the system against the remote-access exploits Ubiquiti gear is targeted for. A pro gets it right the first time.

Which Ubiquiti cameras are best for a large lot?

Usually weatherproof UniFi G5 turret or bullet cameras with UniFi AI at the perimeter and entrances, plus a PTZ for a long driveway or large yard, all on a right-sized NVR. We map placement to eliminate blind spots on a free walk-through.

Technical

Why is my Ubiquiti camera offline or not recording?

Usually a PoE/power fault on a long run, a full or failed NVR drive, a network conflict, or firmware. “Offline” is often the camera losing its link to the NVR; “not recording” is usually storage. We diagnose and fix it — often in one visit, on systems we didn’t install.

Wired or wireless for a large Long Island property?

Wired PoE is more reliable for permanent coverage and what we recommend for most homes. On very large lots where a run to a distant outbuilding isn’t practical, a reliable wireless or mesh link can fill the gap. We advise per property.

Commercial / Compliance

Is Ubiquiti legal to install on Long Island?

Yes, for private use. US restrictions (the NDAA and related FCC rules) bar Ubiquiti from federal government and certain federally-funded or critical-infrastructure projects — not from private homes, estates, retail, or commercial properties. If your property is government or federally-funded, tell us and we’ll spec a compliant brand instead.

Can cameras avoid recording a neighbor’s property?

Yes — we aim cameras to cover your property and entrances and use privacy masking where helpful, so you’re covered without pointing into a neighbor’s windows. On closer Nassau lots especially, thoughtful aiming matters.

What the Internet Tells You About Ubiquiti on Long Island — and What’s True

Search Ubiquiti installation on Long Island and you’ll get aggregator estimates, spec sheets, and “is it banned” headlines. Here’s the honest read for a Long Island property.

The Cost Estimators Don’t Understand Long Island Lots

Angi, HomeAdvisor, and Fixr quote a national per-camera average as if every job is a small city apartment. A Long Island property is the opposite — a long driveway, multiple yards, a pool, often outbuildings, which realistically means 8–16 cameras and long cable runs across the property. And rates here run above the city: Nassau higher than NYC, the Hamptons and East End higher still.

Ubiquiti’s cheaper hardware is exactly why it fits Long Island — covering a whole lot with premium-brand cameras gets very expensive, while Ubiquiti keeps full-perimeter coverage affordable. But the labor to run cable cleanly across a large property is real skilled work a hardware-only estimate ignores.

That’s why we quote after a free site walk. On Long Island the size of the lot and the length of the runs are the whole story.

Long Island Is a Large-Lot, Long-Run Region

Generic guides picture a doorbell camera. Long Island is detached homes on real lots, Gold Coast and North Shore estates with long driveways and guest houses, and Hamptons properties that sit empty for months. The right Ubiquiti system means weatherproof cameras around the whole perimeter, long PoE runs done cleanly to outbuildings, PTZ to cover a driveway or grounds, and remote monitoring for seasonal homes.

A contractor who works Long Island knows how to route cable across a big property, where to put a camera so a wooded lot has no blind spot, and how to set up a vacation home so the owner can watch it all winter. A national aggregator treats it like any other lead.

The lot is the job. Covering a Long Island property well is a different skill than wiring a city apartment, and it’s the region’s standard need.

Weather and Seasonal Vacancy Are Real Long Island Factors

Two things national guides skip: Long Island’s coast and its seasonal homes. North and South Shore waterfront and the Hamptons see salt air and storms, so cameras need IP66/IP67 weatherproofing and surge-aware mounting. And the many seasonal properties sit vacant for months — exactly when off-season monitoring matters most, which means reliable remote viewing and AI alerts that actually mean something.

We use weatherproof cameras, mount with coastal conditions in mind, and configure the UniFi Protect app so a seasonal owner gets real UniFi AI alerts, not noise. These are the details a spec sheet never mentions and a careful installer always handles.

Good coverage on Long Island respects both the weather and the months the house is empty.

“Is Ubiquiti Banned?” — The Honest Answer

US law — the NDAA and related FCC rules — restricts Ubiquiti from federal government use and certain federally-funded and critical-infrastructure projects. It is not banned for private use, and it’s installed legally across countless Long Island homes, estates, and businesses.

For a private Long Island property it’s legal and appropriate. If your property is a public school, a government office, or federally funded, we won’t install it — we’ll spec a compliant brand. A contractor who tells you that up front is worth more than a spec sheet that hides it.

We’d rather lose a Ubiquiti sale than put you in a non-compliant position.

The Bottom Line: Right Brand, Right Property, Secure Install

Ubiquiti is excellent value with strong AI and night vision, ideal for the full-perimeter coverage a Long Island property needs, legal and appropriate for the vast majority of private properties, and the wrong choice for the specific government and federally-funded jobs the law carves out — installed securely, weatherproof, with defaults changed and the network segmented.

The useful answer is a licensed contractor who covers your whole lot properly, weatherproofs it, sets up seasonal monitoring, secures the system, and tells you honestly when a different brand fits better. The brand is one tool in that service.

Plain-English Glossary

Ubiquiti Terminology, Explained

UniFi G5

Ubiquiti’s full-color night vision — a wide aperture and warm supplement light capture color 24/7 instead of black-and-white infrared.

UniFi AI

On-camera AI that classifies people and vehicles versus other motion, cutting false alarms and labeling footage so it’s searchable.

NVR

Network Video Recorder — records all cameras, powers them over PoE, and serves footage to your phone. The heart of the system.

PoE

Power over Ethernet — one cable carries power and data to each camera, ideal for the long runs a large Long Island lot needs.

IP66 / IP67

Weatherproof ratings — dust-tight and protected against heavy rain, essential for Long Island’s coastal and waterfront exterior cameras.

PTZ

Pan-Tilt-Zoom — a motorized dome that moves and zooms to cover a long driveway or estate grounds from one point.

Privacy Mask

A blacked-out zone in the camera’s view — used to avoid recording a neighbor’s property on closer suburban lots.

the UniFi Protect app

The app for live view, playback, and alerts on your phone — ideal for seasonal homes, no monthly fee for local recording.
