The Neighborhood

Why Morris Heights' Towers, Clinics and Riverfront Buildings Need Fire Alarms Designed Floor by Floor

Morris Heights is the West Bronx neighborhood between Jerome Avenue and the Harlem River. One local history draws it from Burnside Avenue on the north to the Cross Bronx Expressway on the south (The Neighborhoods); CityNeighborhoods.nyc puts the northern line at West 179th Street. University Avenue, also signed Dr. Martin Luther King Jr. Boulevard, is the main street, according to Bronx Community Board 5, and the land falls from Jerome Avenue toward the river in ridges and terraces. The 4 train runs elevated over Jerome Avenue on the east edge; the Major Deegan Expressway and the Metro-North Hudson Line run along the river on the west.

The building stock is a mix you rarely find in one neighborhood. Five- and six-story prewar apartment houses from the 1910s to the 1930s line Sedgwick, University and Harrison Avenues (CityNeighborhoods.nyc). Many were rebuilt after the fires and abandonment of the 1970s. Then come the tall buildings. River Park Towers, completed in 1975 beside Roberto Clemente State Park, has 1,654 apartments in two conjoined towers that the Bronx Times described as the tallest buildings in the Bronx. 1520 Sedgwick Avenue, the 18-story, 102-apartment building from 1967 that New York State recognized in 2007 as the birthplace of hip hop, sits near the expressway.

The neighborhood's institutions add a third layer. Morris Heights Health Center grew from a single site at 70 West Burnside Avenue in 1981 into an operator of 30 sites, and its headquarters at 85 West Burnside Avenue has 33,000 square feet (Wikipedia). Across the street, the center built a mixed-use building that its architect lists as a diagnostic and treatment center, a mental health and wellness center, physical therapy, a medical retail space, covered parking for 80 vehicles and about 70 senior apartments (Terjesen Associates). That is a clinic, a garage and housing in one building, and the fire alarm has to treat each one differently.

The newest work continues the pattern. In September 2026, New York YIMBY reported approved plans for affordable senior housing at 105 West Tremont Avenue, at the corner of Grand Avenue, which its architect describes as ten stories and 100 feet with 95 apartments for seniors, 24-hour reception and Passive House construction (Scott Brownrigg). On Cedar Avenue, the low strip by the Metro-North station that the Department of City Planning describes as parking lots, two- to six-story commercial buildings and light industry, a four-story building with 13 apartments and commercial space was filed in November 2025 to replace a two-story house from 1899 (Marketproof).

And the fires keep the subject from being theoretical. In March 2026, a fire that started in a top-floor apartment at 1775 Davidson Avenue reached the roof, drew nearly 200 firefighters and displaced seven households; FDNY Deputy Chief Michele Fitzsimmons told News 12, "Hydrants were blocked by cars." In August 2026, a fourth-floor fire that News 12 reported burning inside an apartment's walls displaced ten people. We know of no fire alarm failure in either; they are why owners here ask what a building-wide system adds.

This page covers commercial and code-required fire alarm systems only: apartment buildings, towers, clinics, senior buildings, stores, workshops and new construction. Smoke alarms inside individual one- and two-family homes follow separate rules and are not part of this service.

Building Types

Commercial Fire Alarm Systems for Morris Heights' Building Types, From Riverfront Towers to Cedar Avenue

Commercial fire alarm systems are required by what a building is used for and how big it is, not by its neighborhood. These are the building types that bring Morris Heights owners and managers to a fire alarm design, and the detail that drives each one.

Conjoined towers by the river

River Park Towers was designed by Davis, Brody & Associates and completed in 1975. The firm describes a pair of conjoined towers with mirrored, offset plans that widen toward the top, built of its own eight-inch-square "super bricks," with a bridge over the Major Deegan Expressway to the parking (Davis Brody Bond). Published heights run from 38 to 44 stories (Davis Brody Bond; NYC Department of City Planning), and the Bronx Times gave 404 feet; any of those puts the buildings far above New York City's high-rise line, which is a floor more than 75 feet above fire department vehicle access (National Fire Sprinkler Association). In a building like that, fire alarm work is rarely a single project: it is a riser, a floor, a panel or a voice system at a time, while 1,654 households stay at home.

A 1960s tower that left Mitchell-Lama

1520 Sedgwick Avenue was finished in 1967 with 102 apartments on 18 floors. Its owners took it out of the Mitchell-Lama program after 2007, it was sold in 2008, and after a foreclosure the nonprofit-backed buyers took title in November 2011 with help from a city Housing Development Corporation loan (Wikipedia). On a tower with that history, the first job is usually reconstructing what was approved.

Clinics with apartments and a garage in the same building

The health center's newer West Burnside Avenue building stacks a diagnostic and treatment center, mental health services, physical therapy, offices, a medical retail space, covered parking and about 70 senior apartments (Terjesen Associates; Wikipedia). Each use has its own fire alarm trigger in the NYC Building Code, but the building has one panel and one FDNY approval. The design has to decide what the garage gets, how the clinic's devices report, whether apartments hear clinic alarms, and who answers the panel at 2 a.m. when the clinic is closed.

Prewar walk-ups and renovated apartment houses

The five- and six-story buildings on Sedgwick, University and Harrison Avenues were built between the 1910s and 1930s and many were renovated later through city programs such as Tenant Interim Lease and low-income housing tax credits (CityNeighborhoods.nyc). NY Engineers' summary of NYC Building Code section 907.2 puts apartment buildings with 16 or more units, or with apartments three or more stories above the lowest exit level, among those that need a fire alarm. When a renovation touches common areas, the fire alarm usually comes up in the same filing.

A new senior building above the high-rise line

The ten-story, 100-foot building approved at 105 West Tremont Avenue will have 95 senior apartments, community and recreation rooms, consultation rooms and a 24-hour reception desk (Scott Brownrigg). At 100 feet, its upper floors will be above the 75-foot line, so it will be designed as a high-rise, with the voice system and fire command features that brings. Field Notes

Field Notes: Draw the Building as a Stack Before Drawing Any Devices

Our technicians start Morris Heights buildings with a sketch: one box per floor, with each floor's use written inside. In a building where a clinic sits under senior apartments and a garage sits under both, it is the most useful page in the job file. Every fire alarm decision follows from that stack: which floors sleep, which floors close at night, which floor holds cars, and who has keys to each one.

The second habit is to ask who answers the panel when each part of the building is empty. A clinic closes at night, a garage is unattended, a senior building has a reception desk, and a walk-up has a superintendent who may live two blocks away. The panel and annunciator go where someone will actually be, and the central station call list is written to match, with a name for each use.

The third is to treat tall buildings as a series of small jobs. Replacing devices in a 40-story tower happens a floor or a riser at a time while residents stay home, so we plan each night's work so the system goes back into service before the crew leaves. These are working habits, described in general terms; we don't describe specific clients' buildings on this page.

Your Questions Answered

Morris Heights Fire Alarm Questions From Tower Managers, Clinic Operators, Senior Housing Developers and Shop Owners

Towers by the river

Our tower's residents reported an elevator out of service for years. Does a broken elevator change what the fire alarm does in an emergency?

The fire alarm still sends a recall signal, but a car that is out of service can't answer it, and firefighters lose that car for the operation. The fire alarm and the elevator are separate systems tested together: when lobby, machine room or shaft detectors activate, the panel tells each car to return to its designated floor. The Bronx Times reported in 2015 that River Park Towers residents had complained about broken elevators, including one they said was out for more than five years. If a car is taken out of service, tell the FDNY and your fire alarm company, and test recall on that car again before it returns to service.

When firefighters walk into one lobby of a two-tower complex, how do they see an alarm that started in the other tower or in the parking structure?

Through the panels and annunciators, if the system was designed for it. In a complex with conjoined towers and parking reached by a bridge over the Major Deegan, as Davis Brody Bond describes River Park Towers, the design professional decides from the Certificate of Occupancy and the fire separations whether the property is one fire alarm network or several panels that report to each other. Either way, the goal on the ground is the same: an officer at any lobby should be able to read every alarm on the property, by tower, floor and device, without walking to another building to find out where the fire is.

The birthplace of hip hop and other older towers

The FDNY notice for fireproof buildings tells River Park Towers residents to stay inside unless the fire is in their own apartment. Then what is the tower's fire alarm supposed to tell them?

Where the fire is and what to do, through the voice system if the building has one. The FDNY's notice for non-combustible buildings says to "stay inside your apartment and listen for instructions from firefighters unless conditions become dangerous." In a high-rise, those instructions come over the speakers from the fire command station, often to the fire floor and the floors next to it first. That only works if every speaker is audible and every floor is labeled the way residents know it, which is what the annual test should prove. The notice itself doesn't mention the fire alarm, so we explain the link to residents and staff at handover.

1520 Sedgwick Avenue changed owners several times after leaving Mitchell-Lama. How does a new owner find out what fire alarm was approved for a building like that?

Start with the FDNY's records for the address, which a design professional or expediter can request, and the central station's account history, which shows what the panel has been reporting. Then look in the building itself: the panel often has the installer's sticker, and the superintendent may have a binder of test reports. 1520 Sedgwick Avenue went from Mitchell-Lama to a private sale in 2008 to foreclosure and new owners in 2011 (Wikipedia). Each change of hands is a chance for records to disappear, so a new owner should build the file before the first inspection, not during it.

The recreation room where the 1973 party happened is still a community room. If a building rents its community room for events, does the fire alarm change?

It can. A community room used by residents is usually treated as part of the residential building. A room rented to outside groups for parties and meetings is an assembly use, and NY Engineers' summary of the Building Code puts the fire alarm trigger for assembly spaces at an occupant load of 300. A room that holds far fewer people may not cross that line, but its posted capacity, its exits and its notification still have to fit how it is really used. The August 11, 1973 back-to-school party at 1520 Sedgwick Avenue happened in that building's recreation room (Wikipedia).

Clinics and mixed buildings

Our clinic is on the floors below senior apartments. When a clinic detector goes off at night, should the whole building evacuate?

That is a design decision, and in a building like this it has to be made on purpose. Many mixed buildings sound alarms throughout, because a fire on the clinic floor threatens everyone above it. Others use zoned or voice messages that tell some floors to leave and others to stay. The sequence of operations on the sealed drawings spells this out, and the FDNY approves it. What doesn't work is a sequence nobody explained to the residents or the night staff.

Our health center building has covered parking for residents and staff under the same roof as the clinic. What kind of detection does a parking level like that get?

Often none of the smoke kind. Exhaust, cold and dust make spot smoke detectors in parking areas a source of false alarms, so where the design calls for detection there, it is usually heat detection, and the sprinkler system's waterflow and valve switches report to the fire alarm. Whether a garage needs carbon monoxide detection is a separate question tied to the fuel-burning equipment and the code. The design professional decides, based on how the garage is enclosed and ventilated.

The health center's first site on West Burnside Avenue began as a birthing center and is now an outpatient women's health pavilion. When a building stops keeping patients overnight, can its fire alarm be scaled back?

Not on its own, and not without a filing. Places where people stay overnight for care are generally held to stricter fire alarm rules than daytime clinics, so ending overnight care can change how the building is classified, but that change runs through the Certificate of Occupancy and the design professional first. Until the paperwork changes, the approved system stays as it is, and removing devices is a modification the FDNY has to approve. Wikipedia records that the 70 West Burnside Avenue site began as a childbearing and birthing center and now houses the Women's Health Pavilion.

Senior and new buildings

The new senior building at 105 West Tremont Avenue will have a 24-hour reception desk. What does the desk staff have to be able to do with the fire alarm?

Read the annunciator, call the FDNY and the central station, and follow the building's fire safety plan. In a high-rise, the desk often sits next to the fire command station, where firefighters take over the voice system and elevator controls when they arrive. Staff don't need to be fire alarm technicians, but they do need to know what each display means, which buttons to leave alone, and who to call. We train them at handover, in plain language, using the building's own screens.

Walk-ups, water and recent fires

Water has been leaking through the top-floor ceilings of our University Avenue building. What does that do to the fire alarm devices up there?

Water causes ground faults, corrodes detector bases and can trigger false alarms or silence a device altogether. After a leak, every device and junction box in the wet area should be opened, dried or replaced, and the circuit tested for grounds before the system is trusted again. Fix the roof first; a new detector in a wet ceiling will fail like the old one. In July 2026, a tenant in a University Avenue building told News 12: "Nice cabinets, nice floors, sounds nice. But my main concern is the roof."

The March fire on Davidson Avenue started in a top-floor apartment and reached the roof. Where does a building fire alarm look for a fire like that?

In the public hallway outside the apartment, at the top of the stairs and, where the design calls for it, in the spaces above the top floor. A building system doesn't replace the smoke alarm inside each apartment, which is the resident's first warning. Its job is to warn everyone else and report to the central station. In the March 2026 fire at 1775 Davidson Avenue, flames spread from a top-floor apartment to the roof (News 12 Bronx). The top floor and stair bulkhead are where we check device coverage first in buildings like it.

News 12 said the August fire in Morris Heights burned inside an apartment's walls. Can a fire alarm detect a fire inside a wall?

Not directly. Detectors sense smoke or heat in the room or hallway; a fire inside a wall shows up there only when smoke escapes into the space. That is why concealed fires are found late and why the walls themselves are built to slow them. A fire alarm's value in that situation is that the first detector to sense the smoke warns the whole building and calls the central station, instead of only the apartment where it starts. The cause of the August 2026 fire was under investigation when News 12 reported it.

Types of Commercial Fire Alarm Systems We Install in Morris Heights

What is a commercial fire alarm system? It is a set of detectors, manual pull stations, horns, strobes and speakers wired to a fire alarm control panel that watches an entire building, warns the people in it, operates other building systems and reports to a central station that calls the FDNY. How does a commercial fire alarm system work? A detector senses smoke or heat, or someone pulls a station; the panel confirms the signal; and within seconds it sounds the alarm, sends elevators to their recall floor, releases doors, stops fans and transmits the alarm off site. Commercial fire detection systems come in a few families, and these are the ones that turn up in Morris Heights buildings.

Addressable

What is an addressable fire alarm system? Each device has its own address, so the panel reports "smoke, 31st floor, east corridor" instead of a zone number. Addressable fire alarm installation, addressable fire alarm system installation and addressable fire alarm panel installation are the standard for towers, clinics and new buildings.

Conventional

What is a conventional fire alarm system? Devices are wired in zones and the panel knows only which zone is in alarm. Conventional fire alarm installation, conventional fire alarm system installation and conventional fire alarm panel installation still fit a single store or a small workshop on Cedar Avenue.

The difference

What is the difference between addressable and conventional fire alarms? Addressable systems name the exact device and warn when a detector is getting dirty; conventional systems name a zone. On a 40-story riser, that is the difference between sending a technician to one door and sending one to a whole floor.

Voice evacuation

Fire alarm voice evacuation installation and fire alarm emergency voice communication for high-rises, where firefighters speak to selected floors from the fire command station, with fire alarm speaker installation and fire alarm speaker strobe installation on every floor.

Intelligent detection

Intelligent fire alarm installation and an intelligent fire detection system use detectors that adjust their sensitivity and report when they need cleaning, which helps in garages, kitchens and dusty workshops where ordinary detectors drift into false alarms.

Air sampling

Aspirating smoke detection installation, an air sampling smoke detection system, VESDA installation and VESDA service for imaging and equipment rooms, high open spaces and places where a spot detector can't be reached for testing. Data center fire detection installation usually starts with this kind of system.

Devices

Fire Alarm Devices in Morris Heights Buildings and Where Each One Goes

Fire alarm equipment installation in Morris Heights ranges from a single detector in a store to thousands of devices in a tower. Fire detection device installation and fire alarm initiating device installation cover what tells the panel something is wrong; notification covers how people find out.

Smoke detectors

Commercial smoke detector installation covers corridors, stairs, lobbies, elevator lobbies and machine rooms, electrical and storage rooms, and the rooms the design calls for in clinics and senior buildings. Addressable smoke detector installation and photoelectric smoke detector installation are the usual choice. In older buildings, commercial smoke detector replacement, commercial smoke detector testing and commercial smoke detector repair are ongoing work, and smoke detector circuit troubleshooting on an old conventional loop often ends at one corroded base on the top floor. The FDNY recommends keeping detectors at least 36 inches from supply diffusers and return openings, which matters in renovated clinic suites where new ducts landed beside old detector locations.

Duct detectors

Duct smoke detector installation, duct smoke detector testing and duct smoke detector replacement apply to the air handlers that serve clinic floors, tower corridors and new ventilation systems. The FDNY expects fans moving more than 2,000 cubic feet per minute to shut down on alarm, and duct detectors are how the system sees smoke inside the air stream.

Our health center leases floors in a building it doesn't own but runs its own front desk. Should our staff be able to see the building's fire alarm display?
+

Can devices be replaced in a riverfront tower without shutting off the alarm for the whole building?
+

How much notice should tower residents get before the annual fire alarm test, and what will they hear?
+

Post notices in the lobby, elevators and mailrooms several days ahead, with the dates, the floors and the hours. The time, the location shown on the annunciator, what they saw when they checked, who they called and when, when the FDNY arrived and left, and any message from the central station. Then the device that started it goes on a list for the fire alarm company. A short log like that is often the only way to see a pattern, such as a detector outside the same apartment going off every few weeks.

Does the fire alarm in a building with a clinic, a garage and apartments report each part separately to the central station?
+

It can, and in a mixed building it should report enough detail for the central station to tell responders where the alarm is. Addressable systems send device or zone information, and the account can carry separate contacts for the clinic, the garage operator and the building. Ask your monitoring company to read back what it receives during the pre-test, before the FDNY acceptance test.

Do fire alarm cables have to be sealed where they pass through the airtight layer of a Passive House building?
+

Yes. Every cable or conduit through the air barrier, and through any fire-rated wall or floor, has to be sealed with the right product, and in a Passive House building the airtightness test will find any gaps. The fire alarm contractor should know where the air barrier is before rough-in and use the sleeves and seals the architect specifies, so the fire alarm doesn't become the reason the building fails its air test.

How do we keep fire alarm devices working on a floor that has been emptied for repairs?
+

Keep the floor on the system and keep it heated and dry if you can. Devices on an empty floor still protect the building above and below it, and a fire on a vacant floor can grow unseen. If construction dust or water threatens the detectors, the design professional and fire alarm company can arrange covers during work hours or temporary measures, but the floor shouldn't simply be disconnected. That was the situation tenants of a University Avenue building described to News 12 in July 2026, with a top floor emptied by leaks.

If a commercial tenant on Cedar Avenue moves out, what happens to the fire alarm devices in its space?
+

They stay on the system and keep working. An empty commercial space is still part of the building, and its detectors, horns and strobes should be tested on schedule like everything else. When the next tenant fits out the space, devices are moved or added as the new layout requires, and that change is filed with the FDNY.

Should a building's fire alarm be checked after a fire nearby, even if its panel never went into alarm?
+

Yes, after any fire in or next to the building. Water from firefighting, smoke and heat can damage devices, junction boxes and cable without setting off the panel, and a damaged circuit may show up weeks later as a ground fault or a device that no longer reports. A technician should check the panel's event history, test devices in the affected areas and look for water in boxes, then write up what was found.
