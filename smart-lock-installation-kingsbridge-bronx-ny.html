The Neighborhood

Why Kingsbridge Properties Need Smart Lock Installation That Starts at the Lobby Door

Most people who ask us about a smart lock in Kingsbridge are really asking about two doors. One is their own apartment door. The other is the building's street or lobby door, and that is usually the one that worries them. Kingsbridge was built for the people who pass through those doors: six-story brick apartment houses climb the hills off Broadway, Kingsbridge Avenue, Heath Avenue and Sedgwick Avenue, and a neighborhood history ties the rise of the six-story buildings on those avenues to the 1 train's arrival early in the twentieth century. Nearly every one of them has a lobby or courtyard entrance shared by dozens of households. If that door is propped, broken or forced, a stranger reaches every apartment door in the building.

The local record shows why we start downstairs. In April 2024 the Norwood News reported a burglary pattern the NYPD had identified in Kingsbridge: on March 25 and again on April 5, someone used a crowbar to pry open the front door of the lobby of the same residential building near Corlear Avenue and West 231st Street, then fled north along Corlear. Nothing was taken either time. The lobby door still gave way twice. In August 2026 The Riverdale Press described residents of Marble Hill Houses, the public housing towers on Broadway between West 225th and 230th Streets, finding entrance doors propped open or forced and keys that sometimes would not open a freshly repaired entrance lock. Bronx Community Board 8's Fiscal Year 2027 statement of needs put it plainly: “There are security concerns as main doors to buildings do not have proper entry security such as working locks and buzzers.”

That is why a Kingsbridge smart lock plan has two halves. The lobby half depends on who owns the building. A private owner of a rental building is responsible for the entrance door, its lock and its buzzer. In the Amalgamated and Park Reservoir cooperatives that responsibility sits with the housing company. At Marble Hill Houses it belongs to NYCHA. Our job there is to tell you whether the door can be fixed with a strike, a closer or a keypad, what it costs at our published Bronx figures, and who has to approve it. The apartment half is yours to decide, within your lease or proprietary lease: a keyed smart deadbolt that locks itself, keeps a record of who came in, and still has a mechanical key for the day the batteries or the building let you down.

The neighborhood's other features shape the job too. Kingsbridge is hilly enough that several of its streets turn into staircases. A walking guide counts 187 steps on the West 229th Street stairs, 70 on West 231st Street and 72 on West 230th Street. Community Board 8 counts one in five of its residents as 65 or older and has asked the city to rebuild the crumbling West 238th Street step street between Waldo and Irwin Avenues. Older residents at the top of those climbs often need codes for aides and relatives more than they need an app. Manhattan University sits just over the line in Riverdale, so many apartments near it are shared and their tenants change each semester. Under the elevated tracks, the Kingsbridge Business Improvement District's roughly 200 merchants run small shops along Broadway and West 231st Street, and they need staff codes they can switch off in a minute.

Building owners and tenants here are also living with heavy housing-code enforcement. The NYU Furman Center counted 222.4 serious housing code violations per 1,000 privately owned rental units in Riverdale/Fieldston, the community district that includes Kingsbridge, in 2024, and 283.6 in Kingsbridge Heights/Bedford next door. A smart lock that stops a door from latching, or an entrance that cannot be opened from inside, only adds to that count. So before anything else we confirm that the door swings shut, latches and opens from inside with one simple motion.

Two-stage entry: courtyard gate, lobby door, apartment door

Buildings with courtyards often have three barriers between the street and your apartment: a courtyard gate, the lobby door and your own door. Why the lobby door comes first, even when it isn't yours

A smart lock on your apartment door makes your own door harder to get through and tells you when it opens. It does nothing about the stairwell, the mailboxes or the packages in the lobby. In the 2024 Corlear Avenue incidents the crowbar went into the lobby door, not an apartment door. In 2008 The Riverdale Press reported four burglaries in one week in the 50th Precinct in which thieves came in through the victims' front doors. One victim in a Riverdale building was believed to have left her door unlocked. At a Claflin Avenue building in Kingsbridge Heights the burglars broke a key off in the lock so no one could get in behind them. Both kinds of problem have practical answers. A lobby door that relocks every time cuts down on strangers in the halls. An apartment lock that locks itself and keeps a log covers the door someone forgot to lock.

If you rent, you can't replace the lobby strike yourself. You can still help get it fixed. Write down when the door fails, photograph it, report it to the owner and, if nothing happens, to the city through 311. Tenants of rent-stabilized apartments can also file with the state housing agency, whose fact sheet lists front and entrance door security, including the lock and buzzer, among required services. If you own the building, a failing lobby door is usually one of three faults: a closer too weak to pull the door shut against the wind, a strike that no longer catches the latch, or a latch bolt worn too short. We find which one it is before we sell anyone a keypad.

What a good Kingsbridge apartment door looks like when we leave

  • The building's own lock is untouched and working, and its key still works.
  • The smart deadbolt sits in solid metal or wood, with a reinforcement plate if old holes are close.
  • The door closes from a fully open position and latches by itself, with the bolt clearing the strike so the motor doesn't strain.
  • From inside, the door opens with one turn of a thumb-turn or lever and no key or code.
  • Auto-lock is set, the entry log works, and alerts reach the phones you chose.
  • A mechanical key exists, and the people the rules name have one.

Compliance points for Kingsbridge shop and office doors

  • Exit without a key: a business door that people leave through must open from inside without a key, code or special knowledge. We never fit a double-cylinder or keypad-both-sides lock on an exit door.
  • Reach and grip: as a guide we follow the 2010 ADA Standards' door-hardware rule of operable parts between 34 and 48 inches above the floor that need no tight grasping, pinching or wrist twisting, which is why we favor lever trim on shop doors.
  • Roll-down gates: the city's 2009 storefront gate law originally required gates to be at least 70 percent transparent by July 1, 2026. In July 2026 NY1 reported that the City Council had unanimously passed a bill repealing that deadline, so that a compliant gate would be needed only when a gate is replaced for other reasons, and sent it to the mayor. DOB permit rules for gates were not changed. Either way, the keypad goes on the door behind the gate, not on the gate itself.
  • Apartments upstairs: if a shop owner also owns the building and adds keyless entry to the residential street door, the city's Tenant Data Privacy Act limits what data can be collected from tenants, and no tenant can be required to use a smart system to get into their own apartment.
  • Electric strikes and fire doors: a labeled fire door or frame is not cut for a strike without the building owner's approval and the correct listed hardware.

Your Questions Answered

Smart Lock Installation in Kingsbridge: Questions Answered

These are the questions Kingsbridge residents, co-op shareholders, building owners and shopkeepers ask us, grouped by the door they are about. Each answer is written for this neighborhood. Questions that apply across the whole borough are on our Bronx smart lock installation page.

When the lobby door is the weak point

Someone pried our lobby door open with a crowbar — is a smart lock on my own door still worth it if the street door can be forced?

Yes, as long as you know what it can and can't do. A crowbar on a lobby door gets someone into the building, not into your apartment. Your apartment door is the next barrier, and a smart deadbolt with a reinforced strike makes it harder to force. Its log and alerts also tell you right away if it opens when you aren't home. What it can't do is fix the lobby. Report the forced door to the owner the same day, and if it isn't repaired, call 311 so there is a record. After a forced entry we look at the frame and strike on your own door, because a lock is only as strong as the wood or steel its bolt goes into.

The street door in our Kingsbridge building won't relock behind people — whose job is it to fix, and how do tenants report it?

The owner's. In a private rental building the entrance door, its lock and its buzzer are the landlord's responsibility. For newer class A buildings, Multiple Dwelling Law §50-a specifically requires self-closing, self-locking entrance doors. Tell the super or managing agent in writing and include the date and a photo. If it stays broken, file a complaint through 311 so HPD can inspect. Rent-stabilized tenants can also file with the state housing agency, which lists entrance-door security among required services. If you are the owner, call us. Most doors that won't relock need a closer adjustment, a strike realigned or a worn latch replaced, and our published Bronx figures cover the strike work.

If the lobby door is often propped open, what should my apartment door do differently?

It should lock itself and tell you what happened. Set auto-lock to a short delay so the bolt goes out even when you are carrying bags and forget. Turn on alerts for unlocks and for failed code attempts. Keep the key cylinder, so a dead battery or a phone left at work doesn't lock you out. Make sure the door viewer still works, and if the building allows it, add a digital viewer so you can see the hall from your phone. A propped lobby means more strangers in the hallway, so the habits that matter most are a door that is always locked and a clear view of who is knocking.

Burglars have jammed apartment locks by snapping a key off inside them — can that be done to a smart lock?

To its key cylinder, yes. Most smart deadbolts keep a mechanical keyway as a backup, and anything pushed into a keyway can jam it. The difference is that you have other ways in. The keypad, a fingerprint or the app still move the bolt, because the motor drives it whether or not the cylinder turns. That doesn't help if the bolt itself has been forced, which is why we check the strike and frame too. If you ever find something stuck in your lock, don't go in alone. Call 911 first, as the 2008 Kingsbridge Heights burglaries are a reminder that someone may still be inside.

Co-ops in Van Cortlandt Village

Amalgamated and Park Reservoir answer to a state housing agency — who has to approve a new lock on a co-op apartment door?

Start with the housing company's management office. The Amalgamated's own FAQ says it is supervised by New York State Homes and Community Renewal, which approves admissions and apartments. Day-to-day rules for cooperators, including alterations, come from the company and its handbook, not from the state agency directly. We have not found a published Amalgamated or Park Reservoir rule specific to door locks, so we suggest asking management one plain question: “May I install a keyed smart deadbolt, or a retrofit on my existing deadbolt, and do you need a copy of the key?” Get the answer in writing, and show it to us at the walkthrough.

Is a retrofit or a new keypad deadbolt the better fit for an Amalgamated or Park Reservoir apartment?

In most limited-equity co-ops, a retrofit. It mounts on the inside of a single-cylinder deadbolt you already have, swaps only the thumb-turn, and leaves the hallway side and key exactly as they were. That suits housing that restores apartments between households and buildings where hallway hardware is uniform. Its limit is that there's no keypad outside unless you add a separate one, so the person coming in needs a phone, a key or a wireless keypad mounted beside the door. A full keypad deadbolt makes sense where management allows it and an existing bore can be reused. In the Amalgamated's 1949–51 concrete buildings and the Sedgwick Avenue towers, we also check whether the phone can reach a bridge from the hallway before you choose.

Roommates, aides and family

A roommate moved out of our Kingsbridge apartment but still knows the code — can we cancel it without involving the landlord?

If the smart lock is yours, installed as the lock you are allowed to add, yes: whoever holds the admin account deletes that person's code and phone access in the app, and it takes effect at once. Then check the activity log for entries after the move-out date. The landlord's own lock and key are a separate matter, and changing them is the landlord's call. That's why we set up shared apartments with one code per person rather than one shared code. The leaseholder holds admin, and a short note inside the door records who has what. If the lock belonged to the departing roommate and is on their account, transfer ownership or reset the lock before they leave.

Mom's aide has to get through the lobby and then her apartment door — can one code cover both?

Only if the same person controls both doors, which in a Kingsbridge rental they usually don't. The apartment door is easy. Give the aide a code that works only during her scheduled hours, and set alerts so you see when she arrives and leaves. The lobby door belongs to the building. If it has a keypad or fob reader, the owner decides who gets a credential. If it has a key lock, the aide needs a building key, which your mother can get from management. Some families have a video intercom or doorbell let the aide be buzzed in remotely. We set up the apartment side and tell you exactly what to ask the owner for the lobby.

Can a fingerprint or a phone tap open the door when my hands are full after the climb up from Broadway?

Yes. A fingerprint reader on the outside of the lock opens it with one finger, even with grocery bags on the other arm. Phone-based unlocking works by tapping a button in the app or a watch, or by holding the phone near the lock on models that support it. We enroll two fingers for each person in case one is cut or bandaged, test them with cold and slightly damp hands at the door, and keep a code and a key as backups. Fingerprint readers can struggle with very dry or worn fingertips, which is common among older residents and people who work with their hands, so we try them before you rely on them.

Marble Hill is legally part of Manhattan — which rules apply to a smart lock on a Marble Hill door?

Marble Hill is in the borough of Manhattan, but Community Board 8 notes that the Bronx provides its city services. For your door the borough line matters less than who owns your building. The state Multiple Dwelling Law and the city's Housing Maintenance Code are the same in both boroughs. At Marble Hill Houses, NYCHA's resident handbook applies: the bottom lock stays, you may add a lock, NYCHA may break it in an emergency, and alterations need written consent from the property management office. In Marble Hill's private buildings and houses, the same rules apply as anywhere else in the city. We cover Marble Hill from our Fordham Road office, the same as Kingsbridge.

Shops under the elevated tracks

For a small Broadway shop, is a keypad deadbolt or a reader with an electric strike the better choice?

It depends on how many people use the door and how much you need to know about them. A keypad deadbolt on the front door works for a shop with a handful of staff. Each person gets a code, the app shows who opened up, and nothing needs to be wired. A reader with an electric strike costs more, $1,500–$2,500 for a single door at our Bronx figure, but it suits a door used all day, a back or stair door that has to relock after every delivery, or a business that wants fobs it can cancel from a computer. If the door is narrow-stile aluminum and glass, a residential deadbolt may not fit at all, and the reader and strike becomes the practical choice.

Signal under the elevated tracks

Shopkeepers on Broadway sometimes ask whether the 1 train overhead will knock a smart lock offline. We have not found any published measurement of elevated-train interference with smart locks, so we don't guess; we test at the door. What we check on Broadway is ordinary signal reach. A shop router in a back office behind brick and a steel roll-down gate may not reach a lock on the front door, and Wi-Fi bridges generally need to sit within a short distance of the lock. We measure the signal at the door, place the bridge or a Thread border router inside the shop where it reaches, and lock and unlock from mobile data on the sidewalk. If the signal can't be made reliable, we recommend a keypad lock that works fully offline.

AI Overview Reality Check

What AI Gets Wrong About Smart Lock Installation in Kingsbridge

A note on method: Google's AI Overview could not be read from the environment this page was built in. The seven statements below are therefore the ones that turn up again and again on national cost guides, store listings and contractor marketplaces such as Angi, HomeAdvisor and Fixr, set against what Kingsbridge doors actually look like.

“Smart lock installation costs about $300.”

Two national price guides set the number most people arrive with. Fixr's, revised in January 2025, gives a range of $250 to $400 and an average close to $300; LawnStarter's average is $330. Both are describing a single lock going onto a single sound door, with the hardware picked out already.

A Kingsbridge job often has two doors in it. The apartment lock may be a simple retrofit or a bore through steel with a reinforcement plate. The lobby door may need nothing, a strike, or a full keypad entry. National averages cover the first case and not the second.

Our approach is to quote the apartment door flat after seeing it and to price lobby work from figures we have already published for the Bronx: $250–$450 for a new strike, $295–$485 for a strike repair and $1,500–$2,500 for a single-door keypad or fob reader with strike.

“Any renter can add a smart lock.”

New York's Multiple Dwelling Law §51-c does give tenants of multiple dwellings the right to add a lock of their own, with a duplicate key to the landlord on request. Online summaries usually stop there.

The same law excludes buildings supervised by a municipal housing authority, which in Kingsbridge's corner of the map means Marble Hill Houses, where NYCHA's handbook sets the rules instead. And co-op shareholders at the Amalgamated or Park Reservoir aren't tenants in the ordinary sense. Their housing company's rules come first.

So the right first question is not “can I?” but “under which rulebook?” We answer that before recommending a lock, and we tell you what to ask management in writing if the answer isn't published.

“A smart lock makes your home more secure.”

It can make your apartment door harder to defeat and gives you a record of every entry. But security in a Kingsbridge apartment building starts at the entrance. The crowbar in the 2024 Corlear Avenue incidents went into a lobby door, and CB8's 2027 statement complains about main doors without working locks and buzzers.

A smart lock on a door with a weak frame or a strike held by short screws is no stronger than that frame. The motor drives a bolt, and the bolt is only as good as what it goes into.

So our walkthrough looks at the lobby door, the strike and frame of your door, and the lock, in that order. Sometimes the most useful thing we do on a visit is a long-screw strike kit and a closer adjustment, with the smart lock coming second.

“You can install it yourself in about half an hour.”

Retail testers often report that a smart lock went in with little more than a screwdriver. That holds for a wood door with a standard bore where a deadbolt has been removed. Many Kingsbridge doors aren't like that.

Prewar apartment doors are commonly steel-clad, already drilled for a building lock low on the door, scarred by the plates of police locks and rim cylinders, and hung in frames painted many times over. Boring through steel without tearing the skin takes the right cutters and a template, and the new lock has to clear the existing trim.

If your door is a modern wood door with a single-cylinder deadbolt you own, a DIY install can be fine, and we will say so. If it isn't, the time goes into mapping old holes, fitting a reinforcement plate and setting the strike.

“Wi-Fi smart locks just connect to your router.”

Many Wi-Fi locks reach the internet through a bridge plugged in nearby, and makers' own guidance is to keep it close; August, for example, says 10 to 15 feet from the lock. Matter-over-Thread locks need a Thread border router set up first.

In a deep prewar apartment, the router is often in a back bedroom or living room far from the entry hall. In the Amalgamated's concrete high-rises, walls and corridors can weaken the signal again. The app shows the lock as online at setup, then drops it a week later.

We measure signal at the door, place the bridge or border router where it is reliable, and lock and unlock from mobile data outside the building before we leave. If remote access can't be made reliable, we say so and choose a lock that is fully useful offline.

“Auto-lock means you'll never forget to lock up.”

Auto-lock moves the bolt after a delay. If the door hasn't closed fully, the bolt either hits the frame and stops or locks a door that is still open. Neither is what you wanted.

The city requires self-closing doors in multiple dwellings, and HPD tells residents that apartment doors should close and latch by themselves. A door that drags, a closer set too weak, or a strike that has shifted defeats both the fire rule and the auto-lock feature at the same time.

So before auto-lock goes on we adjust the strike and check the closer so the door closes and latches from fully open. Where the lock supports a door sensor, we turn it on, so the lock knows whether the door is actually shut before it throws the bolt.

“Go keyless and you'll never need a key again.”

Advertising for keyless entry suggests the key ring can go. In New York City apartments it can't entirely. The Housing Maintenance Code requires owners to provide a key lock and at least one key, Multiple Dwelling Law §51-c expects a tenant-added lock to come with a duplicate key on request, and co-op proprietary leases commonly ask for one too.

Practical sense says the same. Batteries run down, phones break, and lobby doors in this neighborhood sometimes fail at night. A key cylinder on your smart lock is the backup that doesn't depend on any of those.

So we install keyed smart locks unless you have a specific reason not to, and we write down who holds each key before we leave.

Shared Apartments

Roommates, Students and the Semester Calendar

Manhattan University, which took that name in 2024 after more than a century as Manhattan College, has been in Riverdale since the 1920s, on the hill just west of Kingsbridge. Many apartments within walking distance are shared: two friends splitting a one-bedroom, three students in a converted two-bedroom, a couple renting a room to a third person. Locks in these apartments tend to fail through people, not hardware: a spare key that walked off with a former roommate, a door code written on the fridge, an app account in the name of someone who moved to Ohio.

Who should own the lock

The person on the lease should own the lock and hold its admin account, with a second admin on a trusted phone. If the leaseholder moves out first, ownership transfers before they go. Roommates get their own user profiles, not the admin password. A lock bought and registered by whoever happened to have the credit card is the most common mess we untangle.

One code per person, never one per apartment

Separate codes cost nothing and solve most problems. When someone leaves, only their code goes. When the log shows an entry at 3 a.m., you know whose code it was. When a friend stays for a week, they get a guest code that expires on the day they leave. A single shared code turns every one of those situations into an argument.

The move-out routine

  • Delete the departing person's code, fingerprints and phone access the day they hand back their key, not after.
  • Check the activity log a week later for anything after the move-out date.
  • If the person had a physical key to the smart lock and it hasn't come back, a locksmith can re-key the cylinder. The landlord's own lock is the landlord's decision.
  • Update the card inside the door that lists who holds what.

What the landlord still needs

The lock you add is yours, but in a private multiple dwelling the landlord may ask for a duplicate key under Multiple Dwelling Law §51-c, and the landlord's own lock stays in place. A keyed smart lock handles that without handing anyone a code. Roommates who aren't on the lease should know the lock doesn't change their status with the landlord. It only changes who can open the door.

A home attendant gets a code that works only during scheduled hours, and the family gets a notification when it is used. A visiting nurse who comes once a week gets a separate code. Before You Buy

A Kingsbridge Renter's Checklist Before Buying a Smart Lock

Most of the returns and regrets we see come from a lock bought before anyone looked at the door. Ten minutes with this list saves most of them.

  • Find the building's lock. Note where the landlord's or management's lock sits on the door. Your new lock goes in addition to it, not in place of it.
  • Look for a deadbolt you already control. A single-cylinder deadbolt with a thumb-turn inside, not the building's, may take a retrofit with no drilling at all.
  • Count the old holes. Filled holes, plate scars and a police-lock footprint narrow down where a new lock can go and whether it needs a reinforcement plate.
  • Check the door closes by itself. Open it all the way and let go. If it doesn't latch, that needs fixing first, and in a rental it's the owner's repair.
  • Stand at the door with your phone. Is there Wi-Fi signal there? If not, budget for a bridge or border router placed nearer the door, or choose a lock that works fully offline.
  • Keep the key. Choose a model with a key cylinder. Your landlord may ask for a duplicate, your co-op may require one, and you will want one on the night the batteries die.
  • Read your lease or house rules. If they mention locks or alterations, ask management in writing before drilling. If you live at Marble Hill Houses, NYCHA's handbook applies.
  • Think about who else needs in. Roommates, an aide, a relative, a dog walker. If the answer is more than one person, plan separate codes from the start.

Send us photos of the inside of your door and its edge before you buy, and we will tell you which types of lock will fit. That costs nothing and doesn't commit you to booking us.

Small Building Owners

A Plan for Owners of Small Kingsbridge Apartment Buildings

When the owner of a small Kingsbridge rental building calls us, it is usually for one of two reasons: tenants are complaining about the front door, or the owner wants to stop handing out keys to the building. Both start in the same place. The entrance has to shut and lock by itself every time before any keyless system is worth buying.

Step one: make the entrance relock

We test the closer from fully open and from halfway, check that the latch reaches the strike, and look for wear, a sagging hinge, a strike plate that has moved, or a frame that has been pried. On a hill street the wind and the slope can work against a weak closer. Many entrances are fixed at this stage with an adjustment, a new strike at $250–$450, or a strike repair at $295–$485.

Step two: decide whether the lobby goes keyless

A keypad or fob reader with an electric strike at $1,500–$2,500 for a single door gives each tenant their own credential and lets you cancel one without re-keying the building. It also brings obligations. Under the city's Tenant Data Privacy Act you may not require tenants to use a smart access system to get into their own apartment, and you must limit and protect the data you collect. Fobs and codes avoid most of the privacy issues that come with biometric or facial-recognition entry.

Step three: leave the apartment doors to the rules

Owners must provide each apartment with a key lock and at least one key. Tenants may add their own lock, with a duplicate key on request. Changing or disabling a tenant's lock without giving them a key is unlawful under the city's eviction rules, so we don't put owner-controlled smart locks on occupied apartment doors without the tenant's agreement. Between tenancies, a smart deadbolt on a vacant apartment door is the owner's choice, as long as the new tenant gets a key.

Step four: keep the intercom working

Buildings with eight or more apartments that fall under §50-a need an intercom with a door release from each apartment. A new keypad should work alongside the buzzer, not replace it. Visitors, deliveries and aides still need to be buzzed in. Our intercom repair team in the Bronx can fix the buzzer on the same visit if it has failed.

Field Notes

Field Notes: Reading a Kingsbridge Building From the Sidewalk Up

How our installers approach a Kingsbridge address — our standard routine, not an account of any one job

The check starts before the lobby. We stand on the sidewalk and watch the entrance for a minute. Does the door swing shut behind the person who just went in? Does it latch, or rest against the frame? Is there a wedge or a folded cardboard pad at the foot of it? On the hill streets we also note which way the wind hits the door, because a closer that works on a calm day can lose to a gust.

Inside, we look at the lobby side of the entrance: the strike, the screws holding it, any marks on the frame. Pry marks near the strike tell us the building has been tested before, and they change what we recommend upstairs. Then the stairs or the elevator, and the hallway: whose lock is on every door, whether the hardware matches from door to door, whether there is a fire-safety notice on the back of the apartment door.

At the apartment door we count holes, measure the backset, check the steel, and open the door to its widest and let go. If it doesn't close and latch by itself, that is the first fix. Only then do we stand in the doorway with a phone on mobile data and decide between a lock that works on Bluetooth alone, one with a bridge, or one on Thread.

Before we leave, the person who lives there locks and unlocks the door three ways, the key goes to whoever the rules say should have one, and the card inside the door records who holds what.

FAQ

Kingsbridge Smart Lock Installation FAQ

Can a digital door viewer replace the peephole when the smart lock goes in?
+

Often, yes. A digital viewer fits the existing peephole hole and shows the hallway on a small screen inside the door, and some models send a picture to your phone when someone knocks. The city requires a viewer on apartment entrance doors in multiple dwellings, so we only fit a replacement that still lets you see out, and we keep the original viewer if your lease or co-op asks for it back.

Our building fronts one of the Kingsbridge step streets — how does your crew get tools to the door?
+

We park at the nearest street with curb access, usually at the top or bottom of the stairs, and carry the tools up by hand. A smart lock job needs a tool bag and a small case, not a van at the door. Tell us when you book if your entrance is only reachable by steps so we can allow the time.

Does the city's tenant data privacy law cover a smart lock I put on my own apartment door?
+

Not in the way it covers your landlord. The Tenant Data Privacy Act sets rules for building owners who run smart access systems for tenants. A lock you buy and manage yourself keeps its codes and entry log in your own account with the lock maker, so read the maker's privacy terms before you choose, and give the landlord a key rather than app access unless you want them to see your log.

Our apartment door has a strong closer that pulls it hard against the frame — will a motorized bolt still lock it?
+

Usually, once the door is set up properly. A closer that slams the door can leave the bolt rubbing on the strike, and the motor then strains, beeps or stops part way. We adjust the closer's closing and latching speed and set the strike so the bolt slides in freely, then test the lock several times from a full swing before we leave.

Do the smart locks you fit in Kingsbridge always keep a physical key?
+

We always offer a keyed model and recommend one in almost every case. Apartment rules in New York expect a key to exist, co-op leases commonly require a duplicate, and a key is the backup when batteries, phones or the building's entrance let you down. If you want a keyless model for a specific reason, we explain what you give up before you decide.

Will the lock alert me if someone keeps trying wrong codes or tampers with it?
+

Most current keypad smart locks lock out the keypad for a short time after several wrong codes, and many send an alert to the app when that happens or when the lock is forced. We switch those features on, send the alerts to the phones you choose, and show you where the attempts appear in the activity log.

Will you install a cheap smart lock I ordered online from a brand I have never heard of?
+

We look at it first. If it fits your door, keeps a key where your rules need one, opens from inside with one motion and has a working app with a way to remove users, we install it as labor only. If its app has poor reviews, can't remove a user, or the hardware won't suit a steel door, we tell you before anything is drilled so it can go back.

Can I unlock the door by voice through Alexa or Google, and should I?
+

Locking by voice is harmless and handy. Unlocking by voice is different, because anyone who can be heard by the speaker, including through a door or an open window, could try it. Most voice assistants ask for a spoken PIN before they will unlock. We set that PIN, or turn voice unlocking off altogether, and leave voice locking on.
