## Distinct theses

**Soundview** — large planned developments that already employ maintenance staff. The page argues honestly that an outside contractor is not there to replace them, and earns its place through three specific things instead: diagnosis that has already defeated the ordinary checks, capacity when a backlog builds, and documentation nobody has had time to produce. Matching the property's existing equipment standard rather than introducing a second set of spares is treated as a professional obligation.

**Spuyten Duyvil** — high-rise co-ops on the bluff. Two things drive it: boards that decide on paper and need written proposals with the repair option costed *beside* any replacement figure, and riser height, where the far station is the only honest measuring point and a healthy reading at the head end proves nothing about floor twenty. Also covers how staffed lobbies mask faults until the desk is unattended.

**The Hub** — mixed-use buildings in one of the busiest retail corridors in the borough, where the residential door sits in a shopfront. The core argument is that most reported buzzer faults here are actually door faults, and that propping is a symptom of an unreliable release rather than resident carelessness. Includes the ownership question when a store shares a facade or appears on the residential panel.
