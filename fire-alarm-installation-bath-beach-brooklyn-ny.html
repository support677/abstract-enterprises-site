The Neighborhood

Why Bath Beach Buildings Need Fire Alarm Systems Designed for Two Scales at Once

Bath Beach buildings need fire alarm systems designed for their real scale, and in this neighborhood the scale changed sharply in the last five years. For most of the twentieth century Bath Beach was a district of semi-attached houses, four- and six-story walk-ups and elevator buildings, and low brick commercial rows under the West End elevated line on 86th Street. Then the waterfront started to grow upward. The Shoreline at 2230 Cropsey Avenue, also addressed as 1625 Shore Parkway, rose to 31 stories with 248 apartments and a 150-space attended garage, with move-ins scheduled from January 2024, according to New York YIMBY. A short walk east, 2300 Cropsey Avenue topped out at 23 stories in March 2022, with condominiums on floors four through twenty-three and, as BLDUP reported, 109,213 square feet of community facility space that includes an ambulatory medical center and a supermarket, plus an enclosed garage and a pool.

Those towers are high-rise buildings under the NYC Building Code, and high rise fire alarm installation brings a different set of obligations: emergency voice communication, a fire command station, elevator recall, smoke control interfaces where the design calls for them, and garage detection, all of it coordinated with the elevator, sprinkler and mechanical contractors. The same few blocks still hold the building types Bath Beach has had for generations. 8800 Bay Parkway, between Bath and Cropsey Avenues, topped out in 2023 at seven stories and 70 feet with 40 rentals, a 7,256-square-foot ground-floor community facility and enclosed parking for 20 cars. 1461 Shore Parkway is a six-story, 77-unit elevator building from 1960. The 1949 garden co-op at 1259 Shore Parkway has 192 units in three-story walk-ups. A fire alarm plan for Bath Beach has to start by identifying which of these the building actually is, because the code answer differs for each.

The commercial corridors matter just as much. The city's Department of Small Business Services studied 86th Street, Bath Avenue, Bay Parkway, 18th Avenue and 20th Avenue in its Bensonhurst Commercial District Needs Assessment and counted 1,265 storefronts, with an 11.7 percent vacancy rate overall and 14 percent on Bath Avenue. The mix is restaurants, delis, salons, groceries and pharmacies, the kinds of tenants that change every few years and bring cooking, chemicals and new layouts with them. At the south end of Bay Parkway, Ceasar's Bay Shopping Center added an 88,000-square-foot Target in March 2021, in the space Toys R Us once held. Big-box retail, small storefront rows and new medical space all sit inside one neighborhood.

Bath Beach fire news shows both scales at work. On January 18, 2018, a three-alarm fire that started in a tile showroom at 2162 Bath Avenue, between Bay 29th Street and Bay Parkway, damaged a row of businesses; the Brooklyn Reporter counted 33 units and more than 130 firefighters, and Councilmember Mark Treyger said, "The fire originated in the tile store and spread to other businesses." On January 4, 2025, News 12 Brooklyn reported nearly 60 firefighters at a laundromat fire on Bay Parkway between 86th Street and Benson Avenue. On January 15, 2025, a fire in a second-floor apartment of a seven-story building on Bay 19th Street sent heavy smoke into the stairwells and injured 16 people; FDNY Deputy Assistant Chief Joe Duggan told ABC7, "If you leave the fire door open, that causes all sorts of problems." We do not know, and do not claim to know, what any fire alarm did in those buildings. What they show is why early detection, door control and notification are planned building by building here.

Compliance callouts for Bath Beach corridors

  • Design professional first. The FDNY requires fire alarm plans prepared and sealed by a New York State registered Professional Engineer or Registered Architect, filed on a TM-1 with the certificate of occupancy or Schedule A, or a DOB Letter of No Objection.
  • Certified installer. The installer must be a NYC licensed electrician or a fire alarm company holding an FDNY Fire Alarm Company Certificate, whose principal holds the S-97 certificate of fitness, earned with NICET Level II or higher or a NYC master or special electrician license.
  • Monitoring live before the test. A central station monitoring agreement and an active connection are required before the FDNY schedules the acceptance inspection.
  • Detector placement. The FDNY's top defects include smoke detectors closer than 36 inches to a supply diffuser or return, common in low-ceiling store fit-outs.
  • Kitchens. Restaurants on 86th Street and Bath Avenue with hood suppression systems need that system monitored where the design requires, and heat detection rather than smoke detection near cooking.
Field Notes

Field Notes: Reading a Store Row From the Basement Up

When our technicians survey a store in a Bath Avenue or 86th Street row for a fit-out, they start in the basement, not in the tenant's space. In older taxpayers the basement often runs under several stores, and that is where the panel, the electrical service and the phone line for the communicator usually live. It is also where water collects after a storm off Gravesend Bay, which is why ground faults in these buildings so often trace back to a corroded box or a cable resting on a damp wall. Our technicians photograph every junction box they can see and note anything that belongs to another tenant's system.

Then they go up to the roof or the top-floor ceiling to see whether the cockloft is continuous across the row. If it is, the engineer needs to know before deciding where detection belongs and whether the alteration reaches beyond one store. In a tower fit-out the order is reversed: our technicians start at the fire command station and the base building panel, read the building's device list and the landlord's vendor rules, and only then walk the community facility space. A medical tenant's devices have to appear on the base building's annunciator with names a firefighter will understand at the lobby door.

Your Questions Answered

Fire Alarm Installation in Bath Beach: Questions Answered

Waterfront towers and new buildings

Our clinic is leasing community facility space in a Cropsey Avenue tower. Who files the fire alarm work for our fit-out?

Your design professional usually prepares the fit-out drawings, but the work modifies the tower's existing FDNY approval, so the building's fire alarm company and the condo or rental management must be part of the filing. Ask the managing agent for the base building vendor's name and rules before your engineer starts drawing.

The garage under our Bath Beach building fills with exhaust on busy evenings. Will that set off the fire alarm?

It can if the wrong detectors are installed. Garages are usually covered with heat detection or detectors suited to vehicle exhaust, and CO detection is a separate requirement where the design calls for it. If a garage detector is causing unwanted alarms, the fix is a device suited to the space, approved through the engineer, not disabling it.

We bought a condo unit with retail frontage on Cropsey Avenue. Can our tenant's contractor add devices to the building panel?

Not on its own. The condo board owns the base building system, and its fire alarm company of record is responsible for programming and for the approval. Your tenant's contractor can install devices to approved plans only in coordination with that company, and the board's rules usually say so.

Store rows and corridors

We are opening a restaurant in a one-story Bath Avenue row. Does the whole row need a fire alarm now?

Not automatically. Whether a fire alarm is required depends on your occupancy, occupant load and the scope of the alteration, and in a row of attached stores it also depends on whether the row is one building. Your engineer reads that against the NYC Building Code; the restaurant may need hood suppression monitoring and specific detection even where a full system is not required.

Our 86th Street dental office is on the second floor above a bakery. What should the fire alarm design watch for?

Heat and particles from the bakery below, the shared stair and the landlord's existing system. Heat detection belongs near ovens, smoke detection near the office's equipment and corridors, and every device must report to a panel someone actually maintains. Confirm with the landlord whether there is an existing approved system before design starts.

After the 2018 fire on Bath Avenue, should store owners worry about the space above their ceilings?

They should know what is up there. Many older store rows share a continuous roof space, and a fire that gets into it can reach several tenants. Where the code requires detection in concealed spaces, the engineer places it; where it does not, owners still benefit from knowing whether fire separations between stores are intact.

Elevator buildings, landlords and tenants

Who pays to fix a building fire alarm in a Shore Parkway co-op, the board or the shareholders?

The building-wide fire alarm is a common element, so the board normally pays for its repair, maintenance and inspection through the operating budget. Devices a shareholder adds inside an apartment for a renovation are a separate matter that the proprietary lease and the alteration agreement decide.

Our six-story Bath Beach building's elevator recall has never been tested in front of us. Should we ask for it?

Yes. Elevator recall is one of the functions the FDNY checks at an acceptance test, and it should be exercised during periodic testing with the elevator contractor present. Ask your fire alarm company for the last test report that lists recall by floor and by detector.

A retail tenant in our mixed-use building keeps triggering the alarm with cooking. What can the landlord do?

Find out which device is responding and why. If a smoke detector is near the kitchen, the engineer may approve a heat detector instead; if the tenant cooks without a working hood, the lease and the FDNY rules, not the fire alarm, are the right tools. Unwanted alarms tie up the local engine company and can lead to violations.

Schools, churches and community spaces

Our parish hall in Bath Beach is being rented for events. Does that change the fire alarm requirement?

It can. Assembly occupancy above the code's occupant load threshold triggers a fire alarm requirement, and regular events may raise the hall's real occupant load. The engineer reviews the use and the certificate of occupancy before the parish commits to a rental program.

A day care wants to open in our ground-floor community facility on Bay Parkway. What does the fire alarm need?

The city's Department of Health tells applicants that child care sites with a new permit or material alteration must have a Fire Department approved interior fire alarm system, with manual and automatic alarm systems in preschools and infant-toddler occupancies. Plan the filing early, because the license timeline depends on it.

Can fire alarm work at a Bath Beach school be scheduled around classes?

Usually, by phasing the work and doing noisy testing outside school hours or during breaks. Any time devices are out of service while students are present, the protection measures the FDNY requires must be in place, and the school's facility staff sign off on the schedule.

Process and records

We cannot find the approval letter for our Bath Beach building's fire alarm. Where do we start?

With a records request. The engineer or the fire alarm company asks the FDNY for the building's fire alarm history, and we survey what is physically installed. If no approval can be found, the engineer plans a filing that brings the existing system under a current approval.

Do Bath Beach owners deal with the Brooklyn borough office or a citywide FDNY unit for fire alarm approval?

A citywide unit. Fire alarm plan review and acceptance testing are FDNY functions for the whole city, with the Fire Alarm Inspection Unit at FDNY headquarters, 9 MetroTech Center. There is no separate Bath Beach or community board approval for the fire alarm itself.

Intelligent detection

An air sampling smoke detection system draws air through pipes to a detector that can be serviced from the floor.

Heat detection

Fixed temperature heat detector installation and rate of rise heat detector installation for restaurant kitchens, bakeries, garages and boiler rooms where smoke detection would cause unwanted alarms.

Duct detection

Duct smoke detector installation on air handlers large enough that they must shut down on alarm, a routine item in towers, supermarkets and medical suites.

Voice and speakers

Fire alarm speaker installation and fire alarm emergency voice communication in high-rise buildings, schools and large assembly spaces, so instructions reach the floors that need them first.

Annunciators

Power and monitoring

Addressable versus conventional in a Bath Beach building

Owners in Bath Beach meet this choice in two situations: when a contractor proposes converting the old zoned panel in a Shore Parkway elevator building, and when a small store row wonders whether it needs anything more than a simple system. A conventional system groups devices into zones and tells you which zone is in alarm. An addressable system tells you which device, and where it is. In a two-store taxpayer the difference is small. In a seven-story building with a garage, a community facility and 40 apartments, it decides how quickly the superintendent and the responding firefighters find the problem.

Yes, for a few hours. Alarms sound, strobes flash and elevators recall while the inspector watches each function. Post notices for residents in advance, schedule the test with the managing agent, and make sure the superintendent knows which elevator will be out of service.

Our building is close to Gravesend Bay. Does salt air affect fire alarm equipment?
+

It can. Moist, salty air speeds corrosion in exterior boxes, rooftop devices and conduit fittings, which shows up as ground faults and intermittent troubles. Devices and enclosures listed for the environment, sealed fittings and regular inspection of exterior runs help.

Can we keep our old panel if a medical tenant moves into our Bay Parkway building's ground floor?
+

Sometimes. If the panel has spare capacity, is still supported and the engineer's design allows it, new devices can be added under an FDNY filing. If the panel is full or obsolete, the fit-out is often the right time to replace it.

What records should a Bath Beach managing agent keep near the fire alarm panel?
+

Keep the FDNY Letter of Approval, the approved drawings and as-builts, the most recent inspection and test reports, the central station account details and the fire alarm company's contact. When the FDNY or a technician arrives, those records save time and repeat visits.
