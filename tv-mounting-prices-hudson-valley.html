ADA 4-inch protrusion rule in public spaces

AI Overview Reality Check

What AI Answers Get Wrong About TV Mounting in the Hudson Valley

Ask a chatbot what TV mounting costs and you will get a national average — usually something like $150 to $350 — pulled from cost guides written for suburban drywall. That number is not wrong so much as unhelpful between the Bronx line and the Catskills. Here are seven claims we see repeated, and what actually happens on jobs in Westchester, Rockland, Orange, Putnam, Dutchess and Ulster.

1. AI says: “TV mounting costs the same everywhere — about $150 to $350.”

That average TV mounting price blends apartments in Texas with ranch houses in Ohio. In the Hudson Valley, the gap between a Rockland split-level and a cabin near the Delaware County line is mostly drive time, and drive time is a real cost to whoever actually shows up at your door.

That is why we post three base prices instead of one: $219.99 in Westchester and Rockland, $229.99 in Orange and Putnam, and $254.99 in Dutchess and Ulster. The add-ons for size, wall and mount type are identical in every county, so the comparison stays simple.

If a search result says $150, ask what is included, what wall it assumes, and whether that person will drive to Phoenicia for it. A posted number with a listed scope beats an awkward conversation in your driveway — and it is the honest answer to handyman vs professional TV installer pricing.

2. AI says: “Any stud finder will locate the studs for you.”

An electronic stud finder reads density changes behind half-inch drywall. Behind an inch of plaster on wood lath, as in many pre-war Westchester and Dutchess houses, it lights up everywhere or nowhere, and homeowners end up with a wall full of test holes and no answer.

On plaster we use magnets to find lath nails, measure off door and window framing, and drill small test holes where the TV will cover them. Hand-built farmhouse framing may not sit on 16-inch centers at all, so we confirm every stud before trusting it with the bracket.

When the studs don’t land where the screen needs to be, a plywood backer board ($85) spreads the load across whatever framing is there. The $45 plaster add is posted up front because this careful work takes extra time, not because we found a surprise.

3. AI says: “Just screw into the mortar joints on a stone fireplace.”

Online advice splits on brick versus mortar, and some of it is fine for modern brick with hard mortar. Old Hudson Valley fieldstone is different: the joints in a century-old hearth may be soft lime mortar that crumbles to powder the moment a masonry bit touches it.

We look for solid stone with a hammer drill and set sleeve or wedge anchors into it, keeping drywall anchors far away from masonry. Where the stone is only a thin veneer over framing, the anchor has to pass through the veneer and into the structure behind it.

The cost to install TV mount on brick or stone is a $65 wall add, plus $130 for above-fireplace placement. If an inspection shows the stone can’t carry the TV safely, we tell you plainly and suggest another wall instead of forcing a bad install.

4. AI says: “Mounting a TV above any fireplace is fine.”

A sealed gas insert with a deep mantel is very different from a working wood-burner that heats the whole house in January. Many Ulster and Dutchess homes rely on wood heat, and that heat rises straight up the chimney wall to exactly where the TV wants to hang.

Check your TV manual for its maximum operating temperature, then feel the wall during a real fire, not a cold afternoon. If it is too hot to keep your hand on comfortably, the spot is wrong no matter how good it looks in the listing photos.

Viewing height is the other problem. A pull-down mount (+$160) lets the TV live high while the fire is going and come down to eye level for movie night — the usual fix for neck strain over tall hearths, and better than a TV nobody can watch comfortably.

5. AI says: “You can hide the power cord inside the wall.”

You can hide the wires, but not by pushing the TV’s plug-in cord through the drywall. The National Electrical Code does not allow flexible cords to be concealed in walls, and inspectors, home buyers and insurers do notice when it has been done that way.

Our $169 concealment uses a code-compliant in-wall power and cable kit: a recessed inlet behind the TV fed from a plug-in point near the floor. That handles cable management cleanly with no new circuit and no electrician needed for that particular setup.

We don’t add or move outlets. If you want a true new receptacle behind the TV, bring in a licensed electrician first and we will mount to it afterward. On stone or brick walls, a $59 paintable cord cover is the honest, removable alternative to opening the wall.

6. AI says: “A cheap indoor TV works fine on a covered porch.”

Some people get a few seasons out of one, and Reddit has plenty of those stories. Others lose a set to humidity or condensation in the first year. Hudson Valley porches see muggy summers, foggy lake mornings and freezing, damp winters that are hard on indoor electronics.

For a lake-house deck in Putnam or a patio in Orange County, plan on an outdoor-rated TV, or at least a fully covered, shaded spot, plus an outdoor bracket with weather-rated hardware. Outdoor mounting adds $90 to the base price for your county.

Bracket choices on our list are the Samsung Terrace at $149.99, Sanus outdoor tilt at $239.99 and SunBriteTV full-motion at $589.99. Siding, stone and porch posts are priced by wall type like any other job, so the outdoor quote is just as predictable.

7. AI says: “A handyman can just come by whenever they’re in the area.”

That works until you have taken the morning off in Kingston and nobody comes. Missed appointments are among the most common complaints in TV mounting threads, and they hurt more when the installer is driving an hour each way and the day simply slips.

Every job we book has a 2-hour arrival window, never an open-ended day. Same day TV mounting is possible when a window is open; if not, you choose the next one that fits. Rescheduling is free with 24 hours’ notice, which matters for weekend homes.

Because TV jobs are paid in full online at booking, the scope is settled before we leave. If something you didn’t select turns up on site, you get the price before any extra work starts — one of the real alternatives to big box TV installation.

TV Mounting Prices FAQ

Is the TV mounting labor cost all I pay?
+

Yes. Base prices cover labor: finding studs, mounting and leveling, connecting power plus two existing devices, tidying cables behind the TV and cleanup. The bracket is extra unless you supply a rated one, and you can add it at list price when booking. There are no surprise charges.

How long does TV mounting take on a Hudson Valley stone or plaster wall?
+

A standard drywall install usually takes about an hour once we are set up. Plaster, stone, fireplace, concealment or multiple add-ons take longer. Your 2-hour window is when we arrive, not how long the job lasts. A TV over a stone fireplace with concealment can take a few hours.

Can a landlord prohibit TV mounting, and do you patch holes?
+

Yes, a landlord or condo board can prohibit drilling, so get written approval first if you rent or the home is a short-term rental. Patching and painting are not included in our service, so choose a spot you plan to keep.

In an older Hudson Valley house, do I need an electrician to install an outlet behind my TV?
+

Only if you want a brand-new receptacle — we don’t do electrical work or move outlets. Our in-wall kit ($169) gives you clean, code-compliant power behind the TV without a new outlet. For a new receptacle, hire a licensed electrician before your appointment.

What happens if you can’t start the job?
+

If we can’t begin because the TV or bracket isn’t there, nobody gives us access, or building approvals are missing, a $150 return-trip fee applies. Rescheduling with 24 hours’ notice is free; less than 24 hours keeps $150. So please have the TV, bracket and building approvals ready.

Is your TV mounting service with warranty?
+

Brackets and parts carry the manufacturer’s warranty. Labor is not warrantied, which is why we check the wall, the bracket’s VESA pattern and weight rating, and the anchors before we hang anything. Bring your own mount only if it is rated for the TV.

What is the TV installation price per TV for two rooms?
+

Each TV is priced separately off the same list, so living room TV mounting and bedroom TV mounting are just two line items in the calculator. Very large sets over 85″ include a second technician in the size add. Mixed sizes and wall types simply add their own lines.

Can you install a TV in a gym or medical office waiting room?
+

Yes. We price each screen from the posted list, provide a free certificate of insurance and mount out of circulation paths to respect the ADA 4-inch protrusion limit. We can schedule before opening so patients and members aren’t disturbed. Each screen gets its own line.

Do you do smart TV setup and streaming apps?
