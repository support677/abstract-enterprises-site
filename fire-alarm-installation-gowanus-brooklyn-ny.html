The Neighborhood

Why Gowanus Buildings Need Commercial Fire Alarm Installation Built for the Canal and the Rezoning

Gowanus needs commercial fire alarm installation planned around two facts that no other Brooklyn neighborhood combines in quite the same way: a flood-prone industrial canal running down the middle, and one of the fastest building booms in the city on both of its banks. The neighborhood runs roughly from Wyckoff Street south to the Gowanus Expressway and from Bond Street east to Fourth Avenue, in Brooklyn Community District 6. For a century it was brownstone Brooklyn's back of house: a gas plant, paint and tinware factories, a power station, garages. Those buildings were designed for machines and a few workers who knew every exit.

That changed with the rezoning the City Council approved on November 23, 2021, covering 82 blocks and allowing nearly 8,500 new homes, about 3,000 of them affordable. By January 2026 Commercial Observer was counting finished and rising buildings block by block: Union Channel at 240 Third Avenue with 224 apartments, finished in early 2025; Douglass Port at 251 Douglass Street with 260 apartments; the two Nevins Landing towers on the canal between Union and Carroll Streets, 22 and 17 stories with 654 apartments between them; Anagram Gowanus at 450 Union Street, 20 stories; and the largest of all, 175 Third Street, 27 stories and 1,071 apartments across from the Whole Foods. "I don't think you will find any other large-scale rezoning where you get development like this at this pace," the land use lawyer Anita Laremont told the paper. Each is a commercial fire alarm system installation from the ground up, filed and tested before the first tenant moves in.

The other half of Gowanus is not going anywhere. South of Third Street, between Third Avenue and the canal down to Hamilton Avenue, the Department of City Planning's Industrial Business Zone plan counted almost 4,000 jobs in nearly 300 businesses in 2021: auto body shops, big box stores and artist studios among them. They need industrial fire alarm installation and industrial fire alarm systems sized for high ceilings, dust and exhaust. Many are also changing use: a garage becoming a gym, a warehouse floor becoming a studio, a loft becoming a taproom. A change of use usually changes the occupancy classification, and with it the fire alarm the NYC Building Code requires.

Then there is the water. "The canal is one place where the water levels can rise," the structural engineer Whitney Boykin told Commercial Observer about building in Gowanus. In 2012, Hurricane Sandy pushed the canal into the Gowanus Industrial Arts Complex on Ninth Street and destroyed its electrical main and transformer; three weeks later, DNAinfo found about half of its roughly 35 businesses still without power, and one foundry had stood in about six feet of water. A panel, batteries and communicator in a cellar like that are gone in the same flood. Choosing where equipment lives, above the design flood elevation wherever the code and the building allow it, is a first-day question on every Gowanus survey.

The fire record here is mostly in older residential and mixed-use buildings. On December 21, 2023, a fire in a 100-year-old building on Ninth Street between Second and Third Avenues spread next door. On September 17, 2024, twelve people were hurt in an apartment building fire on 15th Street off Third Avenue, where the FDNY found lithium-ion batteries and pulled out a melted e-bike. In March 2025 a fast fire in a three-story building on Ninth Street between Third and Fourth Avenues left two people hurt and the building under a full vacate order. "It's horrible how fast a fire can break out," a neighbor, Liesa Witt, told News 12 Brooklyn.

The case every Gowanus owner should know is 130 Third Street. In April 2024, THE CITY reported that the former factory beside the canal opened as a 400-bed shelter with a fire alarm not up to fire code, relying on an FDNY inspection from August 2022, when it was a mostly empty factory; the FDNY let it open only with a fire guard on duty around the clock. A fire alarm approved for one use is not approved for the next.

Need Fire Alarm Repair in Gowanus? Call Before the Next Rain or the Next Show

If your Gowanus panel is in trouble, call (347) 934-8335 now: part of the building may not be protected, and the cause here is often water, a contractor's drill or a tenant who moved something. Commercial fire alarm repair starts with an emergency fire alarm technician on the phone. Emergency fire alarm repair, same day fire alarm repair where parts and access allow, and after hours fire alarm service for venues that open at night are booked by phone; simple faults are often traced on the first visit, and bigger ones get a written plan before the technician leaves.

The common Gowanus calls fall into a few groups. A fire alarm ground fault after rain, when water reaches a junction box, a cellar device or a conduit run that dips below grade. A fire alarm open circuit repair after a tenant fit-out where someone cut or disconnected a cable above the ceiling. Fire alarm communication failure repair when the phone lines a communicator depended on were changed or a cellular communicator lost signal deep in a masonry cellar. Fire alarm battery trouble repair when standby batteries have aged out, or were soaked. And fire alarm false alarm troubleshooting in venues, brewhouses and studios, where haze, steam, dust and kiln heat reach detectors that were never meant for them.

Many companies advertise 24 hour fire alarm repair; what matters is whether the company holding your building's service records can be reached when the panel beeps at midnight. Emergency fire alarm service and emergency fire alarm troubleshooting start with the central station, the display and a plan for a fire watch, which the FDNY may require until the fault is fixed.

How a Gowanus trouble call is worked

  1. Read the panel. Why is my fire alarm panel showing trouble? The display says which circuit, device or power source has the problem. Photograph the message before anyone presses reset. A fire alarm panel trouble light with a beeping sounder is a fire alarm system trouble condition, not an alarm, but it still needs action.
  2. Call the central station. Confirm what they see, and whether the building is still reporting. A fire alarm system not communicating is its own emergency: the building may be sounding locally but nobody outside knows.
  3. Fault diagnosis. Fire alarm fault diagnosis and fire alarm system diagnostics start at the panel event history and the circuit with the problem. Fire alarm circuit troubleshooting and smoke detector circuit troubleshooting split the circuit to narrow the fault, and fire alarm short circuit troubleshooting and fire alarm ground fault troubleshooting use a meter on each leg until the bad section is found.
  4. Repair and retest. Fire alarm trouble signal repair, fire alarm ground fault repair, fire alarm wiring repair, fire alarm device fault repair or device replacement, then a functional test of every device on the repaired circuit. If a repair changes the approved drawings, the design professional is told first.
  5. Write it down. What failed, why, and what was replaced and tested goes into the building's records.

What causes a fire alarm ground fault in Gowanus most often? Water. Conduit runs below grade or along a cellar floor, junction boxes on exterior walls, devices in cellars that flooded, and old cable with damaged insulation all leak current to ground when they get wet. A fire alarm intermittent trouble that appears after storms and clears in dry weather is a strong hint, and the fix is usually rerouting or replacing a section, not resetting the panel. Fire alarm supervisory trouble, by contrast, usually points to the sprinkler system: a valve partly closed or a tamper switch out of position. A fire alarm panel beeping repair visit often ends at a sprinkler valve, not the panel.

When the panel itself has failed, fire alarm panel failure repair and fire alarm system down repair are about restoring protection fast. Fire alarm control panel repair may be a power supply, board or charger; when the manufacturer no longer supports the panel, the honest answer is fire alarm control panel replacement, with a fire watch in between. Commercial fire alarm emergency repair, urgent fire alarm repair, fire alarm system malfunction repair, fire alarm system emergency service and fire alarm trouble emergency service all follow the same order: protect the people first, find the fault second, document it third. A fire alarm communication failure emergency repair gets the building reporting again so the central station can do its job.

Building Specialists

Commercial Fire Alarm Systems for Gowanus's Building Types

When does a building need a fire alarm system? In New York City the answer is in Section 907 of the Building Code and depends on the occupancy: assembly spaces at 300 or more occupants, business occupancies at 500 or more or with 100 or more people above or below the exit level, every educational building, factories of two or more stories with 100 or more occupants, larger mercantile spaces, and apartment buildings with units three or more stories above the exit level or more than 16 units, among others. The design professional reads the code for your exact building. In Gowanus, the building types below are where those rules land.

Canal-edge rental towers

The towers going up along the canal and Fourth Avenue are apartment building fire alarm installation at scale. Under the NYC Building Code's Group R-2 rules, smoke detectors in apartment corridors and common areas report to the panel and a central station but do not sound building-wide notification on their own; in-unit smoke alarms warn the apartment where the smoke is. Mechanical, electrical and elevator machine rooms, elevator lobbies and air-handling systems get system smoke detection. A tower like 175 Third Street, with 1,071 apartments, 84,220 square feet of commercial space and roof terraces on three levels, also has to tie its stores, artist workspaces and amenity floors into one coordinated design, and high-rise buildings bring voice communication and fire command requirements. We work as part of the general contractor's team on buildings like these, or on the retail and amenity fit-outs that follow.

Landmarked factories and power houses

On October 29, 2019, the Landmarks Preservation Commission designated five Gowanus buildings at once: the 1884 Somers Brothers Tinware Factory, later the American Can Company, at 238–246 Third Street; the 1901–03 Brooklyn Rapid Transit Central Power Station Engine House at 153 Second Street; the 1911 Flushing Tunnel Pumping Station and Gate House at 196 Butler Street; the 1908 Montauk Paint Manufacturing Company building at 170 Second Avenue; and the 1913 ASPCA office, shelter and garage at 233 Butler Street. Their fire alarms cover tall open floors and tenants who change every few years, while conduit and devices stay off the features that earned the designation.

Fabrication shops, kilns and maker spaces

Powerhouse Arts, in the restored transit power station at 322 Third Avenue, opened in May 2023 with about 85,000 square feet of fee-based fabrication shops for ceramics, print and public art inside a 170,000-square-foot complex. Kilns, torches, welders and spray booths make heat and fumes that would keep ordinary smoke detectors in alarm, so shop floors are usually covered with heat detection, while galleries, classrooms and offices keep smoke detection. Fire detection device installation in a maker building is about matching each device to each room, and keeping the shops on their own zones so an alarm says exactly where it came from.

Music venues, taprooms and games halls

Gowanus has an unusual number of assembly spaces built inside industrial shells: The Bell House on Seventh Street, in a converted 1920s warehouse with a high arched wooden ceiling; Littlefield on Sackett Street; the Royal Palms Shuffleboard Club at 514 Union Street with ten courts; Threes Brewing at 333 Douglass Street, with a brewhouse behind the bar and a music room. Crowds of strangers at night, with haze and loud sound, are why big assembly spaces get voice instructions and a show-stop. Beam detection, air sampling or carefully placed spot detectors handle high ceilings, and the sound system has to mute when the fire alarm speaks.

Auto shops, warehouses and the Industrial Business Zone

South of Third Street the buildings are wide, low and dirty by design, and the work calls for an industrial fire alarm contractor. Industrial fire detection system installation here follows the process, not the floor area. Repair bays, paint booths and storage floors get heat detection where exhaust and dust would fool smoke detectors, and beam smoke detector installation or projected beam smoke detector installation where a tall open warehouse needs smoke detection across long spans. Storage facility fire alarm installation adds its own concerns: what is stored, how high it is racked, and whether lithium-ion batteries are part of the inventory, a question the 15th Street fire in 2024 put back on everyone's list.

Kitchens under apartments

The older streets of Gowanus, especially around Union, Sackett, Douglass and Ninth Streets, still have three- and four-story buildings with a store or restaurant on the ground floor and apartments above. Commercial kitchen fire detection, heat detectors in kitchens rather than smoke detectors, and a kitchen hood suppression system that reports to the building fire alarm are the usual pieces. Restaurants are filed with the FDNY separately for the hood, and the building fire alarm drawings show how the two talk to each other.

NYCHA and shelter buildings

Gowanus Houses, at the north edge, is NYCHA's own project: Congressman Dan Goldman has sought federal funds for its fire alarm, and NYCHA says its replacements take 24 to 30 months of construction. NYCHA hires its own contractors; we do not. Privately operated shelters and supportive housing in converted Gowanus buildings are different, and 130 Third Street showed what happens when the fire alarm is not ready on opening day.

What the code and the FDNY expect on every street

Wherever the building is, the same rules apply. The drawings are prepared and sealed by a New York State registered design professional and filed with the FDNY on a TM-1. Installation starts only after the plans are approved, and it is done by a New York City licensed electrician or a fire alarm company holding an FDNY Fire Alarm Company Certificate. The FDNY's own guide puts the average time from plan submission to final approval at about 20 to 24 weeks. Fire alarm code compliance in Gowanus is not a local variation; it is that citywide process applied to buildings that change use faster than most.

Field Notes

Field Notes: Put the Panel Where the Water Was Not

The first thing we ask in a Gowanus cellar is not where the old panel is. It is where the water came up to. Owners who were here in 2012 usually know to the inch.

Detectors in a cellar can be replaced after a flood; a drowned panel, its batteries, its power supplies and its communicator take the whole building out of service. So we photograph the flood marks, ask the design professional for the design flood elevation, and look for a room on the first floor or above for the panel, with a remote annunciator at the FDNY's entrance. Conduit along a cellar floor and junction boxes on a canal-side wall get moved up and in where we can.

When a building cannot move the panel, we say so and plan the service side: spare batteries, a written flood plan, a call list that starts with the central station. The design professional still decides and the FDNY still approves. Our job is to put the water line on the table on day one.

Your Questions Answered

Commercial Fire Alarm Questions From Gowanus Developers, Venue Owners, Makers and Landlords, Answered

These are the questions Gowanus building owners, developers, venue operators, studio tenants and property managers bring to a first call. The design professional on your project and the FDNY have the final word on any building.

Cost and budgeting

A landlord on Third Avenue wants to turn a one-story auto shop into a climbing gym. What does the fire alarm budget depend on?

Mostly on what the gym becomes in code terms. A garage is usually a storage or light industrial occupancy; a gym full of members is an assembly occupancy, and the design professional calculates the occupant load to see whether it crosses the threshold for a required fire alarm. Then come the tall open room, which may need beam detection, and what exists now, often nothing usable.

Our new canal-side rental is waiting on its temporary certificate of occupancy. Why is the fire alarm the line item everyone keeps asking about?

Because nobody moves in until it is approved. The FDNY must witness a fire alarm acceptance test before a new residential building can be occupied, the central station connection must be live before that test, and the FDNY recommends requesting the test 40 to 70 business days ahead. Every other trade must be finished and present for that test, so the fire alarm schedule is the building's schedule.

Quality and trust

Two proposals for our Douglass Street taproom differ by weeks on the schedule. Which one is being honest?

Usually the longer one. The FDNY's own guide says plan submission to final approval averages about 20 to 24 weeks, with initial plan review alone taking up to eight. A proposal promising an open taproom in a few weeks either assumes no new filing is needed, which only the design professional can confirm, or leaves approval time out.

How do we check that the company maintaining our Nevins Street building's fire alarm is FDNY-certified?

Ask for the company's FDNY Fire Alarm Company Certificate and the name of the principal who holds the S-97 Certificate of Fitness, then check the FDNY's published list of approved companies. Certification is valid for one year and must be renewed, and the FDNY says a company must stop servicing systems if its certificate lapses. A proper inspection report also names the technician and lists every device tested.

Do it yourself or hire a professional

Our ceramic studio wants to move its kiln room to the other end of the floor. Can our own electrician just move the heat detector with it?

Not on his own. Moving a device changes an approved system, so the change goes through the design professional, who decides whether the drawings must be amended and refiled, and the work is done by a company holding the FDNY certificate with a licensed electrician where wiring is involved. The new kiln room needs heat detection rated for what a kiln puts out.

Can studio tenants in a converted factory hang art, lights and rigging from the fire alarm conduit?

No. Fire alarm conduit and cable are supported for their own weight, and loading them can pull boxes loose, break connections and start the ground faults and opens that turn into trouble signals weeks later. Hanging things in front of strobes or under detectors also blocks the devices.

Technical

Where should the fire alarm panel and batteries go in a Gowanus building that took water in Sandy?

Above the water, wherever the code and the building allow it. In a flood hazard area, the NYC Building Code's flood-resistant construction rules tie new equipment to the design flood elevation, and the DOB treats installing new equipment below it where ASCE 24 does not allow it as an increase in noncompliance. The design professional sets the panel location on the drawings; a first-floor or higher room with a remote annunciator at the entrance is the usual answer.

Our brewhouse vents steam and carbon dioxide into the space behind the bar. Which detectors will survive that?

Steam fools photoelectric smoke detectors, so the brewhouse area is usually covered with heat detection, and carbon dioxide is a gas-detection question for the design professional and the mechanical engineer, not something a smoke detector measures. The bar and music room keep smoke detection placed away from the brewhouse door. Separate zones let the panel tell staff exactly where an alarm started.

The new tower next door wants to run fire alarm cable along our shared wall. Who approves that?

Nobody should run a neighbor's fire alarm cable through or along your building without your written agreement, and each building's system is designed and filed for its own lot. If your own system is disturbed, have your service company test the affected devices.

What is a commercial fire alarm system? Kilns, torches and welders produce heat and fumes that keep smoke detectors in alarm, so the design professional normally specifies heat detection there, and keeps smoke detection in the galleries, offices, corridors and classrooms around the shop. Each shop should be its own zone so an alarm shows exactly which room it came from.

Who owns the fire alarm in a new Gowanus tower with ground-floor retail: the landlord or the store?
+

The building owner owns and answers for the building's fire alarm system, and a store connects to it through the owner's vendor and drawings. The lease should say who pays for and files changes in the store, who maintains the devices inside it, and what happens to them when the store leaves.

Do the landmarked Gowanus factory buildings need Landmarks approval for fire alarm devices inside?
+

Interior work in an individual landmark is generally regulated only where the interior itself is designated, but exterior devices, conduit and anything that touches the facade can need Landmarks Preservation Commission review. The five Gowanus buildings designated in 2019 are individual landmarks, so check with the commission early on any exterior work, and keep interior conduit off significant features.

How early should a new canal-side building book its fire alarm acceptance test?
+

Months ahead. The FDNY recommends requesting a fire alarm test 40 to 70 business days in advance, and the request needs the approved plans, the forms and the central station contract. The central station connection has to be live before the test, and the elevator, sprinkler and other trades must be ready to attend.

Can a Gowanus music venue use haze without setting off the fire alarm?
+

Yes, with a plan approved in advance. The design can use detector types and locations that tolerate haze, and some venues follow an approved procedure for show nights. Nobody should cover detectors or switch off zones on their own; that leaves the audience unprotected and can bring an FDNY violation.

Is a shuffleboard club, games bar or taproom a place of assembly for fire alarm purposes?
+

Usually yes, if enough people gather there to eat, drink or play. Assembly spaces get a required fire alarm at 300 or more occupants under the NYC Building Code, and a Place of Assembly permit at lower numbers. The design professional calculates the occupant load from the floor plan and use, and that number drives the design.

Does an all-electric building still need carbon monoxide detection on its fire alarm?
+

It depends on what else is in the building. The FDNY's rules for carbon monoxide detection in commercial occupancies with fire alarm systems look at fuel-burning equipment, enclosed parking and loading docks, so an all-electric tower with an enclosed garage or a loading dock can still need it. A building with none of those may not. The design professional confirms it on the drawings.

What happens to a Gowanus building's fire alarm during a long power outage like the one after Sandy?
+

The system runs on its standby batteries, which are sized by calculation to carry it for a set time and then sound an alarm. After that, the building is effectively unprotected. During a long outage, call your service company and the central station, and expect the FDNY to require a fire watch until power and the system are restored. Batteries that sat in flood water have to be replaced.

Which fire alarm documents should a buyer of a Gowanus industrial building ask for before closing?
+

The FDNY Letter of Approval, the approved drawings, the most recent inspection and test reports, the central station contract, and any open violation or Notice of Defect. Then confirm that the building's current use matches what the system was approved for. If the buyer plans a change of use, the fire alarm budget belongs in the deal, not after it.
