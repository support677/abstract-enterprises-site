The Neighborhood

Why Parkchester's Planned Towers, Built-In Storefronts and Shared Ownership Need Fire Alarms With Clear Lines of Responsibility

Parkchester began as a single decision. In July 1938 the Metropolitan Life Insurance Company announced "a self-contained … apartment community" on 129 acres it bought from the New York Catholic Protectory, and by 1943 all 12,271 apartments were rented. A Board of Design led by Richmond H. Shreve, whose firm drew the Empire State Building, laid out 171 brick buildings in 51 groups, seven to 13 stories tall, with Gilmore D. Clarke planning the grounds and Starrett Brothers & Eken building them. The Historic Districts Council's guide notes that the apartments were built from a standardized design of three central core types and five wing types, with only about 37 percent of the land covered by buildings.

The same guide describes the support systems MetLife built to run the place as one property: a central heating plant near the railroad in the north quadrant with four boilers, about 14,000 feet of steam pipe feeding boiler rooms in 29 buildings, five five-story garages on the outskirts, and a shopping district of roughly 500,000 square feet around Metropolitan Avenue and Unionport Road, largely built into the residential structures themselves. Macy's opened there in October 1941, its first branch outside Herald Square, and John Eberson designed a movie theater for the complex in 1940; Welcome2TheBronx reports the theater is now a Marshalls.

Then ownership split. Parkchester's own history page dates the Parkchester North Condominium to the conversion of the north quadrant and the Parkchester South Condominium, covering the south, east and west quadrants, to 1986. In 1998 the Parkchester Preservation Company bought the unsold sponsor apartments, which that history page counts at 6,362, and the Cooperator reported that the purchase also took in the shopping center and the garages. A 2025 assurance of discontinuance from the New York Attorney General describes the company's affiliates as holding about 6,000 residential units inside the two condominiums today. So a single building can have a condominium board, a large unit owner, hundreds of individual owners and renters, and a store operator under the same roof.

That is the fire alarm problem in Parkchester in one sentence: the buildings were planned as one, but every decision about a fire alarm now has to answer the question "whose is it?" The association is normally responsible for common elements such as the cellar, halls, machine rooms and roof; the business is responsible for its own space; the FDNY looks at whatever system the Certificate of Occupancy and the approved drawings describe. When those lines blur, nobody books the inspection and a trouble signal sits on a panel for weeks. When they are clear, the same survey method, the same documentation and the same service calendar can be repeated building after building without anyone assuming one approval covers 171 addresses.

The neighborhood around the complex is changing too. Wikipedia counts 33,602 residents in 2020, up 12.7 percent in a decade, and the City Council approved a rezoning of 46 blocks across Parkchester, Van Nest and Morris Park in August 2024, tied to the Metro-North station planned for the area. New buildings are already being filed: a six-story mixed-use building at 1780 East Tremont Avenue with about 11,500 square feet of residential and 5,600 square feet of commercial space, according to a Marketproof listing of the March 2026 filing, and mixed-use filings on Odell Street and Unionport Road listed by New York YIMBY. Each one will need a commercial fire alarm system designed from the first drawings.

This page covers commercial and code-required fire alarm systems only: building systems in condominium and rental apartment buildings, mixed-use buildings, stores, garages, schools, houses of worship and new construction. The smoke and carbon monoxide alarms inside individual apartments are a separate requirement enforced by HPD, and one- and two-family home smoke alarms are not part of this service.

Parkchester at a glance

  • Where: the complex runs from East Tremont Avenue to McGraw Avenue and from White Plains Road to Castle Hill Avenue; the wider neighborhood reaches Westchester Avenue and East 177th Street (Wikipedia).
  • Scale: 171 buildings in 51 groups, 12,271 apartments, built 1939–1942 by MetLife (Parkchester history page; Historic Districts Council).
  • Ownership today: Parkchester North Condominium (north quadrant) and Parkchester South Condominium (south, east and west quadrants), with about 6,000 units held by Parkchester Preservation Company affiliates (Parkchester South; NY Attorney General).
  • Support buildings: a four-boiler central heating plant feeding boiler rooms in 29 buildings; five five-story garages, three still operating (Historic Districts Council).
  • Transit: the 6 train at Parkchester station over Hugh J. Grant Circle, opened May 30, 1920, with elevators that opened in January 2026, and at Castle Hill Avenue (Wikipedia).
  • Recent fires: a fourth-floor fire on Unionport Road near the Metropolitan Oval in April 2024, a sixth-floor fire on Purdy Street in September 2025, a seventh-floor fire at 1940 East Tremont Avenue in December 2025, and a fatal fire on Leland Avenue in April 2026 (News 12 Bronx).

Building Types

Commercial Fire Alarm Systems for Parkchester's Building Types, From 1940 Brick Towers to New Infill

What is a commercial fire alarm system? It is the building-wide network of detectors, manual stations, notification appliances and a control panel that protects shared space and reports to a central station, separate from the smoke alarms inside each apartment. How does a commercial fire alarm system work? Initiating devices tell the panel something changed, the panel decides whether it is an alarm, a supervisory signal or a trouble, and then it carries out the approved sequence of operations: report off site, sound or stay silent where the code allows, recall elevators, shut fans and release doors.

The 1940 brick towers

Parkchester's apartment buildings are brick, seven to 13 stories, set at least 60 feet apart, with taller buildings at the main avenue corners, according to the Historic Districts Council. Their sameness is useful: once a survey has traced the cable routes, shafts and closets in one building of a given core type, the next building of that type usually hides its routes in the same places. That shortens surveys and helps a board compare proposals. It does not mean one design fits all; each building has its own Certificate of Occupancy, its own history of renovations and its own filing.

Ground floors that are stores

The shopping district was built into the residential buildings rather than beside them, so a pharmacy, a restaurant or a clothing store can sit directly below apartments. A store may have its own panel, may be a few devices on the building's panel, or may have a small system that reports to the building's panel. Which it is should be written on the drawings, and the store's staff should know which panel to look at when something sounds.

Large retail floor plates

Macy's opened in October 1941 with roughly 175,000 square feet, and the former Eberson theater now holds a Marshalls. Big retail space needs coverage of sales floors, stockrooms, receiving and offices, voice or tone notification sized for a crowd, and coordination with the sprinkler system. We describe the general approach only; we make no claim about any particular store's system.

Garages

Five five-story garages were built on the edges of the complex, and three still operate, per the Historic Districts Council. Garages get heat detection rather than smoke detection where exhaust would fool a smoke detector, supervision of any standpipe or sprinkler valves, and manual stations and notification where the occupancy requires them.

The boiler rooms and the old plant

The central plant once sent steam to boiler rooms in 29 buildings. Boiler and mechanical rooms are classic places for heat detection and for supervising fuel and combustion-air interlocks where the design calls for them, and in a building where the fire alarm is required, the boiler room is usually one of the first rooms on the drawings.

Schools, churches and the library

St. Raymond's Church at Castle Hill and East Tremont Avenues was dedicated in 1898, and its parish runs an elementary school that opened in its current building in 1951 plus a girls' academy founded in 1960 and a boys' high school established in 1962. P.S. 106, M.S. 127 and the Bronx Charter School for Excellence also serve the area, and the NYPL's Parkchester branch has been in its two-story brick building on Westchester Avenue since June 1985. City school buildings are maintained through the city's own agencies, so our part in buildings like these is parish buildings, leased space and private facilities that call their own contractor.

Field Notes

Field Notes: Write the Answer to "Whose Device Is This?" on the Drawing

In a complex like Parkchester, the most valuable thing we bring to a survey is not a meter; it is a marker. Before we price anything, we walk the store, the cellar and the halls with whoever represents the building and mark every device on a set of drawings with who owns it, who services it and which panel it talks to. The answer often surprises someone: a restaurant that thought its heat detectors were "the building's," or a manager paying for a central station account nobody could match to a panel.

The second habit is to use the repetition in the buildings without trusting it. When two buildings share a core type, we expect the same cable routes and check that we were right. Decades of separate renovations mean twins rarely stay identical: a closet becomes a laundry, a shaft gets a new pipe, an old device is abandoned in place. A survey that copies the last building's notes is how a missing device ends up on an approved drawing.

The third is paperwork that outlasts us. Each building should end the job with its own folder: approved drawings, riser diagram, sequence of operations, test reports and the FDNY letter, filed under the building's address and entrance, not under a quadrant. When management changes, the folder stays with the building.

Your Questions Answered

Parkchester Fire Alarm Questions From Boards, Unit Owners, Store Operators and Builders

Many buildings, shared ownership

Parkchester's 171 buildings were drawn from three core types and five wing types. If two condominium buildings share the same floor plan, can one set of fire alarm drawings be filed for both of them?

Not as one approval. Each building is its own structure with its own Certificate of Occupancy, so each gets its own fire alarm construction drawings and its own FDNY plan review, even if the sheets look almost the same. What a shared design does allow is a standard: the same device types, the same panel family, the same sequence of operations and the same drawing format across a group of buildings, which makes later service and fire alarm inspection preparation simpler.

Parkchester South Condominium covers three quadrants and Parkchester North covers one. When a fire alarm project crosses from one association's building to the other's, who signs the paperwork?

Each association signs for the buildings it controls. A project that touches buildings in both, for example a shared central station contract or a coordinated panel replacement program, is really two projects that share a schedule, with each owner authorizing its own filings and each building tested on its own. The NYC Business listing for the fire alarm plan application says any authorized agent can file, but the owner of the building is the one who has to authorize it. Put the authority in writing before the design professional files.

Parkchester Preservation Company affiliates hold about 6,000 apartments inside the two condominiums. Does a large unit owner have any say over the building fire alarm, or is that only the association's job?

The building fire alarm protects common elements, so the association's board and managing agent normally make the decisions, sign the contract and hold the records. A large unit owner votes and pays common charges like any owner, and can raise fire alarm questions through the board, but it does not install or modify building devices on its own. Inside an apartment, the smoke and carbon monoxide alarms are a separate HPD matter for owners and landlords; they are not part of the building fire alarm and do not appear on its drawings unless a design specifically ties them in.

A store sits in the base of a residential building on Metropolitan Avenue. Whose fire alarm panel watches the store's detectors: the condominium's or the store's own?

It can be either, and the drawings must say which. Some stores have a separate panel that reports to its own central station account; some are a set of devices on the building's panel; some have a small panel that sends a common alarm to the building's panel. The right answer comes from the occupancy, the size of the space and how the building's approved system was designed. When a store renovates, a tenant improvement fire alarm installation or tenant fit out fire alarm installation has to keep both systems working and both sets of records accurate.

Plant rooms, garages and renovations

MetLife's central heating plant once fed steam to boiler rooms in 29 buildings. Why do boiler rooms in a steam-heated complex get heat detection instead of smoke detection?

Boiler rooms have combustion air, dust, flue gas leaks and temperature swings that make ordinary smoke detectors false alarm. A fixed temperature or rate of rise heat detector reacts to the heat of a real fire instead. In a building where a fire alarm is required, the design professional decides whether the boiler room is covered by heat detection, a different smoke detector type or sprinkler supervision alone. Where a building's boiler room has been converted, replaced or moved since the central plant era, the drawings should show the room as it is now, not as it was in 1940.

Parkchester's original garages are five-story structures at the edges of the complex. What does a fire alarm in a parking garage usually watch, and why is it designed differently from an apartment building's?

A garage is full of exhaust and big temperature changes, so smoke detection is used sparingly and heat detection where the design calls for detection at all. The fire alarm typically supervises the sprinkler or standpipe valves and waterflow, provides manual stations and notification where the occupancy requires them, and reports to a central station. People in a garage are awake and moving, so the goal is to tell them to leave and to call the fire department, not to protect sleeping rooms.

Parkchester's 1999–2005 modernization upgraded electrical systems in all 12,271 apartments. When a building's electrical service is upgraded, does the fire alarm have to be refiled with the FDNY?

Only if the fire alarm itself changes. Moving the panel's dedicated circuit, replacing a power supply with a different model, adding devices or changing what the system does is a modification for the design professional to evaluate and, usually, to file. Replacing apartment wiring that never touched the fire alarm is not. The practical risk is accidental: electricians working near fire alarm cable cause many of the ground faults and open circuits we later chase, so mark fire alarm cable and boxes before any electrical job starts.

The former Eberson movie theater in the shopping district is now a Marshalls. When a theater becomes a store, what changes in its fire alarm?

Nearly everything that depended on the use. A theater is an assembly space where hundreds of seated people face a stage in the dark; a store is a mercantile space with racks, stockrooms and a constant flow of shoppers. The new design starts from the new Certificate of Occupancy, sets detector spacing for the new ceilings and stock areas, and lays out notification for a sales floor. Existing conduit may sometimes stay if the design professional accepts it, but the approval belongs to the new use. We describe the general method only, not that store's system.

After recent Parkchester fires

After the April 2026 Leland Avenue fire, a neighbor told News 12 she never heard a smoke alarm. In a multifamily building, which alarms are the owner's job and which are the residents' to keep working?

The owner is responsible for providing and installing the required smoke and carbon monoxide alarms in each apartment, and residents are expected to keep them working and report problems; HPD enforces those rules. The building fire alarm, where the building has one, covers common areas and machine rooms and is the owner's responsibility from design through inspection. The news report described neighbors' repeated complaints about dozens of candles burning in one apartment and gave no finding on the cause; we draw no conclusion about that building. The point for owners is that both layers have to work and be checked on their own schedules.

The December 2025 fire at 1940 East Tremont Avenue was on the seventh floor of an eight-story building. How does a building fire alarm help firefighters who arrive at a tall Parkchester building?

A good system tells them where to go before they climb. An annunciator at the entrance and a central station report naming the building, floor and device save minutes in a building with long corridors and several stairs. If the building has elevators with fire service recall, the fire alarm sends the cars to the designated floor. A well-kept zone chart or floor map next to the panel matters as much as the hardware. News 12 reported that crews had that fire under control in just over 20 minutes; we make no claim about that building's system.

Our building is near the Metropolitan Oval and had a fourth-floor apartment fire in 2024. After a fire, which building fire alarm devices should be checked even if they look undamaged?

Everything that saw heat, smoke or water. Smoke detectors in the halls near the fire can be contaminated; firefighting water runs down risers and into boxes, panels and power supplies on lower floors; and a device that was reset during the fire may have been left in a bypassed state. After any serious fire, ask for a full functional test of the affected floors and a check of the panel's event log, the central station signals and the batteries. If anything has to be replaced with a different model, that is a question for the design professional before the work is done.

Schools, worship and new buildings

St. Raymond's parish runs an elementary school, a girls' academy and a boys' high school around its 1898 church. When a parish campus adds classrooms, does each school building need its own fire alarm?

Usually each building that is a separate structure has its own required system, designed for its occupancy, though one panel can sometimes supervise several buildings if each is clearly identified and the design is approved that way. Schools get manual stations at exits and audible and visible notification throughout occupied areas where the code requires them, because staff must clear rooms quickly. A church sanctuary is judged separately, by its own size and use. The design professional sorts this out from each building's Certificate of Occupancy before anything is priced.

The Parkchester Library moved into its two-story brick building on Westchester Avenue in 1985. What fire alarm work can be done in a public library while it stays open?

Most of it, with planning. Device replacements, cable work above ceilings and panel programming can be done in phases, section by section, with each area returned to service the same day. The loud part, testing horns and strobes, fits into short announced windows or hours when the branch is closed. The library system decides its own schedule and contractors; we describe the method a public building like this one would expect, not a project at that branch.

New six-story mixed-use buildings are being filed on East Tremont Avenue. At what point in a new building's design should the fire alarm designer be at the table?

At the schematic stage, with the architect. Fire alarm system design requirements fix the location of the panel and annunciator, the size of the electrical room, the risers, the sprinkler riser connections, elevator recall and how the ground-floor stores report. Drawn late, the fire alarm ends up squeezed into whatever space is left. New fire alarm system installation in a building that has never had one is a full design, filing and acceptance test, and the Certificate of Occupancy depends on finishing it.

Types of Commercial Fire Alarm Systems We Install in Parkchester

Parkchester has 1940 brick towers, big retail floors, garages and new concrete buildings. These are the system types a design professional weighs for them.

🔍

Addressable

What is an addressable fire alarm system? Each device has its own address, so the panel names the exact detector, station or module in alarm or trouble. Addressable fire alarm installation is the usual choice for a tall condominium building, a department store or a new mixed-use building.

🔌

Conventional

What is a conventional fire alarm system? Devices are wired in zones, and the panel knows the zone but not the device. Conventional fire alarm installation still suits a small store or a single-tenant space with a few zones.

⇄

The difference

What is the difference between addressable and conventional fire alarms? Addressable tells you which device; conventional tells you which zone. On a 13-story riser, that is the difference between going to one closet and checking a whole floor.

🔊

Voice evacuation

Fire alarm voice evacuation installation plays spoken instructions through speakers. It suits large assembly or retail floors and taller buildings that move people in stages.

🧠

Intelligent detection

Intelligent fire alarm installation uses detectors that combine smoke, heat and sometimes carbon monoxide sensing and adjust to their surroundings. An intelligent fire detection system cuts nuisance alarms in store kitchens and dusty stockrooms.

The central station listed on the store's account first, then the fire alarm company that services the store's devices, then building management. Evacuate as the store's plan says and call 911 for any sign of fire. Afterward, ask for the event log from both panels so the store and the building can see whether the two systems talked to each other as designed.

Do the terra cotta figures and plaques on Parkchester buildings limit where exterior fire alarm devices can go?
+

They should be treated as features to work around. Exterior devices such as a strobe or a bell at a fire department connection are few, and they can usually be placed on plain brick away from ornament. The association's architect should confirm placement before drilling, and any review the facade requires is checked before the work is scheduled.

What does the FDNY charge to file a fire alarm plan for a Parkchester building?
+

The NYC Business listing shows $420 for a new fire alarm plan application and $525 where a complex technical analysis is required, with no fee to resubmit within six months of a letter of deficiency. Those are city fees; design, installation and testing are separate.

Does a new mixed-use building near Parkchester need its fire alarm approved before residents and stores move in?
+

Yes. The fire alarm has to pass its FDNY acceptance test, witnessed by the Fire Alarm Inspection Unit, before the building can be signed off for occupancy, so the test date belongs on the construction schedule from the start.

How far ahead should a Parkchester board plan a panel replacement?
+

Start at least a budget year ahead. The FDNY puts plan submission to final approval at about 20 to 24 weeks, the design professional needs time before that, and the work itself is phased around residents and stores. Boards with several buildings often replace panels over a few years, starting with the oldest or least supported models.

Will residents of a Parkchester building hear anything during an annual fire alarm inspection?
+

That depends on what the building's system does. Where notification appliances are installed, they are tested in short, announced windows with notices posted in advance. Detector and module testing in machine rooms and cellars is usually quiet. Ask the inspection company for the schedule and post it in the lobby.

What should a Parkchester superintendent write down when a trouble signal appears at night?
+

The time, the exact words on the display, any device address or zone, whether the central station was called and what it said, and whether anything was being worked on in the building that day. A photo of the screen is the single most useful thing a technician can receive the next morning.

Can a contractor keep servicing a Parkchester building's existing panel if its FDNY company certificate has lapsed?
+

No. The FDNY's FAQ says a company has to stop servicing fire alarm systems if its certificate expires, and that the FDNY does not send renewal notices. Ask your service company for its current certificate each year.

Can a small store in Parkchester keep a conventional panel when the building above it has an addressable system?
+

It can, if the approved design allows it and the store's panel reports correctly to its central station and, where required, to the building's panel through a monitor module. Many stores move to addressable devices when they renovate, but there is no rule that a store must match the building's panel type.

Who keeps the fire alarm records when a Parkchester building changes managing agent?
+

The building does. Approved drawings, test reports, central station agreements and FDNY letters belong to the owner, not the outgoing agent or contractor. Ask for the full folder at handover, check it against the panel, and give the new agent and the fire alarm company the same copy.
