The Neighborhood

Why a Gated Cottage Colony on a Bluff Still Has Buildings That Need a Real Fire Alarm

Silver Beach sits on a bluff along the East River shore of the Throggs Neck peninsula, just west of the Throgs Neck Bridge, in Bronx Community District 10 and ZIP code 10465. Most of it is Silver Beach Gardens, a private cooperative that started as a 1920s summer colony of bungalows and later turned year-round. Forgotten New York describes the homes as tiny dwellings built for summer and since winterized, along narrow lanes, with street names painted on wooden boards nailed to the poles. The New York Times reported in May 1973 that about 400 homeowners formed a co-op and bought the 83 acres their houses stood on. The Architect's Newspaper describes closed-off streets, a single point of entry and security guards.

The city's tax records show how unusual the place is. The whole co-op is one tax lot: Bronx block 5506, lot 1, owned by Silver Beach Gardens Corp. The Department of City Planning's PLUTO file lists 220 buildings on that one lot, 208 residential units, a lot area of about 2.4 million square feet, a 1921 build year, R4A zoning, and a building class for one-family homes. It also flags the lot inside both the 2007 and the 2015 preliminary federal flood maps. The same record assigns the lot to FDNY Engine 72, whose firehouse is listed in the city's firehouse data at 3929 East Tremont Avenue.

Read those numbers the right way. Two hundred cottages and rebuilt houses do not need, and generally cannot use, a commercial fire alarm panel; one- and two-family homes follow the household smoke and carbon monoxide alarm rules, and that is not what this page is about. Apartment building fire alarm installation, multifamily building fire alarm installation, condominium building fire alarm installation and mixed use building fire alarm installation start where a building stacks homes over homes or over stores, and the co-op's lot is classed for one-family homes. What does need building fire alarm work here is the short list of buildings that are not homes. The biggest is the corporation's own office: the stone farmhouse built around 1800 for Abijah Hammond, a Revolutionary War veteran and trader, later owned by the sugar-refining Havemeyers, and described in the 1978 Bronx Survey as one of the finest buildings of its type in the city. Then there are the general store at the Plaza that Forgotten New York photographed in 2007, the maintenance and security operation the co-op runs, and whatever service buildings keep a private community of its size working.

Past the gate the stakes rise. Preston High School occupies the 19th-century Havemeyer and Huntington mansion and a 1960 school building at 2780 Schurz Avenue, west of the co-op. St. Frances de Chantal church, whose parish also runs a school, stands at 190 Hollywood Avenue, just west of the co-op. Down Pennyfield Avenue, at the tip of the peninsula, SUNY Maritime College runs a 55-acre campus at historic Fort Schuyler with residence halls, a dining center and classrooms. Those are the buildings where fire alarm installation, inspection and repair are part of ordinary life, and where the FDNY's rules apply in full.

People here know fire. The Bronx Times reported in September 2026 that off-duty FDNY Lt. James McConville, running through Silver Beach on August 27, saw heavy black smoke at a neighbor's home, went in without gear, pulled the homeowner out and revived him with CPR. The paper notes that Silver Beach has housed firefighters and their families for generations. Our part is the code, the drawings, the filing and the installation.

Building Types

Commercial Fire Alarm Systems for Silver Beach Building Types, From a Federal-Era Farmhouse to a Campus Dormitory

First, the plain definitions, because a board that has only ever dealt with cottage smoke alarms deserves them. What is a commercial fire alarm system? It is one connected system for a whole building: initiating devices (smoke detectors, heat detectors, manual pull stations, sprinkler flow and valve switches), notification appliances (horns, strobes, speakers), modules that watch or switch other equipment, and a fire alarm control panel that supervises every wire and reports to a central station. How does a commercial fire alarm system work? When a device changes state, the panel sorts the event into an alarm, a supervisory signal or a trouble, sounds and flashes whatever the approved design says, transmits the signal off site, and runs any connected functions such as releasing doors or shutting down fans. When does a building need a fire alarm system? When the NYC Building Code or Fire Code requires one for its occupancy, size and height, or when a change of use, an alteration or an FDNY order brings it into a requirement.

The Hammond mansion as an office

The corporation's office is a two-story stone house with an attic and a basement, covered in lime stucco, with brownstone lintels and a delicate stone entry, according to the 1978 Bronx Survey text. An office of that size usually sits well under the code's thresholds for a required business-occupancy system. That does not settle the question. If the attic holds records, the basement holds the boiler and the building is the one place where every shareholder's file lives, a board may decide it wants detection anyway. Section 901.4.2 of the NYC Fire Code allows a system the code does not require, provided it meets the code and the rules, so a voluntary system is still designed, filed and tested properly. In a building like this the design challenge is respect for the fabric: surface raceway painted to match, cable fished through the attic and up from the basement rather than chased into old plaster, and, where cable truly cannot go, listed wireless devices the design professional approves.

The store at the Plaza

A small convenience store or deli is a mercantile occupancy, and the code sets its fire alarm requirement by floor area and occupant load, which a single small shop rarely reaches. In a shop like the one at the Plaza, the surrounding details often matter more: a clear exit, and the household-style smoke alarm on the ceiling that nobody tests. If the shop ever adds a grill and seating, the job turns into restaurant fire alarm installation, with heat detection and hood suppression monitoring added. Retail store fire alarm installation in a building this small is often a short job; the drawings and the FDNY review are still the start of it when a system is required or chosen.

Maintenance, security and storage buildings

A community that maintains its own lanes and grounds keeps trucks, salt, tools and fuel somewhere, and exhaust, dust and freezing air make garages poor homes for ordinary smoke detectors. Designers lean on heat detection, sprinkler monitoring where sprinklers exist, and, for a high open room, projected beam smoke detector installation (often just called beam smoke detector installation) spanning it wall to wall. That is the same toolkit used for warehouse fire alarm installation, warehouse smoke detection installation, industrial fire alarm installation, industrial building fire alarm installation and industrial fire detection system installation on a bigger scale, and an enclosed garage on a campus would add parking garage fire alarm installation, with heat detection and its own zone. Forgotten New York quotes a description of one of the city's last remaining barns standing in Silver Beach; we make no claim about its use, but old timber storage space is where heat detection beats smoke detection.

Preston High School on Schurz Avenue

Lehman College's Bronx public art and architecture guide describes Preston High School at 2780 Schurz Avenue, at the southeast corner of Brinsmade Avenue, as a French Second Empire mansion built for Frederick C. Havemeyer Jr., later owned by Collis P. Huntington, and used since 1947 for a Catholic girls' high school that still holds classes in it, with a cream-brick school building added in 1960. PLUTO records about 29,700 square feet of building area on the lot, four floors, and a 1960 build year. In April 2025, according to the New York Attorney General and News 12, the Bally's Foundation bought the property from the Sisters of the Divine Compassion and leased it back to the school for 25 years. A school is an educational occupancy with its own code section, and one that teaches partly inside a 19th-century mansion faces the co-op office's routing problem at a much larger scale. We name it only as a local example, not as a client.

St. Frances de Chantal on Hollywood Avenue

The parish church stands at 190 Hollywood Avenue, according to the parish and Archdiocese listings, and the parish runs St. Frances de Chantal School. A church sanctuary is an assembly space; a parish school is educational; a hall used for events is assembly again; a rectory is residential. Each use carries different fire alarm rules, and a campus that mixes them is designed as one system with clearly labeled zones or as separate systems tied together, at the design professional's call. Church fire alarm work usually rides along with a renovation, an accessibility upgrade or a new parish kitchen.

Residence halls and campus buildings at Fort Schuyler

SUNY Maritime's campus at 6 Pennyfield Avenue is described on the college's site as 55 waterfront acres at historic Fort Schuyler, and its campus map lists several lettered residence halls along with Vander Clute Hall. Dormitories are residential occupancies with their own fire alarm triggers, and the FDNY's tip sheet asks that systems for Group R-2 occupancies be filed separately from other occupancy groups. State-owned campuses also follow state procedures that a private building does not. We mention the campus because it is the largest group of code-required buildings within a short walk of Silver Beach, not because we claim any work there.

Field Notes

Field Notes: One Tax Lot, Two Hundred Twenty Buildings, and Getting the Right One on the Filing

Most Bronx buildings have their own block and lot. In Silver Beach, the city's records put the entire co-op on one lot, so the block and lot alone do not tell an inspector which building a filing is about. What separates them is the Building Identification Number. The Hammond mansion's BIN is 2103125; the cottages each have their own, which is how the Department of Buildings tracks permits at 76 Indian Trail or 30 Soundview Terrace on the same lot. Before we walk a Silver Beach building we confirm its address and BIN, so the drawings, the TM-1 and the acceptance test request all point at the same structure.

The permit history is instructive too. When we pulled the 25 most recent DOB NOW permits on block 5506, lot 1, 23 were rooftop solar installations on cottages along Indian Trail, Sunset Trail, Soundview Terrace and the flower-named places, and two were plumbing permits for a reconstructed one-family house on Geranium Place. None were fire alarm filings, and that is expected: since Local Law 195 of 2018 took effect, Metropolis reported in 2023, fire alarm design plans no longer go to the Department of Buildings, and the FDNY handles them.

The last note is about getting in. The co-op's maintenance and security operation is listed at 1 Aster Place. The lanes are narrow, Indian Trail along the water is a walking path, and a van with a ladder rack needs a place to stand that does not block a neighbor. We set the day, the vehicle, the parking spot and the building with the office before the visit, and we keep the same arrangement for every return trip, including the FDNY test.

Your Questions Answered

Silver Beach Fire Alarm Questions From the Board, the Office, School Administrators and Shop Owners

The corporation and its buildings

Silver Beach Gardens Corp owns the land under every cottage. Does that make the corporation responsible for fire alarms inside the shareholders' houses?

Not for a building fire alarm system, because a one-family cottage does not have one. The household smoke and carbon monoxide alarms inside each home are governed by the rules for one- and two-family dwellings and by whatever the co-op's own documents say about who maintains them. The corporation's fire alarm responsibilities, as far as this page goes, are the buildings it uses itself: its office, its maintenance and security buildings, and any shared room. A board that wants to change how home alarms are handled should take that to its own counsel, not to a fire alarm contractor.

If the board packed a shareholder meeting into the office's front rooms, could that change what the building needs?

It can, because fire alarm requirements turn on occupant load as well as use. A dozen board members around a table is an office. A hundred shareholders packed into the same rooms starts to look like an assembly space, and the design professional calculates occupant load from floor area and how the room is set up. If big meetings are regular, the simpler answer is usually to hold them in a space built for crowds; if the office must host them, raise it before the drawings are sealed.

If the board wants detection in the office even though nothing requires it, does a voluntary system still go through the FDNY?

Plan on it. Section 901.4.2 of the NYC Fire Code lets an owner install a fire protection system the code does not require, but only if that system meets the code and the FDNY's rules. In practice the design professional treats a voluntary building fire alarm much like a required one: sealed drawings, the FDNY review path, installation by a certified company or licensed electrician, and testing. What changes is scope, because the board, not the code, decides how much of the building to cover.

Ownership, records and service buildings

Every building in Silver Beach shares one block and lot. How does the FDNY know which building a fire alarm filing covers?

By the address and the Building Identification Number. The city's records give each structure on the co-op's lot its own BIN, and the Hammond mansion's is 2103125. The design professional puts the building's address, borough, block, lot and BIN on the drawings and the TM-1, and the floor numbers on the riser diagram have to match the building's occupancy document, which the FDNY's tip sheet asks for. Getting this right at the start saves a letter of deficiency later.

Silver Beach Gardens Corp and the Silver Beach Association are separate organizations. Which one hires the fire alarm contractor for a shared building?

Whichever one owns or controls the building. The city's PLUTO record lists Silver Beach Gardens Corp as owner of the co-op's lot, and the Silver Beach Association describes itself as a not-for-profit set up to serve the co-operative community. A fire alarm filing names the owner of record, so a building the corporation owns is normally the corporation's to file, even if the Association uses a room in it. If the two groups share a space, settle in writing who pays, who signs and who keeps the records before the design starts.

If a maintenance building keeps gasoline for mowers and snow equipment, does fuel storage change the fire alarm?

It can change the whole building's classification. The fire code limits how much flammable liquid can sit in a building of a given use, and quantities above those limits move the building into a more demanding category with its own protection rules. Within ordinary limits, the fire alarm question is mostly about detection that suits a garage: heat detectors rather than smoke heads, and a design that knows where the fuel is kept. Tell the design professional what is stored and how much; it is the first thing the classification depends on.

Schools, parish buildings and the campus

Preston High teaches inside a 19th-century mansion. How does a school fire alarm handle classrooms in a building that was once a house?

The same way it handles any classroom, with extra care in the routing. Notification must reach every room students use, including former bedrooms and parlors with odd shapes and high ceilings, so the design may need more strobes and speakers than the floor area suggests. Detection in open stairs and attics gets particular attention because old houses move smoke differently. We use the school only as a nearby example and do not claim to work there.

Preston's land now belongs to a tax-exempt foundation that leases it to the school. Does tax-exempt ownership change any fire alarm duty?

No. PLUTO lists the Bally's Foundation as owner of the 2780 Schurz Avenue lot and the property as exempt, but fire alarm requirements follow the building's occupancy, size and height, not its tax status. What a lease does change is housekeeping: the lease should say whether the owner or the school maintains the system, pays for inspections and answers FDNY notices, so nothing falls between the two.

Does a church sanctuary need the same fire alarm as the school its parish runs?

Not necessarily. A sanctuary is an assembly space and a school is an educational occupancy, and the code sets different triggers and different equipment for each. Belonging to one parish does not merge them. The design professional decides whether one system with separate zones and annunciation serves both, or whether each building gets its own, and the filings follow that decision.

Why does the FDNY ask for dormitory fire alarm systems to be filed separately from the rest of a campus?

The FDNY's tip sheet asks that systems for Group R-2 occupancies be filed separately from other occupancy groups, and residence halls generally fall in that group. Residential systems follow their own rules about detection in corridors, sounders in sleeping areas and how signals are handled. On a mixed campus, keeping the residential filing separate makes the review cleaner. State-owned campuses add their own procedures on top of that.

Equipment

Types of Commercial Fire Alarm Systems We Install Around Silver Beach

A two-story office, a corner store, a school in a mansion and a dormitory do not need the same equipment. These are the system types a design professional chooses from for buildings on and near the bluff.

Addressable

What is an addressable fire alarm system? Every detector, station and module reports by its own address and label, so the panel names the exact device. Addressable fire alarm installation and addressable fire alarm device installation suit schools, campus buildings and any building with many rooms.

Conventional

What is a conventional fire alarm system? Groups of devices sit on shared circuits, and the screen names only the circuit that tripped. Conventional fire alarm installation, conventional fire alarm system installation and conventional fire alarm panel installation still fit a small office or a single shop; conventional fire alarm repair or conventional fire alarm replacement keeps older zoned panels going.

Wireless-assisted

Listed wireless devices report to a wired panel through a gateway. They help in a historic office where cable cannot go, with batteries to maintain and radio paths to prove.

Voice evacuation

Fire alarm voice evacuation installation and fire alarm emergency voice communication give spoken instructions instead of tones, the usual choice for larger assembly spaces and some school buildings.

Intelligent detection

Intelligent fire alarm installation puts multi-sensor heads where single-sensor ones misbehave; an intelligent fire detection system weighs heat and smoke together in damp basements and dusty rooms.

Monitoring link

Fire alarm monitoring system installation and fire alarm central station communicator installation send signals off site over cellular or IP paths, replacing copper lines that many buildings no longer keep.

If the survey finds that none of the corporation's buildings needs a fire alarm system, what does the board get from it?

Does the Hammond mansion's attic need detection if the offices below get a fire alarm?
+

Does a security booth at the co-op's gate need fire alarm devices of its own?
+

A small booth used by one or two people sits far below any threshold that would require a building system. Do open-air spots on the lot, like the September 11 monument or the Green Grass, ever need fire alarm coverage?
+

No. A building fire alarm protects enclosed buildings and the people in them. Monuments, lawns, paths and beaches are not buildings with an occupancy, so there is nothing for a fire alarm to cover. Fire safety there is a matter of access for the engine and common sense, not detection.

Can a fire alarm communicator share a network with a gate intercom or the office phone system?
+

Not casually. A fire alarm communicator must be listed for fire service and its signal path supervised, so that a failure shows up as a trouble at the panel. Sharing a network with an intercom or a business system is only acceptable if the design and the equipment listings allow it. A dedicated cellular communicator usually avoids the question altogether.

Is a standalone heat detector in a maintenance garage the same as a fire alarm system?
+

No. A standalone device that only beeps locally does not supervise its wiring, report troubles or call anyone. A heat detector that is part of a fire alarm system is wired to a panel, monitored for faults and reported to a central station. If a garage needs protection that someone will actually respond to, it needs the second kind.

The city counts 208 residential units on the co-op's lot. Does that make Silver Beach an apartment complex under the fire alarm rules?
+

No. The code's apartment-building triggers apply building by building, and each cottage on the lot is its own one-family building with its own BIN. Two hundred homes on one tax lot do not add up to one apartment building with 208 units. That is why the cottages stay outside building fire alarm rules while a shared building on the same lot may not.

Can shareholders see the fire alarm inspection reports for corporation buildings?
+

That is governed by the co-op's own documents and the law that applies to them, not by the fire alarm contractor. We give every report to the owner's contact. Many boards find it simplest to keep a copy in the office where the reports can be shown on request, since the next inspector will ask for them there anyway.

Will the FDNY inspect a building inside a private gated community the same way as one on a public street?
+

Yes. The FDNY reviews and tests fire alarm systems by building, not by whether the street is public or private. The practical difference is access, which the owner arranges so the inspector, the plumber and our technicians can reach the building on the test date.

What fire alarm work is a small shop at the Plaza most likely to need?
+

Usually something modest: replacing a household-style smoke alarm that someone mistook for a fire alarm, correcting items on an inspection list, adding a pull station or a monitored detector where the design calls for one, or confirming that no building system is required at all. A small shop gets the same survey and the same honest answer about what the code asks of it as a large building does.

Will a monitored fire alarm in the corporation's office change its property insurance?
+

Ask your broker, because that is the insurer's decision, not ours. Some insurers ask for proof of central station monitoring or recent inspection reports, and some weigh them in underwriting. We supply the letter of approval, the monitoring contract details and inspection reports so the office has what an insurer requests.

The Hammond mansion was never landmarked. Does that leave the board free to put fire alarm devices wherever it likes?
+

Free of Landmarks review, yes; free of the code, no. The Bronx Survey listing records the building as never landmarked or heard, so no preservation approval stands between the board and the work. Device locations still come from the code and the sealed design: pull stations by exits, detectors where coverage requires them, notification where people are. Within those rules, the designer can choose spots that spare the stone entry and the best rooms.
