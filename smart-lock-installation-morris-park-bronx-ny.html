The Neighborhood

Why Morris Park Properties Need Smart Lock Installation Planned Door by Door

The typical Morris Park smart lock call is not about a lobby or an apartment corridor. It is about a house. The Department of City Planning, writing up the 2005 rezoning of the neighborhood, described it plainly: most of Morris Park is developed with single- and two-family detached homes and semi-detached housing, with small and medium-sized apartment buildings along Morris Park Avenue, which is the main commercial corridor, and along Williamsbridge Road. The rezoning itself locked that pattern in. Eleven full blocks and parts of nineteen more north and south of Morris Park Avenue went to R4A, a district that allows only detached one- and two-family houses with walls no higher than 21 feet, in keeping with what the planners called the area’s two-story homes with attics beneath pitched roofs. A smaller pocket between Bronxdale and Radcliff avenues went to R4-1, which also allows semi-detached houses.

Houses mean more doors per household than an apartment ever has. A Morris Park two-family on a 25-foot lot usually has a front door on the stoop, a separate entrance or inner door for the second unit, a side door off a narrow driveway that leads to the kitchen or the basement stairs, sometimes a rear door to the yard, and a garage at the back. Each of those doors has its own history of locks, and in a neighborhood where many houses have been in the same family for decades, its own unknown number of copied keys. When someone asks us for “a smart lock,” the first job is deciding which door it belongs on. In most of the houses we see here, the answer is the side door, because that is the door the family uses every day and the one a stranger can work at without being seen from the street.

That is not a theoretical worry. In March 2025, News 12 reported that two women broke into a single-family home in Morris Park near Loreto Playground on a Tuesday afternoon, about six minutes after the homeowner’s mother left to pick his daughter up from school. Video from the house shows them knocking twice to check that no one was home, pushing the security camera up so it could not see, and prying open the side door. A safe holding passports and other documents was taken, along with family heirlooms. The NYPD offered a reward of up to $3,500. The same report noted that the 49th Precinct, which covers Morris Park from its station house at 2121 Eastchester Road, had recorded 13 burglaries that year to that date, down from 17 a year earlier. Burglary is not common here, and residents still describe the neighborhood as safe. But when it does happen, it tends to happen at the door nobody watches.

The second thing that shapes a lock job in Morris Park is who lives behind the door. At the 2020 census the neighborhood, counted by the city as tabulation area BX1102, had 25,077 residents in 9,173 households, and 65.8 percent of its occupied homes were rented. In a district of houses, that figure describes something specific: owners living on one floor and renting the other, adult children renting from parents, and whole two-families let to two separate households. Each arrangement changes who should hold the administrator account, who must keep a mechanical key, and what the owner is and is not allowed to do with a tenant’s door. New York’s Multiple Dwelling Law treats a building occupied by three or more families as a multiple dwelling and a building for no more than two families as a private dwelling, so several rules written for apartment buildings simply do not reach a Morris Park two-family, while others, including the state’s ban on locking out an occupant, reach every home.

Then there is the hospital district on the neighborhood’s eastern edge. The Albert Einstein College of Medicine’s Jack and Pearl Resnick Campus sits at 1300 Morris Park Avenue, NYC Health + Hospitals/Jacobi at 1400 Pelham Parkway South, Montefiore’s Jack D. Weiler Hospital at 1825 Eastchester Road and Calvary Hospital at 1740 Eastchester Road. Jacobi alone runs the Bronx’s only burn unit and its Level I adult trauma center. A large share of the people who rent rooms and apartments in Morris Park work shifts in those buildings, and a large share of the families who own homes here care for older parents at home. Both groups ask for the same thing in different words: a door that lets the right person in at four in the morning without anyone getting out of bed to find a key.

The door most families here actually use. A keypad deadbolt on the side door off the driveway, with door-position sensing where the model supports it, so the app reports an open or forced door as well as a locked one.

A keyed smart deadbolt above the existing handleset on the stoop door, chosen to match the hardware finish and the door’s backset, so the street side of the house still looks like it always has.

The owner’s door, the tenant’s door and the shared hall or side entrance, each on its own credentials. The tenant keeps a key lock and a key; the owner never sees the tenant’s log unless the tenant chooses to share it.

Where a good single-cylinder deadbolt is already on the door, a retrofit unit replaces only the inside thumb-turn. The outside cylinder and every existing key keep working, which suits renters and anyone who wants nothing changed on the street side.

Brick party walls, plaster and a router in the finished basement can leave a front-door lock out of reach. We test at the door before choosing Bluetooth, a Wi-Fi bridge or a Matter-over-Thread lock, and place any bridge indoors where it holds a signal.

Building Specialists

Smart Lock Installation for Morris Park’s Architecture

Morris Park was built in layers. City Planning dates the start of house building to the turn of the twentieth century and says it sped up after a streetcar line opened along Morris Park Avenue in 1910, the same year a fire swept the old racetrack whose grounds were then cut into lots. Building went on well after the Second World War. Each layer left a different kind of front door, and each takes a smart lock differently.

The grid itself is a reminder of how old some of these lots are. Several Morris Park streets, including Cruger, Holland, Radcliff, Colden, Paulding and Hone avenues, are named after New York City mayors of the eighteenth and nineteenth centuries, and the neighborhood takes its name from the Morris Park Racecourse, which hosted both the Preakness and the Belmont Stakes in 1890. A house on one of those blocks may have had its front door replaced twice and its side door never; we often find a 1950s lock on the door the family uses most.

Detached Brick Two-Families

The house on most R4A blocks: two stories plus an attic under a pitched roof, one unit per floor, a stoop at the front and a driveway down one side. Usually three entry doors and a basement door, and the most to gain from deciding which door gets the keypad before buying anything.

Semi-Detached Pairs

Two houses sharing one party wall, with front doors often a few feet apart. Keypads face the same walk, so we choose positions and backlight settings with the neighbor’s door in mind, and keep each house’s lock on its own app account and Wi-Fi.

Indian Village Tudors

North of Morris Park Avenue near Jacobi, on streets named Choctaw Place, Narragansett Avenue, Seminole Avenue and Pawnee Place, detached brick Tudor-style houses from around 1940 carry heavy wood doors, often with a small glazed opening. Hardware choice here is as much about looks as fit.

Frame Two-Families

Two-story frame houses with two separate units and a full basement, some on 25-by-95-foot lots. Wood frames that have taken several generations of locks need the strike area checked for softness and old screw holes before a motorized bolt goes in.

Apartment Buildings on the Avenues

The small and medium apartment buildings along Morris Park Avenue and Williamsbridge Road. With three or more families they are multiple dwellings, so tenant lock rights, the Tenant Data Privacy Act and the building’s street door all come into play.

Shop-and-Apartment Buildings

Ground-floor stores on Morris Park Avenue with apartments above. The shop door, the stair door to the apartments and the rear door each have a different keyholder, and none of their codes should open another.

New Buildings Near the Station

The 2024 rezoning around the planned Morris Park Metro-North station on the east side allows larger apartment buildings. New construction usually arrives with its own access system; unit-door locks have to fit what the developer installed.

Door details we check on every Morris Park house

  • Backset and bore: the distance from the door edge to the center of the existing deadbolt hole, and the hole’s diameter. Most smart deadbolts are made for a 2-3/8 or 2-3/4 inch backset in a standard 2-1/8 inch bore; a house that has had four locks in sixty years may have neither.
  • Door thickness: heavy wood front doors on older houses can be thicker than a lock’s listed range, and some side doors are thinner. Either may need a different model or an extension kit.
  • Storm and security doors: an aluminum storm door or an iron gate in front of the entry door changes where a keypad can sit and whether a thick interior unit leaves room for the storm door’s handle.
  • Glass near the lock: sidelights, door lites and the small glazed openings on Tudor doors decide whether a thumb-turn could be reached through a broken pane.
  • Swing and latch: we close the door from a few inches open and let go. If it does not latch on its own, the strike or the hinges get adjusted before a motor is asked to do the work.
  • Old holes: filled rim-lock holes, chain-guard screws and abandoned deadbolt bores, so the new lock lands in sound wood or steel and its trim covers what it should.

The Side Door

The Side Door Off the Driveway Is Morris Park’s Real Front Door

Walk any residential block between Haight Avenue and Williamsbridge Road and count the side doors. On a detached or semi-detached house the driveway runs along one side of the lot to a garage at the back, and somewhere along it there is a door: into the kitchen, into a back hall, or down a few steps to the basement. In most families that door is used ten times for every time someone climbs the stoop. It is also the door with the least light, the least foot traffic past it and, too often, the oldest lock on the house.

The March 2025 break-in near Loreto Playground showed the pattern clearly. The people who entered did not touch the front door. They knocked to see whether anyone would answer, dealt with the camera, and worked on the side door, all within a few minutes of the house emptying for the school pick-up. Nothing on this page claims that a different lock would have stopped them. What a well-installed smart lock does change is what the owner knows and when. A lock with door-position sensing reports that the door is open while the bolt is still thrown, which is exactly the signature of a door pried away from its frame. A lock that logs every operation records whether anyone used a code, a key or the app, and when. And a keypad lock that auto-locks takes away the most common side-door failure of all, which is the door simply left unlocked after someone ran out to the car.

So on most Morris Park houses we plan the side door first and the front door second. For the side door that means a keypad deadbolt rated for exterior use, set to lock itself a short time after it closes, with sensing that confirms the door is actually shut rather than just bolted in the air. It means reinforcing the strike with longer screws into the framing, because a lock is only as strong as the wood it bolts into. It means placing any bridge or hub indoors, out of reach and on a circuit nobody is likely to switch off. And it means connecting the lock to the camera or alarm that already watches the driveway, so a forced-door alert and a video clip arrive together rather than in two different apps an hour apart.

Side-door checklist we run on every Morris Park house

  • Who uses it: which household members, which tenant, which aide or cleaner, and at what hours. That decides how many codes the lock needs and whether schedules matter.
  • What is behind it: a kitchen, a back hall shared by two units, or basement stairs. A door that leads into a shared space needs a credential for each household, not one code for everyone.
  • How it closes: a side door that has to be pulled hard or lifted to latch is fixed first, because auto-lock depends on the door shutting by itself.
  • What it is made of: wood, steel-clad or a storm door in front. The answer changes the lock, the screws and sometimes the strike.
  • What can see it: a camera, a neighbor’s window, a street light, or nothing. A door no one can see benefits most from door-position alerts.
  • Where the signal is: we test the Wi-Fi at the door itself, because a driveway door at the back of a brick house is often the weakest spot on the network.

The front door usually gets something quieter: a keyed smart deadbolt that matches the handleset, or a retrofit that changes nothing on the street side at all. Families who host on Sundays, or whose relatives come and go, often keep the front door on a physical key and the side door on codes. There is no single right answer. The point of the walkthrough is to match each door to how your household really comes and goes.

Business Smart Lock Installation in Morris Park

Morris Park’s businesses sit in two clusters: the shops and restaurants of Morris Park Avenue, and the medical offices that have grown up around the hospitals on its eastern edge. Both want the same things from a door — a code per person instead of a key per person, a record of who opened up, and a way out that never depends on a code — but the doors themselves are very different.

The Morris Park Business Improvement District covers properties on Morris Park Avenue between Unionport Road and Amethyst Street to the west and Williamsbridge Road to the east, with its office at 966 Morris Park Avenue. Storefront doors, rear service doors and stair doors to the apartments above.

The neighborhood’s north-south spine, home to the Morris Park Station post office at 1807 Williamsbridge Road and FDNY Squad 61 at 1518. Small apartment buildings, professional offices and shops, many with a residential entrance beside the commercial one.

Private practices, labs and clinics near Calvary Hospital at 1740 Eastchester Road and Montefiore’s Weiler hospital at 1825. Suite doors that need staff codes, after-hours cleaners and a records room locked separately from the waiting room.

Hutchinson Metro Center

The office campus at Waters Place, where the 280,000-square-foot Tower Two at 1250 Waters Place is leased to Montefiore for medical offices and 1200 Waters Place houses tenants including the Bronx Chamber of Commerce. Suite-level locks inside a building that runs its own lobby access.

Schools, Churches and Community Rooms

Parish halls, schools and meeting rooms whose side doors are used by different groups on different nights. One lock, several schedules, and a log that shows which group last locked up.

Near the Planned Metro-North Station

The east side of the neighborhood, where the state’s May 2026 Downtown Revitalization Initiative award includes $2.75 million for a public plaza linking the planned Morris Park station entrance to the Hutchinson Metro Center campus and Marconi Street.

Compliance notes for Morris Park business doors

  • One motion out: a door on a path of egress must open from the inside without a key, a code, special knowledge or effort. Keypad levers and deadbolts with a plain inside thumb-turn or lever meet that; a double-cylinder deadbolt does not.
  • Electric strikes and magnetic locks: where a shop or office door is wired to buzz open, the inside release, any request-to-exit device and the fail-safe or fail-secure choice are set to the code, and magnetic locks are tied to the fire alarm where the code requires release.
  • Reach and grip: as a guide we place keypads and levers so the operable parts fall between 34 and 48 inches above the floor and do not need tight grasping or twisting, following the 2010 ADA Standards.
  • Street doors of apartment buildings: in a multiple dwelling with fobs, app entry or biometrics, the city’s Tenant Data Privacy Act sets consent, data and retention rules, and a mechanical key must remain available to tenants.
  • Patient areas: a medical office’s compliance officer, not the locksmith, decides what its privacy program requires; we document who holds which code and give the practice an export of the log.

Your Questions Answered

Smart Lock Installation in Morris Park: Questions Answered

These are the questions Morris Park owners, tenants and shopkeepers actually bring to a walkthrough, grouped by what they are really about. Questions about price, booking and brands are in the FAQ further down.

Break-ins, construction next door and ratings

Burglars near Loreto Playground pried a side door open — would a smart lock on that door have warned the owner?

Potentially quite a lot, depending on the model. A lock with door-position sensing knows the difference between a door that is closed and bolted and a door that is open while the bolt is still extended. When a door is forced away from its frame, the second state appears without any code, key or app unlock being logged first, and many locks can push that as an alert. Some keypad models also sound a built-in alarm when they detect tampering or repeated failed entries.

What it cannot do is stop a determined pry bar on its own. That depends on the strike, the screws behind it and the frame, which is why we fit long screws into the framing on every side door we do. The useful combination is a lock that reports, a camera the burglar has to deal with first, and an alarm contact that does not care whether the lock was touched. Any one of the three can be defeated; all three together tell you, within seconds, that something is wrong.

What do the AAA or Grade 1 ratings printed on a smart lock box actually measure?

The Builders Hardware Manufacturers Association grades residential locks in three categories: security, durability and finish. Under its residential scheme a grade of C is good, B better and A best, so AAA means the top grade in all three. The standard for residential deadbolts, A156.40, covers the force needed to turn the bolt, how many open-and-close cycles the hardware survives, a set of seventeen security tests including loading the door against the bolt with 1,350 pounds of force, and a salt-spray test of the finish.

Older packaging still uses the ANSI Grade 1, 2 and 3 language, which was written for commercial hardware. Either way, read the security letter first. For a Morris Park side door off a driveway, a lock with a top security grade fitted to a reinforced strike is worth more than a fashionable finish. For an interior home-office door, a lower-graded keypad lever is perfectly reasonable.

The lot next door could be rebuilt under the station-area rezoning — how should we plan our locks around a construction site?

The August 2024 rezoning around the planned Metro-North stations allows larger buildings on some blocks of Morris Park, Van Nest and Parkchester, and the MTA has scheduled station work along Sackett Avenue. A building site next door changes a house’s exposure for months: scaffolding and sidewalk sheds can screen a side door from the street, fences open up rear yards, and a lot of unfamiliar people pass the driveway every day.

For the length of the work, we suggest auto-lock on every exterior door, door-position alerts on the side and rear doors, and a camera covering the driveway. Do not hand a code to anyone working next door, however friendly; if they need to reach your yard or meter, open the door for them yourself or issue a one-time code and watch the log. And if a door that used to close cleanly starts to bind, call us before the motor wears itself out against it.

Tenants, carers and night-shift households

A resident doctor wants our upstairs apartment on a one-year lease — what should the lease say about the smart lock?

Put it in writing, briefly. The lease should name which doors the tenant uses and which lock is on each, confirm that the tenant receives a working key to their own entrance as well as any code, and say who holds the administrator account. Ideally the tenant administers their own unit door and the owner administers only shared doors, such as a side entrance or the basement.

It should also cover the log: whether the owner can see entries on doors the tenant uses, and that the owner will not use that record to track the tenant. Add who changes the batteries and who pays for a lockout call, and that all of the tenant’s codes, fingerprints and app access are deleted at move-out. Finally, do not let the lease suggest the owner can shut the tenant out by deleting a code. Under New York’s Real Property Actions and Proceedings Law section 768, changing an occupant’s lock without supplying a key is unlawful once they have lived there thirty days or signed a lease. Your lawyer should review the wording.

The visiting nurse service sends whoever is on call that night — should each nurse get a code, or one code for the agency?

Where the lock allows it, one code per regular visitor and a separate code for the agency’s night or on-call staff. Named codes give you a record of who came and when, which is useful for the family and for the agency. A single shared agency code is easier to manage when staff rotate, but you lose the who, and the code tends to spread.

A practical pattern we set up for Morris Park families caring for a parent at home: named, scheduled codes for the regular daytime aide; one agency code limited to the hours the agency covers; a family administrator on two phones who can issue a one-time code from anywhere; and a key kept with a trusted neighbor. When the service ends, every code linked to it is deleted the same day. We write the list on a card kept inside the door, without the codes themselves.

Older houses and family estates

Our late mother’s house is in probate — how can the executor control who gets in until it is sold?

This comes up often in a neighborhood where houses stay in one family for decades. Keys to the house are usually spread among siblings, cousins, neighbors and former tenants, and once a parent has died, appraisers, cleaners, contractors and real estate agents all need access on different days. A keypad lock on the door that is used most lets the executor give each of them a code that works only on the day it is needed, and see from the log when each one came and went.

Practically: re-key or replace the other exterior locks so the old keys stop working, put the administrator account in the executor’s name rather than on a relative’s phone, keep one mechanical key with the executor, and delete every temporary code as soon as its visit is done. When the house sells, reset the lock or take it with you, and hand the buyer the keys and a fresh code. If family members disagree about access, that is a matter for the estate’s lawyer, not the lock.

Our Indian Village Tudor has a heavy arched wood door with a small window in it — where can a smart lock go?

Usually where the existing deadbolt is, with care. Tudor-style doors on the Indian Village streets near Jacobi are often thicker than modern doors and have a small glazed opening near eye level. First we check the door’s thickness against the lock’s listed range and whether an extension kit is available. Then we check the distance from the glazing to the bolt, because a thumb-turn that can be reached through a broken pane defeats the lock.

If the original hardware is part of the house’s character, a retrofit that changes only the inside thumb-turn keeps the outside exactly as it is. If a keypad would look wrong on the arched door, many owners put the keypad lock on the side door and keep the front door on a key. We bring finish samples and photographs of comparable doors so you can decide on looks before anything is cut.

Apps, firmware and the long run

What happens to our smart lock if the company behind the app goes out of business?

It has happened in the smart-home world. In April 2022 Insteon shut down its cloud servers without warning when its parent company failed, and owners lost app control overnight until a new owner brought the service back that June. A lock is less exposed than a light switch, because a keypad and a key work locally, but remote unlocking, alerts, guest scheduling and the log depend on the maker’s cloud.

We reduce the risk in three ways. We favor makers with long histories in locks, not only in apps. Where it suits the house, we choose locks that support Matter, an industry standard that lets a lock work with more than one ecosystem and with local control. And every lock we fit keeps a mechanical key or another non-cloud way in, so a dead app never means a locked-out family.

Who installs the lock’s firmware updates once you have left, and should they run automatically?

The lock’s app does, and for most homes automatic updates are the right setting. Firmware updates fix security problems and compatibility issues as phones and routers change. A lock that has not been updated in years is more likely to be the one that will not pair with a new phone or a replacement router.

At handover we confirm the lock is on current firmware, show the account owner where update notices appear, and leave automatic updates on unless you have a reason to manage them yourself. Two cautions: let an update finish before you change batteries, and expect a lock to be briefly unresponsive while it installs one. If an update ever leaves a lock misbehaving, that is a service call, not something to fix by resetting it to factory settings, which wipes every code.

Can the lock maker see our comings and goings, and could that record be handed to the police?

Cloud-connected locks store their activity on the maker’s servers. Yale’s privacy notice, for example, says lock activity is stored alongside your account information for as long as the account is active, and that deletion requests may be refused where the law requires the data to be kept. Like any company, a lock maker may be compelled to disclose data by legal process. The Electronic Frontier Foundation has argued since 2023 that police should need a warrant for smart lock data and that tenants should be told.

If that concerns you, there are choices. Read the maker’s privacy notice before buying. Keep the log shorter where the app allows it. Use a lock that works locally over Bluetooth, Z-Wave or Thread through a hub you control rather than through a vendor cloud. And for a tenant’s door, remember that the person whose movements are logged is the tenant; the lease should say who sees that record.

Is it safe to text or WhatsApp a door code to the plumber or a relative?

Safer than leaving a key under the mat, but treat the message as if it were the key. Send codes only to the person who needs them, never in a group chat, and make them temporary: most apps can issue a code that works only on certain days or between certain hours, or only once. Delete the message after it is used if you can.

Better still, use the app’s own invitation feature where it exists, which gives the person access tied to their phone and can be withdrawn with one tap, and leaves a named entry in the log. Never send the master or administrator code. And if a code has been passed around more widely than you meant, change it; that takes a minute in the app and costs nothing.

AI Overview Reality Check

What AI Gets Wrong About Smart Lock Installation in Morris Park

Search summaries, cost sites such as Angi, HomeAdvisor and Fixr, and product listings repeat a handful of claims about smart locks. We could not open Google’s AI Overview from our build environment, so the claims below are the ones those published sources make, checked against what we find on Morris Park doors.

“Any smart deadbolt fits a standard door”

The claim assumes a standard door. Lock makers do design around a common bore and backset, and on a modern pre-drilled door most models drop in. The trouble is that few Morris Park front doors are modern. A two-family built between the wars has usually had several locks in its life, and the bore that is there now may be oversized, off-center or patched.

Door thickness is the next surprise. Heavy wood entry doors on older houses, including many of the Tudor-style houses in Indian Village, can be thicker than a lock’s listed range, and some side doors are thinner. Storm doors, iron gates and glass near the lock all narrow the choice further.

That is why we measure before you buy. Five minutes with a tape measure at the walkthrough saves a return, a second trip and, sometimes, a door that has been drilled for the wrong lock.

“Smart locks are less secure than ordinary locks”

Smart locks are graded under the same residential standard as mechanical deadbolts. BHMA’s A156.40 tests security, durability and finish, and several smart deadbolts carry the top grade in all three. A smart lock can be every bit as strong as the brass deadbolt it replaces, and stronger than many.

Where smart locks are weaker is in the things a mechanical lock never had: a cloud account, a phone, a code someone shared. Those are real risks, and they are managed with a strong account password, two-step sign-in, named codes and prompt deletion when someone leaves.

And on any lock, the strike plate and the screws holding it decide how hard the door is to force. A top-graded lock on a strike held by short screws in an old frame is still a weak door.

“Smart lock installation costs about $300”

The number comes from cost guides. Fixr’s January 2025 update gives a $250–$400 range nationally, averaging $300 for a Wi-Fi lock with one accessory, with handymen at $50–$100 an hour. As a sense check for a single pre-drilled door, that is fair enough.

They are not AESS prices, and they leave out the work Morris Park doors often need: reinforcing a side-door strike, re-bushing an oversized bore, fitting around a storm door, or setting up separate codes and keys for an owner and a tenant. Building street doors are a different job entirely.

Our published Bronx figures for building doors are on this page, and house and unit doors are quoted flat after a free walkthrough. The written quote is the bill.

“If the app company shuts down, the lock keeps working”

Partly true. A keypad, a fingerprint reader and a key work locally on most locks, so the door still opens. What stops are the cloud features: remote unlock, notifications, schedules and the activity log.

The smart-home industry has already seen it happen. Insteon’s servers went dark in April 2022 when its parent company failed, and users lost app control overnight until a new owner restored the service that June.

So we favor established lock makers, consider Matter-compatible locks that can be run from more than one ecosystem, and never leave a household without a non-cloud way in.

“Landlord rules for smart locks are the same in every rental”

Online summaries tend to describe one set of tenant rights. In New York the answer depends on the building. The Multiple Dwelling Law, including the section that lets a tenant add a lock of their own, applies to buildings with three or more families. A Morris Park two-family is a private dwelling under the same law.

The city’s Tenant Data Privacy Act, which governs fobs, app entry and biometrics, was also written for multiple dwellings. But the state’s unlawful-eviction law, RPAPL section 768, protects any occupant of thirty days or more, in any kind of dwelling, from having a lock changed without being given a key.

So the owner of a two-family has fewer formal data rules and exactly the same duty not to lock a tenant out. We plan both doors with that in mind, and we suggest the owner’s lawyer reviews anything written into a lease.

“A smart lock does the job of a security camera”

A smart lock records what the lock did: which code, which phone, which key, when. It does not see who was standing there, and it cannot record a door forced open around it unless it has door-position sensing.

The March 2025 side-door burglary near Loreto Playground is a reminder that a camera can be pushed aside. Each device covers the other’s blind spot: the camera shows a face until it is interfered with, the lock and an alarm contact show what the door did whether or not the camera is watching.

For a driveway side door, the strongest setup is all three working together, with alerts that arrive on the same phone.

“Auto-lock makes any door secure by itself”

Auto-lock throws the bolt a set time after the lock thinks the door has closed. If the door did not actually close — it bounced off a swollen frame, or someone left it resting on the latch — the bolt can extend into thin air and the app may still report locked.

That is why we prefer locks with door-position sensing on side doors, and why the first thing we adjust on any door is the latch and strike, so the door closes fully on its own.

Auto-lock is also the feature most likely to shut someone out who has stepped into the yard. We set the delay with the household, not by default.

Field Notes on Morris Park House Doors

How our installers work through a Morris Park house before choosing a lock — working practice, not the story of one job

We start at the curb, not at the lock. How many doors face the street, where does the driveway go, which door has the worn step? The door with the worn step is the one the family uses, and it is usually not the one they called about.

Then we count keyholders. Owner, spouse, children, the upstairs tenant, a grandparent around the corner, the aide, the cleaner, a brother-in-law who did the bathroom five years ago. Most families come up with more names than they expected, and every one of those keys is still out there.

At each door we measure, then do the swing test: open it a few inches and let go. A side door that does not latch on its own gets fixed before anything electronic touches it, because a motorized bolt pushing against a sticking door is the fastest way to flat batteries and a jammed lock.

Last, we stand at each door with a phone on the house Wi-Fi. Brick walls and a router in the basement decide more lock choices here than brand preference does. Before we leave, every tenant has a key and every code has a name.
