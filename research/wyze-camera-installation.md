**Building types, entry and wiring situations**
- NYCHA tenants are allowed a door camera or video doorbell with conditions: the camera or video doorbell must be wireless; 2) the camera or video doorbell must be placed over the apartment's peephole or affixed to the door with adhesive. Tenants may not make holes in or alterations to the door, frame, or walls above and around the door. NYCHA can make the tenant remove it if it's used for anything other than personal safety and security. This is a strong angle for a no-drill Wyze install. — https://www.nyc.gov/assets/nycha/downloads/pdf/pp/house-rules-040821.pdf
- NYCHA also runs its own CCTV in lobby and mail box areas, building entrances and exits, elevators. — https://www.nyc.gov/assets/nycha/downloads/pdf/pp/house-rules-040821.pdf
- Real-world example: a Bronx NYCHA tenant at Parkside Houses received an eviction notice saying that she had breached her lease by installing the cameras. Coverage was from 2012. — https://www.aol.com/2012/09/11/security-cameras-spark-eviction-notice-at-public-housing-complex/
- Rentals and co-ops/condos: A camera mounted on a hallway wall sits on a common area, so it typically needs building approval in a co-op or condo. A peephole camera is often the least complicated route, since it swaps existing hardware without altering the door or the hallway. — https://www.connextivity.com/insights/nyc-apartment-security-safety-camera-laws
- Audio: Whether a camera passively capturing hallway audio meets the definition of unlawful eavesdropping has never been settled cleanly, which is why audio is usually left off. This is useful advice when setting up Wyze audio. — https://www.connextivity.com/insights/nyc-apartment-security-safety-camera-laws
- Brick Underground: you need your landlord's permission to install any equipment in the common areas of your building, which includes the hallway and area outside your apartment. — https://www.brickunderground.com/live/creepy-neighbor-security-camera
- Justia lawyer answer: As long as the camera is not aimed at looking into the apartment when the apartment door is open, video surveillance is legal. Cameras in common areas such as hallways and stairwells are also lawful as long as voice is not recorded. — https://answers.justia.com/question/2026/04/08/can-landlords-use-outdoor-cameras-to-mon-1111841
- Context for rent-stabilized tenants: The landlord had used a motion-activated camera hidden in an electrical box to document the brothers' movements over a period of 15 months. That was a 2017 NYC perjury case about a rent-controlled apartment. — https://www.legalexaminer.com/ebrown/home-family/is-your-landlord-spying-on-you-with-smart-home-tech/
- One NYC vendor says recent amendments to New York's Real Property Law now require landlords to permit tenants to install WiFi cameras inside their own units. **UNVERIFIED. Don't publish without checking the statute.** — https://abstractcameranewyork.net/cctv-camera-installation-nyc
- Wi-Fi limits: Most Wyze devices only work on 2.4 GHz Wi-Fi networks. — https://support.wyze.com/hc/en-us/articles/360050638272-Wyze-Cam-v3-Setup-Guide
- Brick walls block signal. A Wyze forum user wrote: If I stand on the outside of my house on the solid wall side (brick) I have no signal. A Wi-Fi extender fixed it. — https://forums.wyze.com/t/too-far-to-work/14578
- Setup tip: If the camera is mounted in a distant corner or behind thick walls, the signal may be too weak to finish pairing even though day-to-day streaming at that location would be acceptable. The fix is to pair near the router, then move the camera. WPA3-only routers can also block pairing. — https://www.wifispeed.com/blog/how-to-fix-wifi-on-wyze-cam
- Window Cam (a good fit for renters): Install on window glass only. Do not mount on a window behind a screen. — https://support.wyze.com/hc/en-us/articles/40495031377691-How-do-I-mount-Wyze-Window-Cam
- Outdoor use: Wyze Outdoor Power Adapter (sold separately) required for outdoor use. — https://slickdeals.net/f/17172079-wyze-cam-og-1080p-hd-wi-fi-security-camera-indoor-outdoor-19-99

**City rules and data (LPC, NYPD, HPD, DOB, 311)**
- LPC (landmarks): the rules cover equipment commonly used for security measures, such as lighting, security cameras and intercom panels. They prohibit installation of many of these types of objects on a primary façade unless there is no feasible alternative. — https://www.nyc.gov/assets/lpc/downloads/pdf/LPC%20Rules%20Amendments%20Revisions%20August%209%202018.pdf
- The LPC Permit Guidebook, Chapter 11, has an "Installing Security Cameras" section: Staff can approve security cameras if installation meets the rules (the full criteria are in the PDF). — https://www.nyc.gov/assets/lpc/downloads/pdf/LPCPermitGuidebook%20_Chapter11_HealthSafety.pdf
- An LPC permit document cites Section 2-20(c)(4) for security cameras. — https://a860-gpp.nyc.gov/downloads/7h149t85r?locale=en
- NYPD camera sharing (voluntary): You can register your security camera locations with the NYPD at NewYorkCityConnect.org. — https://portal.311.nyc.gov/article/?kanumber=KA-03645
- A mandatory "safe streets security camera registry act" has been introduced in the state legislature repeatedly (2011–2019). I found no evidence it passed. — https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/bills/2019/S487
- HPD Tenant Data Privacy Law: building owners No information may be used to harass or evict a tenant. collected through smart access systems. — https://www.nyc.gov/site/hpd/services-and-information/tenant-data-privacy-law.page
- That law's definition of "authentication data" does not include data generated through or collected by a video or camera system that is used to monitor entrances but not grant entry. So a Wyze camera that only watches an entrance is outside it. — https://codelibrary.amlegal.com/codes/newyorkcity/latest/NYCadmin/0-0-0-133490
