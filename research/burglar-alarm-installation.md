**Building types, ways in, and wiring**
- Recent burglary sprees in Brooklyn mostly came in through basements, not front doors. Police say one man burglarized 11 businesses across six precincts, in neighborhoods including Carroll Gardens, Prospect Heights, Fort Greene, Clinton Hill, Bedford-Stuyvesant, Crown Heights, Bushwick and North Williamsburg, entering primarily through basement-level doors between May 8 and July 17 (2026). Examples: a Prospect Heights bar at 80 Sixth Ave., entered through a basement door around 4:15 a.m. on July 8, with roughly $4,000 taken from an office, and a coffee shop at 1114 Bedford Ave. in Bed-Stuy, entered through a basement door on May 8, with about $1,000 taken — https://brooklyneagle.com/404730/burglar-hits-11-brooklyn-businesses-in-summer-spree/
- Storefront glass was the way in for another pattern. Between Aug. 3 and Sept. 10, 2026, there were 19 incidents in which two people got into businesses by throwing an object through a window. The businesses were in Bensonhurst, Bath Beach, Marine Park, Mill Basin, Bergen Beach, Borough Park, Kensington, Flatbush, Ditmas Park, Midwood, Crown Heights, Park Slope, Prospect Heights, Windsor Terrace, Williamsburg and East Williamsburg. This is an angle for glass-break sensors — https://www.newsbreak.com/news-12-1587832/4885865530489-nypd-seeks-suspects-in-19-commercial-burglaries-across-brooklyn
- In Cypress Hills, three burglaries between May 24 and May 30 were committed by one man, who broke a glass door in one case and a window in another, and allegedly stole a safe containing $100,000 — https://pix11.com/news/crime/queens-brooklyn-businesses-hit-in-burglary-spree-over-100k-stolen-nypd/
- Earlier in 2026, a crew hit more than 10 businesses across the 90th and 94th Precincts over two nights, Feb. 22 and March 4, prying open doors and windows in Williamsburg and Greenpoint — https://www.qchron.com/editions/central/businesses-targeted-in-crime-spree-cops/article_bf66a0e8-255a-41de-846f-6c9b9afcf3f2.html

- Upper windows: after an East Flatbush case, a retired NYPD member advised getting an alarm system that extends to the upper windows of your home (undated) — https://www.cbsnews.com/newyork/news/burglars-armed-robberies-city-suburbs/
- A local installer lists the usual threats in Brooklyn homes as package theft from stoops and lobbies, break-ins through rear windows and basement entrances, and unauthorized access to shared buildings — https://lockandtech.com/areas/brooklyn/
- Wiring costs, from one Brooklyn installer: reusing existing wiring is cheapest, new wired runs through plaster take the most labor, and wireless sits in between, and taking over a working Honeywell or DSC panel is the cheapest path — https://linkedsecurityny.com/services/alarm-systems-installation-new-york/

**Rules and city data**
- NYC noise code §24-238: a building's audible burglar alarm must be able to automatically terminate its operation within fifteen minutes of its being activated. Installers need to program the siren cutoff — https://codelibrary.amlegal.com/codes/newyorkcity/latest/NYCadmin/0-0-0-209190
- DEP and the NYPD share enforcement of the Noise Code, and complaints go through 311 — https://www.nyc.gov/site/dep/environment/noise-code.page
- Landmarks Preservation Commission rule 2-20 covers exterior equipment like window security bars, security cameras, lighting and intercom panels. Cameras are capped at no more than two for every 25 feet of street frontage on small buildings of six stories or less with frontage of 40 feet or less — https://nyc-docs-new-design-test.readthedocs.io/en/latest/d02/c44/
- The LPC rule would prohibit installing many of these objects on a primary façade unless there is no feasible alternative or the location is mandated by an agency. That means exterior bells, sirens and camera wiring on landmarked brownstones should go on secondary facades. This is the 2018 rule document — https://www.nyc.gov/assets/lpc/downloads/pdf/LPC%20Rules%20Amendments%20Revisions%20August%209%202018.pdf
- The sources disagree on permits:
  - One installer says installation itself does not require a permit, but all NYC alarm systems must be registered with the NYPD through the NYC Alarm Registration Program — https://lockandtech.com/articles/what-is-a-burglar-alarm-system/
  - Competitors say the NYPD requires burglar alarm permits (https://commercialfireandsecurity.com/Brooklyn) and that permit fees range from $25 to $100 (https://www.abstractenterprisessecuritysystems.com/alarm-installation-downtown-brooklyn-ny)
  - Neither claim is checked against NYPD's own pages. Verify before publishing.
- Electrical permits: a licensed electrician and electrical permit may be required if installation involves new circuits or other electrical work — https://lockandtech.com/articles/what-is-a-burglar-alarm-system/
- New York State licensing: installers need at least 81 hours of qualifying education and must pass an alarm installer examination — https://dos.ny.gov/get-security-or-fire-alarm-installer-license

**Things changing how installs are done**
- Verizon's copper phone lines are being retired. On Sept. 9, 2026, Verizon filed to stop copper phone service in New York and other states effective on or after November 30, 2026, pending approval, and burglar alarm panels that dial a monitoring station over analog lines are affected — https://dataremote.com/blog/verizon-copper-retirement-what-it-means-for-you
- Verizon has since walked back some cutoffs area by area, but a phone line going down with no backup means the system can't reach the monitoring station at all — https://www.alarmgrid.com/blog/verizon-asked-the-fcc-to-retire-copper-landlines-in-up-to-18-sta
- One NYC vendor recommends a supervised multi-carrier 5G cellular communicator with battery backup, installed before the copper is cut — https://www.lightningvoip.com/verizon-copper-retirement-nyc/
