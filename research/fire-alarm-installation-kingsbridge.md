### 2. Neighborhood background

- **Boundaries:** Van Cortlandt Park to the north, Jerome Avenue to the east, Kingsbridge Road to the south, and the Major Deegan Expressway to the west. — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingsbridge_Heights,_Bronx
- **Alternative boundaries:** Grand Concourse on the east and Broadway on the west — https://cityneighborhoods.nyc/kingsbridge-heights
- **Main street:** Sedgwick Avenue is the primary thoroughfare through Kingsbridge Heights. — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingsbridge_Heights,_Bronx
- **History:** In 1874, the City of New York annexed three towns that later became the western half of the Bronx, including the Town of Kingsbridge. — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingsbridge,_Bronx
- **Before development:** Kingsbridge Heights consisted of farmland, forested ridges, and scattered estates. The early 20th century transformed Kingsbridge Heights from countryside to city. — https://cityneighborhoods.nyc/kingsbridge-heights
- **1970s:** the neighborhood remained relatively intact due to its strong housing stock and active community organizations. — https://cityneighborhoods.nyc/kingsbridge-heights
- **Racetrack to reservoir:** The racetrack was acquired and demolished by the city in 1894, to make way for Jerome Park Reservoir. — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingsbridge_Heights,_Bronx
- **Building stock:**
  - Wikipedia says Kingsbridge Heights is dominated by multi-unit detached homes. It also notes The Fort Independence Street-Heath Avenue Houses are a one, 21-story NYCHA development — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingsbridge_Heights,_Bronx

  - Street-level description from a comment on Forgotten New York: a mix of narrow fronted detached two storey homes and mid rise apartments — https://forgotten-ny.com/2014/02/50th-precinct-kingsbridge-heights/

### 3. What might change how the job is done (my reading of the sources above, not stated by them)
- Many pre-1939 five- and six-story walk-ups have shared cocklofts, so top-floor and cockloft detection deserves attention.
- Several of the fires above started in stores with apartments overhead, so storefront buildings may need detection that covers both the store and the floors above.
- Lithium-ion battery fires recur here: the deli, Devoe Terrace and the borough-wide incidents. That makes detection near where e-bikes and scooters are charged, and near exits, a real local issue.
- On the hillside blocks (Kingsbridge Terrace), the steep terrain and stairs up to front doors could affect access for installers.
- Exterior work on the two landmarks probably needs LPC review. I didn't find the specific rule.
