### Neighborhood background

**History**
- Bath Beach was a portion of the Dutch town of New Utrecht when it was settled in 1652, part of a purchase of land by Cornelius van Werckhover for the Dutch West India Company. — https://brooklyneagle.com/323781/how-bath-beach-got-its-name/
- In the mid-19th century, freed slaves were given parcels of land in Bath Beach and created one of the first African American settlements. — https://brooklyneagle.com/323781/how-bath-beach-got-its-name/
- The neighborhood developed with the opening of the Brooklyn, Bath and Coney Island steam railroad in 1862 — https://brooklyneagle.com/323781/how-bath-beach-got-its-name/
- Wealthy families built villas, yacht clubs and mansions. With the rapid rail transit opening in 1916, Jewish and Italian families poured in from Manhattan's Lower East Side. — https://brooklyneagle.com/articles/2025/06/29/bath-beach-history/
- The Ulmer Park amusement center, with rides and a dance hall, was built by Ulmer Brewery (of Bushwick) in 1893 but lasted only about six years. — https://brooklyneagle.com/323781/how-bath-beach-got-its-name/
- Its current population, heavy with Italian Americans, took form after the 1929 stock market crash left mansions abandoned. — https://brooklyneagle.com/articles/2025/06/29/bath-beach-history/
- Most of the beaches, though, were obliterated when the Circumferential, later simply the Belt (though the signs say Shore)* Parkway was constructed in 1940, give or take a year. — https://forgotten-ny.com/2010/07/bath-beachs-cropsey-avenue/
- Prior to the 1920s the avenue ran through two small towns that were absorbed into Bath Beach, Guntherville and Unionville — https://forgotten-ny.com/2010/07/bath-beachs-cropsey-avenue/
- Since the 1990s, many Asian, West Asian, Eastern European, Central American, and Middle Eastern immigrants have settled in Bath Beach. The 2020 population was 33,070. — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bath_Beach,_Brooklyn

**Boundaries, main streets and shopping**

### 2. History and development
- The neighborhood was developed from the 1840's into the 1870's as a residential community for professionals working in Manhattan and downtown Brooklyn. — https://hdc.org/hdc-across-nyc/brooklyn/brooklyn-landmarked/dil-bk-boerum-hill/
- Charles Hoyt, and his business associate, Russell Nevins, purchased much of the Martenese property in 1834. — https://southbrooklyn.com/boerum-hill/
- The name "Boerum Hill" was coined in early 1964 by Boerum Hill Association founder Helen Buckler. Before that, the area was sometimes called "North Gowanus". — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boerum_Hill
- The historic district was designated by LPC on November 20, 1973, and listed on the National Register in 1983. — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boerum_Hill
- The 2018 extension: approximately 288 buildings, many of which are modest row houses built in the 1850s to 1870s in the Greek Revival and Italianate styles. — https://www.brownstoner.com/architecture/boerum-hill-historic-district-extension-lpc-vote-2018/
- Extension blocks: Dean and Bergen Streets between Smith and Hoyt; Atlantic Avenue between Hoyt and Nevins; Pacific Street between Hoyt and Bond; and Wyckoff and Bergen Streets between Bond and Nevins. — https://bklyner.com/lpc-approves-boerum-hill-historic-district-extension/
- The Gowanus Houses were completed in June 1949. — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gowanus_Houses

## Borough Park, Brooklyn — /intercom-installation-borough-park-brooklyn-ny (factory research, 2026-10-30)

**Intercom-specific findings (most important)**

- **Shabbat entry (the main local angle).** I found no Borough Park source on this, but a general rabbinic Q&A explains why it matters for intercom doors. It says such doors often have a latch that can be moved before Shabbos, which bypasses the door's electric operation and allows the door to be opened manually on Shabbos. It also says these functions can be verified very simply during the week by turning off the power to the intercom and lock system and then checking whether the door can be opened manually without electricity. Neighborhood context: the rhythm of the week bends entirely around Shabbos and the Jewish holidays. — https://dinonline.org/2026/06/15/electric-doors-on-shabbos/ ; https://www.snappyscout.com/borough-park-neighborhood-brooklyn-ny-where-babka-reigns-supreme/
- **Mag locks vs. strikes on Shabbat.** The same source notes that electromagnetic locks use an electric current to hold the door locked, and when the current is temporarily interrupted, the door can be opened. — https://dinonline.org/2026/06/15/electric-doors-on-shabbos/
- **Smart locks and sensors.** A locksmith article explains that motion-sensor lights, automatic door closers, and alarms can also unintentionally activate, and that for homes with smart locks, manufacturers often include a Shabbat mode. Fully mechanical "Shabbos lock" keypad deadbolts are sold widely. — https://driscolllockandkey.com/how-shabbos-friendly-locks-work-a-technical-overview/ ; https://www.amazon.com/shabbos-lock/s?k=shabbos+lock
- **Building types an installer will meet.** Rows of two- and three-family brick houses, older four- to six-family walk-ups, and a steady layer of newer multifamily construction and infill development between Fort Hamilton Parkway and New Utrecht Avenue. — https://yakmanagement.com/areas/borough-park

### 2. Neighborhood background
- **History:** Chinatown emerged during the 1980s, when the first Chinese grocery store in Brooklyn opened in 1983 and a Cantonese-style seafood restaurant opened on 8th Avenue in 1985 — https://www.bkreader.com/lifestyle-culture/brooklyn-chinatown-officially-co-named-in-sunset-park-12938645
- Before that, the avenue was known as "Little Norway", or "Lapskaus Boulevard", and before 1984, there were only about thirty small shops on the entire Eighth Avenue — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinatowns_in_Brooklyn
- The population has shifted from Cantonese to heavily Fujianese since 2000 — https://atlasofhome.com/roots/chinese-in-sunset-park-brooklyn/
- Railway building from the 1890s until the 1920s drove the area's early development — https://www.bkreader.com/neighborhood/neighbhorhood-profile-sunset-park-8566308
- **Recent news:** a ceremony to co-name Brooklyn's Eighth Avenue "Brooklyn Chinatown" was held on Friday at the corner of Eighth Avenue and 60th Street (October 23, 2026). A planned Friendship Archway would be installed across Eighth Avenue between 60th and 61st Streets. However, the plans eventually collapsed. — https://brooklyneagle.com/425030/sunset-park-street-officially-co-named/
- **Main streets:** 8th Avenue is the Chinese corridor, while 5th Avenue boasts many Latin restaurants and shops — https://www.bkreader.com/neighborhood/neighbhorhood-profile-sunset-park-8566308
- The N train stops at the 8th Avenue station, which is near the new projects — https://newyorkyimby.com/2026/05/new-residential-and-mixed-use-developments-coming-to-sunset-park-brooklyn.html
- **Known businesses:** Canton House restaurant (between 55th and 56th Sts), Winley Supermarket (5523 8th Ave), and the Brooklyn Chinese-American Association — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinatowns_in_Brooklyn
- **Historic districts:** Sunset Park North, Sunset Park South, Central Sunset Park and Sunset Park 50th Street were designated June 18, 2019 — https://www.citylandnyc.org/landmarks-designates-sunset-park-historic-districts/
- Central Sunset Park is mostly two-story row houses and small flats buildings built between 1897 and 1907 — https://www.6sqft.com/in-a-historic-stretch-of-sunset-park-this-2m-row-house-is-an-architectural-gem-with-modern-upgrades/
- **Parks:** Sunset Park itself is a 24.5-acre park between 41st and 44th Streets and 5th and 7th Avenues — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sunset_Park_(Brooklyn_park)
- The Sunset Park Recreation Center is a NYC landmark — https://sunsetparkbid.org/discover-5th-avenue/
