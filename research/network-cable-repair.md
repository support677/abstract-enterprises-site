### History and development
- Name: Midwood, derived from the Dutch word, "Midwout" (middle woods), was settled by the Dutch in 1652. The area remained rural until its annexation to the City of Brooklyn in the 1890s. It became more developed in the 1920s, when large middle-class housing tracts and apartment buildings were built. — http://middev.org/our-neighborhood/
- Railroad: The Brooklyn, Flatbush & Coney Island Railroad (1878)—today's Brighton Line—cut diagonally through the woods. In 1894, trolley lines extended along Coney Island Avenue. — https://cityneighborhoods.nyc/midwood
- Growth era: The 1920s–1940s were Midwood's golden age of growth. — https://cityneighborhoods.nyc/midwood
- Who built it: T.B. Ackerson developed Fiske Terrace starting in 1905, and approximately 75 percent of the houses in the Midwood Park part of the district... were constructed by the John R. Corbin Company. — https://www.flatbushgardener.com/2008/03/19/fiske-terrace-midwood-park-historic-district-designated/
- How Ackerson built: In 1905, Ackerson cleared the land, set out streets, installed sewers and water lines, and developed over 150 single-family homes within 18 months. Ackerson built uniform three-story homes in series of ten, using stock plans. — https://www.citylandnyc.org/new-brooklyn-historic-district-to-be-considered/
