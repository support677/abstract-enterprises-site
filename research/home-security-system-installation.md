**Building types and wiring situations**
- Local installers sell to brownstones, townhouses, walk-ups and condos/co-ops, and pitch "masonry-specific anchoring" for brownstone and brick facades. They also warn that wireless security camera installation uses WiFi, which drops through thick walls. — https://www.abstractenterprisessecuritysystems.com/security-camera-installation-brooklyn-ny
- Hybrid wiring is the usual brownstone advice. One installer recommends hardwired perimeter sensors and wireless interior detectors, with a budget of $2,500 to $6,000. — https://www.abstractenterprisessecuritysystems.com/alarm-installation-brooklyn
- The same company's NYC page says wireless motion sensors on upper floors add flexibility without extensive alarm wiring through finished walls. It says the panel reports over cellular, so no phone line is needed, and quotes $2,500 to $5,000 installed for a typical 3-story brownstone. — https://www.abstractenterprisessecuritysystems.com/alarm-installation-nyc
- Camera price, per one vendor: $950 to $2,500 for a 4- to 8-camera system with NVR and professional wiring. — https://www.abstractenterprisessecuritysystems.com/security-camera-installation-brooklyn-ny

One Williamsburg house got a Honeywell alarm tied to an Akuvox intercom and Hikvision cameras, installed in step with a renovation. — https://linkedsecurityny.com/services/alarm-systems-installation-new-york/
- Stoop camera layout advice from a brownstone owners' forum: two cameras mounted on the left and right of the door, up about 6 ft, pointed at the stoop and each other, the third camera above the door pointed down the stairs. — https://ipcamtalk.com/threads/best-layout-setup-practice-for-brownstone-cctv.40722/

**LPC (landmark) rules**
- LPC Rule 2-20 limits cameras to no more than two (2) security cameras for every twenty-five (25) feet of street frontage for small residential or commercial buildings of six (6) stories or less (40 ft of frontage or less). Cameras must also be placed to avoid a cluttered or haphazard appearance. — https://nyc-docs-new-design-test.readthedocs.io/en/latest/d02/c44/
- The rules bar many objects from a primary facade unless there is no feasible alternative or the location is mandated by an agency or entity. — https://www.nyc.gov/assets/lpc/downloads/pdf/LPC%20Rules%20Amendments%20Revisions%20August%209%202018.pdf
- LPC Permit Guidebook Chapter 11 covers security cameras. It shows dome cameras finished to match the facade and asks for color photos of the camera locations. — https://www.nyc.gov/assets/lpc/downloads/pdf/LPCPermitGuidebook%20_Chapter11_HealthSafety.pdf

**Licensing and permits**
- You need a license to install or maintain security or fire alarm systems. This license comes from the New York State Department of State (NYSDOS). — https://nyc-business.nyc.gov/nycbusiness/description/license-to-install-service-or-maintain-security-or-fire-alarm-systems
- DIY exemption: If a purchaser or owner personally installs the alarm system, or has it installed by an employee on his or her own property or place of business, a license is not required. Anyone in the business of installing hard-wired smoke detectors needs a license. — https://dos.ny.gov/frequently-asked-questions-security-or-fire-alarm-installer
- Getting licensed requires at least 81 hours of qualifying education, an exam and fingerprints. — https://statistics.labor.ny.gov/olcny/security-fire-alarm-installer.shtm
- Fire alarm work is different: Fire alarm system installations in New York City require FDNY approval before installation. — https://www.lowvoltagenation.com/posts/new-york-city-new-york-fire-alarm-permit-requirements
- Unverified vendor claim: one site says no permit is needed but all alarm systems in New York City must be registered with the NYPD through the NYC Alarm Registration Program. I couldn't confirm this with an official NYPD source, so check before publishing. — https://lockandtech.com/articles/what-is-a-burglar-alarm-system/

**HPD, 311 and tenant law**
- Local Law 63 of 2021 (Tenant Data Privacy Act): owners of multiple dwellings with keyless entry (fobs, apps, biometrics) must provide tenants with a data retention and privacy policy. — https://www.nyc.gov/site/hpd/services-and-information/tenant-data-privacy-law.page
- The same law says owners may not require a tenant to use a smart access system to gain entry to such tenant's dwelling unit. — https://www.nyc.gov/assets/dcas/downloads/pdf/cityrecord/stated-meetings/2021/stated_meeting_2021_04_29.pdf
- One vendor says entry logs must be destroyed within 90 days, with physical alternatives for tenants who opt out. — https://vertexsecurity.com/our-blog/nyc-building-access-control-compliance/
- Intercoms: under Multiple Dwelling Law §50-a, some buildings need an entrance system that lets a visitor contact a resident and lets that resident release the entrance door without going downstairs. — https://butterflymx.com/blog/nyc-intercom-law/
- A vendor says HPD classifies intercom violations and most door lock violations as Class B hazardous. — https://www.connextivity.com/insights/nyc-intercom-laws-building-requirements
- Example 311/HPD record: one building logged repeated "BELL/BUZZER/INTERCOM – BRKN OR MISSING" and "DOOR – LOCK BRKN/MISS" complaints in 2025–2026. — https://hpdonline.nyc.gov/hpdonline/building/422520/complaints
- Self-closing doors: if one fails, the Department issues a Class C immediately hazardous violation. This matters when adding access hardware. — https://www.nyc.gov/site/hpd/news/079-25/self-closing-doors-save-lives
- Tenant cameras: Tenants can install cameras inside their own apartment without landlord permission. Video doorbells on unit doors are a gray area. — https://www.imperasecurity.com/blog/security-camera-laws-nyc-property-owners
- A wired tenant camera requires drilling and possible electrical work; this needs written permission from the landlord. — https://linkedsecurityny.com/blog/brooklyn-security-camera-system/
