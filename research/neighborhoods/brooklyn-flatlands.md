## Flatlands, Brooklyn — neighborhood background (factory research, 2026-11-11)
- Founded as a farming community in 1636 when Wolfert Gerritse Van Couwenhoven and Andries Hudde acquired about 15,000 acres around today's Kings Highway and Flatbush Avenue; first called Nieuw Amersfoort — https://www.nyc.gov/site/brooklyncb18/about/amersfort-flatbush-flatlands-fraser.page
- 1661: Peter Stuyvesant granted local self-government as one of five Dutch towns on Long Island; 1896: annexed by the City of Brooklyn; growth was slow because the area lacked transit and roads — https://www.nyc.gov/site/brooklyncb18/about/amersfort-flatbush-flatlands-fraser.page
- Flatlands Dutch Reformed Church at 3931 Kings Highway, built as one of three churches Stuyvesant ordered established (1663 per CB18), now a city landmark; present stone church dates from 1848 at Kings Highway and East 40th Street — https://www.nyc.gov/site/brooklyncb18/about/amersfort-flatbush-flatlands-fraser.page ; https://cityneighborhoods.nyc/flatlands
- NYCHA Glenwood Houses built in the early 1950s — https://www.nyc.gov/site/brooklyncb18/about/amersfort-flatbush-flatlands-fraser.page
- Boundaries roughly Nostrand Avenue (west), LIRR Bay Ridge Branch (north), Ralph Avenue (east), Avenue T (south); neat blocks of brick and frame one- and two-family houses built primarily in the 1920s through 1950s; Avenues J, K and L as residential corridors; Flatbush, Ralph and Utica Avenues as commercial edges — https://cityneighborhoods.nyc/flatlands
- After WWII bungalows and semi-detached brick homes filled the last lots; by the 1990s the population was primarily Caribbean American; Flatlands branch of Brooklyn Public Library active since 1941; Amersfort Park and Hendrickson Playground — https://cityneighborhoods.nyc/flatlands
- Main shopping streets Utica Avenue, Flatbush Avenue, Avenue N and Ralph Avenue; Kings Plaza indoor mall on the border with Marine Park and Mill Basin; primary ZIP 11234; Community District 18; NYPD 63rd Precinct; FDNY Engine 309/Ladder 159 at 1851 East 48th Street; no subway inside Flatlands, residents use the 2/5 at Flatbush and Nostrand Avenues and buses — https://www.wikiwand.com/en/articles/Flatlands,_Brooklyn
- Hendrick I. Lott House on East 36th Street, built around 1720 — https://www.wikiwand.com/en/articles/Flatlands,_Brooklyn
- Flatlands Avenue runs from East 35th Street through Flatlands into Canarsie as a shopping street; blocks of detached homes with an occasional shopping strip and apartment building — https://forgotten-ny.com/2020/08/flatlands-avenue-brooklyn/
- Mix of attached, semi-detached and detached houses; single- and two-family homes common, some with driveways and garages — https://www.homes.com/local-guide/brooklyn-ny/flatlands-neighborhood/
- Flatlands/Fairfield is one of the city's Industrial Business Zones in Brooklyn — https://edc.nyc/industry/industrial-and-manufacturing
- Recent local news: News 12 (June 16, 2026) on 1155 East 35th Street, Flatlands — trash, ceiling leaks, no hot water since December, rodents, multiple open HPD complaints, tenants calling 311 — https://brooklyn.news12.com/tenants-complain-of-multiple-issues-at-flatlands-apartment-building
