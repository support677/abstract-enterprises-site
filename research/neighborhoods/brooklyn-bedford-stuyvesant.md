## Bedford Stuyvesant, Brooklyn — neighborhood background (factory research, 2026-11-11)
- Boundaries: Flushing Avenue (north), Classon Avenue (west), Broadway (east), Atlantic Avenue (south); main shopping street Fulton Street, crossed by Bedford, Nostrand and Stuyvesant Avenues — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bedford%E2%80%93Stuyvesant,_Brooklyn
- Contains four smaller neighborhoods: Bedford, Stuyvesant Heights, Ocean Hill and Weeksville; mostly Brooklyn Community District 3; ZIPs 11205, 11206, 11216, 11221, 11233, 11238; 79th and 81st Precincts; population 181,949 (2024) — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bedford%E2%80%93Stuyvesant,_Brooklyn
- Bedford settled by Dutch farmers in the second half of the 17th century (approved by Stuyvesant 1663); part of the City of Brooklyn's 7th and 9th wards from 1834 — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bedford%E2%80%93Stuyvesant,_Brooklyn
- Masonry row houses began in the 1870s; first row 1872 on MacDonough Street for Curtis L. North; brownstones built for the upper-middle class from the 1890s to the late 1910s — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bedford%E2%80%93Stuyvesant,_Brooklyn
- Roughly 8,800 buildings built before 1900, described as the largest collection of intact Victorian architecture in the US — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bedford%E2%80%93Stuyvesant,_Brooklyn
- Transit: LIRR along Atlantic Avenue (1833/1836); IND Fulton Street Line (A/C) opened 1936, replacing the Fulton Street el (closed May 31, 1940); IND Crosstown (G) 1937; BMT Jamaica Line (J/M/Z) along Broadway — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bedford%E2%80%93Stuyvesant,_Brooklyn
- After the 1936 subway, the neighborhood became a major center of Brooklyn's African-American community; gentrification since the early 2000s — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bedford%E2%80%93Stuyvesant,_Brooklyn
- Historic districts: Stuyvesant Heights (National Register 1975, expanded 1996; 577 contributing buildings); Bedford-Stuyvesant/Expanded Stuyvesant Heights HD designated April 16, 2013 (~825 buildings, Halsey/Macon to Fulton, Tompkins to Malcolm X); Bedford HD Dec 8, 2015; Alice and Agate Courts Feb 10, 2009 (36 Queen Anne row houses) — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bedford%E2%80%93Stuyvesant,_Brooklyn ; https://www.citylandnyc.org/bed-stuyexpanded-stuyvesant-heights-historic-district-designated-capping-decades-long-process/
- Bedford Historic District: about 800 buildings, mostly 1870–1900 rowhouses and small apartment buildings in Queen Anne, Italianate, neo-Grec and Renaissance Revival styles, roughly between Bedford, Nostrand, Putnam, Tompkins and Marcy Avenues and Monroe, Fulton and Halsey Streets — https://citylandnyc.org/bedford-historic-district-unanimously-designated
- LPC LP-2496 (Expanded Stuyvesant Heights): row houses with shared first-floor entrances through a vestibule into a hallway; round-headed double-leaf doors; areaways and raised basements; 1890s French flats of four to five stories — https://s-media.nyc.gov/agencies/lpc/lp/2496.pdf
- Landmarks: Herbert Von King Park (opened 1871 as Tompkins Park); Weeksville Heritage Center; Restoration Plaza (Bedford Stuyvesant Restoration Corporation, founded 1967, in the former Sheffield Farms plant); Macon Library on Lewis Avenue; Boys and Girls High School on Fulton Street — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bedford%E2%80%93Stuyvesant,_Brooklyn ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bedford_Stuyvesant_Restoration_Corporation
- NYCHA: Marcy Houses (1949, 27 six-story buildings, 1,705 apartments); Sumner Houses (1958, 13 buildings) — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marcy_Houses ; https://www.nyc.gov/assets/nycha/downloads/pdf/sumner-factsheet-en.pdf
