The Neighborhood

Why Mount Hope's Rebuilt Walk-Ups, New Towers and Tremont Avenue Storefronts Need Fire Alarms That Keep Up With the Building

Mount Hope is a small, steep, crowded piece of the west Bronx. CityNeighborhoods.nyc draws it between the Cross Bronx Expressway on the south and East Burnside Avenue on the north, with Jerome Avenue on the west and Webster Avenue on the east, and Bronx Community Board 5 counts it among the neighborhoods of Community District 5, alongside Morris Heights, University Heights and South Fordham. NYC Parks describes Mount Hope as centered on Tremont Avenue and part of the old village of Tremont, which a postmaster named Hiram Tarbox named in the 1850s for three hills: Fairmount, Mount Eden and Mount Hope.

That high ground filled with five- and six-story apartment houses after the subway arrived. CityNeighborhoods.nyc describes them along Tremont, Burnside and Mount Hope Place with Art Deco, neo-Renaissance and Tudor Revival fronts. The same source records what came next: the Cross Bronx Expressway, built between 1948 and 1972, cut through the southern edge, and the 1970s brought a wave of arson and abandonment. In 1986 residents and local leaders founded the Mount Hope Housing Company to rehabilitate the damaged tenements and build affordable housing.

Forty years later, that rebuilt stock is being rebuilt again with people living in it. In January 2019 The Real Deal reported that the Mount Hope Housing Company and two development partners had closed financing to renovate 13 affordable buildings with 515 apartments between East 175th Street and East Burnside Avenue, with kitchen and bathroom renovations and energy work starting that spring. GlobeSt reported that the buildings would stay under a 40-year regulatory agreement with the city's housing department, and the project's counsel counted about 497 apartments and roughly 9,000 square feet of commercial space. Either way, it is about 500 households and a dozen-plus fire alarm panels under one owner. "We are steadfast in our commitment to meeting the needs of our community," the company's CEO, Fritz Jean, told The Real Deal.

The neighborhood is also getting taller. On July 20, 2022, the city and state broke ground on Mount Hope Walton Apartments at 1761 Walton Avenue, next to the existing Mount Hope Community Center: 103 apartments, 16 of them for families leaving homelessness, over a 9,500-square-foot community gym with a fitness center and a regulation-size basketball court for high school games (NYC HPD). The New York Housing Conference lists the building at 14 stories with two elevators, designed by Aufgang Architects, with the gym reached through the community center next door. "Today's groundbreaking marks the realization of a dream 20 years in the making," Fritz Jean said at the ceremony.

And the side streets are next. New York YIMBY has reported permit filings for a 15-story, 145-foot building with 99 apartments at 23 Mount Hope Place and a 15-story, 150-foot building at 29 Mount Hope Place, both between Jerome and Walton Avenues near the 176th Street station on the 4 line, the second replacing a two-story house. On Morris Avenue between East Tremont Avenue and East 177th Street, a filing calls for a 12-story, 124-foot building with 99 new apartments on a lot that will hold 154 residences in all. Blocks that have topped out at six stories for a century are about to get buildings that count as high-rises.

The neighborhood's fire history explains why the details matter. On January 23, 2005, the day the FDNY calls Black Sunday, fire spread through a tenement on East 178th Street where apartments had been illegally divided with drywall partitions; six firefighters trapped on the top floor jumped, and Lieutenant Curtis Meyran and Firefighter John Bellew were killed (Wikipedia; DNAinfo, which files the building under Mount Hope). More recently, on October 4, 2024, nearly 60 firefighters fought a fire that started on the fifth floor of a building on Weeks Avenue between East 175th Street and the Cross Bronx Expressway, and one person was hospitalized in serious condition (News 12 Bronx). Neither report blamed a fire alarm; both describe the building type that fills Mount Hope.

This page covers commercial and code-required fire alarm systems only: the building systems in apartment buildings, mixed-use buildings, storefronts, schools, community spaces and new construction. Smoke alarms inside individual one- and two-family homes follow separate rules and are not part of this service.

Building Types

Commercial Fire Alarm Systems for Mount Hope's Building Types, From Bow-Front Tenements to 150-Foot Towers

Commercial fire alarm systems are triggered by what a building is used for and how big it is. These are the building types that bring Mount Hope owners, boards and managers to a fire alarm design, with the point that matters most in each one.

Five- and six-story prewar apartment houses

Most of Mount Hope's housing is here. Under the NYC Building Code, as NY Engineers summarizes it, an R-2 apartment building needs a fire alarm when dwelling units sit three or more stories above the lowest level of exit discharge or the building has 16 or more units, which describes nearly every walk-up on Walton, Morris and Ryer Avenues. Many were rehabilitated after the losses of the 1970s and renovated again since, so a panel may be decades old, its parts may be hard to find, and its drawings may not match what is on the wall.

Buildings in a preservation portfolio

The 13 buildings in the Mount Hope Housing Company's 2019 renovation share an owner, a regulatory agreement and a management office, but each has its own fire alarm, its own approval history and often a different panel brand. A renovation that rebuilds kitchens and bathrooms with tenants in place opens ceilings in apartments and hallways where fire alarm cable runs, so the fire alarm work has to be planned with the renovation, not after it.

The new 14-story building over a gym

Mount Hope Walton Apartments puts 103 homes over a 9,500-square-foot community gym with a regulation court for high school games (NYC HPD). The apartments are an R-2 use, the gym is an assembly room, and the NY Engineers summary puts the assembly trigger for a fire alarm at an occupant load of 300. A full gym on game night and a quiet apartment floor at 3 a.m. need different things from the same system.

12- to 15-story concrete towers on side streets

The filings at 23 and 29 Mount Hope Place (15 stories, 145 and 150 feet) and 1917 Morris Avenue (12 stories, 124 feet) are well past the 75-foot line where the International Building Code, on which the city's code is based, starts treating a building as a high-rise (National Fire Sprinkler Association). That brings floor-by-floor alarm zoning, voice communication and a fire command location into the design from day one, on narrow streets where FDNY apparatus access and the response entrance need thought.

Schools and community spaces

Bronx Community Board 5 lists P.S. 28 Mount Hope on Anthony Avenue, I.S. 117 Joseph H. Wade on Morris Avenue and St. Margaret Mary School on East 177th Street among the area's institutions. The NY Engineers summary of the code says every Group E educational occupancy needs a fire alarm, whatever its size. City school buildings are designed and maintained through the city's school system; private, parochial and charter schools in leased space, and the after-school programs in community centers, are where owners and operators call a contractor.

The Morris Avenue Historic District

Designated on July 15, 1986, the district preserves buildings that John Hauser designed in five campaigns between 1906 and 1910, mostly three stories with curved bow fronts (Historic Districts Council). The district includes two-family row houses, which are outside this service, and tenements, where a building system may be required depending on the units and stories. Exterior work in a historic district, like a strobe on a facade or a new conduit on the front, is reviewed by the Landmarks Preservation Commission; most fire alarm work stays inside.

Field Notes

Field Notes: Walk the Walls Before You Trust the Drawings

The first thing our technicians do in an older Mount Hope building is compare the rooms on the fire alarm drawings with the rooms that actually exist. Rehabilitated buildings have been renovated more than once since their systems were approved, and walls move. A hallway detector that once covered an open area may now sit on the wrong side of a partition, a storage room may have become an office, and a cellar may have gained a laundry. The Black Sunday fire on East 178th Street is the starkest reminder that a building's real layout can differ from the one on paper.

The second habit is to follow the cable during a renovation, not after it. When contractors rebuild kitchens and bathrooms with tenants at home, fire alarm wire in hallway ceilings gets cut, spliced and buried. We ask the general contractor for a walkthrough before ceilings close, and we test the circuits again before the painters arrive, because finding a fault after the plaster dries means opening it up again.

The third is to ask who will be standing at the panel when it goes off. In a building with a gym full of spectators, a store on the ground floor or a lobby desk that is staffed only part of the day, the answer is often unclear until someone writes it down. We don't discuss specific clients' buildings on this page.

Your Questions Answered

Mount Hope Fire Alarm Questions From Owners, Developers, Store Owners and Program Directors

Renovations and portfolios

The Mount Hope Housing Company's 2019 renovation rebuilt kitchens and bathrooms in 13 occupied buildings. When work like that opens hallway and apartment ceilings, does the fire alarm have to be brought up to today's code?

Not automatically. A fire alarm approved under an earlier code can stay as it is while it is maintained, and a kitchen and bathroom job doesn't by itself require a new system. What changes things is the scope: if the renovation alters the fire alarm, adds devices, moves rooms, changes how a space is used or is large enough that the Department of Buildings treats it as a major alteration, the fire alarm work has to be designed, filed and approved like any other. Settle it with the design professional before demolition starts; a renovation that cuts fire alarm cable without a fire alarm scope leaves a damaged system and no approved path to fix it.

A nonprofit owner in Mount Hope may run a dozen buildings with a dozen different panels. Can the monitoring, testing and repairs for all of them be put on one schedule?

Yes, and for a portfolio it is the most useful thing an owner can do. Each building keeps its own approved system, its own records and its own central station account, but the inspection and testing dates, battery replacement cycles, open FDNY items and service history can live in one list. Sorting the buildings by panel brand also shows which panels are old enough that parts are getting scarce. A fire alarm service contract that covers several buildings works best when it names each building's panel, communicator and required tests separately rather than as a lump.

Black Sunday in 2005 happened in an East 178th Street tenement whose apartments had been split up with drywall partitions. If walls are added in a space covered by a building fire alarm, what has to happen to the devices?

The devices have to follow the walls. A smoke detector covers the room it is in, so a new partition can leave a section of a hallway, office or common room with no detection and can block a strobe from view. Any change to the layout of a space covered by the building's system should go back to the design professional, who decides whether devices are added or moved and whether the change has to be filed with the FDNY. Illegal partitions are a far more serious problem, as the Black Sunday fire showed, and no fire alarm fixes one.

The building on Walton Avenue

Mount Hope Walton Apartments puts 103 homes over a gym with a regulation court for high school games. How does one fire alarm serve a packed gym and the apartments upstairs?

It treats them as separate areas with different needs. The gym is an assembly space: during a game it holds a crowd that doesn't know the building, under a high ceiling, with loud noise, so notification has to be heard and seen over the crowd, and the alarm should shut off the gym's music or announcement system so the fire signal isn't drowned out. The apartments upstairs are a residential use with people asleep at night. The design professional zones the system so that the panel and the FDNY can tell exactly where an alarm started, and decides which areas get notification first based on the building's code requirements.

The gym at 1761 Walton Avenue is reached through the existing Mount Hope Community Center next door. When two buildings are joined by a doorway, does an alarm in one set off the other?

Only if the drawings say so. Two buildings can each have their own fire alarm and still share an opening, and the opening itself is usually protected by a fire-rated door that closes on alarm, often held open by a magnetic door holder that the fire alarm releases. Whether an alarm in the community center also sounds in the gym, or only reports to the other building's panel, is a design decision based on how the spaces are separated and used.

The new towers

The filings at 23 and 29 Mount Hope Place call for 15 stories and about 150 feet on a short block between Jerome and Walton Avenues. What changes in the fire alarm when a side-street building is that tall?

Almost everything about the design. Once an occupied floor is more than 75 feet above the lowest level of fire department vehicle access, the building is a high-rise under the model code definition, and high-rise rules bring automatic smoke detection, an emergency voice and alarm communication system, and a fire command location where firefighters take control. On a narrow block the measurement point matters: height is counted from where a fire truck can actually park, and the FDNY response entrance, the fire command location and the main annunciator all have to line up with that entrance.

The 12-story building filed for 1917 Morris Avenue will add 99 apartments to a lot that will hold 154 homes in all. When a new building shares a lot with an existing one, do the two share a fire alarm?

Usually not, but the drawings have to show how they relate. A new 12-story building will get its own new system designed to the current code, while the existing building keeps the system it was approved with. Where they connect, through a shared cellar, a courtyard door or common mechanical equipment, the design professional decides whether signals pass between the two panels and how the FDNY will see each building. If the existing building's system is going to be modified at the same time, its existing approval letter has to be part of the filing, because the FDNY reviews additions against what was already approved.

A building permit can be issued for a 15-story concrete tower long before anyone moves in. When does its permanent fire alarm have to be working?

Before people occupy it. During construction, fire safety on site runs on the contractor's construction fire safety plan, not on the permanent system. The permanent fire alarm is installed as floors are finished, tested by the installer, and then presented to the FDNY for acceptance with central station monitoring active, permanent power and the sprinkler system working, as the FDNY's own checklist requires. A temporary certificate of occupancy for lower floors, if the developer seeks one, depends on the fire alarm serving those floors having passed.

Storefronts, schools and historic buildings

A Burnside Avenue storefront under apartments is turning into a bakery with ovens. Which fire alarm devices change when a store starts cooking?

Smoke detectors near the ovens are usually swapped for heat detectors, because baking and frying produce enough smoke and steam to set off ordinary detection. If the kitchen has a hood with a suppression system, the fire alarm normally monitors it so that a discharge reports to the panel and the central station. And because the store sits under apartments, its devices may report to the building's panel rather than a system of its own, so the store owner, the landlord and the building's fire alarm company have to agree on the work before the oven goes in.

The October 2024 fire on Weeks Avenue started on a fifth floor. In a five- or six-story Mount Hope apartment building, what does the building fire alarm actually cover?

It depends on how the building was approved, but a building fire alarm typically covers the public parts of the building: hallways, stair landings, the cellar, boiler and meter rooms, the compactor or trash room and the elevator machine room, with manual pull stations and notification in the common areas. Smoke and carbon monoxide alarms inside apartments are usually separate devices required under housing rules, and they don't necessarily report to the building panel. The approved drawings and the panel's device list show exactly what your system covers.

I.S. 117 on Morris Avenue and P.S. 28 on Anthony Avenue are city public schools. Who does fire alarm work for a charter school or private school that leases space in a Mount Hope building?

For a city-owned public school building, the fire alarm is designed, installed and maintained through the city's school system and its contractors. A school leasing space in a privately owned building is different: the landlord and the school decide in the lease who pays for and maintains the fire alarm, and the school's space must meet the code for an educational use, which in New York City means a fire alarm in every Group E occupancy.

Do strobes or a new panel in one of John Hauser's buildings in the Morris Avenue Historic District need approval from the Landmarks Preservation Commission?

Work on the outside of a building in a historic district, such as a strobe, a horn, conduit or a key box on the facade, is reviewed by the Landmarks Preservation Commission before it goes up. Interior fire alarm work in an ordinary apartment building usually is not, though the LPC can also review interior work that needs a Department of Buildings permit. The FDNY approval and any landmarks review are separate, and both have to be in place.

Equipment

Types of Commercial Fire Alarm Systems We Install in Mount Hope

What is a commercial fire alarm system? It is a supervised network of detectors, pull stations, horns, strobes and speakers wired to a control panel that watches every circuit, sounds the alarm, runs the building's safety functions and reports to a central station. How does a commercial fire alarm system work? A device senses smoke, heat or a pulled handle, the panel confirms the signal, notification starts, elevators, fans and doors respond, and the central station passes the alarm to the FDNY. These are the system types we install in Mount Hope buildings.

Addressable

What is an addressable fire alarm system? Every device has its own address, so the panel says "4th floor, east hall" or "gym, north wall" rather than just a zone. Addressable fire alarm installation, addressable fire alarm system installation and addressable fire alarm panel installation are the standard for new Mount Hope towers and most replacements.

Conventional

What is a conventional fire alarm system? Devices are wired in zones, and the panel knows only which zone is in alarm. Conventional fire alarm installation, conventional fire alarm system installation and conventional fire alarm panel installation still suit a single storefront or a small building with a few zones.

The difference

What is the difference between addressable and conventional fire alarms? Addressable systems name the exact device and report a dirty detector before it causes a false alarm; conventional systems name a zone, so finding the problem takes longer. Conventional to addressable fire alarm conversion is the most common upgrade in the neighborhood's rehabbed walk-ups.

Voice evacuation

Fire alarm voice evacuation installation and fire alarm emergency voice communication for towers and for the gym, with fire alarm speaker installation and fire alarm speaker strobe installation so messages can tell residents and spectators what to do instead of only sounding a horn.

Intelligent detection

Intelligent fire alarm installation and an intelligent fire detection system use detectors that adjust for slow drift and report when they need cleaning, which cuts down on nuisance alarms near laundry rooms, boiler rooms and ground-floor kitchens.

Air sampling and beams

Aspirating smoke detection installation, an air sampling smoke detection system, VESDA installation and VESDA service for tall spaces and equipment rooms, and beam smoke detector installation or projected beam smoke detector installation across a gym ceiling where spot detectors are hard to reach.

Mass notification goes further than a building alarm: fire alarm mass notification integration lets one system deliver fire and non-fire messages, which suits a building that shares space with a community center. Data center fire detection installation for a server or network room follows the same detection choices as air sampling, sized to the room.

Devices

Fire Alarm Devices in Mount Hope Buildings and Where Each One Goes

Fire alarm equipment installation is a series of choices about which device belongs in which room. Fire detection device installation and fire alarm initiating device installation cover everything that starts an alarm; notification covers everything that warns people. Here is how those choices play out in Mount Hope's buildings.

Smoke detectors

What should a superintendent write down when the panel shows a trouble signal?
+

The date and time, the exact words on the display, the device or zone named, what was going on in the building at the time (a leak, a renovation, a power outage) and who was called. That record shortens the repair visit and belongs with the building's fire alarm records.

Why does the FDNY want the Certificate of Occupancy before it reviews fire alarm drawings?
+

The Certificate of Occupancy, or an approved Schedule A, tells the plan examiner how the building is legally used, floor by floor, and that use decides what the fire alarm must do. If neither is available, the FDNY's guide says to get a Letter of No Objection from the Department of Buildings first.

Can a fire alarm panel be moved from the cellar to the lobby during a renovation?
+

Often, and it can be worth doing, because firefighters should find the panel or an annunciator at the entrance they use. Moving it is a change to the approved system, so it is designed, filed and tested like any modification, and the old location's wiring has to be rerouted or extended.

What does supervisory mean on the panel in a sprinklered Mount Hope building?
+

A supervisory signal means something that protects the building is not in its normal state, most often a closed sprinkler control valve or a tripped duct detector. It isn't a fire, but it needs attention the same day, because a closed valve can leave floors without sprinkler protection.

In a 14-story building, which floor do the elevators go to if a smoke detector in the main lobby trips?
+

They go to an alternate floor chosen in the design, not the lobby where the smoke is. If a detector anywhere else in the recall group trips, the cars return to the main lobby. Firefighters then take control with a key, and the sequence is proved during the acceptance test.

How should a store owner under apartments report a problem with the fire alarm devices in the store?
+

Call the building's manager or superintendent first, because the devices in your store may belong to the building's system and be serviced by its fire alarm company. Don't hire your own technician to work on them unless the landlord agrees, and keep a copy of whatever was done.

Can our new tower's fire alarm be monitored by the same central station that monitors our older buildings?
+

Yes, as long as the central station is FDNY-approved and the new system's communicator is compatible with it. Owners with several buildings often prefer one central station so alarm and trouble calls follow the same procedure everywhere. The central station agreement is one of the documents the FDNY asks for at the acceptance test.

When a Mount Hope building is refinanced or changes hands, which fire alarm papers should the new owner ask for?
+

The FDNY Letter of Approval, the approved drawings and as-built riser diagram, the last few years of inspection and test reports, the central station agreement and any open FDNY defect notices or violations. If the approval letter or drawings can't be found, start a records search early, because any later change to the system will be reviewed against them.
