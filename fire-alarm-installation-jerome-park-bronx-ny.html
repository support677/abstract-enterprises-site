The Neighborhood

Why Jerome Park Buildings Need Fire Alarm Installation Planned Around Classrooms and Towers

Jerome Park takes its name from Leonard W. Jerome, the financier who built a racetrack here with August Belmont. The track opened on September 25, 1866, ran the Belmont Stakes from 1867, and was condemned by the city in 1890 so the land could become a reservoir for the New Croton Aqueduct (NYC Parks; Bronx Community Board 7). The Jerome Park Reservoir was finished in 1906. It covers 94 acres inside stone walls about two miles around and holds 773 million gallons (Bronx Community Board 7). It was designed with more than one basin, but only the western half was ever built; a planned eastern basin of 1.13 billion gallons was dug and never filled, which is why today's reservoir is about half its intended size (NYC Parks; The Science Survey).

That accident of engineering explains the neighborhood. Along Goulden Avenue, from Reservoir Avenue north to Mosholu Parkway South, sits what Community Board 7 calls the Education Mile: the Walton campus, Lehman College, the Bronx High School of Science, the High School for American Studies and DeWitt Clinton High School (Bronx Community Board 7). The Bronx High School of Science alone had 2,900 students enrolled in June 2026, according to a school staffer quoted by Norwood News. Lehman College's facilities office manages about 1.6 million gross square feet across eighteen buildings (Lehman College, Campus Planning and Facilities). Few residential neighborhoods in the city put that many students, teachers and staff on so few blocks, and every high school among them is an educational occupancy, a class of building that always requires a fire alarm (NY Engineers, summary of NYC Building Code 907).

The second reason is the housing. At the north end of the neighborhood, Tracey Towers stands at 20 and 40 West Mosholu Parkway: two concrete towers of 38 and 41 stories, 871 apartments, designed by Paul Rudolph, completed in 1972 and opened in 1974 as Mitchell-Lama housing, built over the subway yard where 4 train cars are stored (Wikipedia, Tracey Towers). When it opened it was the tallest building in the Bronx at 400 feet. A few blocks south, Scott Towers at 3400 Paul Avenue is a 21-story cooperative of 352 apartments built in 1967 (Homes.com). Around them, the five- and six-story Art Deco and Tudor Revival apartment houses that followed the 1917 subway extension still line the side streets (CityNeighborhoods.nyc). In the census tracts City-Data maps as Jerome Park, 59.4 percent of homes are in buildings of five or more units, and the largest age group is housing built in 1939 or earlier (City-Data).

The third reason is that the big buildings are almost always being worked on. Lehman opened a 52,000-square-foot Nursing Education, Research and Practice Center in February 2024 (Bronx Times), started replacing the air handling units in Shuster Hall, an occupied building of more than 90,000 square feet, in November 2025 (ConstructConnect project listing), and has requested design funding for a 10-story glass tower at Goulden Avenue and Bedford Park Boulevard West, the second phase of its science complex, with completion anticipated in spring 2029 (Lehman College, Capital Projects). Tracey Towers residents rallied in June 2024 over leaks and elevators that had malfunctioned for several years (Norwood News, August 12, 2024). Every one of those projects touches the fire alarm: new air handlers bring duct detectors and shutdown relays, a new building brings a new system, and a failing elevator bank brings the recall interface back into question.

This page covers commercial and code-required fire alarm systems only: building-wide systems in schools, college buildings, apartment towers, mixed-use buildings, stores, offices and community spaces. Smoke alarms inside individual houses follow separate rules and are not part of this service.

Building Specialists

Commercial Fire Alarm Systems for Jerome Park's Architecture, From the Walton Campus to Tracey Towers

Commercial fire alarm systems in Jerome Park are shaped by who uses a building and when. Here is how we approach each building type common in the neighborhood; the sealed drawings always decide the details.

Multi-school campus buildings (the Walton campus)

The former Walton High School building at 2780 Reservoir Avenue was built in 1930, held about 6,000 students by 1933, closed as a single school in June 2008, and now houses the Walton Educational Campus: the High School for Teaching and the Professions, Discovery High School, the Celia Cruz Bronx High School of Music, Kingsbridge International High School and the International School for Liberal Arts (Wikipedia, Walton High School). One building, one fire alarm system, five schools with five principals. The design question is not only where the devices go but how an alarm is announced and answered when the floor in alarm belongs to one school and the panel sits near another school's main office. We help the design professional and the campus write an alarm-response plan that matches the zones on the panel to the schools that occupy them, and we label the panel and annunciators by floor and wing rather than by school name, because schools move between floors over the years and the building does not.

Large single-school buildings on the Education Mile

The Bronx High School of Science and DeWitt Clinton High School are big public school buildings whose fire alarm work is planned through the city's school construction and facilities system rather than hired by a principal. With 2,900 students enrolled at Bronx Science in June 2026 (Norwood News), a full-building evacuation fills the sidewalks: when a threatening voicemail forced an evacuation that June, hundreds of students waited on Paul Avenue and outside DeWitt Clinton High School (Norwood News). For buildings like these, notification is designed for long corridors, gyms, auditoriums and cafeterias, and school fire alarm installation work is staged by floor and wing so the rest of the building stays protected and in session.

College classroom, lab and office buildings

Lehman College's buildings run from older classroom halls to new construction. The Nursing Education, Research and Practice Center at 2800 Goulden Avenue, at West 197th Street, was filed in 2018 at 51,670 square feet and about 60 feet tall (New York YIMBY) and opened in February 2024 with a 20-bed nursing skills lab, research labs and maternity, pediatric, ICU and medical-surgical wards with 22 simulators (Bronx Times). Classrooms for students above the 12th grade are a business occupancy under the building code, not an educational one (NY Engineers, summary of NYC Building Code 907), so a college building's fire alarm triggers and features follow different rules from the high school across the street. Lab floors, simulation wards and clinical training rooms each get detection chosen for what happens in them.

Occupied buildings under mechanical renovation

The Shuster Hall project at 2900 Goulden Avenue replaces air handling units in an occupied building of more than 90,000 square feet, with new controls tied to the campus building management system, a start date of November 10, 2025 and a contract term running to January 30, 2028 (ConstructConnect). A separate request for qualifications covers a unit ventilator system replacement on the campus (ConstructConnect). Mechanical replacements like these are where fire alarm problems are born: duct smoke detectors are removed and reinstalled, fan shutdown relays are rewired, and a building runs for weeks with part of its sequence of operations disconnected. We work alongside the mechanical contractor so every detector and relay that comes off is logged, bypassed only with the FDNY notification and fire watch the situation calls for, reconnected and retested before the area is handed back.

Concrete towers over the subway yard

Tracey Towers is two concrete towers of 38 and 41 stories with 871 apartments, built over Jerome Yard, where the 4 train's rolling stock is stored beneath the parking lot, partly on air rights bought from neighboring properties (Wikipedia, Tracey Towers). At 400 feet it was the tallest building in the Bronx when completed in 1972. A tower like this is a high-rise by any measure, and its fire alarm is a life-safety network: emergency voice/alarm communication, firefighter communication, a fire command center in the main entrance lobby (NYC Building Code 911), booster power supplies on many floors, and elevator recall on every bank.

1960s cooperative towers

Scott Towers at 3400 Paul Avenue is a 21-story, 352-unit co-op built in 1967 (Homes.com). Buildings of that age often carry a fire alarm system that has been patched over decades: a replaced panel here, a new communicator there, and devices from several manufacturers on the same circuits. For a co-op board, the useful first step is a written survey that separates what the code requires today from what was installed voluntarily, and that says which parts can be kept in a fire alarm system upgrade and which must be replaced.

Prewar apartment houses on the side streets

The five- and six-story Art Deco and Tudor Revival apartment houses that followed the 1917 Jerome Avenue subway extension (CityNeighborhoods.nyc) are the oldest large buildings here after the reservoir itself. Our first job is to read the building's records and tell the owner which rules apply. When a system is required or chosen, fire alarm wiring installation follows existing paths first: the old riser, the stair, a dead shaft, the hallway ceiling, so apartments stay closed.

Commercial Fire Alarm

Business Fire Alarm Installation on Jerome Avenue, Bedford Park Boulevard West and Kingsbridge Road

Jerome Park's commercial life is thin compared with its institutions, and it sits on a few streets: Jerome Avenue under the elevated 4 train, Bedford Park Boulevard near the Lehman College station, and Kingsbridge Road at the southern edge (Homes.com). Jerome Avenue itself began in 1874 as a plank road called Central Avenue, built to carry racing crowds from the Central Bridge to the track, and was widened into a boulevard in 1888 (NYC Parks).

Business fire alarm installation on these streets usually means one of four jobs. A storefront under apartments may need its devices tied into the building's system, or a separate system that reports to it, as the drawings direct. A restaurant fire alarm installation adds heat detection in the kitchen and kitchen hood suppression monitoring. An office or medical office fire alarm installation inside a mixed-use building is filed as a tenant fit-out on top of the base building, and an office building fire alarm installation on an upper floor follows the same route. And a ground-floor space changing from one use to another, such as a store becoming a tutoring center or a clinic, is a change of use the design professional has to file correctly before devices go up. Business fire detection systems and commercial fire detection systems in these spaces are sized to the use, not to square footage alone.

Compliance callouts for stores, offices and program spaces

  • Design comes first. A New York State registered design professional prepares the plans and files them with a TM-1 application; installation is by a NYC licensed electrician or a fire alarm company holding an FDNY Fire Alarm Company Certificate (FDNY, Tips for Fire Alarm Systems).
  • Certificates can be checked. At least one principal of a certified fire alarm company holds an S-97 certificate of fitness, which requires NICET Level II or higher or a NYC master or special electrician license (FDNY, Fire Alarm Company Certificate FAQ).
  • Fees are public. The FDNY charges $420 for a new fire alarm application and $525 more where complex technical analysis is needed, and work cannot begin until a Project Authorization is issued (NYC Business).
  • Monitoring comes before inspection. The central station connection has to be active before the FDNY schedules the fire alarm inspection (FDNY, Tips for Fire Alarm Systems).
  • Business occupancies have thresholds. A sprinklered business occupancy needs a fire alarm once it reaches 500 occupants across all floors or more than 100 above or below the lowest level of exit discharge (NY Engineers, summary of NYC Building Code 907), which is why a busy office floor or a college building crosses the line where a small shop does not.
  • Visible alarms count. Fire alarm ADA strobe installation in public and common spaces is part of the design wherever the code calls for it, including classrooms, lecture halls and shared restrooms.
Field Notes

Field Notes: The Bell Schedule Is Part of the Drawings

On a school or campus job, the first meeting is never only about devices. It is about time. A school below college level in New York has to run twelve drills a year, eight of them between September 1 and December 31 (NYS Education Law 807). A college has to run at least three. Add testing days, exam weeks, evening classes and summer programs, and the number of hours when a contractor can safely take part of a fire alarm out of service gets small very quickly.

So on a building with several schools inside, we ask for every school's calendar before we write the work plan, not after. Panel cutovers go on days with no drill scheduled. Circuits are swapped one wing at a time so the rest of the building still sounds. Any device we have to leave disconnected overnight is listed on a sheet the custodian engineer keeps, with the fire watch or FDNY notice that goes with it. And the first drill after a new panel goes in is one we like to watch, because it is the first time five schools hear the new tone and read the new screen together.

Your Questions Answered

Jerome Park Fire Alarm Questions From Principals, Facilities Offices, Boards and Owners

Questions we expect from school leaders, campus facilities staff, co-op boards, Mitchell-Lama managers and storefront owners in Jerome Park.

Schools and drills

Eight of our twelve required drills fall between September and December. How do we replace a school panel without losing drill days?

By putting the drill calendar into the work plan before the contract is signed. NYS Education Law 807 requires at least twelve drills a year in schools below college level, eight of them between September 1 and December 31, so the fall is the worst time to have a panel half installed. We schedule the cutover itself for a break or a long weekend, keep the old panel live until the new one has been pre-tested, and swap circuits wing by wing so that on any school day the building can still sound a full evacuation.

When the alarm sounds in a building shared by five schools, does the whole building leave or only the school in alarm?

In almost every school building, the whole building leaves. A general alarm sounds throughout, because fire and smoke do not respect which principal runs which floor. What the campus decides is how each school's staff confirm their rooms are empty and where each school assembles outside, so that five schools do not end up on the same sidewalk. The fire alarm supports that plan with clear zone labels by floor and wing and, where the design includes voice, with messages the staff have heard before the day they matter.

Students evacuated for a threat in June gathered on Paul Avenue. Should a fire alarm ever be used to empty a school for something that is not a fire?

Not as a habit. Norwood News reported that a threatening voicemail led the police to evacuate the Bronx High School of Science on June 12, 2026, and the article does not say how the building was cleared. A fire alarm is a fire signal; using it for other emergencies teaches people that the tone can mean anything. Schools and campuses that want one system for many kinds of messages ask for fire alarm mass notification integration, where non-fire messages are designed, recorded and prioritized so a real fire signal always overrides them. That is a design decision for the engineer and the school system, not a setting a technician changes on the panel.

College buildings

Is a college classroom building treated like the high school across Goulden Avenue when the engineer designs its fire alarm?

No. The building code classes education above the 12th grade as a business occupancy, while schools through the 12th grade are educational occupancies, and every educational occupancy requires a fire alarm (NY Engineers, summary of NYC Building Code 907). A college building's alarm is triggered by its occupant load, sprinklers and height instead, and it usually has to suit offices, labs and lecture halls at once. Colleges also run fewer required drills: at least three a year under Education Law 807, against twelve for a high school.

Our building's air handlers are being replaced while classes continue. What happens to the duct detectors and the fan shutdown sequence during the job?

They come off and go back on in a controlled order. The duct smoke detectors on each unit, and the relays that shut fans down on alarm, are part of the fire alarm's sequence of operations. When the mechanical contractor removes a unit, we disconnect and log its devices, the panel shows the expected trouble, and the facilities office makes any FDNY notification and fire watch the outage calls for. When the new unit is set, its duct detector is mounted to the new housing as the drawings show, rewired, tested in front of the facilities staff and recorded. If the new units add capacity or change the shutdown list, the change may need its own filing.

Our campus project is being run by a state construction agency. Who signs the fire alarm filing: the college, the agency or the contractor?

The design professional files the TM-1 with the FDNY, and the owner of record and the project team are named on it; the installer is a NYC licensed electrician or an FDNY-certified fire alarm company (FDNY, Tips for Fire Alarm Systems). On a public campus job, the agency's contract decides which firm is the engineer of record and which contractor installs, and the campus facilities office usually carries the system after acceptance. Ask at kickoff who will hold the Letter of Approval and the as-built drawings.

Towers

Our tower sits on top of a subway yard. Does anything in the train yard below report to our building's fire alarm?

Not unless the drawings say so. Tracey Towers was built over Jerome Yard, where 4 train cars are stored under the parking lot (Wikipedia, Tracey Towers), and the transit facility below is a separate property with its own systems and its own operator. Your building's fire alarm covers your building: apartments, corridors, lobbies, the garage and the spaces you control. Where the two meet, such as a shared wall, a vent or a door between the parking level and the yard, the engineer shows who protects what, and we do not connect devices across that line without written direction.

One of our tower's elevators has been out of service for months. How does elevator recall get tested at the annual inspection?

The working cars are tested and the out-of-service car is written up. Fire alarm elevator recall integration sends each car to its designated level when a lobby, machine room or hoistway detector activates, and NFPA 72 testing proves that on every car that runs. A car shut down for repairs cannot be recalled, so the report lists it as untested with the reason. When the elevator contractor returns it to service, the recall should be retested with the elevator mechanic present before residents use it again. Long elevator outages, like the ones Tracey Towers residents protested in 2024 (Norwood News), are exactly when that retest gets forgotten.

Our co-op tower was built in 1967. Do today's high-rise fire alarm rules apply to us, or only to new buildings?

Mostly the rules that applied when the building was built and approved, plus whatever later laws reached existing buildings, plus anything triggered by work you file now. That is why the first step in a 1960s tower such as Scott Towers on Paul Avenue is reading its records: the Certificate of Occupancy, the FDNY approval letters and the drawings. A panel replacement or a fire alarm system code upgrade is designed to current standards for the parts being replaced, and the engineer tells the board in writing what the filing will and will not require.

Special rooms

Our music school's practice rooms are sound-isolated. Can a student practicing alone inside one actually hear the alarm?

That is exactly what the design and the acceptance test have to prove. Rooms built to keep sound in also keep a corridor horn out, so a sound-isolated practice room usually needs its own notification appliance, audible and visible, inside the room. The NYC Building Code sets audibility relative to the room's ambient sound (NYC Building Code 907.5.2.1.1), and in a music school the ambient sound can be a piano or a brass section. We take readings inside the rooms with doors closed during the pre-test.

A school pool room has been closed for decades. Does the fire alarm still have to cover it?

Usually yes. A closed room inside a protected building is still part of the building, and a room nobody visits is where a fire can grow unseen. The Walton building's pool closed in 1983 after deterioration from the skylight and was never repaired (Wikipedia, Walton High School). Spaces like that are often damp and dusty, so the engineer picks devices that tolerate the room, and testing has to reach them, which means a key, lighting and safe footing on test day.

Owners and storefronts

Our Jerome Avenue store wants to rent its back room to a tutoring program after school. Does that change the fire alarm?

It can. A room where a group of students sits for lessons is a different use from a stockroom, with a different occupant load and different exit and notification needs. The change has to be reviewed by a design professional and filed correctly with the Department of Buildings, and any fire alarm devices it adds are designed, filed with the FDNY and tested before the program opens. A quick conversation with an engineer before signing the sublease is far cheaper than undoing it.

Types of Commercial Fire Alarm Systems We Install in Jerome Park

What is a commercial fire alarm system? It is a building-wide network: initiating devices that sense smoke, heat, waterflow or a manual pull, a control panel that decides what to do, notification appliances that warn people, and interfaces that recall elevators, stop fans and release doors, all supervised and reporting to a central station. How does a commercial fire alarm system work? "A school is a school, so a college building follows the same fire alarm rules"

Generic answers lump every classroom together. The building code does not. How long does fire alarm installation take? For a new campus building or a full replacement in a tower, plan on the FDNY's 20 to 24 weeks from plan submission to final inspection, with the physical work fitted inside that window (FDNY, Tips for Fire Alarm Systems). These are the steps.

1. First call

Address, floors, who occupies the building and when, the panel brand, any renovation already running, and the dates that cannot move: breaks, semester start, drills, board votes, inspections.

2. Survey and calendar

3. Design and sealed drawings

A New York State licensed Professional Engineer or Registered Architect designs the system to the NYC Building Code and NFPA 72 and seals the drawings, including the riser diagram and sequence of operations.

Rough-in, conduit where required, cable, device mounting and termination, panels and power supplies, programming and configuration, one wing or floor at a time while the rest stays protected.

Every device, interface, audibility reading and central station signal is tested and documented before the FDNY is invited, including inside closed and sound-isolated rooms.

With the central station connection active and a certificate of fitness holder present, the FDNY Fire Alarm Inspection Unit witnesses the test; a pass leads to the Letter of Approval.

As-built drawings, programming backup, test reports, a zone chart by floor and wing, and a walk-through for custodians, desk staff or campus safety before the first drill on the new system.

Every initiating device is put into alarm and the panel's response checked; every notification appliance is proven for sound and light; every interface, from elevator recall and door release to fan shutdown and sprinkler monitoring, is operated; the central station receives its signals; and the system is run on batteries. FAQ

Jerome Park Fire Alarm Installation FAQ

Do the classroom unit ventilators being replaced on a campus need their own duct smoke detectors?
+

Usually not, but the engineer decides. Duct detection is tied to the size of an air system and what it serves, and small room units often fall below the point where detectors are required, while large central air handlers usually do not. The mechanical drawings and the fire alarm drawings should agree on which units shut down on alarm, and that list should be confirmed before the new units are ordered.

The High School for American Studies sits on a college campus. Do college rules or high school rules govern its fire alarm?
+

The use of the space decides, not the landlord. Rooms used by students through the 12th grade are an educational occupancy, while college classrooms are a business occupancy (NY Engineers, summary of NYC Building Code 907), so a high school inside a college setting is designed and drilled as a school. Where the two share a building, the engineer shows each area's classification on the drawings and one system usually serves both.

A new school is moving into our shared campus building in the middle of the year. What has to change on the fire alarm?
+

Usually the paperwork more than the hardware. The zone chart, the alarm-response plan, the central station call list and the evacuation assignments all need the new school added, and its staff need a walk-through of the panel before their first drill. If the move comes with renovated rooms, new walls or a changed use, that work is designed and filed like any other alteration.

Do science and research labs on a college campus get the same smoke detectors as classrooms?
+

Not always. Labs can produce steam, heat and fumes that trip a standard smoke detector, so the engineer may choose heat detection, a different detection technology or careful placement away from equipment. Labs also tend to have fume hoods and special exhaust whose interaction with the fire alarm is spelled out in the sequence of operations.

Our school wants students to help clear rooms when the alarm sounds. Does the fire alarm design account for that?
+

The fire alarm tells everyone to leave; who checks the rooms is part of the school's emergency plan, not the panel. What the design can do is make the zone display clear enough that staff know which floor started the alarm, and make sure strobes reach every room so nobody in a closed room misses it. Room-clearing duties belong to staff.

Our school gym hosts graduations with hundreds of family members. Does that change the fire alarm?
+

It can. A gym full of seated guests is a different use from a gym class, and the occupant load the space is approved for drives exits, notification and sometimes voice messaging. If the room is approved for large gatherings, the design should already reflect it; if it is not, the school should ask its facilities office before booking the event rather than after.

Is the firefighter communication system in a 41-story tower part of the fire alarm work?
+

Often it is. In high-rise buildings, firefighter communication and emergency voice/alarm communication are usually controlled from the fire command center alongside the fire alarm, which the code places in the main entrance lobby (NYC Building Code 911). Which systems a proposal includes should be listed line by line, so a board knows whether the firefighter phones or radio equipment are in scope or belong to another contractor.

Our campus has its own public safety desk. Can the fire alarm report there instead of to a central station?
+

In addition, not instead. The FDNY requires the central station connection to be active before it will schedule a fire alarm inspection (FDNY, Tips for Fire Alarm Systems), and that connection is what gets the fire department dispatched. A campus desk can get its own annunciator or workstation so officers see the same alarms, which helps them meet responders at the right door.

A chemistry teacher wants to run a demonstration with a small flame. Should the fire alarm be bypassed for the lesson?
+

No. A small classroom demonstration done to the school's safety rules should not need any change to the fire alarm, and teaching staff should never cover or bypass detectors. If a planned demonstration would produce smoke or heat near a detector, the school's facilities office and the design professional decide what is acceptable, and any bypass is done by the fire alarm company with the notifications it requires.
