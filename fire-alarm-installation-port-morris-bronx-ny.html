The Neighborhood

Why Port Morris Warehouses, Factory Lofts and Waterfront Rentals Need Fire Alarms That Watch the Sprinklers

Port Morris has been a working waterfront since Gouverneur Morris Jr. set up a deep-water port on the East River in 1842 and ran a short railroad up to Melrose, according to Wikipedia's history of the neighborhood. It filled with factories: R. Hoe and Company, Cutler-Hammer and a string of piano makers whose brick buildings still line Lincoln Avenue and Bruckner Boulevard. The 1970s brought a wave of arson that damaged or destroyed many of them. What survived is a district of heavy brick and concrete buildings, one to six stories, which the Department of City Planning describes as housing light manufacturing, warehousing and open storage.

The newest buildings are bigger and much taller inside. The Sterling Organization's two-story logistics building at 307 Bruckner Boulevard, about 100,000 square feet in all, puts roughly 46,000 square feet of covered fleet parking and storage on the ground floor and a 46,000-square-foot high-bay warehouse with 36-foot clear heights above it, with an 8,200-square-foot mezzanine, four loading docks, two drive-in doors, 4,000 amps of power and an ESFR fire protection system, as New York YIMBY and the Bronx Times reported in August 2026. "The facility is on track to open sometime in the third quarter of 2026," the Bronx Times wrote. A few blocks away, the 90,000-square-foot distribution building at 295 Locust Avenue was built in 2002 with 50-foot clear ceilings, eight interior docks and ESFR sprinklers, per the New York Real Estate Journal; Wikipedia records that Silvercup turned it into a film and television production facility in 2016.

ESFR stands for Early Suppression Fast Response. Those sprinklers are designed to knock a fire in high storage down early, from the roof. That changes the fire alarm's job. In a building like this, smoke detection across a 36-foot roof is rarely the main defense. The alarm's work is to report the instant water flows, to supervise every valve that could starve the sprinklers, to watch the fire pump and the emergency generator if the building has them, and to make the rest of the building cooperate: close rolling fire doors, stop air handlers and conveyors, recall the freight elevator and send the signal to the central station.

More warehouses may be coming. Streetsblog NYC reported in November 2026 that Prologis told South Bronx Unite in October it planned to demolish the former New York Post printing plant across from Randalls Island and build a last-mile warehouse; Prologis said it was only evaluating the site and was not under contract. FreshDirect has run its Bronx campus at 2 St. Ann's Avenue on the Harlem River Yards since 2018, with robotic pick towers and nine miles of conveyor belts, per the Bronx Times. "There's this feedback loop of 'Oh well, there's warehouses here, so it's a great place to put another warehouse,'" David Rosales of South Bronx Unite told Streetsblog. Each of those buildings needs a fire alarm designed around its sprinklers and machinery.

The other half of Port Morris is people moving into industrial blocks. The city created its first Special Mixed Use District here in 1997, allowing homes between Bruckner Boulevard and East 134th Street, expanded it in 2005, and in 2017 extended the Special Harlem River Waterfront District to allow taller mixed-use buildings in the west. The 1886 Estey piano factory became the Clock Tower apartments and studios; The Arches on East 135th Street opened in 2020 as the neighborhood's first market-rate high-rise rental, with 25 stories and 430 apartments; 101 Bruckner Boulevard added a seven-story, 55-unit building. A converted factory, a new tower and a working warehouse can now share a block, each with its own fire alarm and its own rules.

This page covers commercial and code-required fire alarm systems only: building systems in warehouses, industrial and logistics buildings, converted factories, stores, taverns and restaurants, and multifamily and mixed-use buildings. The two- and three-story row houses on East 133rd and East 134th Streets are not part of this service, and the smoke and carbon monoxide alarms inside individual apartments are a separate requirement enforced by HPD.

Building Types

Commercial Fire Alarm Systems for Port Morris Building Types, From 1886 Brick to 36-Foot Clear Steel

What is a commercial fire alarm system? It is the building-wide network of initiating devices, notification appliances, modules and a control panel that protects a building's shared and working space and reports to a central station. How does a commercial fire alarm system work? Detectors, manual stations and switches on the sprinkler system tell the panel that something changed; the panel sorts that into an alarm, a supervisory signal or a trouble; then it runs the approved sequence of operations: report off site, sound and flash where people are, and tell doors, fans, elevators and machines what to do.

High-bay logistics buildings

The new last-mile buildings are steel boxes with very tall clear heights, ESFR sprinklers at the roof, loading docks along one side and, at 307 Bruckner Boulevard, a fleet parking level underneath the storage floor. Fire alarm design here centers on the sprinkler system's signals, on keeping each level and each sprinkler zone separate on the panel, and on notification that reaches drivers at the docks, pickers in the aisles and staff in the mezzanine office. Detection is used where it adds something: elevator lobbies and machine rooms, electrical and communication rooms, offices, and any area the design professional decides needs it.

Fleet parking under storage

Putting vehicles beneath a warehouse floor is efficient on a tight lot and unusual for a fire alarm designer. Parking garage fire alarm installation under storage is its own design problem: the level has its own exhaust, heat and traffic, and usually its own sprinkler zone. The alarm has to tell firefighters which level is burning, protect the stairs and lifts that connect them, and tie any garage ventilation into the sequence. Heat detection, sprinkler supervision and notification on the parking level are designed for a space where people are awake and moving.

Field Notes

Field Notes: Walk the Valves Before You Walk the Ceiling

In an industrial building, our survey starts in the sprinkler room, not under the roof. We find every control valve, every flow switch and every tamper switch, then match each one to an address or zone on the panel. In older buildings the list on the panel and the list on the pipe rarely agree: a valve added during a tenant fit-out never got a tamper switch, or a flow switch was wired to the wrong zone, so water on one floor reports as another.

Next we look at what the building does when water moves. Which doors drop, which fans stop, does the freight elevator recall, does the conveyor stop? The answers should be on the approved sequence of operations. If nobody can find it, that is the first item on our report.

The Parallel Products fire in 2019 started in a ceiling and was blamed on a faulty electrical panel, the Bronx Times reported. We make no claim about that building's system. What we take from fires like it is simple: electrical rooms and the spaces above them deserve real attention on the drawings, and the alarm should reach the central station the moment something trips, at midnight as much as at noon.

Your Questions Answered

Port Morris Fire Alarm Questions From Developers, Operators, Landlords and Managers

ESFR warehouses and last-mile buildings

The high-bay floor at 307 Bruckner Boulevard is protected by ESFR sprinklers. When the sprinklers are designed to put a storage fire down rather than just hold it, what is left for the fire alarm to do?

Plenty, just different work. The alarm reports waterflow as a fire alarm the moment water moves, supervises every control valve so a half-closed valve shows up as a supervisory signal instead of a surprise, watches the fire pump if there is one, and runs the sequence that keeps the building cooperating: fire doors close, air handlers stop, the freight elevator recalls and the central station calls the FDNY.

That building also puts about 46,000 square feet of covered fleet parking under the warehouse floor. How does one fire alarm keep the parking level and the storage level apart, so firefighters know which one is burning?

By giving each level its own identity on the panel. Waterflow switches are arranged so the parking level and the storage level report separately, detectors and manual stations on each level carry their own addresses and labels, and the annunciator at the fire department entrance names the level in plain words. The acceptance test proves each level reports the right place.

Our new high-bay building has roof hatches that older drawings call smoke vents. Should the fire alarm open them automatically when ESFR sprinklers protect the floor?

Usually not early, and that is a design decision to settle in writing. ESFR systems depend on heat collecting at the roof to open the heads fast; venting that heat too soon can work against them, which is why sprinkler standards limit how vents operate over ESFR protection. Whatever is chosen, the sequence of operations should say so plainly.

FreshDirect's building at the Harlem River Yards runs on robotic pick towers and nine miles of conveyors. When a conveyor passes through a fire-rated wall, what does the fire alarm have to do about the opening?

The opening has to be closed in a fire, and the conveyor has to get out of the way first. The usual arrangement is a listed fire door or shutter at the opening and a signal from the fire alarm that stops the conveyor, clears the opening and then lets the closure drop. Each is an interface on the drawings, operated at the acceptance test and every annual test. We make no claim about that company's building.

A new Port Morris warehouse is being marketed with 4,000 amps of power. Does the building's emergency generator report to the fire alarm, and what does the alarm do when the utility power fails?

The fire alarm panel and its power supplies run on their own dedicated circuit with standby batteries sized by calculation, so a utility outage should never silence the system. Where the building has an emergency generator, the design professional decides which of its conditions the fire alarm supervises, such as generator running, generator trouble or a transfer switch position, and how those signals report. Fire alarm battery load testing and generator tests each keep their own records.

Old factories and industrial plants

The Estey Piano Factory was built in 1885–86 and added to in 1890, 1895, 1909 and 1919 by different builders. Why does a fire alarm survey treat each addition of an old factory as its own piece?

Because each addition can hide its own surprises. Floors that look continuous may sit at slightly different levels, old exterior walls become interior walls with openings that may or may not be rated, and shafts, chases and ceiling cavities do not line up across the joint. Mapping each section separately shows where cable can travel and where a fire separation must be respected.

Union Crossing's owners marketed its seven floors to tech firms, trade schools, event companies, nonprofits and a climbing gym. When uses that different share one building, does each new tenant change the whole building's fire alarm sequence?

Not always, but a new kind of tenant can. A school, a large assembly space or an event venue can change the building's occupancy picture, add notification requirements, call for voice messages or change how the building is evacuated, which reaches beyond one floor. The landlord's design professional should review each new use against the base-building design before the tenant's architect draws anything.

The 2019 fire at the Parallel Products recycling plant on East 136th Street began in a ceiling and was blamed on a faulty electrical panel. In a recycling plant full of bales and dust, where does detection go so it sees a ceiling fire early?

Into the places where a fire would start unseen, chosen for the conditions. Spot smoke detectors on a dusty sorting floor tend to false alarm, so designers lean on heat detection, beam detection or air sampling that can be filtered and maintained, and they look hard at electrical rooms, the ceiling cavity over them and any space where wiring runs above combustible storage. The Bronx Times reported 138 FDNY members at that fire; we make no claim about the plant's system.

Gun Hill Tavern on East 133rd Street opened with a still on site. Does a working still change how the fire alarm treats that room?

It changes what the designer has to think about. Distilling puts heat, steam and alcohol vapor in one room, so ordinary smoke detectors are a poor fit there; heat detection is more typical, and some designs add a separate gas or vapor detection system that reports to the fire alarm as a supervisory or alarm signal, depending on what it is for. The bar and dining room next door are judged as an assembly or restaurant space in their own right. We describe the general method, not that business.

The July 2024 fire on East 136th Street sent smoke into a nearby residential building and forced residents out. Can smoke from a burning warehouse next door set off our building's duct detectors, and is that a false alarm?

It can, and it is not false: the detector did exactly what it was put there to do. A duct smoke detector samples air moving through the system, and if an outdoor intake draws in smoke from a neighboring fire, the detector shuts the fan down, which keeps more smoke from being pushed into the halls. Afterward, have the duct detectors cleaned and tested and the panel log checked. News 12 reported the roof of that commercial building partially collapsed; the cause was under investigation, and we make no claim about either building.

Demolition, new towers and changing blocks

Streetsblog reports that Prologis looked at demolishing the former New York Post printing plant for a last-mile warehouse. If an old industrial building is going to be torn down, how long should its fire alarm stay in service?

Until the building is empty of people and combustibles and the demolition contractor takes over under its own permits and fire protection plan. Vacant industrial buildings are a known fire risk, as Port Morris learned in the 1970s. When the system is finally taken out of service, close the central station account in writing, follow what the demolition paperwork requires, and keep the old drawings.

The Arches on East 135th Street was marketed with smart-home technology in the apartments. Can an apartment's smart-home system be connected to the building fire alarm?

Not as a casual add-on. The building fire alarm is a listed, FDNY-approved system, and anything that connects to it has to be on the approved drawings and listed for that connection. Apartment smart hubs and app-controlled devices can live alongside it, but they do not replace or alter the building's detectors and notification.

Our block of Bruckner Boulevard storefront buildings could be designated by the Landmarks Preservation Commission someday. If the fire alarm was approved before designation, does it have to be approved again?

No. An approved, working fire alarm does not lose its FDNY approval because a building is later designated. What changes is the next round of work: once a building is landmarked, exterior changes and some interior work that needs a Department of Buildings permit also go to the commission, so a future panel move, an exterior strobe or a new conduit run on the facade may need its review.

Per Scholas trains technicians at its main branch on East 138th Street. Can students in a training program help install or service a live building fire alarm?

Only within the rules for the company doing the work. Fire alarm installation in New York City is done by a NYC licensed electrician or by a company holding an FDNY Fire Alarm Company Certificate, and the FDNY ties servicing to that certified company and its qualified people. A trainee or apprentice can work under that company's supervision as its own rules and the FDNY's allow, but cannot sign for the work, hold the certificate or take responsibility for the test.

Types of Commercial Fire Alarm Systems We Install in Port Morris

A 36-foot steel warehouse, a five-story brick piano factory and a 25-story rental need very different systems. These are the types a design professional chooses among.

🔍

Addressable

What is an addressable fire alarm system? Every detector, station and module has its own address, so the panel names the exact device and its label. Addressable fire alarm installation is the normal choice for a warehouse with dozens of sprinkler switches and interfaces, a converted factory with many tenants or a tower.

🔌

Conventional

What is a conventional fire alarm system? Devices share zone circuits, so the panel reports a zone, not a device. Conventional fire alarm installation still fits a small tavern, a single shop or a one-room workshop with a handful of devices.

⇄

The difference

What is the difference between addressable and conventional fire alarms? One names the valve or detector; the other names a zone. In a building with 46,000 square feet on each level, that is the gap between walking to one switch and searching a floor.

🔊

Voice evacuation

Fire alarm voice evacuation installation replaces tones with spoken instructions. It belongs in the high-rise rentals and can suit large assembly or event space carved out of a converted factory.

🧠

Intelligent detection

Intelligent fire alarm installation uses multi-sensor heads that compare smoke, heat and other signals and adjust for their surroundings. An intelligent fire detection system helps where trucks, dust or steam fool simple detectors.

🌀

Air sampling and beams

Aspirating smoke detection installation draws air through pipe to a sensitive detector at floor level, where it can be serviced without a lift; VESDA installation is the best-known version. Beam smoke detector installation spans a tall open bay wall to wall.

Racking, offices and conveyors that the operator adds later can move detectors, block strobes or change the sprinkler design, so expect a second, smaller filing for the fit-out once the layout is fixed.

Do the rolling steel doors in a warehouse's fire walls have to close when the fire alarm activates?
+

Where a rated wall has a rolling fire door held open for trucks or forklifts, the door is usually closed by its own fusible link and, where the design calls for it, by a release signal from the fire alarm. The design professional decides which doors are tied in, and each one is dropped and reset during the acceptance test.

Does the mezzanine office in a last-mile warehouse need its own horns and strobes, or do the ones on the warehouse floor cover it?
+

Treat the mezzanine as its own space. Walls, glass and the noise of the floor below mean devices on the warehouse side rarely reach desks and restrooms upstairs, so the design adds notification in the office and checks that people at their desks can hear and see it.

Can fire alarm cable or detectors be hung close to ESFR sprinkler heads?
+

Not without checking. ESFR heads need a clear spray pattern, and conduit, cable trays, beam detectors or sampling pipe placed in the wrong spot can block it. The fire alarm layout should be coordinated with the sprinkler drawings before anything is hung, and the sprinkler contractor should see the routing.

Does an automated picking area with no people in it still need horns and strobes?
+

Notification is there for people, so a space nobody enters while it runs may need less of it. Maintenance staff still go in, though, and the design professional decides what the code requires for that area. Detection and sprinkler supervision continue either way, and the robots or conveyors are usually stopped by the fire alarm signal.

An antique dealer on Bruckner Boulevard keeps furniture stock in the cellar under apartments. Does that cellar need detection on the building's system?
+

If the building has a required fire alarm, the design professional decides whether the cellar storage is covered and by which panel. A cellar packed with old wood, finishes and fabric under occupied apartments is a space owners usually want watched, and any change to the store's use or layout is a reason to check.

How does a Port Morris warehouse keep its fire alarm working while the operator re-racks the floor?
+

Plan the move like any impairment: tell the fire alarm company what will be moved and when, keep lifts and pallets away from detectors and sampling pipe, and never bag or remove a device without a written plan. If any part of the system has to be taken out of service, the FDNY and the central station are notified and a fire watch kept as required.

A climbing gym wants space in the basement of a converted factory. Where do the strobes go when climbers face a wall?
+

Strobes are placed so the light reaches people wherever they are, including someone looking at a wall 30 feet up or standing in a corner behind a bouldering face. The design professional lays them out for the gym's actual walls, and the gym's own fire alarm work is coordinated with the building's panel.

Can an old freight elevator shaft in a Port Morris factory be used as a riser for fire alarm cable?
+

Sometimes, if the shaft is no longer used for an elevator and the design professional and building owner agree. Rated shafts must keep their rating, and cable in a shaft still needs the protection the code requires. A shaft that still carries a car is not a cable route.

Do artists' live-in studios in an old piano factory count as apartments for the fire alarm?
+

It depends on what the Certificate of Occupancy says about the floors. Live-in studios are often treated as dwelling units, which brings residential rules about sleeping areas and notification. The design professional starts from the legal use, not the marketing name.

A delivery operator wants to cut a new loading door into an outside wall. Does that touch the fire alarm?
+

Possibly. A new opening can put a detector, a strobe or a cable run in the way, and if the wall is rated the change goes well beyond the alarm. Show the fire alarm company the drawings before the saw cut, and if devices move, the change goes through the design professional.

Can a restaurant inside a sprinklered Port Morris warehouse skip the kitchen hood suppression system?
+

No. Commercial cooking under a hood needs its own hood suppression system whether or not the building has sprinklers, and in most designs the fire alarm watches that system and reports when it discharges. The building's ESFR heads protect the space; they are not a hood system.
