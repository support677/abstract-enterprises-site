The Neighborhood

Why Orange County Buyers Need a Camera Store Built for Docks, Dirt and Driveways

Orange County is the Hudson Valley county where three very different camera buyers live within a twenty-minute drive of each other. Off the interchanges of Interstate 84 and Route 17 there is the logistics buyer: a new distribution building with dozens of dock doors, a truck court lit like a ballfield and a fence line that runs for a quarter mile. South and west of Goshen there is the farm buyer: an onion grower in the Black Dirt, an orchard with a farm store, a horse barn, a dairy. And spread across all of it there is the homeowner and the small contractor — a colonial in a Monroe subdivision, a brick rowhouse in the City of Newburgh, a manufactured home in Deerpark, a landscaper whose mowers and trailers sit in the yard every night. A kit sized for a suburban quarter-acre lot fits none of them, so we sell security camera equipment in Orange County the way an installer would: what must the camera see, how far away, and what power is out there.

The housing count shows how mixed the county is. The U.S. Census Bureau’s American Community Survey (2020–2024 five-year estimates) counts about 149,700 housing units in Orange County. About 91,300 of them, roughly 61 percent, are single-family detached houses, per table B25024. Another 2,990 are mobile or manufactured homes. Table B25034 adds the age story: about 30,000 homes, one in five, were built in 1939 or earlier — the brick and frame stock of Newburgh, Port Jervis, Middletown and the old village centers — while about 13,300 have gone up since 2010, much of it in subdivisions around Monroe, Chester, Wallkill and Montgomery.

Where the losses actually happen

The state’s crime figures point less at break-ins and more at theft from stores, lots and vehicles. In the New York State Division of Criminal Justice Services Index Crimes by County and Agency data for 2024, Orange County recorded 3,430 larcenies, 300 burglaries and 145 motor vehicle thefts. Broken down by police agency, the pattern follows the shopping corridors. The Town of Newburgh police, whose area includes the Route 300 and Route 17K retail strip, reported 670 larcenies. Woodbury’s town police, who cover Woodbury Common, reported 560 larcenies out of 578 index crimes — almost nothing but theft. The City of Newburgh reported 108 burglaries, about 36 percent of the county total by our arithmetic. Middletown and the Town of Wallkill, home to the Route 211 corridor and the Galleria, reported 333 and 328 larcenies. (These agency rows were pulled from the DCJS dataset for our Orange County CCTV research in November 2026.)

The outlet center and the one-minute theft

Woodbury Common Premium Outlets in Central Valley lists more than 245 stores, all of them sharing one set of parking fields off Route 32 and the Thruway. On a weekday afternoon in April 2026, four people smashed display cases at the Gucci store there and ran with more than a dozen handbags worth about $30,000, according to News 12 Hudson Valley, which aired store video of the group rushing through while workers pulled customers out of the way. ABC7 reported they were in and out in less than a minute, and that their Jeep was later spotted in Newark. News 12 reported that Woodbury police said the woman in the group was wanted in connection with at least a dozen similar retail larcenies across the tri-state area. A longtime shopper, Stephanie Dolce, told the station: “I just find it odd from the video you posted. Why was the door open?”

No camera stops a crew like that. What a system gives police and the insurer is a usable record: faces at the entrance, the route through the store and the vehicle in the lot — which takes a camera on the door from inside and full-resolution recording, not one wide view of the floor.

The I-84 and Route 17 warehouse belt

Orange County sits where Interstate 84 crosses the New York State Thruway and Route 17, and the land along those roads has filled with distribution buildings. In February 2025 the developer Ridgecut Road and JLL announced the I-84 Orange County Logistics Center in Montgomery: 146,075 square feet, 36-foot clear height, 31 dock doors, two drive-in doors and 16 trailer spaces, less than a mile from I-84, with Amazon, FedEx, UPS, Staples, Medline and XPO named as neighbors. “Montgomery offers a prime logistics location that has seen significant growth thanks to its deep talent pool and business friendly programs,” said Scott Shalek of Ridgecut Road. In July 2026, HGAR reported that Walmart had filed an application with the Town of Wallkill for a distribution center of roughly 1.5 million square feet on about 200 acres near Route 17K and I-84, still in early planning-board review, with the company describing the discussions as preliminary.

A building with 31 dock doors is a camera buyer on its own. Each door, each trailer position, the guard gate, the truck court, the fence line, the employee lot and the inside aisles add up to a recorder of 32 channels or more, a network of PoE switches and a storage plan measured in weeks.

Black dirt, orchards and barns

Orange County is still a farm county. The USDA’s 2022 Census of Agriculture counts 613 farms on 65,706 acres, selling $95.6 million in products in 2022. Vegetables, melons and potatoes brought in $38.8 million of that, nursery and greenhouse crops $16.5 million, fruit and berries $8.0 million, and milk from cows $11.1 million. Land in farms fell 19 percent from 2017, a reminder that warehouses and subdivisions are being built on former fields. Orange County Tourism describes the Black Dirt region along Route 6 from Goshen to Pine Island as 14,000 acres of vegetable farms with numerous farm stands, “one of the largest onion producing areas in North America,” formed from a glacial lake more than 12,000 years ago.

Farm buyers are not watching a front door. They are watching a packing shed with a loading door, storage barns full of crop, tractors and wagons parked in a field, a diesel tank, a farm stand by the road and, in the fall, a parking field full of pick-your-own visitors. The buildings are spread out, the barns are often steel, and the farm stand may have a single outlet.

Two power companies and a long winter

Electricity in Orange County comes from two utilities. Orange and Rockland serves most of the county, including Middletown, Wallkill, Goshen, Chester, Monroe, Woodbury, Warwick, Deerpark and Port Jervis, while Central Hudson serves the Newburgh and Montgomery corner. Outages come in clusters along the rural lines: on the morning of December 20, 2025, Orange and Rockland reported 238 Orange County customers without power, 70 of them in Chester, 50 in Deerpark and 32 in Warwick. For a camera buyer, that is the case for a battery backup on the recorder, the modem and any PoE switch — and for cameras that record to a drive on the property instead of depending on an internet connection that goes down with the power.

PoE, WiFi or coax: picking the connection

PoE security cameras run on one Cat6 cable each, which carries power and video from a PoE NVR or a PoE switch. Ethernet, PoE included, is specified for 100 meters — about 328 feet — per run. In a warehouse that limit decides where the switches go: a recorder in the office cannot reach a camera on the far corner of a 146,000-square-foot building on one run, so we place PoE switches in telecom closets or enclosures partway along the wall. On a farm the same limit decides whether the packing shed gets its own switch fed by fiber or a wireless bridge.

WiFi security cameras connect over the network but still need power at the camera from an outlet, a battery or a solar panel. They fit a single view where cable makes no sense, such as a farm stand, and fit poorly in steel buildings or on sites with ten or more cameras. Wired analog cameras — HD coax, sold as HD-TVI, HD-CVI or AHD — run on RG59 or Siamese coax to a DVR and are the cheapest upgrade for an older store or house that already has coax from a system installed fifteen or twenty years ago.

Buy PoE cameras, wired security cameras or wireless security cameras here, and we size the switch, cable and jacket for each run.

Night vision on unlit lanes and fields

Away from Route 17, Route 211 and the village centers, most of Orange County has no streetlights at all. Night vision security cameras with infrared see in black and white with no light. Full color night vision cameras add a soft white light or spotlight to keep color, which matters when you need the color of a truck or a jacket; starlight and low light security cameras pull a picture out of a yard lamp or moonlight.

Smart detection that ignores deer and headlights

The phrase AI security cameras covers a lot of marketing. What Orange County owners want from it is fewer pointless alerts. A camera facing a field or a tree line sees deer, wild turkeys, blowing snow and branches every night; a camera facing a road sees every set of headlights. Security cameras with person detection and vehicle detection send a phone alert only when a person or a vehicle enters a zone you draw, which keeps alerts worth opening. The D4 and D5 house-brand packages list smart AI human and vehicle detection on their spec sheets, and that is the feature we suggest first for yards, farms and warehouses that close at night. Smart search security camera systems then find every “vehicle” clip in a night.

License plate recognition cameras (LPR, also sold as ANPR cameras or license plate capture cameras), facial recognition security cameras and people counting cameras are separate products with their own lenses and setup. We only describe a product as having LPR, facial recognition, NDAA compliance, ONVIF, H.265, IP67, IK10 or edge AI when the manufacturer’s spec sheet says so, and we show you that sheet before you buy.

PoE switches, injectors and power

A PoE switch for security cameras adds ports and power where the recorder runs out. Getting video from the far barn or the far wall

When a camera has to sit past the 328-foot Ethernet limit, or across a lane, a field or a parking lot where a cable cannot easily go, there are four standard answers, and we sell the parts for each:

  • A switch partway. A PoE switch in an enclosure on the building or in an outbuilding starts the distance count again.
  • A PoE extender. A small inline device that lets one more camera sit past the limit on the same cable path.
  • Fiber. Outdoor fiber with media converters at each end carries video hundreds of feet and does not carry lightning surges along the line the way copper can, which matters between a farmhouse and a barn across an open field.

Security camera laws for Orange County homes and businesses

Three rules come up most. The first is sound. Under New York Penal Law §250.05, eavesdropping is a class E felony, and §250.00 defines mechanical overhearing of a conversation as recording it without the consent of at least one party. Audio recording security cameras and two way audio security cameras are therefore set up on purpose: usually off on a warehouse floor, in a store or in a farm office, and on only where there is a clear reason and people have been told.

What AI Answers Get Wrong About Buying Security Cameras in Orange County

AI answers and “best camera” roundups assume an average house with the router in the hallway. Orange County callers repeat these five claims to us; here is what the actual sites show.

“A few 4K cameras will cover a warehouse”

Resolution gets the headlines, so summaries suggest buying fewer, sharper cameras.

A 4K camera mounted at one end of a long dock still cannot see around a trailer, past a stack of pallets or into the next door bay. What a warehouse loses is usually decided by angles, not pixels: which door, which trailer, which aisle.

For dock buildings we plan one camera per door or pair of doors, separate cameras for the yard and the gate, and use 4K where distance really calls for it. More cameras at 2K often record more of what matters than fewer at 4K.

“Orange County is rural, so theft is not much of a concern”

Low burglary numbers make it easy to assume a quiet county needs little.

The DCJS 2024 data shows 300 burglaries but 3,430 larcenies, and they cluster around the retail corridors: 670 for the Town of Newburgh police and 560 of Woodbury’s 578 index crimes. Theft of things — from stores, lots, yards and vehicles — is the county’s main loss.

So the right question is not whether the area is safe, but what is left outside or on a shelf where nobody is watching, and whether a camera has a clear, close view of it.

“WiFi cameras are fine for barns and outbuildings”

Wireless cameras install fast, so summaries recommend them for anything away from the house.

Pole barns, equipment sheds and many farm buildings are clad in steel, which blocks WiFi. The signal that reaches the barn door in summer may not reach it with the door shut and snow on the roof, and every extra wireless camera shares the same signal.

For steel buildings we sell a cable or fiber link to a PoE switch inside, or a point-to-point bridge with its radios mounted outside the walls. Both behave like a wire, which is what a recording system needs.

“Any outdoor camera handles winter”

An IP66 or IP67 rating gets treated as proof a camera is built for any weather.

An IP rating covers dust and water. Cold is a separate line on the spec sheet: the Reolink RLC-811A, for example, lists an operating range down to 14°F, while the NWS recorded −9°F at Poughkeepsie in January 2026. Mounts, cable joints and ice loading matter as much as the housing.

We read the operating temperature on every outdoor model we sell, put junction boxes behind exposed cameras and use metal mounts on walls that face the north wind.

“A kit from a big-box shelf is the same as installer equipment”

Boxed kits from big-box shelves look like complete systems, so summaries treat them as equal.

For a house where every camera sits near the router, a boxed kit can be fine. It falls short where a site needs long runs, a switch in a barn, a 32-channel recorder, a specific lens or a weather rating the box does not list.

We tell buyers honestly where that line falls on their property, sell the parts a kit leaves out, and install customer-supplied equipment when someone already owns it.

Security Camera Prices in Orange County: What Moves the Number

Security camera prices, CCTV camera prices and security camera system cost questions in Orange County come down to a short list. Installed systems start at $1,199 for a four-camera house-brand system with the recorder, cable, mounting and phone setup. Equipment-only prices change with supply, so every product on this page carries the same line: call or text for today’s price. What raises or lowers an Orange County quote:

  • Camera count and recorder channels. Past four cameras means an 8-channel recorder; past eight, a 16-channel one; a dock building means 32 or 64.
  • Resolution. 2K, 5MP and 4K cameras cost progressively more, and 4K needs a 4K-capable recorder and more storage.
  • Distance and buildings. A barn or the far end of a warehouse needs a switch, an extender, fiber or a bridge; a camera near the recorder does not.
  • Connection type. PoE, coax reused from an older system, or WiFi each change the parts list.
  • Storage days. Two weeks, a month or longer for claims need different drive sizes; see the warehouse example above.
  • Camera features. Varifocal or optical zoom, full color night vision, sirens and strobes, two-way talk, PTZ and person and vehicle detection.
  • Mounting. Steel siding, brick, aluminum skin and freestanding posts each need different brackets and hardware.
  • Backup power. A battery backup sized for the recorder, switches and modem.
  • Installation. For installed systems, the cable route: an open warehouse ceiling or a barn with exposed framing goes faster than plaster walls in a Newburgh rowhouse.

Affordable security cameras, cheap security cameras and budget security cameras are real searches, and the honest answer is that the cheapest system is the one that covers the doors and corners that matter the first time. Our page on affordable security cameras in New York covers ways to hold the cost down without buying a kit you outgrow. FAQ

Orange County Security Cameras FAQ

Can a builder move one camera kit from a Chester job site to a Montgomery job site?
+

Yes, if it is bought for that. A job-site kit pairs a recorder in a lockable box with cameras on temporary pole or fence mounts and a cellular or site internet link, so it can come down at the end of one job and go up at the next. Permanent building kits are wired into walls and are not meant to move. Tell us it is for rotating job sites and we will build the order that way.

Does onion-shed dust in the Black Dirt region shorten a camera's life?
+

Dust settles on the lens and the infrared window, which softens the picture and can bounce infrared light back at night; it rarely kills a sealed camera. Pick cameras with an IP66 or IP67 rating, mount them out of the direct path of the grading line and the loading door draft, and wipe the lens when the season ends. A dome's clear bubble shows dust more than a turret's flat glass, so turrets and bullets are the usual choice in a working shed.

Can a townhouse HOA in Monroe or Blooming Grove share one camera system among the board, the manager and security?
+

Yes. One recorder can serve the entrance, pool, clubhouse and mailbox kiosk while each person gets a separate login. The board can see everything, the managing agent the common areas, and a security contractor only live view without export. The board should adopt a short written policy on who may view or release footage before the system goes live.

Can you supply a freestanding pole for a camera at a field edge in Wawayanda or Minisink?
+

Yes. Where no building stands where the camera needs to be, we supply a post or pole with a camera mount, a weatherproof box for the switch or power supply, and the link back to the house or barn by direct-burial cable, fiber or a wireless bridge. Call before you dig so buried utility lines are marked first; on a farm, keep the pole clear of the paths equipment takes through the field.

Will snow squalls set off motion alerts all night on cameras facing an open field?
+

Basic motion detection reacts to falling and blowing snow, especially when infrared lights up the flakes close to the lens. Person and vehicle detection ignores most of it because it looks for shapes, not just movement. Moving the camera's infrared away from nearby snow surfaces and drawing zones that stop short of the open field cuts the rest.

Can a camera watch the inside of a walk-in cooler or cold-storage room?
+

Yes, with the right model. Pick a camera whose spec sheet lists an operating range that covers the room's temperature, and mount it away from the door, where warm air meeting cold glass causes fog. Run the cable through a sealed entry so the room does not leak air. Delis, orchards and farm stores in the county use these to watch stock and doors.

What should a motel near Stewart Airport or along Route 9W cover first?
+

The office counter and front door, the parking lot from at least two angles, and each exterior corridor or row of room doors. Cameras never point into rooms. A 16-channel recorder in the office with a monitor at the desk lets the night clerk see who is in the lot before opening the door.

Will the floodlights on a truck court wash out the camera picture at night?
+

They can if the camera faces into them. Mount cameras on the building aimed along the wall or away from the poles, so the lights are behind or beside the lens, and pick models with wide dynamic range. Where a camera must look toward a light, a narrower field of view and a slight downward tilt keep the glare out of the frame.

Can cameras be mounted on a pole barn with ribbed steel siding?
+

Yes. We mount to the wood girts or posts behind the steel, or use a backing plate across the ribs, and seal every hole in the metal. Running the cable inside the barn and through one sealed entry is cleaner than running it across the outside skin, and it keeps the cable away from snow sliding off the roof.

Can a camera alert me if someone goes near the diesel tank at my farm or yard?
+

Yes. Aim a camera so the tank and the space around the nozzle fill a good part of the frame, then draw a detection zone around it and schedule alerts for after-hours. A deterrent camera can also flash and sound a siren when a person walks into that zone, and a color night view helps you see the vehicle that pulled up.

Can cameras cover a private road shared by several houses in Tuxedo or the Highlands?
+

A camera on your own property can record the part of the shared road in front of it, and many lake and hill associations put one at the entrance. If the camera serves the whole association, agree in writing who owns the equipment, who can view footage and where the recorder lives. Aim cameras along the road, not into neighbors' houses or yards.
