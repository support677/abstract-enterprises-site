The Neighborhood

Why Kingsbridge Heights Properties Need Fire Alarms Designed for Crowds, Walk-Ups and Towers

Kingsbridge Heights is the ridge between the Major Deegan Expressway and Jerome Avenue, running from Kingsbridge Road up to Van Cortlandt Park, with Sedgwick Avenue as its main street (Wikipedia, Kingsbridge Heights). About 32,500 people lived on its roughly 300 acres at the 2010 census, the vast majority of households rent, and the housing runs from multi-unit detached houses to tenement apartment buildings in the central and southern blocks (Wikipedia). Most people here live in buildings someone else owns and maintains.

The biggest project on the hill is the Kingsbridge Armory. Designed by Lewis Pilcher and opened in 1917, it was designated a city landmark in 1974, listed on the National Register in 1982, left by the military in 1994 and taken over by the city in 1996 (6sqft, July 2025). Its column-free drill hall covers about 180,000 square feet and rises about 126 feet (NYC City Planning Commission report C 250292 ZSX, September 17, 2025). The City Council voted yes on the redevelopment on October 29, 2025 (Norwood News). The approved plan puts an indoor arena of 17,000 seats, roughly 95,600 square feet of community facility space, about 31,300 square feet of offices and a light-manufacturing floor of more than 90,000 square feet in the cellar into the old building, along with 248 public parking spaces below grade (City Planning Commission). "This project has been decades in the making, and now we're finally getting it done," said Sandra Lobo of the Northwest Bronx Community and Clergy Coalition (Norwood News, October 2025).

On the lot across West 195th Street, the same approval allows a 16-story building of about 389,000 square feet with roughly 500 affordable apartments, about 20,750 square feet of retail and about 80 cellar parking spaces, with a maximum height of 165 feet (City Planning Commission). Landmarks approved the design in July 2025 (6sqft). A 17,000-seat venue, a factory floor and a public garage under one landmark roof, with a mixed use building fire alarm installation of high-rise size next door, is a design problem the northwest Bronx has rarely seen.

The rest of the hill is older and smaller, and its fire record is the second reason this page exists. On January 15, 2025, a fire at 3030 Heath Avenue, a four-story, non-fireproof, H-type multiple dwelling with six apartments on each floor, went to four alarms; "Fire extended to the cockloft," FDNY Assistant Chief Kevin Woods said, and the undivided roof space let wind push it across the building (Norwood News, January 2025). The Department of Buildings vacated the whole building, the Red Cross helped 45 people from 21 households, and residents told reporters they had not heard a fire alarm, a question the FDNY referred to its investigation (Norwood News). On December 18, 2023, a fire in a one-story, non-fireproof deli at 69 West Kingsbridge Road, set in front of a five-story apartment building, went to three alarms and drew 138 FDNY members; fire marshals blamed lithium-ion batteries (Norwood News, December 2023). On November 1, 2021, a fire that started in a Bailey Avenue restaurant spread to two more restaurants and a corner grocery, went to four alarms and was traced to electrical wiring (ABC7 New York).

The third reason is what is being built between those old buildings. Recent filings on Sedgwick Avenue describe six-story apartment buildings: 17 homes in a 57-foot building between West 195th and West 197th Streets at 2788 Sedgwick (New York YIMBY, April 2026) and 22 homes in a 62-foot building on a vacant lot between Perot Street and West 231st Street at 3057 Sedgwick (New York YIMBY, October 2025). New buildings like these are designed under today's code from the first drawing, unlike the walk-ups beside them.

Our scope here is the NFPA 72 building system: the panels, detectors and notification that code requires in apartment houses, towers, mixed-use buildings, venues, shops, restaurants, schools, churches and community buildings. In-home smoke alarms are a separate matter and outside this service.

Commercial Fire Alarm Systems for Kingsbridge Heights' Building Types

Commercial fire alarm systems on the hill fall into six groups, and the first question in each is a different one. Here is how we approach them; the sealed drawings decide every detail.

The armory: a landmark drill hall turned into an arena, factory floor and garage

The approved reuse adds a new level above the drill hall, with the event venue and community space on the top floor, a public concourse with food and drink vendors in the old hall, and parking and light manufacturing at the lowest level (6sqft, July 2025). The drill hall's roughly 126-foot height (City Planning Commission) also rules out ordinary spot detectors on the ceiling, so high-volume detection, from projected beams to air sampling, is chosen by the engineer early. Assembly occupancies with large loads normally need emergency voice communication, which means fire alarm speaker installation tuned to a hall where sound bounces off brick and steel. Anything mounted on historic surfaces also goes through Landmarks review. Shopping center fire alarm installation habits apply to the concourse vendors: each tenant adds devices to a landlord's system.

Prewar apartment houses on Sedgwick, Heath and the side streets

Many of the hill's apartment houses were built long before building-wide fire alarm rules existed, and most were never required to add one. The Heath Avenue building that burned in January 2025 was a four-story, non-fireproof H-type building with an undivided cockloft (Norwood News). In buildings like that, the legal requirement is usually triggered by change: a filed gut renovation, a change in the number of units, a new use on the ground floor, or a conversion that adds floors. When that happens, the design starts with where the stairs and cocklofts are and where cable can actually run in old plaster and block, and we tell the owner in writing which work is required and which is a recommendation.

New six-story buildings on infill lots

The 2788 and 3057 Sedgwick Avenue filings describe six-story residential buildings of 17 and 22 homes (New York YIMBY). Under the NYC Building Code's residential triggers, which look at stories above the lowest exit level and the number of units, buildings of this size are commonly designed with a building fire alarm; the design professional confirms the exact requirement from section 907.2 (NYC Building Code 907.2, summarized by NY Engineers). New fire alarm system installation in a six-story building begins on the design development set, and the building cannot be occupied until the system is approved.

Stores, delis and restaurants with apartments around them

Storefronts like the 69 West Kingsbridge Road deli may belong to the apartment building's lot or stand alone, and the answer decides whether the store reports to the building's system, has its own panel or has no required system at all. Restaurant rows add cooking: commercial kitchen fire detection installation means heat detectors, not smoke detectors, near the line, and fire alarm kitchen hood suppression monitoring so the panel knows when the hood system discharges. Business fire detection systems and commercial fire detection systems in these spaces are sized to the use and the code, not to floor area alone.

A 21-story public housing tower

Fort Independence Street–Heath Avenue Houses is a 21-story brick and concrete building of about 376,000 square feet, with elevator banks in its lobbies and a one-story community center attached (draft National Register nomination, December 2025). It is one of 14 buildings with 1,669 apartments in NYCHA's Northwest Bronx PACT project, whose homes and community spaces are slated for renovation under new private partners (NYCHA). At 21 stories it is well past the 75-foot line the code uses to define a high-rise (National Fire Sprinkler Association), and with 122 apartments set aside for seniors, how residents learn about a fire, and how quickly they can act on it, is the heart of the design. Assisted living fire alarm installation habits apply: plan the signals for the people who move slowest.

Schools, a landmark community center, churches and a federal hospital

The Kingsbridge Heights Community Center works out of the former 50th Precinct station house at 3101 Kingsbridge Terrace, a Beaux-Arts building from 1902 that the Landmarks Preservation Commission designated in 1986; it opened there in 1975 with a teen program and a Head Start program (Wikipedia). NYC Parks owns the building. P.S. 86 sits on Reservoir Avenue across from the armory and in 2010 held 1,767 students in space built for 1,200, split between a main building and a satellite (Norwood News, December 2010); school fire alarm installation there is phased around the school calendar. Our Lady of Angels on Sedgwick Avenue closed its parish school in 2013 after 85 years (The Good Newsroom). The James J. Peters VA Medical Center on West Kingsbridge Road, opened in 1922 with 311 hospital beds and 120 nursing home beds today (U.S. Department of Veterans Affairs; Wikipedia), is federal property, so hospital fire alarm installation and nursing home fire alarm installation there answer to the VA's own fire marshal (VA Fire Protection Design Manual, 2023). Medical office fire alarm installation and office building fire alarm installation in private buildings nearby follow the FDNY path instead.

Who owns the system in a store under apartments

Ask a deli owner on West Kingsbridge Road who owns the detector over the slicer and you may get a shrug; ask the landlord and you may get a different shrug. Leases rarely say, so our storefront surveys begin with a written list: every device and its owner, the name on the central station account, and the person who receives the annual report.

What the FDNY and the code expect from stores, venues and shops

  • Drawings come before devices. The plans are drawn, sealed and filed on a TM-1 by a registered design professional licensed in New York State; the people installing must be a NYC licensed electrician or hold an FDNY Fire Alarm Company Certificate (FDNY, Tips for Fire Alarm Systems).
  • Ask for the numbers. Certification is required of any firm that installs, inspects, tests or services anything beyond plain smoke detectors, and one of its principals must carry an S-97 certificate of fitness, earned through NICET Level II or better or a NYC master or special electrician license (FDNY, Fire Alarm Company Certificate FAQ).
  • The fees are published. A new fire alarm application costs $420, complex technical analysis adds $525, and no work may begin before the Project Authorization arrives (NYC Business).
  • Monitoring is live first. The FDNY schedules the inspection only after the central station link is working (FDNY, Tips for Fire Alarm Systems).
  • Additions ride on the old approval. New tenant devices are filed as additions, with the system's existing approval letter attached (FDNY, Tips for Fire Alarm Systems).
  • Strobes for everyone. Fire alarm ADA strobe installation in sales floors, public dining rooms, corridors and assembly spaces wherever visible notification is required.
  • Landmarks. At the armory and the 1902 station house, work that touches protected features also goes to the Landmarks Preservation Commission.
Field Notes

Field Notes: On a Hill, the Fire Department's Door Is Not Always the Front Door

Kingsbridge Heights is built on a slope, and that changes where a fire alarm panel belongs. On the streets that climb from Bailey Avenue toward Sedgwick and on to the reservoir, plenty of apartment houses have a lobby on one street and a cellar or service entrance a full story lower on another. Firefighters arrive at whichever entrance their apparatus reaches first, and that is not always the one with the address numbers.

So on a survey here, before we count devices, we walk every entrance and ask three questions. Where will the first engine stop, which door will they open, and what will they see? The answers decide where the control panel goes, whether a remote annunciator belongs at a second entrance, and how the floor numbers on the panel match the floor numbers on the Certificate of Occupancy, which the FDNY checks against the drawings (FDNY, Tips for Fire Alarm Systems).

The armory will have the same problem on a giant scale: a building open on four corners, a new level over the drill hall, a garage and factory floor below grade, and 17,000 people inside on a show night. That question gets answered on the drawings years before opening night.

Your Questions Answered

Kingsbridge Heights Fire Alarm Questions From Developers, Owners, Store Tenants and Program Directors

Questions we expect from developers, owners, store tenants and program directors on the ridge.

The armory project

The armory arena will seat 17,000 people for concerts and graduations. How does a fire alarm tell a crowd that size what to do without starting a stampede?

With spoken instructions, not just horns. In a large assembly space the design professional normally specifies emergency voice communication, so a recorded or live message can tell people which exits to use, and the sequence of operations decides whether the whole venue is told at once or in stages. Every one of those links is listed on the drawings and proven at the FDNY acceptance test.

Inside the drill hall the roof trusses sit about 126 feet up. Can smoke detectors even work on a ceiling that high?

Ordinary spot detectors on the roof deck are a poor fit, because smoke from a small fire cools and spreads before it rises that far, and every detector would need a lift to test. Engineers usually turn to projected beam detection, which spans the hall at a set height, or aspirating systems that draw air samples through pipes to a detector at floor level. Which one, at what heights and in how many layers is decided on the drawings for the hall's roughly 126-foot height (City Planning Commission), with stage rigging, balconies and the new upper level taken into account.

The cellar will hold small manufacturers next to a public parking garage. Can one fire alarm serve both, or do they need separate systems?

One building system can serve both, provided the drawings treat them as different spaces with different detection. A public garage usually gets heat detection and carbon monoxide-related ventilation controls; a maker's shop gets detection chosen for its process, and an incubator whose tenants change every few years needs a layout that can be adjusted without refiling the whole building. The approved plan puts about 248 public parking spaces and more than 90,000 square feet of manufacturing in the cellar (City Planning Commission), so the panel's labels must tell a firefighter which is which at a glance.

Apartment houses on Sedgwick and Heath

After the Heath Avenue fire, tenants in our older walk-up are asking why the hallways have no fire alarm pull stations. Is the building out of compliance?

Not necessarily. Many prewar apartment houses were built before building-wide fire alarm rules and were never required to add a system; the current residential requirements apply to new buildings and to existing ones when certain filed work triggers them. Residents at 3030 Heath Avenue said they did not hear a fire alarm, and the FDNY referred that question to its investigation (Norwood News, January 2025). The useful next step is a records review: we read the Certificate of Occupancy and past filings and tell the owner in writing what applies, what is optional, and what a voluntary building system would involve.

We are gutting a five-story Sedgwick Avenue walk-up and adding units. Does that bring in a building fire alarm?

It can. A filed alteration that changes the number of units, the use or the layout is reviewed under today's code for the work being done, and the residential triggers look at stories above the lowest exit level and the number of dwelling units (NYC Building Code 907.2, summarized by NY Engineers). Your architect or engineer makes that call on the drawings; we help by surveying what cable paths exist in the old walls and what the cockloft and cellar look like, so the design reflects the building rather than a blank page.

Stores and restaurants

Our deli on West Kingsbridge Road is a one-story store in front of a five-story apartment building. Does the apartment building's fire alarm have to cover our store?

It depends on whether the store is part of the same building on paper. If the deli is a separate one-story structure on its own lot, it follows its own rules; if it is a one-story extension of the apartment building's lot and Certificate of Occupancy, the building's system and separations may need to account for it. The 2023 fire at 69 West Kingsbridge Road began in exactly that kind of one-story, non-fireproof store set in front of a five-story apartment building (Norwood News). The records answer the question, and we read them before quoting.

Delivery riders charge e-bike batteries in the back of our store. Can a fire alarm detect a battery fire early?

Detection can raise the alarm sooner, but it cannot make the charging safe. FDNY fire marshals traced the 2023 West Kingsbridge Road deli fire to lithium-ion batteries, and the Fire Commissioner's advice afterward was blunt: "Don't charge them overnight or near exits" (Norwood News, December 2023). Where a store is required to have a system, the design professional can place detection in back rooms and storage areas, but the first fix is moving charging out of the building or into a space designed for it.

The 2021 Bailey Avenue fire spread through a row of restaurants. If our restaurant installs a fire alarm, will it warn the shop next door?

Only if the two spaces share a building system. Separate storefronts on separate lots usually have separate systems, or none, and one restaurant's horns are not designed to reach a neighbor. Fire marshals traced the Bailey Avenue fire to electrical wiring after it started in one restaurant and spread to two more and a grocery (ABC7 New York, November 2021). What does protect the neighbor is fire separation between the spaces, a hood suppression system that works and is monitored, and electrical work done to code, all of which the design reviews.

Towers, schools and the community center

Our 21-story tower is changing management under a public housing conversion. What fire alarm records should the new manager ask for on day one?

The FDNY approval letters for the system and any changes, the as-built drawings and riser diagram, the current device list and programming backup, the last annual inspection and test reports, any open Notices of Defect or violations, and the central station account details. Fort Independence Street–Heath Avenue Houses moved into NYCHA's Northwest Bronx PACT program, with new private partners managing 14 buildings and 1,669 apartments (NYCHA). A clean handover of fire alarm records is what lets the new manager answer the first trouble call with facts.

A third of the apartments in our tower are set aside for seniors. Should the fire alarm work differently on those floors?

The code sets the minimum, and the design can go further where residents need it. In a high-rise the system normally gives voice instructions, and many buildings plan visible notification and staff procedures around older residents who may move slowly or not hear well. At Fort Independence Houses 122 of 344 apartments were set aside for seniors (draft National Register nomination, December 2025). Talk with the design professional about where strobes are needed, how messages are worded, and how building staff check on residents who stay in place.

Our tower has been nominated for the National Register of Historic Places. Will that limit where new fire alarm devices can go during the renovation?

Probably less than people fear. National Register listing is not the same as city landmark designation, and on its own it does not send interior fire alarm work to the Landmarks Preservation Commission. Projects that use federal or state funding or approvals can, however, trigger a review of their effect on historic features, so the development team should say early whether that applies. Fort Independence Street–Heath Avenue Houses was the subject of a draft nomination prepared by the State Historic Preservation Office in December 2025, which describes the building as highly intact.

Our elementary school is directly across the street from the armory construction site. Will the venue's alarms or construction affect our building's fire alarm?

The venue's alarms are designed to stay inside the venue, so they should not reach your building. Construction is the more likely issue: dust drifting through open windows and fresh-air intakes can make smoke detectors dirty or trigger them, and street work can cut a phone or data line the communicator uses. P.S. 86 sits on Reservoir Avenue across from the armory (Norwood News). Ask the school's facilities staff to check detector sensitivity reports and the communicator's backup path once the armory work begins in earnest.

Types of Commercial Fire Alarm Systems We Install in Kingsbridge Heights

What is a commercial fire alarm system? Sensors such as smoke and heat detectors, pull stations and sprinkler flow switches start the alarm; a control panel judges the signal; horns, strobes and speakers warn the occupants; and relays tell elevators, fans and doors what to do, while a communicator reports to a central station. How does a commercial fire alarm system work? The panel polls its devices and checks its wires around the clock. If a detector trips, water flows in a sprinkler pipe or someone pulls a station, it sounds the building, fires its relays and sends the signal on for dispatch. Fire detection and alarm installation, or fire detection system installation, puts all of it in and proves it works together.

The armory's venue, community floor, offices, manufacturing incubator and garage may not all be finished at once. Can parts of the building open before the whole fire alarm is approved?
+

Possibly, if the filing is planned for it. The FDNY tests and approves what is filed, so a building that will open in stages needs drawings that show which parts of the system serve which areas, and each stage has to be complete, tested and protected from the construction still going on around it. Agree on the phasing before the first TM-1 is filed; changing it later means amendments and new reviews.

Is the fire alarm at the James J. Peters VA Medical Center inspected by the FDNY?
+

The VA hospital is federal property, and the VA's own fire protection design manual names the VA's fire marshal as the authority having jurisdiction for its projects, while giving local fire officials a chance to review and comment (VA Fire Protection Design Manual, 2023). Private clinics, offices and residences around the hospital on West Kingsbridge Road are different: they follow the FDNY's TM-1 filing and acceptance test like any other building in the Bronx.

Our 21-story tower has a one-story community center attached at the base. Is the community center on the tower's fire alarm or its own?
+

That depends on whether the two are one building on the Certificate of Occupancy and how the original system was designed and approved. An attached wing is often covered by the main building's panel, with its own labeled devices, but some were built or later renovated with a separate system. Fort Independence Houses has a one-story community center attached to its Bailey Avenue wing (draft National Register nomination, December 2025). The as-built drawings and FDNY approval letters answer the question; if those are missing, the panel's device list will.

The Kingsbridge Heights Community Center's building belongs to NYC Parks. Who is responsible for its fire alarm, the city or the program inside?
+

The answer is in the lease or license between the city and the program, and it is worth reading closely. Often the owning agency handles capital work such as a new panel and the occupant handles day-to-day items, but every agreement differs. The center says its 1902 landmark building is owned by NYC Parks and needs major roof and facade work (KHCC). Get the split in writing.

Does a Sedgwick Avenue landlord have to warn tenants before the building fire alarm is tested?
+

Good practice is to give clear notice every time. A surprise test sends residents into the stairs and teaches people to ignore the alarm. Post notices in the lobby and on each floor with the date and hours, tell the central station so it does not dispatch, and have the technician announce the start and end of testing.

Our new six-story building will have storage cages for tenants in the cellar. Does the cellar need fire alarm coverage?
+

Usually the cellar is part of the building system, and storage rooms full of cardboard, furniture and battery-powered equipment are exactly where early detection helps. Whether the code requires detection there, and what kind, depends on the building's sprinkler protection, the room's size and use, and the design professional's reading of the code. Show the storage area and its real use on the drawings from the start.

A church on Sedgwick Avenue wants to open its hall as an overnight warming center in winter. Does people sleeping there change the fire alarm requirements?
+

Yes, potentially a great deal. A hall approved for daytime gatherings is a different occupancy from a room where people sleep, with its own fire alarm, detection and exit rules. Talk to the design professional before the first cot goes in.

Our building sits on the slope, and its back wall is below the street behind it. Does that change how many stories the fire alarm rules count?
+

It can. The code counts stories from the lowest level of exit discharge and uses defined grade planes, so a building that has five stories facing one street and six facing the other may fall on either side of a threshold depending on where its exits are. Residential fire alarm triggers look at stories above the lowest exit level and the number of units (NYC Building Code 907.2, summarized by NY Engineers). The design professional makes the call on the drawings; we bring the survey of every entrance and level.
