Why Bedford Park's Six-Story Apartment Blocks and Campuses Need Fire Alarms Designed for Them

Bedford Park needs fire alarm work that fits a very particular mix: block after block of five- and six-story apartment houses, a strip of schools and a college along the reservoir, and commercial streets where stores sit under homes. Bedford Park runs from Jerome Avenue east to Webster Avenue and from East 196th Street north to Mosholu Parkway. Leonard Jerome's racetrack once stood here, the Belmont Stakes ran on it until 1890, and the track was turned into the Jerome Park Reservoir. When the Grand Concourse was finished in 1914 and the Jerome Avenue elevated line arrived in 1917, the villas and wood-frame houses of a quiet suburb gave way to apartment buildings, built from the years after the First World War through the 1950s. That is the building stock our technicians walk into today.

Fire is not an abstract subject here. In April 2025 alone, the FDNY sent 12 units and 60 firefighters and EMS members to a third-floor fire in a six-story multiple dwelling at 3154 Grand Concourse between East 205th and East 206th Streets, and eleven days later sent the same size response to a second-floor fire in a six-story building at 2707 Creston Avenue, where one resident was taken to St. Barnabas Hospital with serious injuries. In October 2024 an e-bike fire at 15 East 199th Street hurt eight people and led to vacate orders. In April 2026, more than 100 fire and EMS members fought a top-floor fire in a five-story Bedford Park apartment building before dawn, and 15 apartments were placed under a vacate order. One tenant told News 12: "I just heard the firetrucks coming and that's all I heard and then I just ran outside."

That quote is the reason this page exists. Most of Bedford Park's older apartment houses were built long before today's code required a building fire alarm, and many still rely on the smoke alarms inside each apartment. When an owner renovates, adds a store or a community facility on the ground floor, converts a cellar, or builds new on Webster Avenue, the design professional decides what the code now requires, and that is where commercial fire alarm installation in Bedford Park begins: detection in the cellar, boiler room, compactor room and elevator machine room, supervision of the sprinkler where one exists, and a signal that reaches a central station operator instead of depending on a neighbor who happens to be awake.

The campuses bring a different kind of work. Lehman College's 37 acres at 250 Bedford Park Boulevard West hold 13,111 students and a 2,310-seat performing arts center. The Bronx High School of Science at 75 West 205th Street and DeWitt Clinton High School on Mosholu Parkway South are large public school buildings with their own capital processes. Around them sit day cares, tutoring centers and after-school programs in storefronts and lower floors, each one a small assembly or educational space that needs its own fire alarm decision. And on Webster Avenue, the 2011 rezoning replaced old manufacturing zoning with residential districts and commercial overlays so that mid-rise apartment buildings with stores at the base could go up. Every one of those new buildings needs a fire alarm designed, filed and tested before it can open.

Building Specialists

Commercial Fire Alarm Systems for Bedford Park's Building Types

Commercial fire alarm systems in Bedford Park come in five broad shapes, and each one is designed differently. The design professional starts with the occupancy and the code, but the installer's survey shows what the building will physically allow.

Prewar and postwar six-story apartment houses

The typical Bedford Park building is a five- or six-story masonry apartment house with an elevator, a cellar with a boiler and a compactor or refuse room, a lobby and a roof bulkhead. For a new or substantially altered Group R-2 building of this size, NYC Building Code 907.2.9 calls for a fire alarm system without alarm notification appliances in the apartment corridors, plus smoke alarms in the units: smoke detectors go in mechanical, electrical and elevator machine rooms, in elevator lobbies and in air distribution systems, and the signal goes to a central station or a constantly attended location rather than sounding horns throughout the building. Many existing Bedford Park buildings predate that rule and have no building system at all; what triggers one is a renovation, a change of use or new work that the design professional reads as requiring compliance. Multifamily building fire alarm installation here means fitting detectors, monitor modules and cable into 90-year-old masonry without disturbing the residents more than necessary.

Co-ops and condominiums on the Grand Concourse

The taller apartment houses on the Concourse are often co-ops with a board, a managing agent and a long list of shareholders. Fire alarm work there is a governance project as much as a technical one: the board approves the scope, the managing agent arranges access, and the system's records have to outlive the next change in management. We write proposals boards can read and compare, and we explain in plain language what is required and what is optional.

The Educational Mile

DeWitt Clinton High School has occupied its Mosholu Parkway South building since 1929, on a 21-acre campus. The Bronx High School of Science moved into its Emery Roth and Sons building on West 205th Street in 1959. Lehman College, founded in 1931 as the Bronx branch of Hunter College, hosted the first American meetings of the United Nations Security Council in its gymnasium in 1946 and now covers 37 acres. These institutions run their fire alarm work through their own capital programs and agencies, and we do not suggest otherwise. What we do see are the private schools, tutoring centers, day cares and campus-adjacent offices around them, where school fire alarm installation and assembly-space fire alarm work are smaller, but still go through a sealed design and an FDNY filing.

New mixed-use buildings on Webster Avenue

The 2011 rezoning aimed to "encourage mid-rise residential development and promote new commercial uses" on Webster Avenue between 193rd and 211th Streets. New buildings here are mixed use building fire alarm installation jobs from the ground up: the residential floors, the stores at the base, a cellar or parking level, an elevator, sprinklers and often a community facility. These systems are addressable, the cable goes in during construction, and the fire alarm construction drawings coordinate with the electrical, plumbing and elevator trades from the start.

Houses of worship and community buildings

Bedford Park has a long list of churches and religious buildings, including St. Philip Neri, Bedford Park Congregational Church, Bedford Park Presbyterian Church and the former Convent of Mount St. Ursula. When one of these buildings adds a school, a day care, a food pantry or a hall used for events, the occupancy can change, and the design professional may call for a fire alarm where none was needed before. We survey these older buildings carefully: wood, plaster and high ceilings shape where detectors go and how cable is run.

What Bedford Park does not have, and what that means

Bedford Park has no hotel, no shopping center and no hospital inside its borders. North Central Bronx Hospital and Montefiore's Moses campus are in Norwood next door, and the tall Tracey Towers stand across Jerome Avenue in Jerome Park. So hospital fire alarm installation, hotel fire alarm installation, shopping center fire alarm installation and high rise fire alarm installation, with their fire command centers and voice systems, are not the everyday work here. Where nursing home fire alarm installation or assisted living fire alarm installation comes up in or near the neighborhood, it follows healthcare occupancy rules that are stricter than anything an apartment house needs, and the design professional sets the scope.

Business Fire Alarm Installation on Bedford Park's Commercial Streets

Business fire alarm installation in Bedford Park happens mostly on four streets, and each one has its own pattern. Business fire detection systems here are usually small, but they sit inside or under apartment buildings, which makes the design and the coordination with the landlord the hard part.

Bedford Park Boulevard

The boulevard, renamed from 200th Street in 1906, carries the neighborhood's shopping between the Concourse and Webster Avenue, with the B and D trains at Bedford Park Boulevard station (opened July 1, 1933) and the 4 train at Bedford Park Boulevard–Lehman College. Delis, pizzerias, pharmacies, laundromats, tutoring centers and phone stores fill the ground floors of apartment buildings. A store here that adds a cooking line, a basement storage room or a classroom may need its own detection tied into a building system, or a stand-alone system the landlord agrees to host.

Jerome Avenue

Jerome Avenue is the neighborhood's western edge, under the elevated 4 line, with auto shops, small groceries, restaurants and storefront churches. Work under the el means noise, vibration and dirt that affect detector selection and maintenance; a storage room or an auto bay can call for heat detection where dust would fool a smoke detector.

Webster Avenue

Webster Avenue is the eastern edge, where the old C8-2 auto and manufacturing zoning was replaced in 2011. Storage facility fire alarm installation, parking garage fire alarm installation and work in the remaining auto-related businesses sit alongside the new mixed-use buildings. Across the avenue lie the New York Botanical Garden and the Metro-North Botanical Garden station.

The Grand Concourse

The Concourse is mostly residential through Bedford Park, but the ground floors of its larger buildings hold medical offices, dental practices and community spaces. Office building fire alarm installation in these converted ground floors is usually a modification of the building's system, filed by the building's design professional with the tenant's scope included.

Compliance points that come up on these streets

  • FDNY approval comes first. Under the NYC Building Code, fire alarm documents go to the FDNY for approval before installation; the FDNY's tip sheet says plans must be prepared by a registered design professional and filed with a TM-1 application.
  • Installer qualifications. The FDNY requires the installer to be a NYC licensed electrician or a fire alarm vendor holding an FDNY Fire Alarm Company Certificate, and a company that installs, tests or services fire alarm components needs at least one principal with an S-97 Certificate of Fitness (NICET Level II or higher, or a NYC master or special electrician license).
  • Accessibility. Where notification appliances are required, visible signals are part of the design so that people who cannot hear the horn are warned; fire alarm ADA strobe installation follows the approved drawings.
  • Lithium-ion batteries. E-bike shops and delivery workers' storage rooms are a known fire risk in the Bronx, and the October 2024 fire at 15 East 199th Street was an e-bike fire. Storage and charging rooms deserve a conversation with the design professional about detection.
Field Notes

Field Notes: The Compactor Room Nobody Had Checked

This is a typical Bedford Park call, described as it usually goes rather than as one particular job. A managing agent for a six-story building off the Concourse calls because the panel in the cellar has shown "TROUBLE" for a week and the super has been pressing silence every morning. The panel is a conventional zone panel installed decades ago; there is no Letter of Approval in the office, and the central station account is in the previous owner's name.

On site, the technician reads the zone, opens the end-of-line resistor box and meters the circuit. The fault traces to a heat detector in the compactor room, where a leaking chute drain has been dripping on the detector base for months. That is a fire alarm ground fault repair and a fire alarm open circuit repair in one, and it takes about an hour once found. The longer conversation is about everything else: the account in the wrong name, the missing approval, a panel whose manufacturer stopped making parts, and a board that now has to decide whether to keep patching or plan a fire alarm system replacement with a design professional.

The lesson our technicians repeat to every Bedford Park super: a trouble light is the system telling you it cannot do its job. Silence the sound if you must, but call the same day.

Your Questions Answered

Bedford Park Fire Alarm Questions From Boards, Supers, Store Owners and Schools

These are the questions Bedford Park owners, boards, supers, store tenants and school operators ask us, grouped by subject. Each answer explains how the FDNY process applies; the specific requirement for your building always comes from your design professional.

Cost and budgeting

Our Creston Avenue co-op board has to budget for a fire alarm next year. What should the reserve study include?

Budget for four separate items, because each is billed by someone different: the design professional's fee for sealed drawings, the FDNY filing and any resubmission, the installation itself, and the acceptance test with its retests and closeout records. Add the central station contract as an ongoing cost. The biggest unknown in a six-story masonry building is the cable route, so the most useful thing a board can do before budgeting is let a contractor and the engineer walk the cellar, the stairs and the roof bulkhead. A fire alarm installation bid built on that walk is far more reliable than one written from the lobby.

A Webster Avenue developer asked us for a fire alarm installation proposal before the drawings were finished. Is that normal?

It is normal to ask for a budget early, but a real fire alarm system installation proposal can only be written against the design professional's drawings. Early on, what you can get is a scope outline and a list of assumptions: addressable system, number of floors and apartments, stores at the base, elevator recall, sprinkler monitoring. Once the fire alarm construction drawings exist, ask every bidder to price the same set, and compare what each excludes as carefully as what each includes.

Quality and trust

How can a Bedford Park managing agent tell whether the company that serviced our panel was qualified?

Ask for the company's FDNY Fire Alarm Company Certificate and the name of the principal who holds the S-97 Certificate of Fitness, and ask for the inspection and test reports from the last visit. The FDNY requires a company that installs, inspects, tests or services fire alarm components, other than only smoke detectors, to hold the certificate. A company that cannot produce it, or that never leaves written reports, has not given you the records you are responsible for keeping. We name the certified company and electrician of record on every scope we write, and we do not claim certificates we do not hold.

After the April 2025 fires on the Concourse and Creston Avenue, tenants asked our board whether our alarm would have helped. How do we answer honestly?

Start with what your building actually has. If it has only the smoke alarms in each apartment, those alarms warn the people in that apartment; they do not alert the rest of the building or a central station. If it has a building system, find out what it covers: often only the cellar, boiler room and elevator machine room, which is what the current R-2 rule calls for in new buildings. Explain to tenants what the system does, what it does not do, and when it was last tested, and if the answers are uncomfortable, that is the start of a conversation with a design professional rather than a reason to stay quiet.

Who does what

Can our building's super replace a cellar heat detector himself if he buys the same model?

No. Replacing a device on an approved system is service work on a fire alarm, and the FDNY requires that work to be done by a certified fire alarm company or a licensed electrician. The super's job is to notice the trouble, keep the cellar dry and accessible, keep the panel log, and call the company that services the system. Even a like-for-like swap has to be tested and recorded, and a wrong base or wiring can put a whole zone out of service.

Our tutoring center on Bedford Park Boulevard is moving into a former bank. Who decides whether we need a fire alarm at all?

Your design professional decides, by reading the occupancy classification, the occupant load and the building's existing systems against the NYC Building Code. The landlord's existing system, if there is one, matters a great deal. Our role is to survey the space, tell you what the existing building has, and give the design professional the information needed for the drawings. If the code does not require a system, we will tell you so.

Technical

A Con Edison crew blamed a basement "blast" at our East 198th Street building on the boiler. Should the boiler room have had a detector that caught it?

News 12 reported in March 2026 that FDNY units went to a six-story building at 18 East 198th Street after a reported basement blast, and Con Edison traced it to a problem with a customer's boiler rather than an explosion. Smoke and heat detectors are not gas or pressure detectors, and they would not "catch" a boiler malfunction as such. What a boiler room detector does is warn the central station when a fire starts there, which is why boiler and mechanical rooms are among the first places a design professional puts detection in an apartment building. Carbon monoxide detection is a separate requirement with its own rules.

Our elevator machine room is on the roof bulkhead. Why does the FDNY care about a smoke detector up there?

Because the detector in the machine room and the elevator lobbies is what tells the elevator to stop being used in a fire. On a building with an elevator recall interface, the fire alarm sends the cars to the designated floor and shuts them off for public use, so that nobody rides into smoke. The detector, the relay to the elevator controller and the test with the elevator mechanic present are all part of the acceptance test.

Why does our Valentine Avenue building's panel show a ground fault every time it rains?

Water is finding the wiring. In older Bedford Park buildings the usual places are a roof-level device or junction box on the bulkhead, a cable run along a leaking parapet, or a cellar device near a wet wall. A ground fault means some part of the circuit is touching earth through moisture or damaged insulation. Fire alarm ground fault troubleshooting is a process of splitting the circuit and metering each half until the fault is isolated, and the repair is often a new box, a sealed fitting or a rerouted run rather than a new device.

Owners, tenants and boards

The store on our ground floor wants to add a kitchen. Whose fire alarm has to change, theirs or the building's?

If the building has an approved fire alarm, the store's new kitchen is usually a modification of the building's system, filed with the landlord's sign-off, because the FDNY approval belongs to the building. A restaurant cooking line typically brings heat detection where smoke detectors would false alarm and, if a hood suppression system is installed, monitoring of that system. The lease should say who pays for the design, the filing and the work; we price it either way, but we need to know who the client of record is.

Our building's alarm went off at 1:30 a.m. and the FDNY found nothing. Will we be fined?

A single unnecessary alarm is usually recorded and closed, as when the FDNY reported "Reported alarm activation, no reported injuries, incident closed normally" at 3114 Villa Avenue in November 2025. Repeated unnecessary alarms are different: the FDNY can treat them as a failure to maintain the system, and owners have faced penalties for that. Every alarm with no fire should lead to a finding, such as a dirty detector, steam from a shower near a corridor head or a contractor's dust, and a fix.

Complaints and problems

Residents say they never hear the alarm in our building. Is the system broken?

Not necessarily. Many apartment fire alarm systems built under the R-2 rule are designed without horns in the corridors: they report to a central station and the FDNY, and residents are warned by the smoke alarms in their own apartments. If your system was designed that way, residents will not hear it, and they deserve to have that explained. If it was designed with notification appliances and people cannot hear them, that is a deficiency to report to the company that services the system immediately.

Our previous contractor left an open Notice of Defect and stopped answering. Can someone else finish it?

Yes. Another certified company can take over the correction, but the new company needs the approved drawings, the defect notice and access to the panel's programming. Corrections are answered with an FA-27 document submission to the FDNY, which the FDNY's instructions list with a $105 fee. If the previous company kept the programming software or passwords, plan time to recover them, and ask the central station for the account history.

The Jerome Park high-rise across the avenue had a fire where residents said no alarm sounded. Could that happen in our Bedford Park building?

Norwood News reported that after a July 2021 fire at Tracey Towers, just across Jerome Avenue in the Jerome Park neighborhood, one resident said he heard no alarm and questioned when the system had last been tested, while management texted residents to stay in their apartments. Whether residents hear an alarm depends on the design, not only on whether the system works. The useful lesson for a Bedford Park building is to know which kind of system you have, to test it on schedule with records, and to tell residents in plain words what will happen, and what they should do, if there is a fire.

Equipment

Types of Commercial Fire Alarm Systems We Install in Bedford Park

What is a commercial fire alarm system? It is a building-wide network of detectors, manual stations and notification appliances wired to a control panel that monitors every device, decides what each signal means and reports it to a central station. How does a commercial fire alarm system work? It watches its circuits for a change, such as smoke, heat, a pulled station or a sprinkler flowing water, then activating the outputs the design calls for and sending the signal off site. In Bedford Park the two basic families are conventional and addressable, and the choice shapes almost everything else. Every new fire alarm system installation here is NFPA 72 fire alarm installation as adopted and modified by New York City, whichever family the design professional chooses.

📈

"Every apartment building needs a fire alarm with horns on every floor"

This is the most common mistake. For new or substantially altered Group R-2 buildings in New York City, Building Code 907.2.9 calls for a fire alarm system without alarm notification appliances, with smoke alarms in the apartments and detection in mechanical rooms, elevator areas and air distribution systems, reporting to a central station or attended location.

Many of Bedford Park's six-story apartment houses predate that rule entirely, and the code does not reach back to require a new system in every existing building. Whether yours needs one depends on its history and on the work being done now, which is a reading only a design professional can make.

"A licensed electrician can install a fire alarm system without any approval"

Being licensed is necessary but not sufficient. In New York City, fire alarm plans are prepared by a registered design professional and filed with the FDNY on a TM-1 application, and the installation follows the approved drawings.

The installer has to be a NYC licensed electrician or a fire alarm vendor holding an FDNY Fire Alarm Company Certificate, and the company's principal holds the S-97 Certificate of Fitness. An installation done without an approved design will not pass the acceptance test.

"Fire alarm installation takes a few days"

The hands-on work in a small Bedford Park store may take a few days. The project does not. The FDNY's own tip sheet puts the time from plan submission to final inspection and approval at roughly 20 to 24 weeks, and the initial plan review alone can take up to 8 weeks.

"A trouble light means you need a new panel"

A trouble signal means the panel has detected a condition that stops part of the system from working: a ground fault, an open circuit, a low battery, a lost phone line or a failed device. Most are repairable.

Why is my fire alarm panel showing trouble? That is the question supers ask most.
