Why Orange County Buildings Need a Fire Alarm That Speaks Clearly and Reports Fast

Orange County buildings need commercial fire alarm installation that does two jobs well: tell the people inside exactly what is happening, and tell the fire department at once. Both jobs were tested in public in the first week of 2026. On January 4, a commercial water-flow alarm was the first signal of a third-floor fire at Senior Horizons at Silver Lake on Bert Crawford Road in Middletown; the call went to a second alarm with mutual aid, and while nobody was hurt, 20 apartments were left unlivable and the Orange County Arson Investigation Task Force took over the cause. The next afternoon, January 5, roof welding at the Galleria at Crystal Run in the Town of Wallkill sent sparks into a storage room in Dick's Sporting Goods. Smoke filled the mall, several hundred people were evacuated, sprinklers sent water to the lower level, and one shopper told News 12 what the alarm sounded like from the inside: "Somebody said we had a code red."

That shopper's sentence is the clearest argument for good fire alarm design we have heard all year. A system that only makes noise leaves people guessing. In a mall, a hotel, a casino floor or a large senior building, the fire alarm voice evacuation installation, the placement of the speakers and strobes, and the way the message is recorded decide whether people walk calmly to the right exit or stand around asking each other what a code red is. And in buildings with sprinklers, the sprinkler waterflow switch monitoring and the central station connection decide how many minutes pass before the first engine is on the way. At Silver Lake the waterflow alarm did exactly that job.

The building stock is also changing quickly. Orange County has become the warehouse county of the Hudson Valley: Medline's nearly 1.4-million-square-foot distribution center on Route 416 in Montgomery and Amazon's roughly 1,015,740-square-foot building at 635 International Drive are two of the largest buildings ever put up in the county, and more are proposed. At the same time, older retail is taking on new uses. Resorts World Hudson Valley opened a 60,000-square-foot gaming floor with more than 1,200 slot machines inside the Newburgh Mall, which has been open since 1980. Every one of those changes of use, additions and conversions runs through a fire alarm plan review somewhere in the county.

And there is no single "somewhere." Orange County has 3 cities, 21 towns and 19 villages, and each one either runs its own code enforcement or arranges for it. The City of Newburgh has a Bureau of Fire Prevention whose chief must inspect and accept a private alarm system before it connects to the municipal system; the Town of Newburgh next door has two full-time fire inspectors who do annual inspections and plan reviews out of its Code Compliance Department; the City of Port Jervis publishes its own fire alarm permit application with a stated turnaround. Commercial fire alarm installation in Orange County starts with a simple question that a surprising number of owners get wrong: which of the 43 municipalities is this building actually in?

Distribution centers: designing for a ceiling 40 feet up

Warehouse fire alarm installation at Orange County scale is mostly about getting reliable detection and clear notification across a huge open volume. Spot smoke detectors on a roof deck 40 feet up are slow to see smoke and miserable to test, so designers often specify beam smoke detector installation, where a projected beam smoke detector installation spans hundreds of feet between a transmitter and a reflector, or aspirating smoke detection installation, where an air sampling smoke detection system draws air through pipes back to a sensor at floor level. Either way, the sprinkler system usually carries most of the protection, which makes fire alarm sprinkler monitoring installation, sprinkler waterflow switch monitoring and sprinkler tamper switch monitoring the most important points on the panel. Notification has to reach someone on a forklift with hearing protection at the far end of an aisle, so strobes and horns are spaced and synchronized carefully, and the fire alarm synchronization module installation is part of the design, not an afterthought.

Older malls taking new uses: the change-of-use trap

When a mall store becomes a gaming floor, a gym, an indoor play space or a church, the occupancy changes even if the walls do not. A change of occupancy is one of the clearest points where the local code official asks whether the fire alarm system still fits the building. In practice that can mean a new zone or a new voice evacuation area tied back to the mall's main panel, new strobes for the visual notification the new use needs, and a new round of testing with the landlord's fire alarm company present. Resorts World's 60,000-square-foot gaming floor inside the Newburgh Mall is the largest local example of a new assembly-type use moving into an older retail building, but the same question comes up every time a plaza storefront on Route 300 or Route 211 turns into something new.

River-city mixed use: the people upstairs

In the City of Newburgh, Middletown and Port Jervis, the classic building is three or four stories of masonry with a business on the ground floor and apartments above. On July 25, 2024, a fire in a third-floor bedroom at 103 Liberty Street in Newburgh set off both the fire alarm and the sprinkler flow alarm; one sprinkler head put the fire out, and the tailor shop downstairs was closed by the water damage instead of the fire. That is the system working as intended, and it is a good model for mixed use building fire alarm installation in the older cities: monitored sprinklers, smoke detection in the common stair and halls, and notification that reaches every apartment. On Broadway near William Street in August 2025, City of Newburgh Fire Chief F.J. Spinelli said crews were "severely hampered by the amount of personal belongings and debris in the apartment" during a fire that sent seven firefighters for treatment. Early detection is what buys time in buildings like that.

Senior housing and nursing homes: defend in place

Nursing home fire alarm installation and assisted living fire alarm installation follow a different logic from an office. Many residents cannot walk out quickly, so the building is designed with smoke compartments and doors that close automatically, and the fire alarm is what closes them. Fire alarm door release integration and fire alarm magnetic door holder release are inspected closely in these buildings, along with the speaker and strobe coverage in resident rooms and the signal that tells staff which compartment to clear first. Upgrades are phased one wing at a time so residents never lose protection overnight.

Two Orange County multifamily fires show why alarm audibility at night matters. At 2:47 a.m. on June 15, 2026, a fire at the Tuxedo Heights condominiums on Parkview Drive in Tuxedo destroyed eight units and left the building condemned; residents fled, in the superintendent's words, "in only their pajamas and flip-flops." In March 2024 in Kiryas Joel, flames reached the third and fourth floors of a building at 7 Stropkov Court that was half occupied and half under construction behind a firewall, and six families, about 60 people, were evacuated. Apartment building fire alarm installation in buildings like these is about corridor and stair detection, sounders that wake people inside their units, and careful phasing when part of a building is occupied while the rest is still a construction site.

Compliance callouts for Orange County businesses

  • Drawings first. A New York State Professional Engineer or Registered Architect designs and seals the fire alarm construction drawings before anything is filed.
  • Licensed installer. New York State requires a security or fire alarm installer license from the Department of State, Division of Licensing Services, for anyone in the business of installing or servicing fire detection systems.
  • Local approval. The city, town or village code enforcement office reviews the plans, issues the permit and witnesses the acceptance inspection under the Uniform Code.
  • Accessibility. Visual notification is required where the code and the ADA call for it, so fire alarm ADA strobe installation is part of most commercial projects.
  • Records on site. Inspection and testing reports, as-builts and the record of completion stay with the building for the next inspector.
Field Notes

Field Notes: Read the Tax Map Before You Read the Panel

The first thing we do on an Orange County fire alarm job is confirm the municipality, and it is not a formality. A "Newburgh" address may be in the City of Newburgh, where the Bureau of Fire Prevention accepts alarm systems, or in the Town of Newburgh, where the Code Compliance Department's fire inspectors do the plan review. A "Middletown" address may actually be in the Town of Wallkill. A building near Monroe may be in the town, the village or the Village of Kiryas Joel. Filing with the wrong office costs weeks, so we check the tax map before we look at the first device.

The second thing is distance and timing. Orange County runs from the Hudson at Cornwall to the Delaware at Port Jervis, and the local fire inspector may cover a large territory alone. Acceptance tests are booked around that person's calendar, with the sprinkler contractor, the elevator company and the HVAC technician all confirmed the week before. A failed test that needs a second visit can push an opening back, so our pre-test is a full rehearsal of the inspector's test, device by device.

The third thing is the federal neighbors. West Point and Stewart Air National Guard Base, home of the 105th Airlift Wing, are federal installations with their own fire protection programs, and buildings inside them are not filed with a town building department. When an owner says their project is "near Stewart," we confirm whether it is on the civilian airport property, on base, or on private land outside the fence, because the approval path is different for each.

Your Questions Answered

Fire Alarm Installation in Orange County: Questions Answered

Cost and quotes

Our Montgomery distribution lease makes the tenant pay for fire alarm changes. What should we pin down before signing?

Pin down who owns the existing system, which changes your racking and layout will trigger, and who pays for the drawings. A floor plan alone does not show what decides the fire alarm scope: the sprinkler zones, the racking heights, the ceiling height, the HVAC units that need shutdown, the doors that need release and the monitoring path. Those come from the engineer's sealed drawings. A fire alarm installation quote written before the drawings exist is a guess, and guesses on buildings this size are expensive in both directions. We can give you a written fire alarm installation estimate of the installation scope once the design is far enough along to count devices, and a firm fire alarm installation bid once the drawings are sealed.

Why does a fire alarm quote for our Port Jervis building list the engineer separately from the installer?

Because they are separate professional services, and seeing them apart is how you compare offers fairly. Line quotes up item by item: one may include the engineer's drawings, the permit filing, the central station communicator and the acceptance test, while the other quietly leaves them out or assumes the existing wiring can be reused. Ask each company to list the panel model, the number and type of devices, who prepares and files the drawings, who attends the test and what the warranty covers. A commercial fire alarm quote that cannot answer those questions is not complete enough to compare.

Quality and trust

How do we check that the fire alarm company working on our Middletown building is actually licensed in New York?

Ask for the company's New York State security or fire alarm installer license number and look it up with the Department of State, Division of Licensing Services. Every installer employee also has to be registered with the state. Then ask who the installer of record will be on the local permit. A legitimate fire alarm company will hand you those details without hesitation; a company that answers with an electrician's license number alone is telling you something.

The last company left our Goshen office with no as-built drawings. Is that normal?

No. After acceptance the owner should receive fire alarm as built drawings, the programming record, battery calculations and the completed NFPA 72 record of completion. Without them, every future repair starts from scratch and the next inspector has nothing to compare against. If you are missing them, a survey can rebuild the record: we trace circuits, list every addressable point and produce updated drawings for your files.

Doing it yourself versus hiring a professional

Our school district's facilities crew wants to swap old pull stations themselves over the summer. Is that allowed?

Installing and servicing fire alarm devices is licensed work in New York, and swapping a manual station means disconnecting and reconnecting a supervised circuit, then testing it. A district crew can certainly help with access, scheduling and patching, but the devices themselves should be installed and tested by a licensed fire alarm company, and the change documented for the code official. Fire alarm pull station testing after replacement is part of the job, not an extra.

Can a New Windsor contractor's electrician run the fire alarm wiring and leave the rest to a vendor?

Often, yes, and on larger jobs it is common. The electrician handles fire alarm conduit installation and fire alarm cable pulling to the drawings, and the fire alarm company handles fire alarm device termination, programming and testing. What matters is that the cable type, the circuit class and the routing match the sealed drawings, and that one company takes responsibility for the system at the acceptance test. We coordinate with the electrician from the first walk-through so the pull is done once.

Technical

Why does our high-bay warehouse in Wallkill keep showing beam detector trouble in the summer?

Projected beams depend on the transmitter and reflector staying lined up. Steel buildings move as they heat and cool, and a beam that was aligned on a cool morning can drift out by a hot afternoon. Dust and condensation on the lenses cause the same symptom. The fix is a proper alignment with the building at working temperature, a check of the mounting, and sometimes moving the beam to a stiffer structural member. Beam detector troubles that come and go with the weather are one of the most common fire alarm intermittent trouble calls in big warehouses.

Does a sprinkler waterflow signal in our Newburgh building have to go to the fire department automatically?

Where the code requires the sprinkler system to be supervised, the waterflow and the valve supervisory signals go to an approved supervising station, which then notifies the fire department. The Silver Lake fire in Middletown in January 2026 was reported first by a commercial water-flow alarm, which is exactly what that connection is for. The specific requirements for your building come from the sealed design and the code official's review.

Our casino-style venue inside a mall wants voice messages instead of horns. What does that change?

Quite a lot. Fire alarm voice evacuation installation uses speakers and amplifiers instead of horns, a recorded message for each scenario, and often a microphone for live announcements. Speakers have to be placed so the message is intelligible over crowd noise and music, which is tested with instruments, not by ear. The voice system also has to coordinate with the mall's main fire alarm so the right areas hear the right message at the right time.

Residential and commercial

We are converting a former retail building in Middletown into apartments over stores. Do we need a whole new fire alarm?

A conversion from retail to mixed use is a change of occupancy, and the fire alarm is designed for the new use. Sometimes parts of the old system can stay, but the apartments, stairs and corridors usually need new detection and notification, and the system has to be filed and tested as a whole. The design professional decides how much of the existing system can be reused; we survey what is there so that decision is based on facts.

Does a four-story condominium in Monroe fall under commercial fire alarm rules?

Larger multifamily buildings are covered by the fire alarm requirements in the building and fire codes, which are based on the number of stories, the number of units and the presence of common corridors. When a building meets those thresholds, the fire alarm system is designed, installed, tested and inspected under NFPA 72 just like any commercial system. We work on those code-required systems; we do not install household smoke alarms in houses or two-family homes.

Our church in Warwick is starting to rent its hall for weddings. Does that affect the fire alarm?

It can. Renting the hall for large events can change how the code classifies the space and how many people it holds, which may trigger requirements for manual stations, notification or a voice system that did not apply before. Talk to your code enforcement officer early and have a design professional look at the occupant load before the first booking.

Complaints and problems

Our tenants in a Newburgh walk-up are pulling detector heads off the ceiling because of false alarms. What should the owner do?

Stop the cause rather than fighting the tenants. Detectors placed too close to kitchens or bathrooms, the wrong detector type and dirty detectors are the usual reasons for nuisance alarms in older apartment buildings. A survey of the device locations, sensitivity readings and the panel's event history will show where the alarms are coming from. Removed detectors are a serious safety problem and an inspection deficiency; replacing the wrong devices with the right ones in the right places is the lasting fix.

The company that monitored our Goshen building stopped answering. How do we switch without losing protection?

FAQ

Orange County Fire Alarm Installation FAQ

Where do your crews travel from to reach Orange County jobs?
+

No. Our office is at 460 E Fordham Road in the Bronx, and our crews travel to every part of Orange County from there. We book surveys within 2 hours of your request.

Is this page about Orange County, California?
+

No. This page covers Orange County, New York: Newburgh, Middletown, Port Jervis, Goshen and the surrounding towns and villages in the Hudson Valley. California rules and agencies do not apply here.

Our address says Newburgh. Do we file with the city or the town?
+

It depends on which side of the city line the tax parcel sits. The City of Newburgh has its own Bureau of Fire Prevention; the Town of Newburgh reviews through its Code Compliance Department. We confirm from the tax map before filing.

Do buildings at West Point or Stewart Air National Guard Base go through a town building department?
+

No. Federal installations follow their own fire protection programs. Private buildings outside the fence, including those on civilian airport land, follow the local and state process instead.

What signal reports a sprinkler activation to the fire department?
+

The waterflow switch on the sprinkler system. It sends an alarm to the fire alarm panel, which reports to the central station, which notifies the fire department. Valve tamper switches send supervisory signals the same way.

Roofers are welding above our Wallkill store next week. What should happen with the fire alarm?
+

Hot work needs a plan: the landlord or contractor arranges the hot-work permit, any detectors that must be bypassed are disabled only for the work area and only for the work period, the monitoring company is told, and a fire watch stays after the work ends. The January 2026 Galleria fire started with roof welding sparks reaching a storage room.

How many people need to be on site for an acceptance test of a warehouse system?
+

At least the fire alarm technicians, the sprinkler contractor and anyone responsible for interfaced equipment such as fire pumps, doors or HVAC, plus the owner's representative and the code official. Big buildings need extra hands to activate devices across long distances.

Do you replace phone-line dialers in Orange County buildings?
+

Yes. We install cellular or IP communicators that report to a central station and test them end to end, coordinating the switch with your monitoring company so the building stays monitored.

Can part of a building be occupied while the rest is still under fire alarm construction?
+

Sometimes, if the code official approves a phasing plan and the occupied part has working protection. The plan is agreed before work starts, not improvised once tenants move in.

Who should we tell before our annual fire alarm test?
+

The monitoring company, so test signals are not dispatched; the occupants, so nobody evacuates or ignores a real alarm; and the local fire department or code office if they ask to be notified. Our work is NFPA 72 commercial only, so these tests are always in commercial, mixed-use or code-required multifamily buildings.

What should we keep on site for the municipal fire inspector?
+

The as-built drawings, the record of completion, the latest inspection and testing reports, any deficiency corrections and the monitoring contract details. We hand these over after every job.

Do you need the engineer's drawings before you visit?
+

No. We can survey first and help you scope what the design professional needs to draw. For new systems and major changes, the sealed drawings come before the final quote and the filing.
