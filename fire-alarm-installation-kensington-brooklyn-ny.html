Why Kensington Fire Alarm Work Follows Its Two Car Corridors and Its Parkway

City Planning's history of the area says that when the 1961 zoning code was approved, "modern elevator apartment buildings defined Ocean Parkway. Church Avenue at the north end and Ditmas Avenue at the south are shopping streets with apartments over stores.

The parkway came first. Fire Alarm Repair in Kensington: Finding Out Whether the Building Is Still Protected

Fire alarm repair in Kensington starts with one question: is the building still protected right now? A trouble beep in an Ocean Parkway co-op lobby may mean one dirty detector; the same beep in a cellar under a Church Avenue store may mean the panel has lost its central station connection. Why is my fire alarm panel showing trouble? Because it has found something wrong with its own wiring, power or devices; trouble is not a fire signal. In prewar parkway buildings, look for a swollen battery or a detector painted over during an apartment turnover. In Church and Coney Island Avenue buildings, a store fit-out is often to blame: a ceiling hung below a detector or a cable cut by a sign installer. A fire alarm panel trouble light, a fire alarm system trouble condition, a fire alarm trouble signal repair and a fire alarm panel beeping repair all begin by reading the event history and testing the circuit it names.

What causes a fire alarm ground fault? Current leaking from a circuit to conduit, a box or a wet wall. Your Questions Answered

Fire Alarm Questions From Kensington Garage Owners, Co-op Boards, Congregations and Shopkeepers, Answered

These are questions people in Kensington bring to a first survey. The answers are general; the design professional on your project applies the code to your building.

Our auto body shop on McDonald Avenue may be sold if the South of Prospect plan rezones the block. Is it worth fixing the shop's fire alarm trouble now?

Yes. No heights or districts have been drawn yet, so a sale could be years away, and until then the shop is full of fuel, paint and tires and empty every night. Fix what keeps the system reporting, such as batteries, the communicator and any open circuit, and keep the records for a buyer.

We would rather build apartments over our McDonald Avenue repair garage than sell it. What does the fire alarm have to do once people sleep above the service bays?

It has to protect a residential building with a hazardous use beneath it: heat detection in the bays that tolerates exhaust and welding, corridor and stair detection upstairs, notification that reaches residents and monitoring of any sprinkler system. The garage's small existing panel almost certainly will not carry that design.

Hundreds of worshippers pray on the Chester Avenue open street on Fridays. Do the people outside count toward our building's occupant load?

Occupant load is calculated for the building's rooms from their area and use, so people praying in the street are not counted in it. What the outdoor crowd does change is how the building is used: if the prayer hall inside is filled to capacity every week, the design professional should check that the room's real occupant load, not the number on an old certificate, is what the fire alarm design is based on, and that people in the street can hear or see the evacuation signal at the doors.

Smoke from the fryers in our ground-floor sweet shop drifts up the stair and sets off the corridor detector on the apartment floor above. Whose equipment has to change?

Usually the shop's. The hood should capture smoke at the fryers, the shop's door to the shared stair should close and seal, and makeup air should not push smoke into the building. A detector in an apartment corridor protects sleeping residents and should not be swapped for a less sensitive one to suit a kitchen.

Our sixteen-story Ocean Parkway co-op went up in 1965. Does an older high-rise have to rebuild its fire alarm to today's code?

Not simply because it is old. An existing system generally continues under the rules it was approved under, as long as it is maintained and tested. What triggers new work is change: a major alteration, a panel replacement filed as a new system, a violation order, or a change of use on some floors. When the board does replace the panel, the design professional decides how far the new design must go, and that is the time to plan voice communication and fire command center work properly rather than piecemeal.

Our elevator apartment house on upper Ocean Parkway has a parkway lobby and a side-street service door. Which entrance should the annunciator face?

The one the FDNY will use. The design professional confirms the responding entrance during plan review; on the parkway that is usually the main lobby off the service road, but a building whose panel sits near a side-street door may need a remote annunciator at the front so the first officer is not sent around the block. Label the service door and keep the panel room key where the drawings say it is.

Firefighters stretched hose through our lobby during a neighbor's fire, and our panel has shown trouble ever since. What probably happened?

Something got wet, knocked or opened: a lobby device soaked by runoff, a cellar junction box that took water, a door holder pulled off the wall, or a detector that ingested smoke. The panel's event history names the device or circuit; have it tested and cleaned or replaced, and tell the insurer.

A five-story building replacing a one-story store on Church Avenue will have shops, a community facility floor and apartments. Can the apartments open before the community floor's fire alarm is finished?

Only if the approved design and the FDNY allow the building to be accepted in phases, and that has to be planned at the design stage. Base-building devices required on the community facility floor, including the corridors and the protection that serves the apartments above, usually have to be installed and tested before anyone moves in. It depends on the design. Many apartment house systems keep devices in corridors, stairs, lobbies and service rooms, but some designs place notification or detection inside units, and cable routes sometimes pass through them. If apartment access is needed, the proposal says so, the board or landlord gives written notice, and the work is scheduled in short visits with the space left clean the same day.

Why does the FDNY want to see a central station contract before it tests a new Kensington system?
+

Because a required system is not finished until its signals reach an FDNY-approved central station. The FDNY's list of documents for a fire alarm test request includes the central station contract, and during the test the inspector confirms that alarms, supervisory and trouble signals actually arrive there. Sign the contract early; a changeover between central stations needs its own lead time.

The cellar under our Church Avenue grocery is used only by the store. Whose fire alarm covers it?
+

If the building has a code-required system, the cellar is part of the building and is covered as the approved drawings show, usually by the building's panel, whoever rents the space. The lease should say who pays for devices that serve only the store and who must keep stock clear of them. If the building has no system, ask a design professional whether the cellar's use and size trigger one.

What does a Kensington owner get in writing after a fire alarm survey?
+

A short report of what we found: the panel make and condition, what the event history shows, devices that are damaged, blocked or missing, records that exist and records that do not, and whether the current use matches what the system was designed for. Then a written proposal that lists design, filing support, installation, programming and testing separately and names the certified company and electrician.

Do Kensington buildings in Community Board 12 and Community Board 14 follow different fire alarm rules?
+

No. Wikipedia notes that most of Kensington sits in Community Board 12, with some eastern tracts in Community Board 14, but community boards advise on land use and local services; they do not set fire alarm requirements. Every building follows the same citywide Building Code, Fire Code and FDNY procedures. The board matters only when a rezoning or variance for your lot goes through public review.

Does a Kensington building's FDNY Letter of Approval ever expire?
+

The Letter of Approval covers the system as it was approved and tested. It stays meaningful only as long as the system is kept that way: inspected and tested on schedule, connected to its central station and not changed without a filing. Adding devices for a new tenant, replacing the panel or changing the central station all need their own approval steps, so an old letter does not cover a system that has since been altered.

Does a one-story auto repair shop on McDonald Avenue need a building fire alarm at all?
+

Often not under its current use. The code's fire alarm triggers for factory and storage buildings depend on stories, the number of people and floor area, and a small single-story shop may fall below them. Some shops keep a monitored system for their own protection anyway. The answer changes as soon as the building adds apartments, a large showroom, a mezzanine office or a different use, so check with a design professional before any of those.
