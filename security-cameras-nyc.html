Who Buys What

Who Buys Security Cameras Across the Five Boroughs

New York City buyers are not one market. A Bronx bodega owner, an Upper West Side co-op board, a Bed-Stuy brownstone owner and a Maspeth warehouse manager all search for the same words, and each needs a different shopping list. Here is how the orders we see break down, and what each type of buyer usually takes home.

New York City facts that shape what people buy

  • 40,835 retail theft complaints citywide in 2026 through November 15, down 12.4% from 46,631 at the same point in 2025 (NYPD CompStat, week of November 9–15, 2026, preliminary).
  • 9,794 burglaries citywide in the same period, down 13.6% from 11,332 (NYPD CompStat).
  • 88,419 petit larceny complaints year to date, down 4.3% from 92,381 (NYPD CompStat).
  • About 54% of the city's homes, roughly two million, were built before 1947, so masonry walls and cellar equipment rooms are the norm (Citizens Housing & Planning Council, from the 2023 NYC Housing and Vacancy Survey).
  • 62% of housing units sit in buildings of six or more apartments, and in Manhattan the figure is 97% (CHPC), which makes most residential cameras a building, board or landlord purchase.

Sources: NYPD CompStat citywide report; CHPC housing stock analysis. Crime is down, and that is good news. But forty thousand retail thefts in about ten and a half months is still a heavy load on small stores, and a camera is what turns a complaint into usable evidence.

Bodegas, delis and corner stores

Deli security cameras and bodega security cameras are the single biggest category we sell in the city. The typical corner store order is six to twelve cameras: one on each street frontage under the awning, one on the door from inside, one over the register, a cash register camera close enough to read bills and the screen, one per aisle, one on the cooler wall, and one in the stockroom or cellar. The recorder usually goes in a locked cabinet in the back or the basement, with a monitor by the counter so customers can see they are on camera.

What corner stores buy most is durability and night performance. Turret cameras under an awning avoid the infrared glare that a glass dome can get, color night vision helps when police ask what color a jacket was, and a point of sale surveillance camera set at the right angle is worth more than two extra wide shots. Owners who already have an old analog DVR often buy an HD analog upgrade that reuses the existing coax rather than opening walls.

Walk-ups, co-ops and condo buildings

Apartment building CCTV is usually bought by a landlord, a managing agent or a board, and the shopping list follows the path a stranger takes through the building: the street door, the vestibule and mailboxes, the lobby, the stair halls, the elevator, the roof door, the cellar door, the laundry room and the trash area. Lobby security cameras, hallway security cameras, stairwell security cameras and elevator security cameras are bought by the dozen for buildings like this, almost always with a 16- or 32-channel recorder.

Co-op security cameras and condo security cameras come with an approval step. Lobbies, halls and facades are common elements, so a shareholder or unit owner cannot simply buy a camera and mount it outside the door. Boards usually want a written camera plan, the camera models, the retention period and a policy on who can view footage. We supply all of that with the quote. Individual owners and renters who want a camera inside their own unit, or a front door security camera the building allows, are the other half of these orders. If you are buying for a single unit, our affordable security cameras in New York page covers the low-cost end of the market.

Brownstones, townhouses and two-family homes

Brownstone security cameras and townhouse security cameras are a different shopping list again. Owners want the stoop and front door, the areaway under the stoop, the garden or rear yard, the back fence and sometimes the roof. Home security cameras here need to look tidy on a historic facade, survive salt and slush on the stoop, and record to a recorder hidden inside the house. Most brownstone owners choose a four- to eight-camera PoE system with turret cameras and a small NVR in a closet or the cellar.

House security cameras for two-family homes and detached houses in the outer boroughs add the driveway, the garage and the side yard, so driveway security cameras, garage security cameras and backyard security cameras with longer night range make up most of those orders. Residential security cameras on these properties often pair with a doorbell security camera at the front door.

Retail corridors and storefronts

Retail store security cameras on busy commercial strips need to deal with glass fronts, bright sidewalks and crowds. The biggest problem is backlight: a dark entrance with a bright street behind it turns faces into silhouettes, so we steer retail buyers toward cameras with strong wide dynamic range aimed inward at the door, plus a varifocal camera that can be tightened on the register. Pharmacies, phone stores, laundromats, beauty supply stores and clothing shops all buy from this list, and many add a second recorder or extra drive so they can keep a month of footage.

Warehouses, garages and lots

Security cameras for warehouses, loading dock security cameras, parking garage security cameras and parking lot security cameras are the long-range end of our catalog. These buyers want bullet cameras and long-range domes with 100- to 120-foot night range, varifocal or motorized zoom lenses for gates and plates, PTZ cameras for big yards, and 32- or 64-channel NVRs with large drive arrays. Logistics facility security cameras and storage facility security cameras also lean on smart detection, so the yard camera alerts on a person or vehicle after hours and not on every blowing bag.

Borough by borough, briefly

Manhattan orders lean toward co-ops, condos, offices and storefronts under apartment houses, with tight building access rules and lots of board approvals; our security cameras in Manhattan page goes into those buildings in detail. Brooklyn orders mix brownstones and row houses with walk-ups, new condos and industrial lofts; see security cameras in Brooklyn for that borough's buying guide. Queens, the Bronx and Staten Island buyers lean more toward driveways, two-family homes, strip-center stores, auto shops and lots, and we deliver and install there on the same terms.

Outdoor cameras and outdoor surveillance cameras

Outdoor security cameras in New York face rain, wind off the rivers, road salt, soot and the occasional broom handle. Buy weatherproof security cameras with a stated ingress rating: IP66 and IP67 are the ratings on our house-brand outdoor packages, and IP67 security cameras are sealed against dust and temporary immersion. For anything a person can reach from the sidewalk or a stoop, look for vandal resistant security cameras; IK10 security cameras carry the top impact rating, and we only call a camera IK10 when its spec sheet says so. Outdoor cameras also need the right night vision for the distance: an awning camera covers 20 feet, a yard or lot camera may need 100 feet or more.

Outdoor surveillance cameras are where buying cheap costs the most. A camera that fogs after the first freeze, or a plastic mount that cracks in January, means a ladder trip and a gap in the footage. That is why our outdoor recommendations start with the housing, the mount and the operating temperature, and only then get to megapixels. More on winter-rated cameras further down this page.

Indoor cameras and indoor surveillance cameras

Indoor security cameras have an easier life but harder light. Store ceilings have bright light panels that flare a lens, hallways have a bright window at one end, and cellars have almost no light at all. Indoor surveillance cameras for a drop ceiling are usually small turrets or domes; for a stair hall or a basement, a turret with infrared or a full-color low light camera works best. Indoor wireless cameras are fine for a single room in a modern apartment, but in a store, an office or a building we sell wired cameras because they keep recording when the Wi-Fi does not.

By resolution: 4K and 8MP, 5MP, 2K and 4MP, 2MP

Every camera quote we send goes out as a 2K and a 4K option, so you can see what the extra detail costs before you decide. Here is what each tier is for:

  • 4K security cameras (8MP). 3840 by 2160 pixels. Buy 4K CCTV cameras and 8MP IP cameras for the shots where detail matters most: the door, the register, a gate, a driveway or a long hallway where you need a face at the far end. Ultra HD surveillance cameras also let you zoom into a recording after the fact without the picture falling apart.
  • 5MP security cameras. A middle tier that suits most aisle, yard and hallway views. 5MP IP cameras and 5MP analog cameras appear in several of our house-brand packages.
  • 2K security cameras (4MP). What most of the industry sells as 2K is a 4MP sensor. It is our usual “2K option” in a quote: sharp enough for general coverage, lighter on storage than 4K.
  • 2MP security cameras (1080p). Our entry tier. High definition security cameras at 1080p still cover a small store, a stockroom or a basement well when the camera is close to what it watches.
  • 12MP security cameras. A specialty tier, usually fisheye or panoramic models, ordered for wide open floors. Ask and we'll quote what is available.

By connection: PoE, wired analog, and WiFi

PoE security cameras (Power over Ethernet) run on one Cat6 cable each, which carries both video and power from a PoE NVR or PoE switch. They are our default for new work, and they are what most people mean by IP camera systems or network security cameras. Wired security cameras on coax are the HD analog side of the catalog, recording to a DVR; they are the smart buy when a building already has good coax runs. WiFi security cameras and wireless cameras send video over Wi-Fi but still need power at each camera, which surprises a lot of buyers. In masonry buildings they are the least reliable option; we sell them for single rooms and for spots where no cable path exists.

When people ask us to buy PoE cameras, we also ask how many: that number decides whether the cameras plug straight into the back of a PoE NVR or need a separate PoE switch, and how much power budget is needed. If you are looking for where to find PoE security cameras in NYC with a recorder that matches, this is the conversation that gets it right the first time.

By feature: night vision, deterrence, audio and AI

Night vision security cameras. Infrared security cameras see in total darkness in black and white; color night vision cameras and full color night vision cameras use a little ambient light or a built-in white light to keep color after dark. Starlight security cameras and other low light security cameras are tuned to hold color at very low light levels. Our house-brand packages list night range from 60 feet up to 120 feet depending on the series.

Active deterrence security cameras. Several house-brand kits include cameras with a built-in alarm, siren and flashing lights; the D Series deterrent kits add two-way talk and human and vehicle detection. These are popular for rear yards, roll-gates and lots after hours.

Audio recording and two way audio security cameras. Many of our cameras have a built-in microphone, and some have a speaker for two-way talk. Before you turn audio on in New York, read the legal section below: recording conversations has its own rules.

AI security cameras. AI human detection cameras and AI vehicle detection cameras (security cameras with person detection and security cameras with vehicle detection) cut false alerts from headlights, rain and cats, and smart motion detection cameras let you search recordings for people or cars instead of scrolling. Thermal security cameras detect heat rather than light and are a specialty order for perimeters and large yards. Motion detection cameras are standard: every recorder we sell can record on motion, on a schedule, or around the clock.

Recorders & Accessories

NVRs, DVRs, Hard Drives, PoE Switches and Camera Accessories

The camera on the wall gets the attention, but the recorder and the parts behind it decide whether you have footage when you need it. This is the half of the store that installers, superintendents and repeat customers buy most often.

Network video recorders and digital video recorders

A security camera NVR (network video recorder) records IP cameras over network cable; a PoE NVR has PoE ports on the back that power the cameras directly, and a 4K NVR can record 8MP cameras at full resolution. A security camera DVR (digital video recorder) records HD analog cameras over coax. We sell both in the channel counts buildings actually use:

  • 4 channel NVR – a small store, an apartment, a brownstone front and back.
  • 8 channel NVR – the most common size for a deli, a restaurant or a house with a driveway and yard.
  • 16 channel NVR – walk-up buildings, larger stores, small offices, with room to grow.
  • 32 channel NVR – elevator buildings, supermarkets, warehouses and multi-floor offices.
  • 64 channel NVR – large buildings, campuses and logistics sites, usually with several drives.

When you buy an NVR, size it for the cameras you will want in two years, not just today. If you are wondering how to choose an NVR, the short list is: enough channels, enough PoE power or a matching switch, support for the resolution of your cameras, enough drive bays for the retention you want, and an app you are willing to use. If you want to buy a DVR to replace a failed one without touching the cameras, send us the model of the old DVR and the number of cameras; we match the analog format so the existing cameras keep working.

CCTV hard drives and surveillance storage

Surveillance hard drives are built to write video around the clock, which a desktop drive is not designed for. We stock the sizes most buyers need: a 4TB surveillance hard drive for a four-camera system, an 8TB surveillance hard drive for an eight-camera store, and 16TB surveillance storage, as one large drive or several, for 16 to 32 cameras or a month of 4K footage. CCTV hard drives in a warm cellar wear out, so we also sell replacements and set up drive health alerts on the recorders we install. The storage math section below shows how to work out the size.

PoE switches, injectors, power supplies and adapters

Buy a PoE switch for security cameras when you have more cameras than PoE ports on the NVR, or when cameras are far from the recorder. A security camera PoE injector powers one camera where there is no PoE switch. For analog systems, a CCTV power supply box feeds many cameras from one cabinet, and a CCTV power adapter powers a single camera. We size all of these from your camera list.

A security camera mount is not an afterthought in New York. Brick, stone, stucco, metal awnings and drop ceilings each need a different fastener and bracket. A camera junction box, also called a CCTV junction box, sits behind the camera and hides the cable splice and connectors from the weather and from anyone with wire cutters; on a facade it is the difference between a neat install and a tangle of exposed cable.

Storage Math

How Much Recording Time You Get: Storage Math for NYC Systems

The question behind “how much storage do security cameras need” is really “how many days of footage will I have when the police ask.” You can estimate it with one formula. This is an estimate method, not a guarantee: real bitrates change with the scene, the light, the compression setting and how much motion there is.

Storage = bitrate × seconds per day × days to keep × number of cameras.

Worked example: eight cameras, two weeks of recording

  • Assume each camera streams at 4 megabits per second (a planning figure for a busy 4K or 5MP view; check the camera's actual setting).
  • 4 megabits per second is 0.5 megabytes per second.
  • 0.5 MB × 86,400 seconds in a day = 43,200 MB, about 43 GB per camera per day, recording around the clock.
  • Eight cameras: 43 GB × 8 = about 346 GB per day.
  • Fourteen days: 346 GB × 14 = about 4.8 TB. An 8TB surveillance hard drive covers it with room to spare.
  • Thirty days: 346 GB × 30 = about 10.4 TB, so 12TB to 16TB of surveillance storage.

Halve the bitrate to 2 megabits per second, typical of a quieter 2K view, and the same eight cameras need about 2.4 TB for two weeks and about 5.2 TB for a month. Record on motion instead of around the clock and the numbers drop further, though we prefer continuous recording on doors and registers so nothing falls between motion events.

Two weeks or one month?

Our installed four-camera packages are set up for two weeks of recording. Security cameras with two weeks recording suit most homes and small stores, because thefts and incidents are usually noticed within days. Security cameras with one month recording suit buildings where a tenant complaint or an insurance claim can arrive weeks later, and stores that want a full billing cycle on record. Compression helps: cameras that support H.265 security cameras or H.265 Plus CCTV settings, where the spec sheet lists them, store the same picture in less space than older H.264. If you want to run your own numbers by camera count and days, our security camera calculator does the planning math.

NYC Rules

Security Camera Laws and Privacy Rules in New York City

This section explains the rules that come up most when people buy cameras in New York City. It is general information with sources, not legal advice; for a specific situation, talk to a lawyer.

Are security cameras legal in NYC?

Yes. Video security cameras are legal for homes and businesses in New York City, and the security camera laws that matter are mostly about three things: recording sound, using biometric technology such as facial recognition, and mounting cameras on property you do not control. Video of a public sidewalk or of your own store floor is ordinary; pointing a camera into a neighbor's window, a bathroom or a changing area is not. We never sell or install a camera for bathrooms, changing areas or other private spaces.

Audio recording: NY Penal Law §250

Many cameras in our catalog have built-in microphones, which raises the most common CCTV regulations question we get. Under New York Penal Law §250.00, “mechanical overhearing of a conversation” means the intentional overhearing or recording of a conversation without the consent of at least one party to it, by a person not present. Under §250.05, eavesdropping is a class E felony. In plain terms, a camera that records conversations the owner is not part of, such as customers talking at a counter after hours or tenants talking in a hallway, is where audio becomes a legal risk.

Our practice: we set up audio-capable cameras with audio recording switched off unless the owner decides otherwise, and we explain this before handover. Audio recording security cameras still have their place; two-way talk lets an owner speak through a deterrent camera, and video with audio can be useful where every party has consented. Make that decision deliberately, not by default.

Facial recognition and the NYC biometric identifier law

New York City has its own rule for biometric data in businesses. NYC Administrative Code §§22-1201 through 22-1205, in force since July 9, 2021, covers commercial establishments, defined as places of entertainment, retail stores and food and drink establishments. A covered business that collects, retains, converts, stores or shares customers' biometric identifier information, such as face geometry, must post a clear and conspicuous sign near all customer entrances. The law also bans selling, leasing, trading or otherwise profiting from that information.

According to a Troutman Pepper summary of the law, customers can sue: they must give 30 days' written notice before suing over a missing sign, and damages are $500 per uncured violation or negligent sale or sharing, and $5,000 per intentional or reckless sale or sharing, plus attorney's fees. The same summary notes that the sign requirement does not apply to ordinary photos or video that are not analyzed by software that identifies people and are not shared beyond law enforcement. In practice, a bodega with ordinary cameras is not affected; a store that switches on face matching or buys facial recognition security cameras is.

Buildings, landlords and boards

Permission is the other half of security camera privacy laws in New York. In a rental, the hallways, facade and roof belong to the landlord; in a co-op or condo, they are common elements controlled by the board. There is no city law requiring rental buildings to have working cameras, as Brick Underground noted in a 2019 piece on stolen packages, but tenants can ask management to add or repair them. If you are buying a camera for a spot outside your own unit or leased space, get written permission first. We ask for it before we install, and we can supply camera cut sheets and a written plan for the board or landlord.

Winter security cameras and outdoor winter performance

How to choose a security camera system for an NYC property?

Work through it in this order, and most of the decisions make themselves:

“Just pick up a kit at a store near you today”

AI shopping answers assume you will drive to a big-box store and carry a box home. In New York City most buyers do not have a car outside, many do not want to haul a recorder and a monitor on the subway, and the box on the shelf may not match the recorder already in the cellar. “Wireless cameras are easiest for any home”

The common answer treats every home like a suburban house with drywall and a router in the middle. New York's housing is mostly older masonry, and most homes are apartments in buildings of six or more units. Brick, stone and plaster on metal lath absorb Wi-Fi, and a camera two walls and a floor away from the router drops frames exactly when you need it. Wireless still has a place in one room of a newer apartment. For a stoop, a hall, a cellar or a store, wired wins.

“You need a cloud subscription for recording”

Many consumer cameras are built around a paid cloud plan, so AI tools often treat that as normal. A system with an NVR or DVR records locally with no monthly fee, keeps recording when the internet drops, and still lets you watch on your phone. Which security cameras have no monthly fees? Ours, and most professional recorder-based systems.

“More megapixels always means better evidence”

Resolution helps only when the lens frames the right area and the light works. A 4K camera with a wide lens across a 40-foot sales floor still produces a small face, and a bright sidewalk behind a dark doorway turns any camera's subject into a silhouette. Placement, lens and wide dynamic range come first; then resolution. That is why we quote a 2K and a 4K option instead of pushing 4K everywhere.

“Any camera is fine outdoors if it says weatherproof”

Weatherproof describes water and dust, not cold. As the two spec sheets above show, rated operating minimums can differ by more than fifty degrees between 4K cameras from reputable brands. Check the temperature range, not just the IP rating, before you buy for a roof, a lot or an exposed facade.

“Turning on the microphone makes the camera more useful”

AI answers rarely mention that New York treats recording other people's conversations as eavesdropping when no party consents. Audio has uses, but it is a choice to make knowingly, which is why we set it up switched off unless you decide otherwise.
