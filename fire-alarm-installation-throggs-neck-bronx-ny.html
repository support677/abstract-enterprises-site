The Peninsula

Throggs Neck: One Commercial Spine, Two Bridge Approaches and Water on Three Sides

Throggs Neck is a peninsula in the southeast Bronx. Wikipedia bounds the neighborhood by Baisley Avenue and the Bruckner Expressway on the north, Westchester Creek on the west, and the East River and Long Island Sound on the remaining sides. It sits in Bronx Community District 10, carries ZIP code 10465, and covers about 1.9 square miles. The Throgs Neck Bridge lands at its southern end and the northern approach to the Bronx-Whitestone Bridge is also inside the area, so a large share of the land is taken up by highway ramps, parkway edges and the parkland underneath them.

What matters for a fire alarm designer is the pattern of building. In the 1920s, Wikipedia says, the large estates were cut into smaller row homes and dense bungalow lots, and the peninsula stayed fairly suburban through the decades when much of the Bronx struggled. That means most blocks are houses, which sit outside this page. The commercial and code-required buildings cluster in a few places: the long East Tremont Avenue retail strip that runs down the spine of the neck, the shopping center at Lafayette Avenue and the Hutchinson River Parkway, the public housing campus that the city built in 1953, the schools and parishes spread through the middle, and the waterfront institutions at the tip.

East Tremont Avenue is the commercial spine. The Bronx Times described it in 2014 as a strip of local, family-run stores worried about the then-new Throggs Neck Shopping Center, quoting the president of the Throggs Neck Merchants Association and the owner of a variety store on the avenue. The strip is mostly one- and two-story commercial buildings, some with apartments upstairs, and it is where the borough's FDNY companies for the peninsula sit: Engine 72 at 3929 East Tremont Avenue and Engine 89 with Ladder 50 at 2924 Bruckner Boulevard, per Wikipedia. The post office's Throggs Neck Station is on the same avenue at number 3630.

The avenue has also seen what a fire in a row of connected low commercial buildings looks like. News 12 Bronx reported in April 2022 that a rubbish fire started before 7:30 p.m. next to a two-story commercial building in a commercial area of Throgs Neck, then spread into that building and another two-story building, drawing a third alarm. The report does not say whether either building had a fire alarm system, and we draw no conclusion. We mention it because shared walls, cocklofts and rear yards full of trash and storage are exactly what a designer studies on this strip.

Then there is the water. The neck is low along much of its shore, and the National Weather Service's coastal flood impact notes for the Kings Point gauge describe water overtopping the seawall onto Pennyfield Avenue near SUNY Maritime College during high-water events. Bronx Ink reported after Hurricane Sandy in 2012 that the eastern Bronx waterfront, Throgs Neck included, took some of the borough's worst damage from flooding and power loss. A fire alarm panel, its batteries and its communicator in a waterfront cellar are a design decision, not an afterthought, and we come back to that below.

  • Largest retail site: the Throggs Neck Shopping Center at Lafayette Avenue and the Hutchinson River Parkway, with more than 600 parking spaces when it opened its Target in 2014 (Bronx Times; Throggs Neck Shopping Center).

  • Police: the 45th Precinct at 2877 Barkley Avenue (Wikipedia).
Trouble Calls

A Panel Beeping Behind the Counter on East Tremont Avenue? What to Do Before the Technician Arrives

Storekeepers on the avenue usually notice a fire alarm problem at the worst moment: the panel in the back room starts chirping during a busy Saturday, the strobes flash after hours and the monitoring company calls the owner at home, or a yellow trouble light has been on so long nobody remembers when it started. Call (646) 490-0629 and we book the visit within 2 hours. Have ready the address, where the panel is (back room, cellar, stair hall), the brand name on its door, the exact words on its display, and whether the monitoring company has already been told.

Until we arrive, write down or photograph the screen, call the monitoring company so it knows a technician is coming, and press reset no more than once. Do not pull the batteries, unplug the transformer or tape over a beeping detector. In a building where the store has a panel and the apartments above have none of their own, the store's panel may be the only thing watching the stair and the cellar.

The faults we expect on the peninsula follow its buildings. Cellars that took water in past storms produce ground faults and corroded terminals years later. Two-story commercial buildings with flat roofs and rooftop units bring duct detector troubles after an HVAC contractor's visit. Older panels that dial out over copper phone lines are the ones people describe as a fire alarm system not communicating, because the line was cancelled when the store switched carriers. A true fire alarm panel failure repair, where the board itself is dead, is less common than a battery or line problem, and we prove which it is before ordering parts.

Search terms like emergency fire alarm repair, urgent fire alarm repair, same day fire alarm repair and 24 hour fire alarm repair all describe the same worry. What we commit to is a confirmed booking inside 2 hours and a straight answer on the arrival time. Commercial fire alarm emergency repair, emergency fire alarm service, after hours fire alarm service and fire alarm system emergency service are scheduled around the store's hours and the condition on the panel. A fire alarm system down repair, where a required system is fully out, can also mean the FDNY's fire watch rules apply until protection is restored, and the owner should know that before the next business day starts.

Building Types

Commercial Fire Alarm Systems for Throggs Neck Buildings, From 1953 Public Housing to a Granite Fort

Before the building types, the basics. What is a commercial fire alarm system? It is a supervised network run from a control panel: detectors, pull stations and sprinkler switches report to the panel; horns, strobes and speakers warn occupants where the design calls for warning; standby batteries carry it through an outage; and a communicator sends signals to a monitoring center that calls the FDNY. How does a commercial fire alarm system work day to day? The panel watches every circuit for breaks, shorts and leaks to ground, so a cut wire or a dead battery shows up as a trouble signal, while a real alarm triggers the actions written into the design, such as recalling elevators, stopping fans or releasing doors.

When does a building need a fire alarm system? In New York City the answer comes from Chapter 9 of the Building Code, sorted by occupancy. For apartment buildings, section 907.2.9 requires a system in new work when the building has more than 16 dwelling units, or any unit three or more stories above the lowest level of exit discharge, or any unit more than one story below the highest level of exit discharge. Schools, assembly rooms, large stores and mixed buildings each have their own triggers. Existing buildings are judged against their approved use, their records and the scope of the alteration on the table, which is why every project here starts with the Certificate of Occupancy.

Throggs Neck Houses and the buildings next to it

The New York City Housing Authority completed Throggs Neck Houses on November 30, 1953, and Wikipedia lists it at 29 buildings of three and seven stories with 1,185 apartments. City Limits describes Throggs Neck Addition as four buildings along Balcom Avenue between Randall and Dewey Avenues, with 287 apartments, and Randall Avenue-Balcom Avenue across the street as three buildings for seniors. NYCHA announced in April 2025 that Randall-Balcom residents voted to enter the PACT program, which brings private management and financing for comprehensive renovations, while Throggs Neck Addition residents chose to stay in Section 9. Public agencies and their contractors run these buildings, so we name them as part of the neighborhood's building stock, not as our clients. What they show is the scale of multifamily fire protection on the peninsula, and the kind of renovation program that brings fire alarm work with it: when a large renovation touches corridors, compactor rooms and elevators, the fire alarm design changes too, and a fire alarm system modernization often rides along with the larger project.

Senior buildings

Buildings for older tenants, like the three at Randall Avenue-Balcom Avenue, raise questions a designer answers early: which units are set up for residents with hearing loss, where visible notification is required, and how staff are told about a trouble at night. A building that adds supervised care becomes a different occupancy; nursing home fire alarm installation and assisted living fire alarm installation follow institutional rules that are much stricter than an apartment building's, and that decision belongs to the design professional at the very start.

Fort Schuyler and SUNY Maritime College

SUNY Maritime College's own history page says construction of Fort Schuyler began in 1833, that the armament was completed in 1856, and that the fort is an irregular pentagon of granite brought from Greenwich, Connecticut. When the New York State Merchant Marine Academy moved in, the college says crews removed 31,000 cubic yards of earth from the roof, poured a new concrete roof, built a concrete chamber for electric, water and steam lines around the inside of the fort, inserted a mezzanine floor into the casemate arches, and turned the old mess hall into what is now the library; the cadets came ashore in 1938. The fort now houses the Maritime Industry Museum, which the college lists at 6 Pennyfield Avenue and which opened in 1986. A state college runs its own buildings and procurement, so we mention it as a landmark. For anyone designing in a building like this, the lessons are general: masonry vaults and arches leave little room for cable, a utility chamber is the natural pathway, and a museum gallery with irreplaceable objects is a candidate for early-warning detection rather than an ordinary ceiling head.

Catholic high schools and parish schools

Wikipedia lists four private schools on the peninsula: St. Frances de Chantal School, Preston High School, Monsignor Scanlan High School and St. Benedict School. Preston High School is at 2780 Schurz Avenue, and Wikipedia notes that it occupies the former Havemeyer-Huntington mansion. Monsignor Scanlan High School is at 915 Hutchinson River Parkway; Lehman College's public art survey says it has carried that name since 1972 and was formerly St. Helena High School. A school's fire alarm usually includes manual stations at exits, smoke detection in corridors and stairs, notification in classrooms, gyms and auditoriums, and ties to sprinklers and kitchen hoods. Work is scheduled around the academic year, and a building that started life as a mansion brings attics, wood framing and changed floor levels that a designer has to sort out before choosing devices.

Houses of worship and parish halls

Churches on the peninsula often come with a hall in the basement, a rectory that now holds offices, and a school next door. A sanctuary, a hall used for bingo or a parish dinner, and an office each have different occupant loads, and the hall is often the space that pushes the building into assembly rules. Fire alarm work for a parish is usually scoped one building at a time and phased around the liturgical calendar.

The private co-op colonies, and why they are different

Silver Beach, Edgewater Park and Locust Point are private communities inside or beside Throggs Neck. Wikipedia describes Silver Beach as a 1920s bungalow colony where residents owned their houses but rented the land, and Edgewater Park as a former church campsite that became a bungalow colony; the Department of City Planning's 2015 report counts 675 single-family homes in Edgewater Park with a volunteer fire company. Their homes are houses, covered by household smoke and carbon monoxide alarm rules rather than NFPA 72 building systems, and fire response inside the narrow lanes can depend on volunteer companies as well as the FDNY. The code-relevant buildings there are few: a community or office building, a firehouse, a gatehouse or a club. We cover those in detail on our Silver Beach fire alarm page; on the rest of the peninsula, the work is the commercial and institutional buildings described here.

Stores and Shopping

Business Fire Alarm Installation on East Tremont Avenue, Lafayette Avenue and Harding Avenue

Business fire alarm installation in Throggs Neck is mostly about two very different retail settings: a long strip of small, older buildings on East Tremont Avenue, and a modern shopping center off the Hutchinson River Parkway at Lafayette Avenue. The first brings shared walls, mixed uses and records that may be decades old. The second brings large single-story boxes, separate tenant spaces and a landlord with its own base-building system.

The East Tremont Avenue strip

Most buildings on the avenue are one or two stories with a store on the ground floor and either offices, storage or apartments above. When the Bronx Times covered the strip in 2014, it quoted a tax preparer who served as the merchants association's treasurer and ran his office on the avenue, and the owner of Frank Bee's, a variety store there. Businesses change, so we treat these as examples of the building types rather than a directory. For a fire alarm, the questions on the strip are always the same: does the building need a system at all under its approved use, is there a cellar that runs under neighbors, does the second floor have its own exit or share the store's stair, and is anyone cooking. Business fire detection systems on a building like this are usually small, but the drawings still have to show every room by use.

Restaurants, bars and catering

The avenue and its side streets carry pizzerias, delis, bars and restaurants, which makes restaurant fire alarm installation one of the most common requests on the strip. A kitchen with a hood suppression system usually means a heat detector rather than a smoke detector near the cooking line, a monitor module that tells the panel when the hood system discharges, and a decision by the designer on whether that signal is an alarm or supervisory; that fire alarm kitchen hood suppression monitoring is tested at every annual inspection. A bar or restaurant that adds a back room for parties can cross into assembly rules, and that is the moment the fire alarm conversation should start, not after the tables arrive.

The Throggs Neck Shopping Center

The Bronx Times called the center "a massive mall at Lafayette Avenue and the Hutchinson River Parkway" with more than 600 parking spaces when Target opened there in July 2014. The center's own site today lists tenants including Target, TJ Maxx, Petco, several fast-casual restaurants, an urgent care office, a dental office, a physical therapy practice and a dermatology group. Each of those is a tenant space inside a building that already has a landlord system, and the commercial fire detection systems in the boxes have to work together. Tenant improvement fire alarm installation in a center like this means connecting new devices to the existing panel under an amendment, which the FDNY's tips say should include a letter of approval for the base building system. A medical or dental office adds its own questions about oxygen, sterilizers and exam rooms; medical office fire alarm installation in a retail center is still filed and tested like any other change.

Harding Avenue, Lafayette Avenue and the side streets

Away from the two big retail settings, commercial buildings are scattered: a professional office in a converted house, a small warehouse or contractor's yard near the highways, a funeral home, a day-care center on a residential block. Storage facility fire alarm installation, parking garage fire alarm installation under newer buildings and warehouse fire alarm installation come up near the expressways, where a building's use may have changed several times. Each is a fresh read of the Certificate of Occupancy.

Marinas, yacht clubs and the golf clubhouse

The shore has private clubs and boat storage. The Locust Point Yacht Club sits at the end of Longstreet Avenue, according to the Open Water Data listing, and Bally's Golf Links at Ferry Point, a public course on city parkland at 500 Hutchinson River Parkway, advertises a 20,000-square-foot clubhouse with a restaurant and event space. A clubhouse with a banquet room is an assembly space; a boat storage building with fuel, batteries and engines is a hazard the designer will want to treat with heat detection and careful notification; and any building on the water needs its equipment placed with flood elevation in mind.

Field Notes

Field Notes: On a Two-Story Commercial Row, Check the Cellar and the Cockloft First

On East Tremont Avenue, the first place we look is down. Many of the strip's buildings have cellars used for stock, and in older rows a cellar wall can be thinner than anyone expects or have openings to the building next door. Where the cellar is, what it stores, whether it is sprinklered and whether it has its own exit decide a lot about detection and about which panel watches it. If the store's lease covers only the ground floor, we also need to know who controls the cellar.

The second place is up. Two-story commercial buildings often share a cockloft, the space between the top ceiling and the roof. The April 2022 fire that News 12 reported spread from a rubbish fire beside one two-story commercial building into that building and a second one. Whatever the cause of that spread, cocklofts and concealed spaces are where a designer asks whether detection is required or advisable, and where cable routes and firestopping have to be planned so new work does not open a path between neighbors.

The third is the water line. On the blocks near the shore, we ask where water reached in past storms before we choose a panel location. A panel in a cellar that floods is a panel that will be replaced; the same panel one floor up, with its batteries off the floor and its communicator above the flood line, keeps reporting. The National Weather Service notes describe water topping the seawall onto Pennyfield Avenue near the college, so the question is not hypothetical at the south end of the neck.

Your Questions Answered

Throggs Neck Fire Alarm Questions From Storekeepers, Landlords, School Business Offices and Club Boards

Stores on the avenue and at the center

How many pull stations does a two-story store on East Tremont Avenue need, and where do they go?

First the designer decides whether the building needs manual stations at all; many occupancies with sprinklers and automatic detection are allowed to skip them, while schools and assembly spaces usually are not. Where stations are required, NFPA 72 places one within about five feet of the entrance to each exit on each floor, and adds more so nobody walks more than 200 feet to reach one. In a small two-story building that often means a station at the street door, one at the rear exit and one at the top of each stair, all mounted at the height the code sets so a person in a wheelchair can reach them.

The stores at the Throggs Neck Shopping Center sit side by side in long buildings. If one store's fire alarm goes off, do the neighboring stores hear it?

It depends on how the building was designed and how the tenant spaces are separated. Where each store is a separate fire area with its own system, an alarm in one usually sounds only there and at the landlord's panel. Where one base system serves a whole building, the designer decides which spaces receive notification, and that may include neighbors if the code or the evacuation plan calls for it. Tenants should ask the landlord for a copy of the sequence of operations so their staff know what to expect when the store next door goes into alarm.

The fort, the clubs and the waterfront

Fort Schuyler holds a museum, a library and classrooms inside one historic structure. Is that one fire alarm system or several?

Usually one building has one fire alarm system, even when it holds several uses, but the design treats each use on its own terms: a museum gallery may get early-warning detection, a library may need protection for its stacks, and classrooms need notification that reaches students. The designer also checks how the uses are separated by fire-rated construction and who staffs each area after hours. The panel's labels and the sequence of operations then make clear which part of the building each signal comes from, so security staff know whether to send someone to the gallery or the classrooms.

Who signs the fire alarm filing for a privately run clubhouse on city parkland, like the one at Ferry Point?

The filing is prepared by a New York State licensed engineer or architect on behalf of the owner, and on parkland the owner of the land is a city agency while a private operator runs the building under an agreement. Which party contracts for design, pays for the work and keeps up inspections is set by that agreement, so the operator's first step is to check it with the agency. Whoever hires us, the FDNY's requirements for design, filing, a certified installer and a witnessed test are the same as for any private building.

Storms and big renovations

A coastal flood warning came and went and no water reached our cellar. Does the fire alarm still need a check afterward?

Not a full test, but a quick look is worth the few minutes. Read the panel's screen and event history for ground faults, AC power loss or communication troubles logged during the storm, confirm the monitoring company received no unexplained signals, and look at any devices in the lowest rooms for condensation or rust. Wind-driven rain and high humidity can cause trouble even when the floor stays dry. If anything was logged, call the service company before the next storm rather than after it.

The Randall-Balcom buildings are moving to PACT renovations. When a large housing renovation touches hallways and elevators, what happens to the fire alarm?

In general, the fire alarm is redesigned wherever the renovation changes what it protects. New corridor finishes, relocated compactor rooms, elevator modernization and new mechanical equipment each affect detection, recall and fan shutdown. The renovation's engineer folds those changes into a fire alarm filing, and the work is tested before residents rely on it. Public housing renovations are run by the agency and its partners; we describe the process only because it is the same one any large building on the peninsula goes through.

System Types

Choosing a Fire Alarm System Type for Throggs Neck Stores, Schools and Waterfront Buildings

A one-room shop on East Tremont Avenue and a campus building at the tip of the neck share a code, not a design. The engineer chooses from these families, and many buildings end up with more than one.

Addressable

What is an addressable fire alarm system? Each device has its own address on a shared loop, so the panel reports "heat detector, cellar stockroom, rear" instead of a zone number. Addressable fire alarm installation, addressable fire alarm system installation and addressable fire alarm panel installation suit schools, larger mixed buildings and anything with many rooms; addressable fire alarm device installation and addressable fire alarm system programming keep the names current, while addressable fire alarm system upgrade and addressable fire alarm system repair extend its life.

Conventional

What is a conventional fire alarm system? Devices share zone circuits and the panel knows only the zone. For a single small storefront, conventional fire alarm installation, conventional fire alarm system installation and conventional fire alarm panel installation can be enough. Conventional fire alarm repair and conventional fire alarm replacement keep older zone panels running until an owner converts.

Apartment-building pattern

The 907.2.9 layout: detection in public halls and service rooms that reports to the monitoring center without sounding every floor, plus the building's elevator, sprinkler and fan ties.

Voice evacuation

Fire alarm voice evacuation installation and fire alarm emergency voice communication replace tones with spoken instructions. The code calls for them in certain assembly spaces and buildings; a large school auditorium or banquet room is where the question usually comes up on the peninsula.

Multi-criteria detection

Intelligent fire alarm installation uses detectors that weigh smoke and heat together before deciding. An intelligent fire detection system helps in kitchens, boiler rooms and boat storage where plain smoke heads misbehave.

Signal transmission

Fire alarm monitoring system installation for buildings losing their copper lines: fire alarm communicator installation and fire alarm central station communicator installation move the path to cellular or internet radio.

What is the difference between addressable and conventional fire alarms when something goes wrong on a Saturday? In a deli with three detectors, a zone that says "kitchen" is enough. In a school wing with dozens of rooms, knowing the exact device saves an hour of searching every time. The time goes into testing each device, checking batteries and power supplies, confirming signals at the monitoring center and writing the report. Access to every room, including tenant spaces and the cellar, is what keeps it short.

Can a Throggs Neck school's fire alarm speakers also be used to announce a lockdown?
+

Sometimes, if the system is designed for it. A voice evacuation system can be planned as a combined fire and mass notification system, with priorities written so a fire alarm is never blocked by a lower-priority message. That is a design decision made at the start, filed with the drawings and tested. Adding lockdown announcements to an existing voice system later is a change to the filed system, not a programming tweak.

Can a heat detector in a Throggs Neck boat storage shed tell the difference between a warm engine and a fire?
+

A properly chosen one can. Fixed temperature detectors are set well above the temperatures a shed normally reaches, and rate of rise detectors respond to a fast climb rather than a slow warm-up. The designer picks the type and rating for the space, keeps detectors away from engine exhaust paths where possible, and avoids smoke detectors where fumes and open bay doors would cause false alarms.

What should a Throggs Neck owner receive when a fire alarm job is finished?
+

The FDNY approval letter once the acceptance test passes, record drawings that match what was built, a copy or backup of the panel program, the test reports, the monitoring agreement and the manufacturer's manuals. We add a one-page guide to the panel's signals and the next inspection dates. Keep copies away from the cellar, especially near the water.
