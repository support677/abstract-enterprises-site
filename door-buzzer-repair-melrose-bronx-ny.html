TERMINOLOGY

Melrose Entry-System Vocabulary, Decoded

Names first, parts second: entry buzzer, apartment entry system, building entrance system, visitor entry system, door release system, building access system — in Melrose those labels stretch from a rowhouse’s front vestibule buzzer to a mid-rise’s lobby entrance buzzer, and every one of them runs on the same anatomy underneath: the door release button, the electric strike it commands, the buzzer transformer and buzzer power supply feeding the circuit, the entry system wiring connecting it all, and — on the current platforms — a remote door release that lives on a phone.

Phone-entry board. The rebuild era’s directory brain — calls tenants’ phones instead of wired handsets. Its failures are overwhelmingly programming, which is why so many “broken” Melrose systems come back without a part.

Callout line. The path a directory uses to reach a phone. Dead callouts strand working hardware — and get repaired at the routing, not the panel.

Relay. The switch that fires the release on newer systems. Rated for a finite number of cycles, and the mid-rises are reaching it on schedule.

Strike. The electric latch in the frame. Worn strikes account for more “replace the intercom” quotes than every other part combined.

Transformer. The prewar stock’s shared voltage source. First suspect on any all-dead walkup, and one of the fastest swaps in the trade.

Riser. The vertical wiring trunk. Scattered dead lines with a healthy system point here — a trace-and-splice repair, not a rewire.

Station. The in-unit wall unit, handset to touchscreen. Fails individually, replaced individually, on every generation of platform.

Door station. The street-side camera-speaker unit on video systems. Tested before any lobby monitor takes the blame.

Release path. Button to strike, end to end. Visitors heard but never admitted means the fault is on this path and nowhere else.

First-generation. Our shorthand for the rebuild era’s original equipment — old enough to fail on schedule, young enough that its wiring is usually excellent. The best repair candidates in the borough.

In-kind. Like for like — the honest replacement for small buildings whose needs never changed.

Platform vs. part. The whole decision in four words: parts fail cheap and often, platforms fail rare and dear. Diagnosis exists to keep them separate.
