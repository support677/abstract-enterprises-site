The Terms on a Westchester Camera Proposal, Decoded

NVR / DVR

Two recorder eras. NVRs ingest digital IP streams over network cable; DVRs digitize legacy analog coax. Which box hums in your closet decides whether you’re buying a migration or a clean build.

PoE

Power over Ethernet: one Cat6 run carries electricity and video together, which is how a camera reaches a gate pier or a gym eave without an electrician trenching power up a hillside.

WDR

Wide dynamic range — the processing that keeps a face legible in a shaded doorway against a bright quad behind it. On tree-canopied Westchester campuses, half your entrances live in exactly that light.

Person / Vehicle Classification

Detection that fires on humans and cars and stays silent for deer, coyotes, and wind-thrown branches. In this county it’s the difference between alerts your staff read and alerts they’ve muted.

Retention

Days of recorded history before overwrite. Our Westchester builds hold a thirty-day floor, extending to sixty or ninety as the drive budget permits.

NDAA §889

The federal exclusion rule. When NPSE, SSBA, or federal safety dollars fund the purchase, certain manufacturers are barred — our default hardware list clears the requirement before anyone asks.

EoC

Ethernet-over-coax converters that push modern IP video down cable a 2009 installer left in the walls. Clean copper means closed ceilings and a lighter invoice.

IK10 / IP66

Impact and weather ratings. Gyms and reachable mounts carry the first; anything facing a Westchester winter — or Sound air — carries the second with stainless fasteners.

LPR

License-plate reading at gates, lot entrances, and bus yards. Every arriving vehicle becomes a timestamped, searchable record before a door opens.

New York School Camera Law, Read for Westchester

Facial Recognition: Off the Table Statewide

The State Education Department’s September 2023 determination governs every school in New York — district, charter, Catholic, independent, and religious without distinction: facial-recognition technology may not be purchased or operated. A Bronxville academy and the district building beside it answer to the same sentence. Conventional video — cameras, recording, motion analytics — was never restricted and remains entirely lawful. Our installs ship with any face-matching capability disabled in firmware, the setting logged in your commissioning file, which is the page that ends the conversation whenever a vendor or trustee raises it.

Where Lenses Belong, and Where They’re Felonies

Penal Law 250.45 makes recording in bathrooms, locker rooms, changing areas, or any space with a reasonable expectation of privacy a felony — no school exception exists. Everywhere else on the property is lawful ground: entries, corridors, stairs, cafeteria, gym, lots, fields, drives, mechanical rooms, perimeter. Classroom interiors we route to your board and counsel rather than defaulting into; our standing Westchester drawing watches each classroom doorway from the corridor, logging entries and exits with no lens inside the teaching space, and any deliberate exception gets initialed on the plan. On tight village lots and estate edges alike, any head that could see a neighbor’s property or a public road is composed onto school ground — and that composition is documented at survey, because in this county the neighbors read documentation.

When Footage Becomes an Education Record

Under FERPA, identifiable video that drives a disciplinary decision can become part of that student’s education record — carrying parental viewing rights and disclosure limits — and New York’s Ed Law 2-d binds any outside vendor holding student-identifiable data to contract terms with teeth. The architecture that keeps all of it manageable is our default: recorder on premises, drives on the school’s asset ledger, a written review-and-release procedure delivered at handover, and off-site duplication only under a 2-d-conforming agreement. The first heated parent request then meets a policy, not an improvisation.

Who Funds the Work, and What Each Program Demands

District buildings draw on the state’s $2 billion Smart Schools bond, which covers camera projects so long as facial recognition never enters the scope. Westchester’s tuition schools — archdiocesan, independent, and Jewish day schools alike — file NPSE reimbursement, and federal school-safety grants can stack above it, importing the §889 hardware exclusions when they do. Whichever pot pays, the deliverable from us is identical: an itemized proposal formatted to that reviewer’s checklist, ready to forward without edits.
