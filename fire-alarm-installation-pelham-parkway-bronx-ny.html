The Neighborhood

Why Pelham Parkway's Co-ops, Storefront Rows and Community Houses Need a Fire Alarm Matched to How Each Building Is Used

Pelham Parkway takes its name from the road, and the road shaped everything around it. NYC Parks describes the Bronx and Pelham Parkway as a boulevard modeled on Eastern and Ocean Parkways, with land acquired by condemnation in 1884, center plots, two marginal streets and a bridle path. Wikipedia gives its width as 400 feet and notes that building was barred within 150 feet of its center line and that the Dyre Avenue line had to pass beneath it in a tunnel. The result is a neighborhood that faces a long park instead of a commercial avenue.

Wikipedia places the Pelham Parkway neighborhood south of the road: Pelham Parkway South on the north, the Dyre Avenue tracks on the east, Bronxdale Avenue on the south and Bronx Park East on the west, inside Bronx Community District 11, with ZIP codes 10461 and 10462 and 30,073 residents in the 2010 census. It describes the area as dominated by six- and seven-story elevator apartment and co-op buildings, with detached houses and larger Art Deco and Tudor-style apartment houses on the side streets, and calls it one of the few cohesive examples of the six-story elevator apartment type in the city. Wikipedia says apartments were rehabilitated in the 2000s, many buildings became co-ops, and White Plains Road and Lydig Avenue filled with restaurants, kosher and Italian butchers and bakeries.

That mix is what makes fire alarm work here different: block after block of buildings of roughly the same height that do very different jobs. Some are co-ops whose boards inherited whatever the sponsor left behind; some have a deli or a dry cleaner in the base and apartments upstairs. Bronx House, a settlement house Wikipedia says moved to the parkway in the 1950s, offers social services, English classes, free lunches for seniors and a gym. The Pelham Parkway Jewish Center at 900 Pelham Parkway, founded in 1930 according to the Bronx County Historical Society, now houses a congregation formed by a merger. And News 12 reported in April 2026 that the Bowery Residents' Committee plans a nine-story building of more than 100 rooms with private bathrooms and shared kitchens on the former Apple Bank site on Pelham Parkway South, expected to open in 2029.

In the NYC Building Code, a fire alarm is required because of how a building is used and how many people use it, and each use carries its own triggers. An apartment house with more than 16 units, or with units three or more stories above the street exit, needs a building system that reports off site but, under the city's rule for apartment buildings, does not sound throughout for a single smoke detector. A store, an assembly room, a classroom and a building of rooms have different thresholds and different notification rules. So the most useful thing a fire alarm contractor can do on the first visit is help the owner get the occupancy question answered by the design professional before anyone counts detectors.

Recent fires show the stakes. In July 2025, 1st Responder News reported a boiler room fire in the basement of a six-story apartment building at 1144 Pelham Parkway South, fought past barred windows and tight access while smoke spread through the building. In October 2023, News 12 reported that a Lydig Avenue juice bar fire under an apartment building started in the cellar, was put out on a Friday night and reignited early Saturday. Both started below the apartments, in rooms a fire alarm design has to label, reach and keep working.

This page covers commercial and code-required fire alarm systems only: building systems in apartment buildings and co-ops, stores and restaurants, community houses, houses of worship, offices and new construction. The smoke and carbon monoxide alarms inside individual apartments are a separate requirement enforced by HPD, and one- and two-family house smoke alarms are not part of this service.

Building Types

Commercial Fire Alarm Systems for Pelham Parkway's Building Types, Sorted by Use

What is a commercial fire alarm system? It is the building-wide system of detectors, manual stations, notification appliances and a control panel that protects shared spaces and reports to a supervising station, separate from the smoke alarms inside each apartment. How does a commercial fire alarm system work? Initiating devices report a change to the panel; the panel decides whether it is an alarm, a supervisory signal or a trouble; and it carries out the approved sequence: reporting off site, sounding or staying quiet where the code allows, recalling elevators, stopping fans and releasing doors. Commercial building fire alarm installation is NFPA 72 fire alarm installation to the NYC Building Code, and what the sequence includes depends on what the building is used for, so the building types below are sorted by use rather than by age or height.

Six- and seven-story elevator apartment houses

These are the neighborhood's signature, like 2081 Cruger Avenue, six stories and 85 units, built in 1931: brick, plaster, an elevator, a cellar with the boiler, meters and storage. Many have only detectors for elevator recall. Where a building system is required or added, the city's apartment rule means smoke detectors in common areas report to the panel and to the central station without sounding through every hallway, while heat detectors, sprinkler waterflow and manual stations follow the approved design. Very little of the work happens inside apartments.

Co-ops

A co-op is the same building with a different owner. A board that took over from a sponsor may have inherited a panel without drawings, a central station account in a former agent's name, or nothing at all, so the first job is often finding the approval letter, the drawings and the last inspection report.

Stores and restaurants under apartments

A needs assessment by NYC Small Business Services counted 33 delis and bodegas, 16 salons and barbershops, 16 restaurants and 10 medical offices among Lydig Avenue's storefronts. Most sit under apartments. A store may have its own panel, may be a few devices on the building's panel, or may have both. Kitchens, steam and cellar stock decide detector types.

Community houses

Bronx House combines what would be several buildings elsewhere: classrooms for young children, after-school programs, a gym and pool, fitness classes, and programs for the Pelham Parkway NORC, a program for residents 60 and older, as its program pages describe them. In a building like that, the design professional decides how the different uses are separated or combined under the code, and the fire alarm follows: notification that reaches every program room, detection suited to a pool room or a kitchen, and staff who know what the panel is saying.

Houses of worship

The Pelham Parkway Jewish Center began in 1930 and acquired its temple and building complex at 900 Pelham Parkway in 1953, according to the Bronx County Historical Society, which also records that it once leased space to a children's school and that Young Israel of Pelham Parkway began renting space there in 2010 before the two congregations merged. A building that has housed several organizations needs records that say who is responsible today.

Buildings of rooms with shared facilities

The BRC building planned on Pelham Parkway South is described by News 12 as individual rooms with private bathrooms plus shared kitchens and laundry, most reserved for formerly homeless people, with on-site social services. Buildings arranged around shared facilities and on-site staff are classified by the design professional from their plans and program, and that classification, not the building's height, sets how the fire alarm notifies residents and staff.

Field Notes

Field Notes: Ask "What Happens on This Floor?" Before Counting Devices

On the parkway's blocks we start every survey with a list, floor by floor, of what actually happens there. The Certificate of Occupancy says one thing; the building often says another. A cellar listed as storage holds a store's walk-in cooler. A ground-floor room listed as a store has become a doctor's office. A community room on the second floor now hosts evening classes for forty people. Each of those is a question for the design professional, and each can change the fire alarm design more than the building's height does.

The second habit is to read the cellar as carefully as the lobby. In this building stock the boiler room, the meter room, the store's stockroom and the laundry room are where fires start and where devices are easiest to forget. We label every cellar device by the room and the side of the building, not by a zone number, so a firefighter reading the annunciator knows which door to go through.

The third is to leave the board or the owner with a short written answer to "what is this building, for fire alarm purposes?" that stays in the building's file with the drawings. The next store tenant, the next board president and the next inspector all start from that page.

Your Questions Answered

Pelham Parkway Fire Alarm Questions From Co-op Boards, Store Owners, Congregations and Builders

The parkway's six-story elevator houses

Many Pelham Parkway rental buildings became co-ops when the neighborhood revived in the 2000s. Does converting a rental building to a co-op change which fire alarm the building is required to have?

No. A co-op conversion changes who owns the building, not what the building is used for, and the fire alarm requirement follows the use. A building full of apartments is residential before and after the offering plan. What the conversion does change is who is responsible: the new board inherits whatever the sponsor had, including any panel, central station account, open FDNY items and drawings, or the absence of all of them.

Our 1931 six-story building on Cruger Avenue has smoke detectors only in the elevator machine room and the top-floor landing. Is a setup like that a fire alarm system in the code's sense?

It is usually an elevator recall arrangement, not a building fire alarm system. Detectors that exist only to bring the elevator down are part of the elevator's safety equipment, and they may report to a small panel or a recall relay rather than to a central station. A building fire alarm system, where required, is designed by a PE or RA, approved by the FDNY, monitored off site and tested on a schedule. A design professional can tell you whether anything more is required for your building as it stands.

The July 2025 fire at 1144 Pelham Parkway South started in the basement boiler room, and firefighters had to work past barred windows and tight access. What can the fire alarm in a cellar tell firefighters before they open the cellar door?

A well-labeled addressable system tells them which room. If the boiler room heat detector is programmed as "Cellar boiler room, rear" rather than "Zone 1," the panel, the lobby annunciator and the central station's call to the FDNY all carry those words. A sprinkler waterflow signal tells them water is already moving. A supervisory signal from a closed valve tells them the sprinkler protection may not be working. The labels are written during programming and checked at the acceptance test.

Lydig Avenue and White Plains Road storefronts

In October 2023 a juice bar fire on Lydig Avenue started in the cellar, was put out, and reignited hours later. Can a building fire alarm do anything about a fire that comes back after the trucks leave?

Only if it is working again when the fire comes back. After a fire, detectors may be damaged, circuits shorted by water and the panel left silenced; if nobody restores it, a rekindle in the cellar has nothing watching it. The steps that matter are the same every time: the fire alarm company checks and restores the system before the building is left unattended, damaged devices in the fire area are replaced or covered by a fire watch, the central station confirms it is receiving signals again, and the FDNY is told if the system has to stay out of service.

A dry cleaner on Lydig Avenue runs steam presses and a solvent machine in a store under apartments. What does the fire alarm design have to account for in a space like that?

Steam, heat and lint. A smoke detector placed over a pressing station will false alarm on steam, so the design professional usually chooses heat detection or a carefully located detector type for the work area, and smoke detection where it actually belongs, such as the corridor or the stockroom. The boiler or steam generator, if there is one, gets its own detection. And because apartments sit above, the store's devices have to report where the building's design says they report, so a fire in the plant room is known to the building, not only to the store.

A Lydig Avenue store owner is taking over the empty store next door and opening the wall between them. Does joining two storefronts into one store change the fire alarm?

Usually, yes. Two small stores that each had a handful of devices become one larger space with a new layout, a new floor area and sometimes a new kind of business, and each of those can change what the code asks for. Devices placed for two rooms rarely suit one, and the combined store may cross a size threshold the separate stores did not. If either store's devices were on the building's panel, the change is a modification of the building's approved system. Have a design professional look at the combined plan before the wall comes down, and expect a filing and a test before the new store opens.

Community buildings on the parkway

Bronx House at 990 Pelham Parkway South brings in older adults for lunch and English classes, many of whom don't know the building. What helps a first-time visitor get out when the fire alarm sounds?

Clear notification and clear exits working together. Strobes and horns, or speakers with recorded voice messages in buildings designed for them, tell a visitor something is wrong; exit signs and staff tell them where to go. The design should make sure notification reaches every room the public uses, including program rooms, the lunch room and rest rooms, and that any voice message is short and plain. Staff who run the program should know which stair to use and where the group meets outside.

Young Israel of Pelham Parkway merged into the Pelham Parkway Jewish Center at 900 Pelham Parkway. When two organizations combine into one building, whose name should be on the fire alarm records and the central station account?

The organization that now owns or controls the building, with a contact who will actually answer. After a merger, the central station account, the FDNY records and the inspection company's file often still carry the old names, an old treasurer's phone number or the name of a congregation that no longer exists as a separate body. Update the central station account, tell the inspection company in writing, and file the approved drawings under the current name.

BRC's planned nine-story building at the former Apple Bank site on Pelham Parkway South was described as "as of right." Does an as-of-right project skip review of its fire alarm too?

No. "As of right" means the building fits the zoning without a public hearing or a special approval, which is what News 12 reported about this project. It says nothing about the building code or the fire code. A new nine-story building with more than 100 units still needs a fire alarm designed and sealed by a New York State Professional Engineer or Registered Architect, filed with the FDNY, installed after Project Authorization and accepted at an FDNY test before people move in.

The same building is planned as individual rooms with private bathrooms and shared kitchens and laundry. Is a building of rooms around shared kitchens treated like an ordinary apartment house for the fire alarm?

Not automatically. How the building is classified depends on how it is designed and used, and that classification decides a great deal: whether every resident must hear the alarm, which devices go in rooms and common kitchens, and how staff are expected to respond. Ordinary apartment houses get a system that does not sound throughout for one smoke detector; other residential classifications are treated differently. The design professional settles the classification first, from the plans and the program, and the fire alarm design follows from it.

New buildings and small developers

2184 Muliner Avenue was filed as a six-story, 28-unit building with drafting by a consulting firm. On a small job like that, who has to prepare and seal the fire alarm plans?

A New York State licensed Professional Engineer or Registered Architect. The FDNY's tip sheet says the registered design professional evaluates the occupancy, decides the type of system and certifies the plans, and that the same professional completes the TM-1 application. A drafting firm can produce sheets for a licensed professional who reviews and seals them, but the seal and the responsibility belong to the PE or RA. The panel location, riser path and power belong on the drawings before construction starts.

A new six-story building replaces a two-story store on a Pelham Parkway side street. Does the fire alarm have to be finished before the developer can rent the ground-floor store?

The store can be leased whenever the owner likes, but it cannot open for business in a space that is not covered by an approved, accepted system where one is required. Developers often deliver the store as a shell, and the tenant's fit-out adds devices later. That later work is its own design and filing, usually as a modification to the building's approved system. The lease should say who pays for those devices.

The FDNY puts submission to approval at roughly 20 to 24 weeks. For a small developer building one six-story building near Pelham Parkway, what should happen in those months besides waiting?

The installation should be getting ready. While the plans are under review, the developer can order long-lead equipment once the design is settled, coordinate the sprinkler contractor's flow and tamper switches, decide the central station company so the account and the communicator are ready. Work on the fire alarm itself waits for the FDNY Project Authorization, but nothing stops the team from planning the sequence of work so the building is 100 percent complete and on permanent power when the inspection is requested on form B-45.

Pelham Parkway's co-op boards often learn about the fire alarm only when a bill or a violation arrives. What is one document every board should be able to find in five minutes?

The FDNY Letter of Approval for the building's current fire alarm system, if it has one, filed with the approved drawings. Any addition or modification is filed against it. The FDNY's own tip sheet asks for the Letter of Approval of the base building system when devices are added.

Types of Commercial Fire Alarm Systems We Install in Pelham Parkway

A co-op, a deli and a community house do not need the same system. These are the types a design professional weighs.

🔍

Addressable

What is an addressable fire alarm system? Every device has its own address, so the panel reports the exact detector, station or module. Addressable fire alarm installation suits elevator buildings with a cellar full of rooms, community houses and every new building.

🔌

Conventional

What is a conventional fire alarm system? Devices are wired in zones, and the panel knows the zone but not the device. Conventional fire alarm installation can still fit a small store with a handful of devices or a one-room office.

⇄

The difference

What is the difference between addressable and conventional fire alarms? Addressable names the device; conventional names the zone. In a cellar with a boiler room, meter room, laundry and store stockroom, that is the difference between one door and four.

Which government fees come with a fire alarm filing for a building on Pelham Parkway South, and who pays them?
+

The NYC Business listing for the fire alarm plan application shows $420 for a new application, $525 for a complex technical analysis where one is required, and no fee to resubmit within six months of a letter of deficiency. The building owner pays them, usually through the design professional who files. They are separate from design, installation and testing.

Can a Lydig Avenue store stay open while fire alarm devices are installed in its ceiling?
+

Usually, with planning. Ceiling work can be done before opening or after closing, a section at a time, with loud testing outside business hours. Any area where the old devices are out of service before the new ones work needs a plan to keep it protected, which the design professional and the FDNY rules set.

How soon after the FDNY approves the plans can work start in a Pelham Parkway building?
+

After the Project Authorization. The NYC Business listing describes the Project Authorization as FDNY approval to start the work in the approved application, and it is requested online after the plan approval. Once it is issued, the installer can begin.

If our panel is disconnected so a new one can be installed, what keeps the building protected overnight?
+

A plan agreed before the old panel comes off the wall. Replacements are normally phased so the old system stays in service until the new one takes over, and any period when a required system is out of service calls for notifying the FDNY and keeping a fire watch, as the Fire Code and the FDNY require. The proposal should say how that will be handled.

Can a store on White Plains Road and the apartments above share one central station account?
+

They can share one if the building has one system that covers both, and the drawings show it that way. If the store has its own panel, it usually has its own account, and the building's design may also require the store's panel to send a signal to the building's panel. The approved drawings decide which arrangement you have.

What should be written inside the door of a Pelham Parkway building's fire alarm panel?
+

The name of the fire alarm company and its phone number, the central station's name and account number, the date of the last inspection, and where the approved drawings and the FDNY letter are kept. A simple device list helps too.

How can a Pelham Parkway board confirm that the S-97 holder named in a proposal really belongs to the bidding company?
+

Ask for the company's FDNY Fire Alarm Company Certificate and the S-97 certificate of fitness of the principal named on it, and check that the names match the proposal and the filing. The FDNY's FAQ says at least one principal on the company application must hold an S-97, though that person need not be an owner. If the bidder can't produce both, keep looking.

Does a building facing the parkway need an outside bell or strobe for the fire department?
+

Where the building has a sprinkler system, an exterior alarm on waterflow is a common part of the sprinkler design, and the fire alarm may supervise it. Whether your building needs one, and where it goes, is set by the design professional from the code and the building's sprinkler arrangement. It should be visible from the street the FDNY uses to approach.

Can the FDNY acceptance test for a store and the apartments above it be done in one visit?
+

If they are one system on one approved application, yes, they are tested together. If the store's work was filed separately, for example as a tenant fit-out, it is inspected on its own application, and the schedule depends on when each filing is ready. Ask your design professional how the filings are organized before you plan an opening date.

After the BRC building opens, can neighbors on Pelham Parkway South find out whether its fire alarm was approved?
+

Building records such as Certificates of Occupancy and many filings are public through the city's online systems, and a building cannot be legally occupied without the approvals its Certificate of Occupancy requires. The fire alarm itself is between the owner and the FDNY. Neighbors with a specific safety concern can raise it with the FDNY or through 311.

When is the deposit due on a fire alarm job in a Pelham Parkway building, and what does it cover?
+

Payment terms are set out in the service agreement, which includes a 50 percent deposit. Before signing, ask that the proposal show which steps the deposit covers, such as design coordination and ordering equipment, and which later payments are tied to milestones like the FDNY Project Authorization, completion of installation and the acceptance test.
