The Neighborhood

Why Cadman Plaza Buildings Need Fire Alarms That Work Floor by Floor

Cadman Plaza's buildings need commercial fire alarm systems designed for height, age and constant occupancy. Within a few blocks of the park there are 27- and 32-story co-op towers from the late 1960s, a 38-story condominium tower with a public library at its base, an 11-story state courthouse, a 15-story federal courthouse, a post office building begun in 1885 and a city hall finished in 1848. Almost none of them can be emptied for a long renovation. Shareholders sleep in the towers, judges sit in the courtrooms, and the Municipal Building issues marriage licenses every weekday. A fire alarm here has to be installed, repaired and tested around the people who use the building, and when it sounds it has to tell the right floors the right thing.

The plaza itself was made by clearing, not by slow growth. The city condemned most of a ten-square-block district in 1935, named it Cadman Plaza in 1939, tore down the Fulton Street elevated line in 1941 and razed the Sands Street terminal in 1943, according to NYC Parks. Brownstoner and the Cultural Landscape Foundation describe the result as the largest post-war civic-center urban renewal project in the country. Three residential complexes went up at the north end in the late 1960s and early 1970s under the Mitchell-Lama program: Cadman Plaza North at 140 Cadman Plaza West, Whitman Close at 75 Henry Street, and Cadman Towers at 101 Clark Street and 10 Clinton Street. Those towers are now fifty-odd years old, and so is much of the wiring and many of the original life-safety assumptions inside them.

Money and governance shape fire alarm decisions in those towers more than anywhere else in Brooklyn. In April 2024 THE CITY reported that Cadman Towers, with 421 apartments, faced a large debt coming due and major repair needs, and that it became the first Mitchell-Lama co-op converted to a city-sponsored HDFC. "It's to make sure we're affordable for the next 50 years and beyond," board president Toba Potosky told THE CITY. A board planning capital work on that scale needs fire alarm decisions it can schedule and finance: what can be kept, what must be replaced, which floor goes first, and how the FDNY filing fits the larger renovation. That is the work we help with before anyone picks up a drill.

The towers have also shown how fires behave inside them. In March 2022 the Brooklyn Eagle reported a smoky fire in a fourth-floor apartment at 75 Henry Street, a 32-story co-op, in a unit that had been under renovation for six months with construction materials inside; smoke reached the hallways, worried residents came out of their apartments, and the doorman alerted tenants near the fire over the building intercom. In December 2022 a ninth-floor fire at Cadman Plaza North injured two people, according to the Eagle. Both stories point to the same design questions: which floors hear the alarm, how residents learn what is happening, and how renovation work inside an occupied apartment is kept from creating the next emergency.

The civic buildings carry their own history. Brooklyn's City Hall, now Borough Hall, lost its cupola and top floors in a fire started by waste paper on February 26, 1895, and the 1876 Brooklyn Theatre fire, which killed at least 278 people, happened on the site where the post office complex now stands. Today's risks are quieter. In May 2023 the FDNY rushed to the Brooklyn War Memorial in the park after smoke was seen pouring from its roof, a building that holds Parks Department and Cadman Park Conservancy offices. Each of these buildings sits under a different owner and, for the federal ones, a different set of rules, which is why the first question on every Cadman Plaza job is who owns the building and who approves the work.

Need Fire Alarm Repair in Cadman Plaza? Start With the Panel History, Not the Ladder

If a fire alarm panel in a Cadman Plaza building shows trouble, keeps beeping or has stopped reporting to the central station, call (347) 934-8335 and send a photo of the panel display and its history log. We book a fire alarm service technician within 2 hours of your request. Emergency fire alarm repair in a tower is a sequence: read what the panel recorded, find the circuit, find the floor, then find the device or splice. Walking 27 floors with a ladder before reading the history wastes the board's money and the residents' patience.

Why is my fire alarm panel showing trouble? In the plaza's buildings the answers tend to follow the building's age and how it is used. In the late-1960s towers, original riser cable and junction boxes in trash-chute rooms, mechanical rooms and stair landings have had decades of heat, moisture and repainting. A fire alarm ground fault appears after a basement water event like the January 2024 water main break that the Brooklyn Eagle reported flooded the Cadman Towers mechanical room. Apartment renovations damage cable in the walls, and the panel reports an open circuit on a riser. Standby batteries age out and the panel shows battery trouble. A switch from copper phone lines to a cellular or IP path that nobody tested leaves the communicator failing. Each points to a different fix, and a technician who reads the history before climbing finds it faster.

Our fire alarm troubleshooting in these buildings covers fire alarm fault diagnosis and fire alarm system diagnostics on conventional and addressable panels: fire alarm trouble signal repair, fire alarm ground fault repair and fire alarm ground fault troubleshooting, fire alarm open circuit repair, fire alarm short circuit troubleshooting, fire alarm battery trouble repair, fire alarm communication failure repair, and fire alarm supervisory trouble on sprinkler valves and tamper switches. Fire alarm intermittent trouble, the kind that clears before anyone arrives, is common in tall buildings where a loose splice on a riser expands and contracts with the seasons; we log it, chase the pattern and trace it to the splice. Fire alarm false alarm troubleshooting matters in a 350-unit tower, where every unwanted alarm sends people into the stairs and erodes trust in the next real one.

When the whole system is down, the response changes. A fire alarm system down repair, fire alarm panel failure repair or commercial fire alarm emergency repair in an occupied high-rise may require a fire watch until the system is restored, and under the Fire Code the FDNY generally has to be notified when a required system is out of service. We tell the managing agent exactly what the panel shows, what the FDNY expects in the meantime, and what the repair will involve before the work starts. Fire alarm system emergency service, after hours fire alarm service and a 24 hour fire alarm repair request all start with the same call, and urgent fire alarm repair or same day fire alarm repair is scheduled as fast as the parts and the building allow. We do not promise same-day installation of anything new, because a new system or a changed design goes back through drawings and the FDNY.

What causes a fire alarm ground fault? A conductor touching a grounded surface somewhere on a circuit: water in a junction box on a cellar ceiling, a cable nicked by a contractor's screw inside an apartment wall, a staple driven through a jacket years ago, or corrosion in a roof-level device. On an addressable system the panel narrows it to a loop; on a conventional system it narrows it to a zone. Addressable fire alarm troubleshooting and addressable fire alarm system repair go faster because the panel names devices, while conventional fire alarm repair means splitting a zone in halves until the fault is found. Either way, fire alarm control panel troubleshooting comes before any device is replaced, and FACP repair is a matter of parts, programming and firmware that only the panel maker's authorized tools can handle properly.

When a board or agency calls a fire alarm repair company or fire alarm repair contractor, the question is who answers and how fast. Our emergency fire alarm service books an emergency fire alarm technician within 2 hours of the request, starting with emergency fire alarm troubleshooting over the phone with the super while the technician travels. A fire alarm communication failure emergency repair, the kind that leaves a required system unmonitored, gets the same priority, because a tower whose panel cannot reach its central station needs that path restored, or a plan for it, the same day. Ordinary fire alarm system repair then follows the panel's history and the approved drawings.

Some calls are not really repairs. A fire alarm panel trouble light that comes on every few weeks, a fire alarm system trouble condition that the porter silences each morning, a fire alarm panel beeping repair request that turns out to be a disconnected printer or annunciator, or a fire alarm system not communicating because the central station account was never transferred when the building changed managing agents: all of these are fire alarm system malfunction repair work that starts with paperwork as much as tools. A fire alarm trouble emergency service visit should end with the cause written down and the owner told whether it will come back.

Building Specialists

Commercial Fire Alarm Systems for Cadman Plaza's Building Types

Commercial fire alarm systems around Cadman Plaza fall into a few distinct families, and each needs a different plan. Who owns the building matters as much as how tall it is: a co-op board, a condo board, a private landlord, the City of New York, the State court system's facilities managers and the federal government all make decisions differently and on different calendars.

Mitchell-Lama and HDFC co-op towers

Cadman Plaza North, Whitman Close and Cadman Towers are the plaza's residential backbone. They are high-rise apartment buildings with compactor rooms on every floor, long double-loaded corridors, garages, laundry rooms, community rooms and mechanical spaces in the cellar. Apartment building fire alarm installation in towers like these is a multifamily building fire alarm installation and a high rise fire alarm installation at once. The questions are practical: whether the existing panel can be supported for a few more years, how many floors a new riser can be pulled through in a week without disturbing shareholders, where the fire command location and annunciator go, and how the system's notification is laid out so a fire on one floor brings the right response from the floors that need it. Boards working under a regulatory agreement or a capital plan usually want the fire alarm phased with other work, and the design professional's drawings can be organized that way.

The renovation fire at 75 Henry Street is a reminder that work inside apartments matters too. Construction materials, open walls and temporary power are the conditions in which fires start and in which existing detectors get covered or damaged. A board's alteration agreement can require contractors to protect detectors, report any fire alarm device they touch, and leave the system in normal condition at the end of each day.

Condominium tower with a public library

One Clinton is a 38-story condominium building with the 26,000-square-foot Brooklyn Heights branch of the Brooklyn Public Library at its base. Condominium building fire alarm installation work in a mixed building like this means two owners and one life-safety system: changes in the library's space or the residential floors must be coordinated with the system as approved, not handled as if each owner had its own. That kind of mixed use building fire alarm installation is common in new construction and depends on clear records of who controls the panel.

City- and state-run civic buildings

Borough Hall, the Municipal Building at 210 Joralemon Street and the Supreme Courthouse at 360 Adams Street are managed by the city's Department of Citywide Administrative Services. Work in them is let through public procurement, often with general contractors and their electrical and fire alarm subcontractors under long-running capital projects like the major program of work the city filed plans for at the Municipal Building in 2021. Office building fire alarm installation in public buildings has extra layers: security screening for crews, work restricted to nights and weekends near courtrooms and public counters, and landmark review where a device would be visible in a designated interior or on a designated facade.

Federal buildings

The Theodore Roosevelt U.S. Courthouse at 225 Cadman Plaza East and the Federal Building and Post Office at 271–301 Cadman Plaza East belong to the federal government. Under 40 U.S.C. § 3312, federal public buildings follow nationally recognized building and fire and life safety codes "to the maximum extent feasible," consult local officials, submit plans for local review on request and allow local inspection during construction, but no local fine or penalty may be imposed on the Government. Fire alarm work there runs through the federal agency's own contracting and approval path, not a private owner's FDNY filing. If you are a contractor or a tenant agency in one of these buildings, we can explain how the work is organized and whether there is a role for a subcontractor; we do not suggest anyone bypass the agency's process.

Offices, restaurants and smaller buildings at the edges

Office towers on the blocks running off Cadman Plaza West, law offices near the courts, restaurants that feed jurors and lawyers at lunch, and the occasional small mixed-use building fill out the picture. Retail store fire alarm installation and restaurant fire alarm installation here are usually tenant work inside a larger building's system, while a standalone building may need its own system once a change of use or renovation triggers it under the Building Code.

Garages and storage

Code compliance first

Fire alarm code compliance in New York City is not satisfied by NFPA 72 alone. Fire alarm code compliance upgrades in the plaza's older buildings usually come from a renovation, a change in use, an FDNY inspection or a board deciding to stop patching an obsolete panel.

Field Notes

Field Notes: Replacing a Tower Panel While Everyone Is Home

This is a typical scenario, not one specific job. A co-op board near the plaza has an original-generation conventional panel in the cellar, a riser that has been spliced on half the floors, and a central station that keeps calling the super about troubles. The board wants a new addressable system but cannot close the building, cannot run a fire watch for months, and has to explain the project to hundreds of shareholders. The first visit is not about equipment. It is a walk through the cellar, the stair landings, a few compactor rooms and the roof with the super, photographing every panel, power supply and splice box, and reading the panel's history to see which floors cause the most trouble.

The design professional then lays out the new system so the riser can be pulled through the stairs or chases a few floors at a time, with the old and new systems running side by side until the new one is accepted. Corridor devices go in first, then the compactor rooms and elevator lobbies, then the cellar and roof spaces. Each floor is pre-tested before the next begins. Shareholders get dated notices for their floor, and the managing agent gets a calendar showing when alarms will sound and when the FDNY acceptance test is planned. When the inspector signs off and the old panel comes out, the board has drawings, test reports and a zone chart it can hand to the next super, and the central station stops calling about the same three floors.

Your Questions Answered

Fire Alarm Installation in Cadman Plaza: Questions Answered

These are the questions co-op boards, managing agents, building engineers, office tenants and contractors around Cadman Plaza tend to ask before a fire alarm project. Each answer is written for the buildings around the park.

Cost and paperwork

Our Mitchell-Lama tower is planning a capital program. Should the fire alarm be its own project or part of the larger one?

Usually part of the larger one, with its own drawings and filing. Riser work, ceiling openings and electrical upgrades overlap, so doing them together saves disruption. The fire alarm still needs a design professional's sealed drawings and an FDNY approval of its own, and the board should see it as a separate line in the capital plan.

What should a board ask for before approving a proposal to replace the building's fire alarm panel?

Ask who the design professional is, which FDNY-certified company and licensed electrician will sign the filing, how the work will be phased floor by floor, how long the old system stays live, and what the proposal includes for the acceptance test. A proposal that skips any of these is incomplete, whatever the total says.

Trust and track record

How can a co-op confirm that the company working on its fire alarm is allowed to do that work in the city?

Ask for the FDNY Fire Alarm Company Certificate and the name of the principal who holds the S-97 Certificate of Fitness, and ask which licensed electrician is on the filing. The FDNY's own FAQ says any company that installs, tests or services a fire alarm system, other than smoke detectors alone, must hold the company certificate.

The central station keeps calling our super about the same floors. Is the monitoring company the problem?

Rarely. The central station only reports what the panel sends. Repeated signals from the same floors usually mean a failing device, a damaged riser splice or a detector in the wrong place, and the fix belongs to the service company, not the monitoring contract.

Who can do the work

Can our building's porters or handyman replace a corridor smoke detector on the building fire alarm?

No. A detector on the building system is part of an FDNY-approved fire alarm, and the FDNY requires a certified fire alarm company for installing, testing or servicing its components. Porters can report damaged or covered devices, which is a real help, but the replacement and test belong to the certified company.

A shareholder renovating an apartment wants to move a ceiling device. Who approves that?

The board, through its alteration agreement, and the fire alarm company that services the building. If the device is part of the approved system, moving it can change the approved drawings, so the building's service company should review it before the contractor touches it.

Technical

In a 27-story tower, does every floor hear the alarm when one detector trips?

Not necessarily. High-rise systems in New York City are usually designed so the floor of the alarm and specific floors around it are notified first, with voice messages guiding occupants. Exactly which floors hear what is set in the design professional's sequence of operations and approved by the FDNY.

Our original riser cable runs through stair landings and compactor rooms. Can it be reused in a new addressable system?

Sometimes, if it tests clean, meets the new system's requirements and the design professional approves it. Fifty-year-old cable in damp or hot rooms often fails insulation testing, and survivability rules for high-rise circuits may require new routing or protection. The survey and testing decide, not the age alone.

Owners, boards and tenants

Who pays for fire alarm work when a law firm's fit-out in our office building needs new devices?

That depends on the lease. Typically the tenant pays for devices and programming its fit-out requires, and the landlord controls how the work ties into the base-building system. The landlord's fire alarm company normally does the programming so the building's records stay in one place.

The library and the condo share one building. Who is responsible for the fire alarm?

The building's governing documents decide, but the system is one system with one FDNY approval. Changes on either side have to be coordinated with whoever controls the panel and the service contract, so both owners should know who that is before a renovation starts.

Our federal agency office is in a federal building. Can we hire our own fire alarm company?

Generally not directly. Work in a federally owned building is controlled by the federal agency that manages it. Talk to the building manager first; any contractor works within that agency's approvals, not a private owner's FDNY filing.

Complaints and problems

Residents say the hallway alarm is too quiet to hear inside their apartments. What can the board do?

Document where and when, then have the service company measure sound levels during the next test. Audibility inside apartments is governed by the code and the approved design; if the system was designed to an older standard, the design professional can advise what an upgrade would involve.

An FDNY inspector left a Notice of Defect for our tower. How long do we have?

The notice states the deadline and what must be corrected. The FDNY's FA-27 process lets the owner submit proof of correction, with a $105 fee per the FDNY's instructions. Start with the service company the day it arrives, because parts and filing time eat the window quickly.

Construction next door keeps setting off our lobby detector. Who fixes that?

Start with the service company to confirm which device and why; dust from demolition is a common cause. The fix may be cleaning or replacing the detector, and the construction contractor may need to control dust at the source. Do not cover or disable the device.

Equipment

Types of Commercial Fire Alarm Systems We Install Around Cadman Plaza

What is a commercial fire alarm system? It is the panel, detectors, manual stations, notification, power supplies and communication path that together detect a fire, warn occupants, report to a central station and operate building functions like elevator recall and fan shutdown. How does a commercial fire alarm system work? Initiating devices send signals to the control panel, the panel applies the programmed sequence, and outputs sound alarms, flash strobes, play voice messages, release doors and call the central station. "Any building in New York City files its fire alarm with the FDNY."

"The whole building evacuates when the alarm goes off."

In many high-rises, it does not. Systems are designed so the alarm floor and selected floors around it are notified first, with voice messages directing what to do. The building's fire safety plan explains what residents and staff should expect.

"Grandfathered" is a description of a current approval, not a promise. Ask the design professional what a planned renovation will trigger before the board votes on it.

NFPA 72 is the national standard for installation and testing, but New York City adopts it with its own amendments through the Building Code's Appendix Q and its own filing rules. Meeting NFPA 72 alone does not satisfy the city's requirements.
