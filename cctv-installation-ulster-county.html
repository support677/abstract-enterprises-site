The Neighborhood

Why Ulster County Properties Need CCTV Planned for Two Very Different Places

Ulster County is one county on the map and two places on the ground. Its county seat, Kingston, is an old river city: the Census Bureau counts 11,049 homes there, 54.1% of them built before 1940, and 51.5% of the occupied ones are rented. Drive forty minutes west on Route 28 and the numbers flip. In the Town of Shandaken, 886 of 2,675 homes, about one in three, are held for seasonal or occasional use; in Hardenburgh it is 204 of 292, and in Denning 237 of 432. Kingston's camera problems are shared hallways, back alleys and storefronts. Shandaken's are houses nobody is in for weeks at a time, or houses full of strangers who rented them for the weekend.

That split decides how a CCTV system gets designed here. A Kingston two-family needs a recorder the tenants cannot reach, separate views for each entrance, and cable fished through plaster and lath in a house that may be 120 years old. A Catskills rental needs exterior cameras that satisfy the listing platform, a recorder that keeps writing when the satellite internet drops, and alerts that reach a host who lives two or three hours away. Our CCTV installers plan the two jobs differently, and the rest of this page explains how.

What the state's crime counts show for Ulster

The New York State Division of Criminal Justice Services publishes index crimes for every police agency in the state. For 2024, Ulster County agencies reported 1,469 property crimes. Of those, 1,301 were larcenies, 116 were burglaries and 52 were motor vehicle thefts. In other words, nearly nine in ten property crimes in the county were thefts of things left reachable, from packages and tools to merchandise and the contents of unlocked cars, not break-ins. That is the kind of crime CCTV surveillance documents best, because the thief is in view of a well-placed camera for long enough to be identified.

Kingston already watches its own streets

Kingston's government runs a camera network of its own. In September 2026 the Daily Freeman reported Police Chief Egidio Tinti's count: 78 public safety cameras, three on city buildings, three at Dietz Stadium, ten in city parks and 62 on street poles and corners, plus two-way license plate readers at six spots on the city line. The chief said plate-reader data is kept for 14 to 21 days and then written over because of storage limits. The city's communications director, Summer Smith, told the paper that data sharing with the vendor is off by default and that "the data belongs to, and can only be accessed by, Kingston unless we share it."

"The public was screaming that (crime) was getting out of hand. There was a hesitation about sharing info with police. Instead of requiring people to be witnesses, we invested in a system we have access to."
— Egidio Tinti, Kingston Police Chief, as quoted by the Daily Freeman, September 5, 2026

None of that covers your back door, your stockroom, your rental's driveway or the porch where packages land. A pole camera on Broadway sees the street; it does not see the alley behind a Wall Street shop or the side yard of a Ponckhockie house. The city's system also answers to the city's retention schedule, not to yours. A private CCTV system sees the places on your property that matter, keeps the footage as long as you size the drive for, and puts the decision to share a clip in your hands, the same principle the city applies to its own data.

Rental Hosts

CCTV for Ulster County Short-Term Rentals: What the Platform and the Towns Expect

Ulster County treats short-term rentals as lodging. The county's Department of Finance requires hotel, motel and short-term rental operators to register and collect the occupancy tax, and several towns add their own permits on top. The Town of Saugerties requires an operating permit from its Building Department under chapter 194 of its code, limited to single-family homes and accessory apartments on the same parcel, and asks for a copy of the county registration with the application. The Town of Olive's rental law, chapter 114, says the owner or host "shall be able to respond in person within one hour." Woodstock adopted short-term rental rules in its zoning law in 2019, and Marbletown requires a town registration number on every online listing. None of these local laws, as we read them, tells a host to install cameras. What they do is make the host responsible for what happens at the house, and a camera system is how an absentee host finds out.

The platform rules are the part hosts trip over. On March 11, 2024, Airbnb announced that it would ban indoor security cameras in listings worldwide, effective April 30 of that year, "regardless of their location, purpose or prior disclosure." Outdoor cameras are still allowed, but the host has to disclose their presence and general location before guests book, they may not monitor indoor spaces, and they are not allowed "in certain outdoor areas where there's a greater expectation of privacy, like an enclosed outdoor shower or sauna." Doorbell cameras and noise decibel monitors are still permitted, and the decibel monitors must be disclosed too.

"Our goal was to create new, clear rules that provide our community with greater clarity about what to expect on Airbnb. These changes were made in consultation with our guests, Hosts and privacy experts, and we'll continue to seek feedback to help ensure our policies work for our global community."
— Juniper Downs, Airbnb Head of Community Policy and Partnerships, Airbnb Newsroom, March 11, 2024

How we lay out a rental so it stays within those rules

  • Exterior only. Cameras go on the outside of the house, the garage and any outbuilding, aimed at the driveway, the approach, the entry doors and the parking area. We do not install cameras inside a listed rental, including the common rooms.
  • Privacy zones blacked out. Where an exterior camera's view would include a hot tub, an outdoor shower, a sauna or a window, we aim it away or set a privacy mask so that part of the picture is never recorded.
  • A list for the listing. At handover you get a plain list of each camera and the area it covers, written so you can copy it into the listing's disclosure.
  • A locked owner's closet. The recorder, the PoE switch and the modem go in a space guests cannot open, on a battery backup, so a guest cannot unplug the system before a party.
  • Alerts that matter to a host. Vehicle and person detection on the driveway, set to send a phone alert during the stay window and to stay quiet when the cleaner's car is expected.

This is general information about published rules and our own practice, not legal advice. Platform policies change, and Vrbo and other sites have their own terms, so read the current policy for every site you list on.

Second Homes

Watching an Empty House in the Mountain Towns

Not every house in the Catskill towns is rented. Many of the 6,545 seasonal homes in the county belong to people who come up for a few weekends a season and leave the house shut the rest of the time. For them, CCTV installation for vacant property is less about catching someone in the act and more about knowing the house is still the way they left it. The questions are practical. Did the plow come, or is the drive still drifted shut? Did the wind bring a limb down on the garage roof? Is that car in the driveway the propane delivery, the caretaker or someone who should not be there?

A system built for an empty house looks a little different from one built for a house with people in it. The first camera goes where a vehicle has to pass to reach the house, usually the narrowest point of the drive, with vehicle detection so a deer or a fox does not set off an alert at 3 a.m. The next cameras cover the doors people would actually use, the garage, and any outbuilding with tools, a generator or a tractor in it. Inside the owner's own home, where no guests stay, an indoor camera on the room with the boiler or the water heater can show a leak or a frozen pipe early; that choice belongs to the owner and only applies when the house is not rented out.

Two settings matter more on an empty house than anywhere else. The first is the camera-offline and drive-failure alert. If the recorder stops seeing a camera, you hear about it the same day rather than finding out in May that nothing has recorded since January. The second is a power-loss signal. A recorder on a UPS keeps running through the short outages that come with ice and wind, and when the battery runs low it can send a last message, which tells you the power is out at the house. With an LTE failover modem, that message still gets through when the cable or DSL line went down in the same storm.

Caretakers are part of most second-home setups in Ulster. We can give a caretaker or a property manager a login that shows live video and alerts but not the full playback archive, and the owner keeps the admin account. When the caretaker changes, the owner deletes that login from the app in a minute, and nothing else about the system changes.

Finally, a second home needs a recorder hidden where a burglar would not think to look and locked where they could not easily take it, because a recorder that walks out the door takes the evidence with it. On a cabin we often use a closet in the owner's locked room, a basement utility space, or a lockbox mounted to the framing. The location goes on your cable map and nowhere else.

Kingston

CCTV Installation Kingston NY: Uptown, the Rondout and Midtown

CCTV installation Kingston NY owners ask us about usually falls into one of three parts of the city, and each one changes the install. Uptown is the Stockade, the oldest part of the city, with Wall Street, North Front Street and John Street lined with shops, restaurants and offices above them. Down the hill, the Rondout runs along Rondout Creek with brick commercial buildings, loft conversions and the waterfront on Broadway and the Strand. In between, Midtown is mostly two- and three-family houses and small apartment buildings along and off Broadway, plus the commercial blocks near the hospital and the arts district.

The Landmarks Commission and anything visible from the street

Kingston has been a Certified Local Government for historic preservation since 1986. Its zoning code, chapter 405, sets up the Historic Landmarks Preservation Commission and lists the Stockade District, the Rondout-West Strand Historic District and the Chestnut Street Historic District among the city's landmark districts. The code says no one may make "any material change in the appearance" of a landmark or a property in a landmark district, including "its light fixtures, signs, sidewalks, fences, steps, paving or other exterior elements visible from a public street or alley," without first getting a certificate of appropriateness. The certificate is in addition to any building permit, not a substitute for one, and the commission may decide without a hearing whether a piece of work is ordinary maintenance.

The code does not mention cameras by name, and we do not decide for the commission what counts. In practice we plan a camera on a landmark-district building as if it will be reviewed: small, dark or painted to match, mounted under an eave, a soffit, a sign band or a porch ceiling rather than on the face of the building, with the cable run inside wherever possible. If the camera would show from the street, we tell you to check with the city's planning office before the install date, and we can give you a cut sheet and a photo mock-up of the mount to bring with you. This is general information, not legal advice.

Stone, brick and plaster in the old city

The Stockade's stone houses have walls that can be well over a foot thick, laid in lime mortar, and the code calls out the four pre-Revolutionary stone houses at the corner of Crown and John Streets by name. We do not core through old stone to get a cable outside. Cable leaves the building through a wooden window frame, a soffit, a sill or an existing penetration, and any anchor that has to go into masonry goes into a mortar joint, which can be repointed later, not into the stone face. Brick buildings in the Rondout get the same treatment. Inside, balloon-framed houses in Midtown have open stud bays from the basement to the attic, which helps a CCTV wiring run, but the plaster and lath is brittle, so we fish rather than cut wherever the bay allows.

Kingston landlords and two-families

With more than half of Kingston's occupied homes rented, many of our Kingston jobs are for landlords. The usual layout covers each entrance, the shared hallway or stair where the law allows it, the yard and the parking area, with the recorder locked in the owner's part of the basement. Tenants can be given a live-view login for the front door without access to playback of the rest of the building. New York's unlawful surveillance law, Penal Law section 250.45, applies to anyone who records people in places where they have a reasonable expectation of privacy, so cameras stay out of apartments, bathrooms and bedrooms entirely. Labor Law section 203-c separately bars video in restrooms, locker rooms and changing rooms at a workplace. This is not legal advice; for a specific building, ask your attorney.

Kingston police have publicized a voluntary program that lets residents and businesses register the locations of their outdoor cameras with the department, so detectives know whom to ask for footage after an incident. Call the department for its current details before you sign up. Registering does not connect your recorder to the city's network; you still decide what to export and hand over.

Building Specialists

CCTV for Ulster County's Stone Houses, Cabins and River-City Rentals

The building decides the cable route, the mount and the camera body more than anything else. These are the building types our CCTV installers see most across the county, and what changes on each.

Dutch stone houses

Uptown Kingston, Hurley, Marbletown and the Huguenot Street area of New Paltz. Thick walls, lime mortar, small windows. Cameras go under eaves and on wood trim; cable exits through frames, never through the stone.

Balloon-framed city houses

Midtown Kingston and the villages of Saugerties and Ellenville. Open stud bays help cable reach the attic, but old plaster cracks easily, so we fish runs and patch small access holes rather than open walls.

Brick commercial blocks

Wall Street, North Front Street, Broadway in the Rondout and Partition Street in Saugerties. Shared basements and upper apartments; recorder in a locked cellar room, cameras on sign bands and under awnings.

Log homes and A-frames

Woodstock, Shandaken, Olive and the Rondout Valley. Solid log walls have no cavities, so cable runs along beams, in chases, through the crawl space or inside a painted raceway that matches the wood.

Cabins with outbuildings

A main house, a bunkhouse, a detached garage or a studio. Each building gets Cat6 in buried conduit where we can trench or a point-to-point wireless bridge where ledge stops the shovel.

Postwar ranches and splits

Town of Ulster, Esopus, Lloyd and Plattekill. Attic and basement access make concealed CCTV wiring straightforward; most get four to six cameras on a PoE NVR.

Converted lofts and mills

Rondout warehouses, old mills along the Esopus and Rondout creeks. Exposed brick and timber; we run cable in conduit painted to match and keep the recorder off the floor in case of water.

Inns, motels and event barns

Route 28, Route 209 and the Shawangunk foothills. Many outside doors, big parking areas and seasonal crowds; usually 12 to 16 cameras on a 16-channel NVR.

Material and method on each type

On cedar shingle and clapboard we mount on a junction box or a block cut to fit the siding, so water sheds around the mount instead of behind it, and seal every penetration. On log homes, a camera on the end grain of a log can loosen as the wood checks and shrinks, so we mount on a face or on trim. On stone and brick, anchors go into joints. Under deep eaves, where ice dams and sliding snow are a winter fact in the mountain towns, cameras sit far enough back that falling ice clears them and the infrared does not bounce off a hanging icicle. In crawl spaces and unheated attics, cable goes in conduit or loom so mice and squirrels have less to chew. Every run is labeled at both ends, and the label goes on the cable map you keep.

Commercial CCTV Installation

Business CCTV Installation on Ulster County's Main Corridors

Commercial CCTV installation in Ulster follows the roads. Most of the county's businesses sit along a handful of corridors, and each one has its own pattern of what goes missing and what a business needs to prove afterward.

Ulster Avenue and Route 9W, Town of Ulster and Kingston

The big-box stores and plazas along Ulster Avenue and Route 9W, just north of the city line, are where the Town of Ulster police reported 240 larcenies in 2024. Retail CCTV installation here means cameras at every customer door, aimed so a face fills enough of the frame to identify, a camera over each register, coverage of the fitting room corridor but never inside the rooms, and a view of the rear receiving door. Parking-lot views are framed for vehicles entering and leaving rather than trying to cover the entire asphalt with one camera. The former IBM Kingston campus, now iPark 87 on Enterprise Drive in the Town of Ulster, is a reminder of how much empty commercial space the corridor carries: the campus covers about 200 acres and more than a million square feet of buildings, and in February 2026 the county's economic development alliance voted to foreclose on two parcels of its West Campus. Vacant and partly leased buildings like those need perimeter cameras and door views far more than interior ones.

Uptown Kingston and the Rondout

Wall Street and North Front Street restaurants, bars, galleries and boutiques usually need four to eight cameras: the entry, the register or bar, the back door to the alley, the stockroom and the kitchen door. Our price list puts a four-camera 4K Uptown Kingston retail system at $2,400 to $3,200 installed, with the recorder in a locked office or cellar. Bar CCTV installation adds a camera that covers the door and the ID check from inside, and a view down the bar itself. In the Rondout, waterfront restaurants and shops on Broadway and the Strand add outdoor seating and dock-side views.

New Paltz Main Street and Route 299

The village's Main Street and Route 299 corridor serve SUNY New Paltz students, weekend visitors heading to the Shawangunk Ridge and Minnewaska, and residents. The New Paltz police agency reported 100 larcenies in 2024, and the SUNY campus police another 21. Restaurant CCTV installation here is about the door, the counter, the delivery pickup shelf and the dumpster area behind the building. Landlords with student rentals register with the Village of New Paltz Building Department, which inspects registered rentals; cameras for those houses cover the exterior entries, the parking and the trash area.

Saugerties, Route 32 and Route 212

Partition Street and Main Street in the village, the Route 32 and Route 9W commercial stretches in the town, and the Route 212 road toward Woodstock. With 19 burglaries and 13 vehicle thefts reported by Saugerties police in 2024, business owners here ask about after-hours coverage more than daytime shoplifting: rear doors, garage bays, equipment yards and the cars parked overnight in a lot.

Route 28, Route 209 and the mountain towns

Route 28 through Boiceville, Phoenicia and Pine Hill, and Route 209 through Stone Ridge, Accord, Kerhonkson and Ellenville, carry the county's inns, motels, outfitters, farm markets, cafes and event venues. Hotel CCTV installation on these roads usually means a camera at the office, cameras that see the outside doors of each room wing, the lot entrance, and the area around the trash and recycling enclosure. Business is seasonal, so the recorder has to keep working through months when nobody checks the screen, and the owner needs an alert if a camera goes dark.

Warehouses, storage and contractors' yards

Contractors' yards, self storage, distribution buildings and fuel dealers sit along Route 9W in Esopus and Lloyd, Route 32, Route 209 and the industrial roads in the Town of Ulster. Warehouse CCTV installation covers dock doors, the gate, the yard and the fuel pumps, with plate cameras at the gate where the business wants a log of every vehicle. Our warehouse security camera installation guide for Ulster County goes deeper on dock and yard layouts.

Licensed cannabis businesses

Adult-use cannabis licensees in New York have a camera rule written into state regulations. Under 9 NYCRR section 125.3, video surveillance is required in all areas that may contain cannabis, at every point of entry and exit, in surveillance rooms and in any parking lot. Cameras at entries, exits and each point of sale must allow "clear and certain identification" of anyone there. Recordings must be continuous during operating hours, motion-triggered at other times, time-stamped to a synchronized clock, exportable as stills and as video in a standard format, and kept for at least 60 days. The system must notify someone if it fails and stay running for at least eight hours in a power outage. A dispensary, processor or farm in Ulster County holding or applying for a license needs a recorder sized for 60 days of continuous 4K video, a UPS sized for eight hours, and an export routine the staff can run without the owner. We design to that list; the regulation itself and your compliance plan are for you and the Office of Cannabis Management to settle. This is not legal advice.

State laws an Ulster business camera has to respect

  • Labor Law section 203-c keeps cameras out of employee restrooms, locker rooms and changing rooms, so we never place one there.
  • Recording a conversation you are not part of without the consent of at least one participant can be eavesdropping (Penal Law sections 250.00 and 250.05), so we leave audio off at businesses unless you decide otherwise with that rule in mind.

Your Questions Answered

Ulster County CCTV: Questions Answered

Where can outdoor cameras point at a rental with a hot tub, an outdoor shower or a sauna?

Away from them. Airbnb's policy bars cameras in outdoor areas with a greater expectation of privacy, naming an enclosed outdoor shower and a sauna, and a hot tub on a deck raises the same concern for most guests. We aim the deck or yard camera at the stairs and the approach instead of the tub, and where the tub would still show at the edge of the frame, we set a privacy mask so that part of the picture is blacked out on the recording itself, not just in the app.

What should a short-term rental listing say about the cameras on the property?

Airbnb asks hosts to disclose the presence and general location of every exterior camera before guests book, for example a camera over the driveway or at the front door. At handover we give you a short list in plain words: which buildings have cameras, which areas each one covers and whether any doorbell camera records. You copy that into the listing's safety disclosure. If you later add or move a camera, update the listing the same day.

Can a driveway camera show a host how many cars arrived for a weekend booking?

Yes, and it is the most common reason Catskills hosts call us. A camera framed on the narrowest point of the driveway, set to detect vehicles crossing a line, logs every arrival and departure with a time stamp. You see three cars on a booking for two, or a stream of cars on a Saturday night, without looking inside the house. Pair it with a disclosed noise decibel monitor indoors if you want a warning before a party gets going.

Can a camera warn us when guests leave a garage door, a gate or a slider open?

An exterior camera can be set to alert when an object or a person crosses into a zone, and on some models when a scene stays changed for a set time, which catches an open garage door left for an hour. It will not replace a door sensor, which is more reliable for an open-or-closed question. For a gate or a garage, many hosts use both: a contact sensor for the status and the camera to see who opened it.

How do you record a keypad or lockbox door without capturing the code?

We frame the entry camera to see the face of the person at the door and the door itself, then mask the keypad or lockbox so the code is never on the recording. Guests and cleaners can be told the camera cannot see the keys, which is true. If you want proof that a code was used, the smart lock's own log does that better than a camera.

Can a camera show whether guests used the bear-proof trash shed?

Yes. Black bears are a fact of life in the Catskills, and a bag left on the porch overnight becomes a mess and a complaint from the neighbors. A camera on the trash enclosure or the route to it shows when bags went in, whether the latch was closed, and what got into it overnight. That gives you something specific to point to if a cleanup fee is disputed.

How do you mount a camera on an old stone house without cracking the stone?

We avoid drilling the stone face at all where we can. Cameras go on the wooden soffit, a window or door frame, a porch beam or a mounting block fixed in a mortar joint. If an anchor must go into masonry, it goes into the joint, which can be repointed, using an anchor rated for soft mortar. Cable comes out through wood, not stone. That keeps the mount repairable and leaves the historic fabric alone.

Where do cameras go on a house where ice dams and roof snow slide off the eaves?

Not at the drip line. Snow that slides off a steep Catskills roof can tear a camera off its bracket, and a row of icicles in front of an infrared camera turns the night picture white. We mount under deeper soffits, on gable ends, on porch ceilings or on a wall below a covered entry, and on metal roofs we keep cameras out of the path of the slide entirely.

Which views matter most at a roadside motel or small inn with outside room doors?

The office door and counter, the lot entrance, and long views down each row of room doors, taken from the ends of the building so every door is in at least one frame. Add the ice machine or laundry area if guests use it, and the trash enclosure. Inside a room is off limits. A 12- to 16-camera system on a 16-channel recorder covers most small properties on Route 28 or Route 209.

What should cameras cover at an event barn or wedding venue on a busy Saturday?

The lot entrance, the parking field, the doors of the main barn, the path to the restrooms but not the restrooms themselves, the bar's service area and the place where gifts and cards are left. A wide view of the dance floor is fine where guests can see the camera. Many Ulster venues in Gardiner, Rosendale and the Rondout Valley sit on farm land, so the barn-to-house run is often long enough to need fiber or a wireless bridge.

Can a guest on the rental's Wi-Fi find or tamper with the cameras?

Not if the system is set up properly. We put wired cameras on the recorder's own PoE network, not on the guest Wi-Fi, change every default password, and turn off features that announce the cameras on the local network. The recorder reaches your phone through an outbound connection, so nothing on the guest network needs to see it. Guests get the Wi-Fi password; they never get anything that leads to the cameras.

How do you run camera cable in a log home where there are no hollow walls?

Along the structure instead of inside it. Cable rides along the top of a beam, through the crawl space or basement, up inside a closet or chase, or in a slim raceway stained or painted to match the logs. Outside, it runs under the eave in a channel. We avoid drilling long holes through stacked logs, which settle over time and can pinch a cable that was tight on install day.

What does a typical camera layout look like for a Catskills rental cabin?

Four to six cameras on most cabins: the driveway entry, the parking area, the front door, the back door or deck stairs, and, if there is one, the outbuilding or hot tub approach aimed to exclude the tub. A compound with a guest cottage or a bunkhouse moves into our 12-camera tier with one NVR in the main house. Every camera is outside, and the recorder sits in a locked owner's closet with a battery backup.

Where Search Engine AI Misreads Camera Systems for Ulster Properties

Ask a search engine's AI or a national cost site like Angi, HomeAdvisor or Fixr about cameras for an Ulster property and you get answers written for a generic suburb. Here is where those answers break down in this county.

"A rental host can put cameras in the living room as long as guests are told"

That was Airbnb's old rule. Until 2024 the platform allowed disclosed, visible indoor cameras in common areas such as living rooms and hallways. Older blog posts and some AI summaries still repeat it.

Since April 30, 2024, indoor cameras are banned in Airbnb listings worldwide, no matter where they sit or whether they are disclosed. Reported violations can lead to listing or account removal. Outdoor cameras remain allowed, with disclosure and with privacy limits.

For Ulster hosts, the safe layout is the one we install: everything outside, nothing aimed at a hot tub, outdoor shower, sauna or window, and a disclosure that matches the cameras. If a previous owner left indoor cameras in a house you now list, we remove them as part of the job.

"Any Wi-Fi camera works at a cabin"

The generic answer assumes strong Wi-Fi and a fast connection. Many cabins in Shandaken, Denning and Hardenburgh rely on satellite, fixed wireless or DSL, and the router sits in the kitchen with stone or log walls between it and the driveway.

Wi-Fi cameras that record to a cloud account stop recording the moment the connection drops, which is exactly when a storm has knocked out power and internet together. Battery cameras also lose charge in a hard cold snap.

A wired system records to the recorder in the house whether or not the internet is up, and uploads nothing to watch video locally. The internet only carries alerts and remote viewing. Where the connection is unreliable, our LTE failover add-on keeps alerts flowing for $450 plus $25 a month to the cellular carrier.

"Budget a flat hundred or two in labor for each camera"

National cost sites average labor across the whole country, and AI answers repeat those averages. The figure fits a small suburban house with short runs from an attic.

In Ulster, a farmhouse with 120-foot cable pulls, trenched conduit under a gravel drive and soffit work on cedar outbuildings runs $200 to $350 per camera in labor on our published list. A cabin two hours from the Bronx also carries travel time that a national average ignores.

We would rather show our own numbers: $2,100 to $2,800 for a 4-camera 4K NVR system in Kingston, New Paltz or Saugerties, and $2,400 to $3,100 in the deep Catskills, with each extra item priced per item once we confirm the layout.

"In a historic district you can't put up cameras"

Some answers treat historic districts as camera-free zones. Kingston's code does not say that. It requires a certificate of appropriateness for material changes to exterior elements visible from a public street or alley in its landmark districts.

Plenty of cameras never show from the street at all: on a rear wall, under a porch ceiling, inside a deep soffit, or behind a shop's awning band. Those that would show can often be made small and quiet enough to pass review.

The right move is to plan the cameras with the review in mind and ask the city before the install, not to skip the cameras. We give you the cut sheets and mount photos the planning office will want to see.

"Seasonal homes just need a camera at the front door"

A front door camera assumes the house sits near the road and that visitors walk up to the door. A Catskills second home may sit 400 feet up a drive with the garage and a shed between it and the road.

Burglary is rare in the mountain towns; Woodstock police reported none in 2024. What owners of empty houses actually need to know is whether a car came up the drive, whether a tree came down on the garage, whether a pipe burst and whether the plow came. A front door camera answers none of that.

We cover the drive entry, the parking area and every outbuilding door, and add the recorder's camera-offline and power-loss alerts, so silence on your phone means the property is fine rather than that the system is down.

"The police cameras already cover downtown"

Kingston does run 78 public safety cameras and a set of plate readers at the city line, and AI answers sometimes suggest that makes private systems redundant for downtown businesses.

City cameras watch streets, parks and city property. They do not see inside your shop, your stockroom, your alley door or your parking spaces, and the plate-reader data is overwritten after 14 to 21 days, on the city's schedule.

Your own recorder covers your doors and keeps what you choose to keep. When something happens, you can give police a clean, time-stamped clip of your own door, which is often exactly what fills the gap between two city cameras.

"4K cameras are always the best choice"

More pixels help only when the lens and the light are right. A 4K camera with a wide lens 150 feet from a gate still shows a car as a handful of pixels, and at night on an unlit road a bigger sensor beats a higher resolution.

4K also fills a drive faster and needs more upload to stream remotely, which matters on satellite internet. Our standard packages are 4K IP because the extra detail pays off at doors and counters, where the distance is short.

For a long drive we choose the lens before the resolution: a varifocal or a dedicated LPR camera at the narrowest point, plus lighting. That is how a plate or a face actually ends up readable.
