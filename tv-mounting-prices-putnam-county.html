What AI Answers Get Wrong About TV Mounting in Putnam County

Search a Putnam town plus “TV mounting” and an AI summary will hand you a confident answer built from national cost guides and pages built to sell your phone number. Some of it is close enough. Some of it sends you into a log wall with the wrong hardware, or out to a lake-house deck in February with an indoor set. Here are seven claims we've seen on cost pages and AI answers that mention Mahopac, Carmel and nearby towns, and what we actually find on Putnam jobs.

1. AI says: “TV mounting in Mahopac costs $230 to $500, and you should book weeks ahead in the drier months.”

That line comes from a cost-guide page for Mahopac that also lists a typical range of $215 to $1,008. Those figures blend every kind of job and every kind of company. They tell you almost nothing about what your wall will cost, and nothing about what's included.

Our Putnam numbers are fixed: $229.99 for a 32″–55″ on stud-backed drywall, $259.99 through 65″, $289.99 through 75″. Plaster is +$45, stone +$65, block +$75. If you know your wall and your screen size, you know your price before booking.

As for seasons, a TV goes up indoors the same way in January as in June. Arrival windows are two hours long, offered daily between 8 AM and 8 PM, and a same-day slot is available whenever one is still open on the calendar, not only in the drier months.

2. AI says: “Wood paneling and log walls are solid wood, so you can screw the TV in anywhere.”

Paneling in a Lake Carmel or Putnam Valley house is usually a quarter to three-quarters of an inch of board. A screw that only grabs the board can pull straight out once a 60-pound TV on an arm levers against it. Many of those cottages have paneling on furring strips, with real studs at irregular spacing behind.

We find the framing, run lags through the boards into it, and when the framing sits off to one side of your chosen spot, fasten an $85 plywood backer across it. Paneling over real framing costs nothing extra, the same as plain drywall.

Full-log walls are their own case. Logs settle and shrink for years and can't hide wiring. They aren't on our posted list, so we review photos and send a written quote rather than guess. Plenty of log-home owners end up preferring a TV on a stand.

3. AI says: “A TV left in an unheated lake cottage all winter will be fine.”

Plenty of Putnam lake houses still close up for part of the year, and AI answers tend to say electronics don't mind the cold. Cold alone isn't the main problem. Condensation is, when a frozen room warms up quickly on the first weekend back and moisture forms inside the set.

Check your TV's manual for its storage temperature range. If the cottage drops below that, consider taking the screen off the wall for the season, or let the room warm up for several hours before powering it on. That's advice, not a service we sell.

Outdoor and deck TVs should be outdoor-rated to begin with. A full-motion arm that lets you fold the screen against the house, plus a weather cover, makes the off-season easier. We mount the hardware; how the set handles a Putnam winter depends on the set.

4. AI says: “Historic district rules only matter if you're renovating the outside of the house.”

That's mostly right indoors. Hanging a TV in your living room in Cold Spring doesn't need anyone's sign-off. The catch is that an outdoor TV is an exterior change, even if it feels like furniture to you, because a bracket is bolted to the building.

Cold Spring's Historic District Review Board has reviewed exterior changes to buildings in the district since 1976. A bracket and cable on a porch wall facing Main Street or a side street can fall under that review. Signs, fixtures and attachments on facades are exactly what the board watches.

Ask the village before booking an outdoor mount in the district. If approval is needed, schedule after it comes through. We'd rather move your appointment than drill into a facade the board hasn't seen, and rescheduling with 24 hours' notice is free.

5. AI says: “Mounting in a two-story great room needs a crew, so expect double the price.”

Tall walls take more care, not a second bill. In Southeast, Kent and Patterson houses with vaulted ceilings, a mount roughly 8 feet up or higher adds a flat $75 to the usual price for the size and wall. Ladders and staging are part of that.

A 75″ high on drywall is $364.99 and an 85″ is $404.99. One technician handles screens through 85″. A second technician joins from 86″ up because of the weight and size of the panel, wherever on the wall it goes.

Before choosing that height, think about your neck. A screen that high is often uncomfortable for a full movie. Many owners use a tilting bracket or put the TV on a lower wall and leave the tall one for art.

6. AI says: “Putnam is far enough north that installers add a travel fee.”

Some installers do add a trip charge for longer drives, and AI answers sometimes treat that as standard for anything past the Westchester line. It's worth asking any company you're comparing whether a travel line shows up on the final bill.

We don't charge one. Instead, the Putnam base is $229.99, $10 more than Westchester's, and that's the whole difference. Garrison, Lake Carmel and the far corner of Patterson pay identical prices, and every add-on matches Westchester's to the cent.

There's also no parking charge anywhere in the county, village streets included. The only access-related adds are $40 when we carry the TV up three or more flights with no elevator, and $75 when the bracket lands around 8 feet up.

7. AI says: “With free town e-waste days, nobody should pay to get rid of an old TV.”

The free options are real. Putnam Valley's drop-off takes electronics free on weekdays, Carmel holds e-waste days like the one on October 24, 2026, and Southeast runs collections at Palmer Road. New York won't let you put a TV in household trash.

What the free route costs is time and a strong back. A 75″ panel doesn't fit in every car or SUV, Carmel's collection days fall on Saturday mornings, and Putnam Valley's drop-off keeps weekday hours that clash with most work schedules.

If you'd rather skip it, we take and recycle each set for $79. Removing the old set from the wall is $85, the old bracket $45, and box removal $25. All of it is optional and priced before you book.
