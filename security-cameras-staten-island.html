The Neighborhood

Why Staten Island Shops Differently for Security Cameras

Staten Island is the one borough where most people own the building they want to protect. That changes what a security camera store has to stock. A Manhattan renter buys one indoor camera and asks the landlord about the hallway. A Staten Island buyer usually owns a detached or semi-detached house with a driveway, a side yard, a back deck, a detached garage or a basement apartment, and wants cameras on all of it. A Staten Island business is more likely to sit in a strip plaza with a parking lot out front than on a sidewalk under somebody else's apartments. Those are different shopping lists, and a camera kit designed for a generic suburb only covers part of either one.

The housing numbers explain it. The NYU Furman Center puts Staten Island's homeownership rate at 67.7 percent in 2024, against a citywide share of 32.7 percent, and counts roughly 185,800 housing units in the borough in its latest figures. The State Comptroller's economic snapshot of the borough notes that two-thirds of Staten Island commuters drive to work, nearly three times the share in the rest of the city. Owners who park in their own driveways and leave the house empty all day buy driveway security cameras, garage security cameras and front door security cameras together, and they want to check them from a phone at work.

That is why most of what we sell on the Island is wired. A detached house has an attic, a basement or a crawl space, and often a garage, which gives cable somewhere to run. PoE network cameras on an NVR, or HD coax cameras on a DVR where an older system already left coax in the walls, record steadily without depending on the home router. We still sell WiFi security cameras where they fit — a camera on a shed at the back of a deep lot, a temporary view during a renovation — and we tell buyers plainly where vinyl siding over foil-faced insulation or a brick-faced first floor will weaken a wireless signal.

For a borough that parks in its driveways, that single line shapes more camera purchases than any other.

Cars taken from driveways and curbs

The Staten Island Advance tracked the same rise through the summer. In July 2026 it reported that vehicle thefts on the Island were up roughly 47 percent, 163 incidents through July 19 against 111 a year earlier, with 16 thefts in the single week of July 6–12. District Attorney Michael E. McMahon called the CompStat numbers "a troubling trend that demands immediate attention" and noted that the borough had reversed a similar spike a few years earlier (SILive.com, Maura Grunlund, July 24, 2026).

A camera does not stop a car from being driven away. What it can do is record the arrival on foot, the face at the driver's door, the getaway vehicle and its direction, and the time to the second. That is evidence the 120th, 121st, 122nd and 123rd Precincts can use. It takes a different camera from a porch camera: a narrower lens or a varifocal model aimed down the length of the driveway, mounted high enough to be out of reach but low enough to see under a hood, plus good night performance, because most of these thefts happen after dark. We cover driveway camera choices in detail in the questions further down this page.

Packages on Island porches

Package theft has its own Island pattern. In August 2024 the Advance reported that the NYPD was warning residents about an organized crew targeting phone deliveries to Staten Island homes. Lt. Francis Depalma of the Staten Island Detective Bureau's grand larceny unit said the thieves knew where the phones were going ahead of time, and added: "year to date, we're looking at a vast amount of package thefts that's affecting Staten Island." His advice was to require a signature for valuable deliveries and to refuse anyone who knocks asking to take a delivered package back.

A year later, an Elm Park homeowner near Morningstar Road and Dixon Avenue told the Advance that a package had been taken from his porch in daylight in plain view of a large surveillance camera. "We have cameras and someone is still stealing," he said; no arrest had been made when the story ran (SILive.com, August 7, 2025). That is the honest limit of any camera. A visible camera deters some thieves, not all, and its real job is to record a face and a vehicle clearly enough to act on. A big camera mounted under the gutter that sees the top of a hood fails that test. A smaller camera at door height with a clear view of the steps passes it.

The East and South Shore after Sandy

No buying guide for Staten Island makes sense without Sandy. The State Comptroller's report records that the storm caused 23 deaths on Staten Island, that the East and South Shores were the hardest hit, and that those two areas accounted for nearly a quarter of the buildings in New York City that were severely damaged or destroyed. The recovery that followed changed the housing on those shores. Under the city's Build It Back program, a substantially damaged home could be repaired and raised to flood elevation standards, or rebuilt as a new elevated home, and the program's modular pilot alone placed 32 new homes on Staten Island. In Oakwood Beach, PBS reported that nearly every one of the neighborhood's 180 homeowners took the state's voluntary buyout within three years of the storm.

The protection project is still being built. The National Park Service describes the Army Corps of Engineers' South Shore project as a 5.3-mile line of protection with a 4.3-mile seawall and promenade, a mile of levees and floodwalls, and a deployable feature at Hylan Boulevard. The Corps' New York District reported in May 2026 that the first construction contract, Interior Drainage Area E at South Beach, was on temporary hold over contractor issues, with designs for the remaining seawall contracts still under way.

For camera buyers along Midland Beach, New Dorp Beach, South Beach, Ocean Breeze, Oakwood and Great Kills, all of this adds up to three equipment rules. First, the recorder does not go on the ground floor or in a basement that can take water; it goes upstairs, in a closet or an office. Second, a raised house puts the front door eight or more feet above the yard at the top of a stair, so the camera that watches the door and the camera that watches the space under the house are two different cameras. Third, these houses face open water and take wind and salt air in a way an inland Mid-Island block does not, so outdoor housings, mounts and junction boxes need to be chosen for that.

The West Shore's warehouses

The fourth Staten Island is industrial. Matrix Global Logistics Park sits on a 400-acre site at the foot of the Goethals Bridge, and the Advance has reported Amazon leasing a 975,000-square-foot building there as its third Staten Island warehouse. Around those big buildings are smaller warehouses, contractor yards, truck lots and auto businesses in Bloomfield, Travis, Charleston and along the West Shore Expressway. Those buyers need long fence lines, unlit yards and loading doors covered, which means bigger recorders, long-range lenses and cameras that can tell a person from a deer in the dark.

Outdoor security cameras built for an island

Outdoor surveillance cameras on a Staten Island property usually go in five places: under the soffit at the front corner of the house, over the garage door looking down the driveway, on the rear corner covering the deck and yard, on a fence post or shed at the back of the lot, and on the sign band or canopy of a store. What matters outdoors is the weather rating, the night picture, the lens and the mount. Our house-brand outdoor cameras carry IP66 or IP67 weatherproof ratings depending on the model, with night vision rated from 60 to 120 feet.

Weatherproof security cameras and outdoor winter security cameras for the Island have to deal with more than cold. A two-story house on a corner lot in Great Kills or a raised house on the East Shore takes steady wind off the water, and that wind shakes a camera on a flimsy bracket enough to blur the picture and slowly loosen the screws. Salt air attacks cheap metal hardware long before it harms the camera. So the outdoor kit we sell pairs each camera with a junction box that seals the connector, a short and rigid mount instead of a long arm, and stainless or coated hardware. On the North Shore and Mid-Island, inland of the worst exposure, a standard mount is usually fine.

For lots without streetlights, typical on the South Shore's deeper blocks and around the Greenbelt, we steer buyers to full color night vision cameras with a built-in light, which keep color after dark and make it obvious to anyone walking up the driveway that the yard is watched. Indoor security cameras

Indoor surveillance cameras cover entry halls, the door from the garage into the house, basements and basement apartments' shared doors, stairwells in two-family houses, store floors, registers, stockrooms and warehouse aisles. Indoors the job is usually a wide view from a ceiling corner, so dome and turret cameras are the default. They are small, they don't get knocked out of position, and they mount cleanly on drywall, drop tile or a store's slatwall.

In a store, cash register security cameras and point of sale surveillance cameras go above and slightly behind the counter so they see the drawer, the hands and the bills. Restaurant kitchen security cameras stay away from the hood line, because grease and steam fog a lens. In a house, basement security cameras earn their place on the Island because so many basements have an outside door, a finished room or a separate unit. If the house also has an alarm, an indoor camera can sit right beside the motion sensor, as in the photo below, so a triggered zone and a clip of the same corner arrive together.

PoE, WiFi or HD coax: how each camera connects

PoE security cameras run on a single Cat6 cable that carries both power and video back to a PoE NVR or a PoE switch. It is our default for new Staten Island systems because detached houses usually have a clear cable path through an attic, a garage ceiling or an unfinished basement, and one cable per camera means no outlet at the soffit. Wired analog cameras on HD coax run to a DVR and are the cheapest upgrade where an older system already left RG59 or Siamese cable in place, which is common in houses wired in the 1990s and 2000s. Wireless cameras connect over WiFi but still need power from an outlet, a battery or a solar panel.

Buy PoE cameras or wired security cameras here and we size the switch or the NVR's PoE ports for the full load, including infrared and spotlights at night. Buy wireless security cameras here and we will tell you up front whether your router can reach the spot. On a deep lot, the shed at the back is often a better job for a PoE camera on a buried outdoor cable, or a point-to-point wireless bridge, than for a battery camera that needs charging every few weeks in January.

Smart detection, analytics and plate capture

AI security cameras is a broad label. For most Staten Island buyers, the feature that matters is person and vehicle detection, so the phone buzzes for a person walking up the driveway at 2 a.m. and not for every raccoon, deer, swaying tree or passing headlight. That matters more here than in denser boroughs, because yards back onto woods, parkland and Bluebelt wetlands full of wildlife. Several of our house-brand packages list smart AI human and vehicle detection on the spec sheet, and that is the first smart feature we recommend. Smart motion detection cameras, security cameras with person detection and security cameras with vehicle detection cut false alerts; smart search security camera systems let you pull every "vehicle" event from last night in seconds.

License plate recognition cameras (LPR or ANPR cameras), license plate capture cameras, facial recognition security cameras and people counting cameras are separate products with their own lenses, settings and legal questions. Plate readers have drawn attention on the Island; a recent r/statenisland thread on Flock cameras noted that the few documented on Staten Island seemed to be on private property. For a home driveway, a well-aimed 4K or varifocal camera usually records a readable plate without dedicated LPR. We describe a product as having LPR, facial recognition, NDAA compliance, ONVIF, H.265, IK10 or edge AI only when the manufacturer's spec sheet says so, and we show you that sheet before you buy.

A digital video recorder records coax cameras. Security camera laws for Staten Island businesses

Two sets of rules come up most often when a business buys cameras anywhere in the city, Staten Island included. The first is New York City's biometric identifier law, Administrative Code §22-1201 and following. Since July 9, 2021, retail stores, food and drink establishments and places of entertainment that collect biometric identifier information from customers, such as face geometry, must post a clear and conspicuous sign near every customer entrance, and may not sell or share that information for value. According to a Troutman Pepper summary of the law, ordinary video that is not analyzed by identifying software and is shared only with law enforcement falls outside the disclosure duty.

The second is audio. Under New York Penal Law §250.05, eavesdropping, including the mechanical overhearing of a conversation, is a class E felony, and §250.00 defines mechanical overhearing as recording a conversation without the consent of at least one party to it. That is why audio recording security cameras and two way audio security cameras are features we configure deliberately: off by default on store floors, on only where there is a clear reason, and never where they would capture conversations nobody is part of.

CCTV regulations and security camera privacy laws for homes come down to common sense more than statute: record your own property and shared areas, not the inside of anyone's home, a bathroom or a changing area, and don't aim cameras into a neighbor's windows. Are security cameras legal on a Staten Island house or store? Video of your own property and the public-facing areas around it generally is; the edges are audio, biometrics and private spaces. This is general information, not legal advice.

Your Questions Answered

Buying Security Cameras on Staten Island: Questions Answered

These are the questions Island homeowners and business owners ask before they spend anything. The answers are the ones we give on the phone.

What are the best security cameras for a detached house with a driveway?

Wired cameras with the lens matched to each view. For most detached Island houses that means a 4K or varifocal camera aimed down the driveway, a 5MP turret covering the front door and porch, a bullet on each side yard that has a gate, and a turret on the rear corner over the deck, all PoE on an 8-channel NVR. Full color night vision helps where the street is dark. Battery cameras are convenient but need charging and can be pulled down; wired cameras record every second and can't be unplugged from the porch. "Best" is less about the brand than about putting the right lens on each spot, which is why we send a 2K and a 4K option to compare.

Which camera can read a license plate at the end of my driveway at night?

A narrow-view camera dedicated to that job. A typical wide camera covering the whole front of the house turns a plate 50 feet away into a few unreadable pixels, especially at night when headlights flare. What works is a 4K or varifocal camera zoomed to frame the driveway apron and the curb, mounted so the plate passes through the middle of the frame, with the shutter and infrared tuned for moving cars. Dedicated license plate capture cameras are an option for a business entrance. For a house, one well-set camera usually does it, and it also records the face of someone walking to a parked car.

Can one recorder cover the house and a detached garage across the yard?

Yes. The garage cameras connect back to the recorder in the house in one of three ways: an outdoor-rated Cat6 cable in conduit, buried or run along a fence; a small PoE switch in the garage fed by one cable from the house; or a point-to-point wireless bridge where digging is impractical. All of the cameras then show in the same app and record to the same drive. We size the recorder so the garage, its door and the alley or rear drive behind it can be added without replacing anything.

Where do cameras go on a house raised after Sandy?

Think of it as two houses stacked. Up top, a camera or video doorbell at the entry landing covers the door and anyone on the stair. Below, a camera on the underside of the floor or on the structure covers the parking pad, the storage enclosure and the bottom of the stair. Add one on the rear for the yard and, on waterfront blocks, the beach or marsh side. Each camera's cable runs inside the structure or in sealed conduit, never loose along the stair, and every outdoor connection gets a junction box because the underside of a raised house is damp and windy.

Should the recorder stay on the ground floor of a house in a flood zone?

No. Put the recorder, its hard drive, the PoE switch and the modem on the living floor, in a closet, a bedroom or an office, ideally with a small battery backup. A recorder in a ground-floor utility room or basement on the East or South Shore is the first thing lost in a flood, and with it every recording. Running the camera cables up to the living floor costs a little more labor and is worth it. The same goes for a store in a low-lying plaza: the recorder goes on a high shelf in a locked office, not under the counter.

Will an outdoor camera on a two-story corner shake in a strong wind?

It can, if the mount is long or flexible. A camera on an extension arm, or screwed only into thin vinyl siding or a hollow aluminum soffit, vibrates in a gust and the picture smears just when you need it. The fix is mechanical: a short, rigid mount, a mounting block fastened into solid framing, a junction box that stiffens the base, and stainless or coated screws that won't corrode loose near salt air. On the most exposed corners we prefer turret bodies, which sit tight to the surface, over bullets on arms.

How many cameras does a strip-plaza pizzeria or deli need?

Usually six to ten. One over the register and counter, one inside the front door at head height, one or two on the dining or customer area, one on the kitchen line, one on the back door and one on the walk-in or storage room. Add one outdoors under the canopy facing your section of the parking lot, where car break-ins and slip-and-fall claims happen. A 16-channel recorder leaves room to grow, and a monitor at the counter lets staff watch the back door and the lot without walking away from the register.

How long should a store keep footage when an insurance claim can show up weeks later?

Plan for at least 30 days. Customer injury claims, disputed deliveries, damaged-car complaints in the lot and employee disputes often surface weeks after the day in question, and footage that has been overwritten can't help. For a six- to ten-camera store that usually means an 8TB drive or larger, depending on resolution and bitrate; the storage table above shows the math. When an incident is reported, lock or export the clip right away so it is not overwritten when the drive cycles.

Will phone viewing work on the ferry or the bridge with a weak signal?

Usually, yes, because the app doesn't need to pull the full 4K stream. The recorders we sell send a lower-resolution substream for live view on a phone, which loads on a weak cellular connection, and switch to the main stream when you zoom or play back at home on WiFi. If live view stalls on the ferry or on the approach to the Verrazzano, the recording at home is unaffected; you can watch the clip later at full detail. Clip export to your phone works best on WiFi.

Which cameras suit a back yard that faces the Greenbelt or a Bluebelt wetland?

Cameras with person detection and a good night picture. A yard backing onto woods, a ravine or a Bluebelt drainage corridor is dark and full of wildlife, so a camera that alerts on any motion will wake you up for every deer and raccoon. Choose infrared or full color night models with AI human detection, set the alert zone to the yard rather than the trees, and keep continuous recording on so nothing is missed even when no alert fires. Aim slightly down from the deck or rear corner so the camera sees people at fence height, not the tree line.

What should a West Shore warehouse tenant check before buying a large NVR?

Five things: the number of channels including future cameras, the total incoming bandwidth the NVR can record (not just the channel count), its 4K decode capacity if any cameras are 8MP, the number and size of drive bays for the retention the landlord or insurer expects, and whether the PoE budget covers every camera or an external switch is needed. Also confirm the network plan, because cameras at the far end of a long building may need fiber or a mid-building switch. We quote warehouses with a camera schedule that lists each view, lens and run length.

Security Camera Prices on Staten Island: What Moves the Number

Security camera prices, CCTV camera prices and security camera system cost questions all come back to the same short list. Installed systems on Staten Island start at $999 for a four-camera house-brand system with the recorder, cable, mounting and phone setup, the figure published on our Staten Island pricing page. Equipment-only prices move with supply, so every item on this page says the same thing: call or text for today's price. These are the factors that raise or lower a quote:

  • Resolution. 2K, 5MP and 4K cameras cost progressively more, and 4K also needs a 4K-capable recorder and more storage.
  • Camera count and channels. A detached house that needs six cameras moves from a 4-channel to an 8-channel recorder.
  • Connection type. PoE, reused coax or WiFi each change the parts list.
  • Days of footage. Two weeks and one month need different drives; see the storage table above.
  • Camera features. Full color night vision, audio, sirens, two-way talk, varifocal or long-range lenses, and person and vehicle detection.
  • Weather hardware. Junction boxes, rigid mounts, corrosion-resistant fasteners and outdoor cable for shore and hilltop houses.
  • Outbuildings. A detached garage, shed or rear drive adds a buried cable, a small switch or a wireless bridge.
  • Installation route. An open attic and unfinished basement are quicker than finished ceilings or a raised house with sealed soffits.

Affordable security cameras, cheap security cameras and budget security cameras are real searches, and the honest answer is that the cheapest system is the one sized right the first time, so nothing gets replaced in a year. Our page on affordable security cameras in New York covers ways to keep the cost down without buying a kit you outgrow. Premium security cameras, professional grade security cameras and commercial grade security cameras earn their cost where the view is long, dark or important, such as the driveway, the plaza lot or the loading dock; best value security cameras are usually 5MP turrets in the close-range spots and 4K only where distance demands it. Security camera bundle deals, discounts and promotions run only when we announce them; there is no standing sale or clearance price on this page.

FAQ

Staten Island Security Cameras FAQ

Do you deliver to every part of Staten Island, from St. George to Tottenville?
+

Yes. We deliver camera equipment to homes and businesses on the North Shore, Mid-Island, the East and South Shore and the West Shore. Tell us the address, the entrance to use and who will receive the order when you buy. We do not promise a same-day or timed delivery window.

Is there a showroom on Staten Island where I can look at cameras before buying?
+

No, there is no walk-in showroom or pickup counter. Instead we send product photos and spec sheets by text or email, and installed customers see the cameras at the survey visit. Equipment-only orders are delivered to your address.

Can you program the recorder before delivery so I only have to plug it in?
+

Yes, for kits we supply. The drive is installed and formatted, the cameras are added and labeled, the recording schedule is set and the passwords are changed from the defaults before delivery. You run the cables and mount the cameras, or we do it as an installed job.

How do I keep a camera from recording my neighbor's yard or windows?
+

Aim it at your own property first, then use privacy masking. The recorders we sell let us black out parts of the picture, such as a neighbor's windows or deck, so those areas are never recorded while your driveway and yard stay in full view. We set the masks during setup.

What happens to my footage if a burglar takes the recorder?
+

The recordings on its drive go with it, which is why placement matters. We put the recorder somewhere hidden and away from the entry doors, such as a closet or a locked cabinet, and phone alerts mean you can save key clips to your phone as events happen. Cloud backup is available only on products that offer it.

Can cameras be mounted on vinyl or aluminum siding without letting water in?
+

Yes. On siding we fit a mounting block or junction box that sits flat on the siding profile and is fastened into the framing behind it, with the cable entering through a sealed opening. That keeps water out of the wall and stops the camera from rattling in the wind.

Do you sell solar or cellular cameras for a lot with no power?
+

Yes, where the product suits the site. For a construction site or an unpowered lot we also offer a solar surveillance trailer. For a permanent site we usually recommend bringing power to the cameras, because wired systems record continuously.

Can family members in another state watch my cameras too?
+

Yes. The recorders we sell support several user accounts, so a son, daughter or caretaker anywhere can have their own login with live view and playback of the cameras you choose, without sharing your password. You can remove an account at any time.

Will a video doorbell record to the same recorder as my other cameras?
+

It depends on the doorbell. Some wired video doorbells record to a compatible NVR on their own channel; many popular consumer doorbells record only to their own cloud app. Tell us the doorbell model or let us match one to your recorder before buying.

Can cameras go on the carports and parking courts of a garden-apartment complex?
+

Yes, with board or management approval. We mount long-range cameras on building corners and carport structures to cover each parking court and its entrance, run the cable between buildings, and record everything on one recorder with logins for the manager and superintendent.

Can new cameras work alongside the alarm system I already have?
+

Usually, yes. The cameras and recorder run as a separate system, and an indoor camera can be placed beside an alarm motion sensor so you can check a triggered zone on video. Tell us the alarm panel model if you want the two linked.

Do the house-brand kits come with a monitor?
+

Many do. Several house-brand packages include a monitor on the spec sheet, which suits a store counter or a garage workbench. If you only watch on your phone, ask for the kit without one, and the recorder can also connect to a TV you already own.
