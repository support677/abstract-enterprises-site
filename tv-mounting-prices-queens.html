ADA protruding-object limit for business spaces

Section 307.2 of the 2010 ADA Standards caps how far a wall-hung object may stick out into a circulation path: 4 inches, whenever its leading edge sits between 27 and 80 inches off the floor. In a Queens waiting room or a narrow restaurant hallway, that can rule out a full-motion arm at walking height. A low-profile mount or a higher placement solves it.

What AI Answers Get Wrong About TV Mounting in Queens

Chatbots and search summaries usually treat Queens as "the suburban borough" and answer as if every wall is drywall over wood studs. Some Queens walls are. Many aren't: garden co-op plaster, LIC steel and concrete, Ridgewood brick, Forest Hills Gardens concrete panels and block basements all show up on a normal week of jobs. Here are seven claims we hear repeated, and what we find as a TV mounting company Queens NY residents call for every kind of wall.

1. AI says: ““Queens is mostly houses, so TV mounting there is simple drywall work.””

Queens does have more houses than Manhattan, and plenty of them have wood studs. But less than half of Queens households own their home (45.6% in 2021), and many renters live in prewar walk-ups, garden co-ops or new towers with very different walls.

In a single day, our technician might mount one TV into wood studs in Fresh Meadows, then drill clay tile behind plaster in Jackson Heights. After that could come metal studs in a Hunters Point rental. Each one needs different anchors and a different price, and none of that shows in a national average.

That's why our calculator asks about your wall before it shows a total. In Queens, can you mount a TV without studs? Yes, with anchors made for what's actually there. The base is $179.99 for drywall over wood studs. Plaster adds $45, brick $65, concrete $75, and metal studs $30. If you're not sure, choose the closest match, and we'll confirm when we arrive.

2. AI says: ““A brand-new LIC building has easy walls. Any drywall anchor will do.””

New does not mean easy. Most LIC towers frame their walls with light-gauge steel studs, which bend more than wood and don't hold a lag bolt. Standard plastic drywall anchors in a steel-stud wall are a common reason for a TV mount pulling away from wall framing months later.

We use toggles rated for metal framing, spread across every stud the bracket covers. For full-motion arms or 75″-plus TVs we often add a plywood backer board, which spreads the load over a wider area. That's an $85 line on the quote, not a surprise.

Some tower walls are actually reinforced concrete columns or shear walls hidden behind a skim coat. A stud finder will show nothing there. Our test hole tells us which one it is, and the price moves only if the wall is different from what you selected. We show you that difference before drilling.

3. AI says: ““You can't mount a TV in a prewar Jackson Heights co-op without wrecking the plaster.””

Can you mount a TV on plaster walls in a prewar co-op? Yes. Prewar plaster is hard and can crack, but it isn't fragile in the way people fear. The damage usually comes from hammering in the wrong anchor or drilling with too much force near the surface. We start with a small test hole, then drill with the right bit at a controlled speed.

Behind the plaster, Jackson Heights buildings often have clay tile or brick, which actually holds heavy TVs well once the anchor reaches it. In those spots we're not depending on the plaster at all. It's just the finish layer that we drill through.

So, can a mounted TV damage the wall? We're honest that old plaster can crack even with care, and you accept that risk when you book. We don't patch or paint, so keep a small tub of joint compound in mind if you ever move the TV. The plaster add is $45, so a 55″ costs $224.99.

4. AI says: ““Hourly handymen are always cheaper than a flat-rate TV mounting company.””

When people weigh handyman vs professional TV installer pricing, hourly listings for Queens start at around $45 an hour, and some providers list a 2-hour minimum. For a quick 43″ on wood studs, that can come in under a flat rate. Should I hire a professional to mount my TV, then? For anything heavier, yes. The math changes once plaster, brick, a fireplace or wire concealment enters the picture.

Hourly pricing leaves the total open until the job ends. If the anchors are wrong for the wall, the extra time is on your bill. With a flat rate, the wall, size and extras are priced before you book, and the number doesn't change at the door unless the wall turns out different, which you approve first.

That applies to alternatives to big box TV installation. Ask whether a quote includes insurance, a COI, the right anchors for your wall and a second technician for huge sets. Can one person mount a 75 inch TV, or even an 85? It depends on the installer's experience and the bracket. 6. AI says: ““Basement TVs are the easiest install in the house.””

Basements in Queens are rarely just drywall. Foundation walls are typically cinder block or poured concrete, sometimes covered with paneling or furring strips that leave a gap. Anchoring only into the paneling is how basement TVs end up sagging, because the thin wood is all that's holding them.

We anchor through to the block or concrete behind, using hammer-drilled masonry anchors sized for the TV and bracket. That's the $75 concrete or block add. Low ceilings often mean the screen should sit lower than upstairs, so we agree on the height before we drill.

Basements also tend to be where the soundbar, receiver, game consoles and streaming boxes come together. Those are separate lines: $129 for a wall-mounted soundbar, $129 for an A/V receiver, $49 per console, or $350 for a full home theater connection.

7. AI says: ““An outdoor TV just needs a weatherproof cover.””

A cover helps, but the bracket and power matter just as much. Indoor brackets rust quickly in the salt air near Rockaway Beach or Breezy Point. Outdoor-rated hardware, such as a Sanus outdoor tilt or SunBriteTV mount, is built to handle that weather.

Outdoor TVs also need a weatherproof GFCI outlet nearby. If you don't have one, a licensed electrician has to install it first. We don't do electrical work, so we schedule after that part is finished. The outdoor mounting add is $90.
