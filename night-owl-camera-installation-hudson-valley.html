The Neighborhood

Why Hudson Valley Properties Need Night Owl Camera Installation

The Hudson Valley is not one place, and a Night Owl kit does not know that. Wikipedia describes the region running from the Capital District around Albany and Troy south to Yonkers on the New York City line, and even the people who live here disagree on where it starts and stops; WPDH points out that most people mix up the whole valley with its "Mid-Hudson" stretch of Dutchess, Orange and Ulster. Our Hudson Valley service covers the six counties closest to the city: Westchester, Rockland, Orange, Putnam, Dutchess and Ulster. Inside those six you can drive from a 1970s office tower on the I-287 corridor in White Plains to a 1700s stone house in Hurley in under two hours, and the camera problems change with every exit. The same box from Best Buy or Home Depot, with the same four cameras and the same 60-foot cables, ends up on all of them.

That is the first reason Hudson Valley owners call an installer for a Night Owl system: the kit was designed around an average American house, and very few properties here are average. Night Owl's own support pages say to mount outdoor cameras at least seven feet up, angled slightly down, and to put them under "some type of minimal protection such as an eave or awning," because the cameras are weather-resistant, not waterproof. That is easy on a suburban colonial with deep eaves. It is harder on a Dutch stone house with a shallow roof edge, on a pole barn with no overhang at all, or on a flat-roofed commercial building on Route 59. Night Owl also says the maximum run from a DVR is 160 feet, and that past that distance you need to power the camera where it is mounted. On a two-acre lot with a detached garage, a horse barn and a gate at the end of a long gravel driveway, 160 feet runs out quickly. Those two numbers, seven feet up and 160 feet out, shape more Hudson Valley Night Owl jobs than anything else.

The second reason is the installation itself. SafeWise's review of Night Owl listed "long installation with wires galore" as a drawback, and an eBay reviewer wrote that the box promised a four-hour install that took closer to ten. An Adorama reviewer called the system easy to set up "other than the effort required to route the wires through the attic and or walls." In the valley that effort is real. The region's older houses are often stone, brick or heavy timber, and HudsonValleyOne notes that Ulster County has more Dutch-influenced stone houses than anywhere in the world except the Netherlands. Hurley's ten stone houses, according to New York State Parks, were all built between 1715 and 1790, and the houses on Historic Huguenot Street in New Paltz date to around 1705. Getting a coax cable from an attic to a camera on the outside of a two-foot stone wall is not a weekend job for most owners, and a hole drilled in the wrong place in a house that old cannot be undone.

The third reason is what the cameras are for. Hudson Valley police have spent the last two years asking residents to check their footage. In January 2025, State Police warned residents of Dutchess, Westchester and Putnam counties about targeted residential burglaries, and Patch reported their advice: turn on security systems and cameras and add motion-sensor lighting. Scarsdale police described organized burglars placing trail cameras or surveillance devices outside homes for days before committing burglaries, and a Scarsdale resident found one in the bushes on Bradford Road. In Dutchess, State Police in Poughkeepsie asked residents of Hoofprint Road in Union Vale, Fountain Place and Weatherford Lane in Millbrook, and Horseshoe, Verbank and Maple Hill Roads and Rodrigo Court in the town of Washington to check home cameras between midnight and 5 a.m. after car break-ins. News 12 Westchester reported in July 2026 that Port Chester neighbors' cameras caught someone trying car doors. Mid-Hudson News reported a run of package thefts at a residential complex in the Village of Haverstraw in April 2026. WPDH reported in June 2026 that store cameras caught thefts at businesses including Alimentari Rossi & Sons at Eastdale Village in Poughkeepsie. A camera that is pointed at the sky, washed out by its own night-vision glare, or offline since the last thunderstorm does not help anyone when the call comes.

The fourth reason is the weather and the power, which in the valley are linked. Earth Networks' 2020 lightning report ranked Orange County first among New York counties for lightning pulses, with 275,059 that year and 41 thunder days, followed by Ulster, Westchester and Dutchess. The Times Union reported that storms on June 27, 2024 knocked out power to a combined 60,000 Orange and Rockland and Central Hudson customers, with winds around 60 mph. In the July 4, 2026 storms, Central Hudson reported 669 active outages affecting 41,323 customers across the region by noon the following Sunday. A Night Owl DVR that sits on a bare wall outlet in a basement, with long copper cables running out to a barn and a gate, is exposed to every one of those storms. Where the recorder goes, what it is plugged into, and how the outdoor runs are sealed and protected decide whether the system is still recording when the lights come back.

The fifth reason is the internet, or the lack of it. Night Owl's wired DVRs are only compatible with a wired internet connection, so the recorder has to sit beside the router or have an Ethernet cable run to it. In much of the valley the router is in the one room where the provider's line comes in, and that room is rarely where you want the recorder. Some homes still have no broadband at all: Ulster County announced in September 2025 a state ConnectALL award to bring service to nearly 1,300 unserved households, businesses and community facilities in remote areas, and HudsonValleyOne reported that the Town of Shandaken alone has 2,866 residents across 120 mountainous square miles where even cell service struggles. The good news is that Night Owl's support pages say the DVR does not need internet for basic recording. The work is in setting the system up so it records well locally first and adds remote viewing only where the connection can carry it.

We want to be clear about what our research did not find. We searched for Reddit threads, local news and community posts about Night Owl installations in Hudson Valley towns and found none specific to the brand. We found no public data on how many Night Owl systems are installed in the region, or how they fail. So where this page describes what happens to Night Owl kits on stone walls, barns and long runs, it draws on Night Owl's own published specifications, on the building types and weather the public record describes here, and on how our technicians install camera systems generally. It does not claim any Hudson Valley business or homeowner as our customer.

Hudson Valley facts that shape a Night Owl install

  • Who lives where: Mid-Hudson Community Profiles counted 1,180,768 residents in the Mid-Hudson region in 2024, with nearly 35 percent in Orange County and 25 percent in Dutchess.
  • Old masonry: Ulster has more Dutch-influenced stone houses than anywhere outside the Netherlands, per HudsonValleyOne; Hurley's stone houses date from 1715 to 1790 and Huguenot Street's from around 1705.
  • Big lots and farms: the USDA's 2022 Census of Agriculture counted 620 farms in Dutchess averaging 161 acres and 486 in Ulster averaging 150 acres, the kind of property where cable length matters.

  • Licensing: 19 NYCRR 195.2 requires a state license for a CCTV or video surveillance system used to detect or monitor intrusion, break-in, theft or movement.

What owners often add once the cameras are up. Once a Hudson Valley property has a working Night Owl system, the next question is usually the gate or the front door: a camera at the end of the driveway, a video doorbell, or motion lighting, which the State Police specifically recommended in their 2025 burglary warning. Tell us when you book if any of that is on your list, so the cable plan leaves room for it rather than sending someone back to drill through the same stone wall twice.

Westchester: suburbs, river villages and the office corridor

Westchester is the most built-up of the six counties and the one where a Night Owl kit most often lands on a house it was designed for: a wood-frame colonial, a split-level or a Cape with an attic, a garage and eaves deep enough to shelter a camera. Those jobs are usually about tidy routing and good aim rather than distance. The county's particular hazard is water. ABC7 reported on September 2, 2021 that Hurricane Ida left some Mamaroneck neighborhoods with as much as 14 feet of water, and that many residents saw basements fill and water rise to the first floor. A Night Owl DVR is a small computer with a hard drive in it; it does not survive being underwater. In river towns and flood-prone streets, we put the recorder on the first floor or higher, near the router where possible, and run the camera cables down to it rather than putting it in the basement where the cables happen to come in. Westchester also has a strong habit of neighbors comparing footage. The Port Chester residents News 12 interviewed in July 2026 had cameras that caught someone checking car doors; Croton-on-Hudson police run a voluntary camera registration program and only contact registrants if a crime happens near their camera. Both are reasons to aim at least one camera properly at the street-facing driveway.

Rockland: shopping corridors, complexes and suburban lots

Rockland's commercial life clusters around Route 59 and the Palisades Center in West Nyack, which Hoodline reported at about 2,359,541 square feet when a Black Diamond affiliate won it at a February 2026 foreclosure auction. Small businesses in the strip centers around it are a natural fit for Night Owl systems: a handful of cameras on a recorder in the back office, covering the register, the door and the rear delivery entrance. Rockland also has many apartment and townhouse complexes. The package thefts Mid-Hudson News reported at a Village of Haverstraw complex in April 2026 are the kind of problem a mail-room or entry camera addresses, but in a complex the entry, the mail area and the parking lot are usually common property, so the management company or board has to approve the camera, its position and who can see the footage. Rockland had the fewest lightning pulses of the six counties in the 2020 Earth Networks report, at 64,575 over 28 thunder days, but fewer is not none; recorders still go on surge protection.

Orange: lightning, warehouses and Newburgh brick

Orange County is where we take surge protection most seriously. In the 2020 Earth Networks report it ranked first in New York for lightning pulses, with 275,059. It is also where the region's warehouse boom is most visible: HGAR reported in May 2020 that an Amazon distribution center of 1,015,740 square feet at 635 International Drive, at Route 17K and Route 747, and a 1.2 million-square-foot Medline facility were both under construction in the Town of Montgomery. A small Night Owl system is not the right answer for a building that size, and we say so, but the many smaller businesses that grow up around those corridors, the contractors' yards, auto shops, self-storage lots and suppliers, often are. Their cameras sit on poles, fences and the corners of metal buildings, with runs that cross gravel lots and end near the 160-foot limit. At the other end of the county, Newburgh's East End Historic District, which Wikipedia describes as 445 acres and 2,217 buildings, mostly two- and three-story Italianate brick townhouses, brings masonry, narrow lots and attached neighbors. A camera on a Newburgh townhouse has to see the stoop and the street without staring into the neighbor's window.

Putnam: woods, reservoirs and long driveways

Putnam is quieter and more wooded, and its properties tend to have long private driveways that wind through trees. That has two effects on a Night Owl install. Branches swaying in wind and the bugs that collect around infrared lights are the classic cause of the "constant alerts for garbage bags rigs bugs tree branches" one Night Owl owner complained about on Trustpilot, so motion zones and camera aim need more care. And a camera at the head of a long driveway is almost always past the 160-foot coax limit from the house, which means powering it at the gate or choosing a different way to get the picture back. Burying a cable along a driveway in Putnam brings in the 811 call that Central Hudson describes as required by New York State law, at least two and no more than ten days before digging, and on reservoir land the New York City DEP watershed regulations, which the city updated in December 2019 for communities in Ulster, Orange, Putnam and Westchester. Putnam's sheriff and police also run their own cameras: Hudson Valley Post reported in August 2026 that the county's license-plate readers store data on a government-owned server controlled by local law enforcement, a reminder that private cameras on a road are part of a wider picture.

Dutchess: farms, estates and the State Police appeals

Dutchess is the county where distance dominates. The USDA counted 620 farms in 2022, averaging 161 acres, and the river towns hold estates from what guidebooks call the Great Estates Region; the Vanderbilt Mansion National Historic Site in Hyde Park alone keeps 211 of its original roughly 600 acres, with a carriage house and its own old hydroelectric plant. A farm or estate wants cameras at the barn doors, the equipment shed, the gate, the farm stand and the house, and the distances between those buildings often exceed what a single DVR cable can carry. The State Police appeal after car break-ins in Union Vale, Millbrook and the town of Washington, asking residents to check footage between midnight and 5 a.m., describes exactly the footage these properties need: clear night video of a driveway and a parked car, from a camera that is not blinded by its own infrared reflecting off a nearby wall. Dutchess also has its share of busy commercial ground, from IBM's campus at 2455 South Road, where the Dutchess IDA describes a new quantum facility with construction starting in September 2026, to the shopping centers like Eastdale Village where WPDH reported store cameras catching thefts.

Ulster: stone walls, historic districts and no-internet properties

Ulster combines the hardest walls with the thinnest internet. Its stone houses, from Hurley to New Paltz to Kingston's Stockade, have walls that a standard drill bit will not cross, and Kingston's historic districts add review: the city's Historic Landmarks Preservation Commission reviews exterior work on historic properties visible from the public right-of-way before a building permit is issued. Ulster's rural towns are also where the Night Owl promise of local recording matters most. Ulster County's September 2025 announcement described nearly 1,300 unserved locations in remote areas waiting for broadband, and Spectrum News reported in February 2026 that the former IBM Kingston campus along Enterprise Drive, 200 acres with more than a million square feet of buildings, now hosts the Ulster BOCES Career and Technical Center and Archtop Fiber on its East Campus. Between those two facts sits the whole range of Ulster jobs: a cabin with no internet where the recorder simply records, and a business on a fiber line that wants to watch its cameras from anywhere.

The Box, the Router and the Barn: What to Check Before a Night Owl Install Visit

Most of the time lost on a Night Owl job is lost before the first camera goes up: a kit that turns out to be coax when the owner wanted PoE, a recorder with nowhere to plug in near the router, a camera bought for a barn that is 250 feet away, or a Wi-Fi camera that was never going to reach the garage. A few photos and measurements prevent nearly all of it. Please do not cut into walls, run cable across lawns or open the cameras yourself; just look, measure and send us what you find.

Decide what each camera has to see

The most useful thing you can do before we arrive is to stand where each camera will go and decide what it has to show. "The driveway" is not enough; it helps to know whether you want to read a license plate at the turn into the drive, see a face at the front door, or simply know when a car has come up the lane. Each of those needs a different camera position. A face at a door needs a camera fairly close and not too high; a plate needs a narrow view at the right angle to the car; a general driveway view needs height and width. Night Owl's minimum of seven feet up protects the camera from being knocked or grabbed, but in a valley yard with sloping ground, seven feet from where the ground is lowest can be much higher than seven feet from where people walk. Walk the yard with your phone camera held where the security camera will hang, and send us the photos.

Look at the night, not just the day

A camera that looks perfect at noon can be useless at midnight. Night Owl cameras light the scene with infrared, and anything close to the lens, a gutter, a white wall, a branch, a spider web, reflects that light straight back and fogs the picture. Go out after dark and look at each camera spot: is there a porch light shining at it, a tree branch that will hang in front of it in summer, a white post right beside it? In rural Dutchess and Ulster, where there is no streetlight at all, the camera's own infrared does all the work, so a clear view matters even more. Tell us what you see and we will plan positions that still work at night.

Think about the power and the internet

Night Owl's support pages list the DVR power supply for wired BNC kits at 12 V, 2000 mA, and the recorder needs a spot near an outlet and, for remote viewing, near the router or an Ethernet run to it. If your router is in a cupboard by the front door and the only sensible place for the recorder is upstairs, tell us; an Ethernet cable between them is part of the plan. If your property has no internet, the system can still record, and we will set it up for local viewing on a monitor. If you are on satellite or a slow rural line, tell us that too, so we set expectations for remote viewing honestly.

Night Owl Camera Installation for the Hudson Valley's Architecture

The valley's buildings span three centuries, and each type changes three things about a Night Owl install: how the cable gets from the recorder to the camera, what the camera is fixed to, and who has to agree before we drill. These are the building types the public record describes in our six counties and how we handle each.

Dutch and Huguenot Stone Houses

Ulster's stone houses, like Hurley's 1715–1790 group and the ca. 1705 houses on Huguenot Street. Thick walls, shallow eaves, historic value. Cable usually goes through the roof framing or an existing opening rather than a new hole through the stone.

Farmhouses with Barns

The working farms of Dutchess and Ulster, averaging about 150 to 160 acres per the USDA. Long distances, buildings on separate power, dust, animals and mice. Distance planning comes first.

River Estates and Gatehouses

The Great Estates properties along the river, with main houses, carriage houses and gate lodges. Cameras at the gate and drive, cable through old service routes, and discretion on the facade.

Suburban Wood-Frame Houses

What a business Night Owl system has to do

A home system is watched when something happens. Rules and practical limits for business cameras

  • A state license for the installer. 19 NYCRR 195.2 requires a state license for a CCTV or video surveillance system used, full-time or part-time, to detect or monitor intrusion, break-in, theft, movement, sound or fire. Ours is NYS license #12000287431. Training and licensing courses in the state warn that unlicensed installers face substantial fines.
  • Cameras at work should be visible. Guidance on New York camera law from Security101 says workplace cameras must be overt and surveillance notices should be apparent and visible. We place business cameras where they can be seen and recommend signs at the entrances.
  • Keep the microphone off unless someone consenting is in the conversation. New York follows one-party consent for audio. An attorney answering on Justia advised disabling audio on outdoor and garage cameras unless a consenting participant will be part of any recorded conversation. Some Night Owl cameras have microphones; we set them deliberately, not by default.
  • No cameras in private spaces. Penal Law 250.45 makes it a felony to record a person in a bathroom, changing room or fitting room without consent. Business cameras cover doors, registers, stock and yards, never restrooms or changing areas.
  • Historic storefronts. In Kingston's historic districts, exterior work visible from the public right-of-way is reviewed by the HLPC before a building permit issues. We found nothing saying cameras are specifically reviewed, so we ask the owner to check with the city and we keep exterior mounts small and finished to the building.
  • Digging needs a call first. A buried camera run across a yard or lot needs a call to 811 at least two and no more than ten working days before digging, per Central Hudson, so utilities can mark their lines. Private lines on the property are not marked by the utilities and need their own care.
  • Power work is an electrician's. Our work is low-voltage: cameras, recorders and their cables. Where a business wants a new outlet on a pole, a fence or the outside of a building to power a far camera, that circuit is for a licensed electrician, and the quote says so.

Weather and mounting

Can Night Owl cameras live outside through a Hudson Valley winter and the spring rain?

Yes, if they are rated for it and mounted properly. One Night Owl wired model is rated to operate from -4°F to 140°F, and ratings vary by model, so we check yours against the coldest exposed spot you want covered. The bigger issue is water. Night Owl describes its cameras as weather-resistant, not waterproof, and recommends mounting outdoor cameras under some type of protection such as an eave or awning. We mount under eaves wherever the view allows, seal every connection and wall penetration, and leave a drip loop in the cable so water runs off the bottom of the loop instead of following the cable into the camera or the house. Ice dams and snow sliding off roofs are another valley hazard, so we keep cameras out of the path of falling snow from roof edges.

Cable, distance and old wiring

Our barn is about 250 feet from the house. Can a Night Owl camera out there still work?

It can, but not on the kit's normal setup. Night Owl has stated that the maximum run from the DVR is 160 feet, and that if you extend further you need to power the camera where it is mounted. At 250 feet, that means the barn camera gets its power from an outlet at the barn, and only its video travels back to the house. If the barn has no power, there are other routes, such as a small recorder or network equipment at the barn, and each has trade-offs in cost and complexity. We pace the distance, check for power at the barn, and put the options in the quote with the route the cable will take and whether it needs to be buried, which brings in an 811 call before digging.

We have coax from an old camera system in the walls. Can a new Night Owl kit use it?

It depends on which Night Owl kit. Night Owl's HD-analog DVR systems can be integrated with existing coaxial cabling, and Night Owl has said owners of older DVR kits can keep their BNC cabling and simply swap the cameras and the DVR. It recommends a minimum of 24 AWG cable. A PoE NVR kit is different: Night Owl says those use Ethernet, Cat5e or Cat6, to carry both power and video, and that older BNC coax will not work with a PoE NVR. So before you buy, decide whether you are keeping the coax. Before we reuse it, we trace each old cable to see where it really runs, test it, and replace any length that is damaged, spliced in an attic or chewed in a barn.

We already own a Night Owl system and want to add cameras. Will any Night Owl camera work with our recorder?

Not always. Before you buy extra cameras, send us the model number from the sticker on your recorder and the model of the camera you are considering. Recording, internet and the app

Yes. Night Owl's support pages say the DVR does not need internet for basic operation; it records to its own hard drive. Is a Night Owl system Wi-Fi or wired, and where does the recorder have to sit?

The cameras on Night Owl's wired DVR and NVR systems connect to the recorder by cable, and the recorder connects to your router by an Ethernet cable; Night Owl's answers on Best Buy describe the DVR as compatible only with a wired internet connection, and SafeWise noted the DVR it reviewed had no built-in Wi-Fi. I upgraded my phone and now I can't see my Night Owl cameras. Can that be fixed without replacing the system?

Usually it can. A Night Owl owner wrote on Trustpilot that after a phone upgrade they could no longer view their cameras because the app they had been using was no longer available. Apps change; the recorder and cameras usually still work. We check the recorder's model and firmware, find which current Night Owl app supports it, update what can be updated, and re-add the system to each phone. Where an old recorder is no longer supported by any current app, we tell you, and the choice is local viewing only, or replacing just the recorder and keeping the cameras and cable if they are compatible.

Our Night Owl alerts go off all night for branches, bugs and blowing trash. How is that fixed?

One Night Owl owner on Trustpilot complained about "constant alerts for garbage bags rigs bugs tree branches," and on wooded valley lots the problem is common. Then we set motion zones to cover only the driveway, path or door you care about, adjust sensitivity, and where your model supports it, switch on person or vehicle detection. Not necessarily. Infrared draws extra power, and a weak supply or a long run can leave enough power for the daytime picture but not for the night lights. The Great Estates landmark, 211 of an original roughly 600 acres, mansion built 1896–1899, with its carriage house and hydroelectric plant, per Wikipedia.

How to read the price page for your property

Start with the number of cameras and the county. Then go through each camera and ask the questions the table asks: what is the wall made of, how does the cable get there, and is it past 160 feet from the recorder? A Westchester colonial with four cameras on soffits fed from an open attic, and a recorder beside the router in a home office, uses only the basic lines. Move the same four cameras to an Ulster stone farmhouse with one of them on the barn, and the quote gains a masonry line for the camera on the stone, an outbuilding line for the barn camera and its local power, and probably an Ethernet line to bring the router connection to a dry room. Nothing else is added on the day unless you ask for it, and if we find something your photos did not show, we stop and tell you before doing it.

FAQ

Hudson Valley Night Owl Camera Installation FAQ

When we're away from the house, does our phone have to be on the home network to see the Night Owl cameras?
+

No, once the recorder is connected to your router and remote viewing is set up, the Night Owl app shows the cameras over the internet from anywhere your phone has a connection. Night Owl's guidance is that the recorder must be wired to the router for remote viewing and mobile alerts. How smooth the picture is away from home depends on your property's upload speed, which on some rural valley lines is limited, so we test it from a phone on cellular data before we leave.

Our cabin has no internet at all. How do we get a clip off the Night Owl recorder if something happens?
+

The recorder keeps recording to its own hard drive without internet, so the footage is there. You view and find it on a monitor connected to the recorder, and you copy a clip using the backup or export option in the recorder's menu, which differs by model. At handover we connect a monitor if you want one, show you exactly how to find and copy a clip on your model, and write the steps down so you are not working it out on the night you need it.

Does an electrician have to be involved in a Night Owl camera install?
+

Usually not. The cameras, recorder and cables are low-voltage work, which we do under our state license. Night Owl's wired kits run on 12-volt adapters that plug into ordinary outlets. An electrician is needed only when a new outlet has to be added, for example on a barn, a gatepost or a pole where a camera past the cable limit needs local power. When that applies, the quote says so and the electrician's work is separate.

Some Night Owl cameras have microphones. Is it legal to record sound outside our house in New York?
+

New York follows one-party consent for audio, which means recording a conversation you are not part of, with nobody in it consenting, can count as eavesdropping. An attorney answering on Justia advised disabling audio on outdoor and garage cameras unless a consenting participant will be part of any recorded conversation. We set audio deliberately at installation and recommend leaving it off on outdoor cameras that could pick up delivery drivers, neighbors or passers-by.

Do you travel to remote parts of the valley, such as the far corners of Ulster or northern Dutchess?
+

Yes, we cover all of Westchester, Rockland, Orange, Putnam, Dutchess and Ulster. Remote properties take more planning, so we ask for good photos and distances in advance and bring enough cable, fixings and parts to finish in one visit. Each county is priced on its own Night Owl price page, and the quote tells you before booking what the visit includes.

We own a townhouse in a complex with an HOA. Can we put Night Owl cameras outside our unit?
+

Inside your unit, generally yes. Outside, it depends on the association's rules, because the walls, entrances and parking areas of a complex are often common property. Ask the board or managing agent what they allow before booking, and send us their answer. We then place cameras so they cover your entrance and parking space without recording into neighbors' windows or units, and we keep mounts and cables as discreet as the rules require.

What should we ask any installer before letting them put up our Night Owl system?
+

Ask for the New York State license number, because the state requires a license for paid installation of camera systems used to watch for intrusion or theft. Ask whether they have installed on your kind of building, such as stone, brick or a barn. Ask how they will handle any run past 160 feet, where the recorder will go, and whether the quote lists each line. And ask what you get at the end: a cable map, the app on your phones, and a walk-through of finding footage.

Why does the same Night Owl install cost different amounts in Westchester and in Ulster?
+

Each county has its own rate, reflecting travel and access across a large region, and each county has its own Night Owl price page. On top of the county rate, the properties themselves differ: Ulster has more stone houses, longer distances and more remote sites, while much of Westchester is suburban frame housing with easy attics. The price page for your county lists every line.

Should we register our new Night Owl cameras with our local police department?
+

If your town offers it, it can be worth doing. Croton-on-Hudson police, for example, ask residents and businesses to register private camera systems voluntarily, and say registrants are only contacted if a crime happens near their camera. Registration does not give police access to your cameras. Check whether your village or town police run a similar program; we can tell you where each camera points and what it covers so the form is accurate.

We found a small camera in our bushes that isn't ours. What should we do?
+

Call your local police first, without touching or moving it. Scarsdale police warned that organized burglars place trail cameras or surveillance devices outside homes for days before committing burglaries, and a Scarsdale resident found one in the bushes on Bradford Road. Once police have dealt with it, a properly placed camera of your own covering that part of the yard helps, along with the motion lighting State Police recommend.

Do we have to pay Night Owl a monthly fee once the system is installed?
+

No. Night Owl says its systems do not require a mandatory monthly subscription or a long-term contract; the recorder stores footage locally on its own drive. Night Owl offers optional cloud backup for those who want an off-site copy. We set the system up to work fully without a subscription and explain the optional cloud plan if you want to consider it.

Will our Night Owl system keep recording when Central Hudson loses power in a storm?
+

Not on its own; the recorder and cameras need power. Storm outages are common here, such as the July 4, 2026 storms when Central Hudson reported outages affecting 41,323 customers. A battery backup unit on the recorder, the router and the camera power supplies keeps a typical system running through short outages and lets it shut down cleanly on longer ones. We size the backup to your system and add surge protection, which matters in a region with the lightning counts Orange and Ulster see.
