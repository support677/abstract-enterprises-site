The Neighborhood

Why Nassau County Homeowners and Shops Buy Cameras From a Local Installer

Nassau County is, above all, a county of owned houses. The U.S. Census Bureau's QuickFacts put the county at 1,398,939 residents and 480,591 housing units (July 2025 estimates), with an owner-occupied rate of 81.9 percent and a median owner-occupied home value of $684,700 in the 2020–2024 survey period. Average household size is just under three people, and 94.8 percent of households have a broadband subscription. Read as a buyer, those numbers say a lot about what kind of camera system makes sense here: most people are buying for a house they own and plan to stay in, there is almost always a working internet connection for phone viewing, and the thing being protected is often the single biggest asset the family has.

A county built in a hurry, house by house

Much of central Nassau was built in a burst after World War II, and the most famous example sets the pattern. Levitt & Sons began building on former potato fields in 1947; by Christmas 1948 there were about 6,000 Cape Cod houses, and over the next three years the firm added 11,447 ranch-style houses in five architectural styles, finishing all 17,447 homes in 1951, according to a Levittown history published by Patch. History.com describes the original house as a two-bedroom, one-bath home of about 750 square feet on a concrete slab, with Levitt's crews averaging 10 to 12 houses a day.

Those details are not trivia for a camera buyer; they decide the cable route. A slab house has no basement to run wire through, so the recorder usually lives in a closet or the attic, and cable goes up through the attic and out through the soffits. Seventy-plus years of additions — dormers raised, second stories built, garages turned into rooms, extensions pushed into the backyard — mean no two of those houses are alike inside any more, even on a block where the rooflines still match. The same is true of the split-levels, hi-ranches and colonials that filled in the rest of the county through the 1950s and 1960s. We ask for photos of the attic hatch and the electrical panel before we recommend a recorder location, because that is where the real decisions get made.

Two cities, three towns, sixty-four villages

Nassau's local government is unusually layered. In the county's own words, "Within Nassau County there are 2 cities, 3 towns, 64 incorporated villages and over 100 unincorporated areas" (Nassau County, Cities, Towns & Villages). The cities are Glen Cove and Long Beach; the towns are Hempstead, North Hempstead and Oyster Bay. A buyer in Garden City, Mineola or Rockville Centre lives under a village government with its own board and code; a buyer in Levittown, Hicksville or East Meadow lives in an unincorporated hamlet governed by the town.

For policing, that patchwork matters when you need to hand over footage. The Nassau County Police Department covers most of the county through eight precincts, while a number of villages and cities keep their own departments. Knowing which agency answers your 911 call tells you whom to email the clip to, and whether you will be talking to a precinct squad or a village detective.

Some village departments are small and report striking numbers. The Long Island Press, using state Division of Criminal Justice Services data, reported that the Old Westbury Police Department recorded its lowest index crime in at least 36 years in 2025, down 14 percent from 2024, with no burglaries or attempted burglaries reported all year; the chief credited technology, criminal intelligence and community outreach. Villages are also building their own camera networks. News 12 reported in April 2026 that the Village of Lawrence approved more than 100 outdoor cameras, including license plate readers, linked to the NCPD command center, at a cost of $250,000 largely covered by county grants. Public cameras on light poles do not replace a camera on your own front door, but they explain why police so often ask residents, "Do you have video?"

What the crime numbers actually say

Nassau is regularly ranked among the safest large counties in the country, and that is true. It is also true that the trend lines move around. PolitiFact, checking a March 2026 claim by the county executive and using state DCJS rate data, found that Nassau's index crime rate fell 13 percent from 2022 to 2024, but that the property crime rate in 2024 was still 29 percent higher than in 2021. In his March 9, 2026 State of the County address in Mineola, County Executive Bruce Blakeman said crime countywide was down 10 percent from the same time the year before, according to News 12 Long Island.

For a camera buyer, the takeaway is not fear. It is that the crimes most Nassau households actually meet are property crimes — a package, a car rummaged in the driveway overnight, a bike from an open garage, a break-in at an empty house — and that those are exactly the incidents where a sharp, correctly time-stamped clip decides whether anything happens next.

Packages on the front step

Package theft in Nassau has a long paper trail, and the cases that end in arrests tend to share one feature: a homeowner's camera. In February 2018 the Herald reported that police arrested a man accused of taking packages from seven homes, five in Rockville Centre, after a South Hempstead homeowner's two outside cameras recorded a man taking a box from his porch; surveillance footage allegedly showed packages going into a maroon Chevrolet Monte Carlo, and Rockville Centre police used license plate readers to find him. The village's police commissioner told the paper, "If it was up to me, I'd have cameras all over the village." In May 2019, Patch reported an arrest in Roosevelt after a Ring doorbell recorded one theft on Grant Street and home video surveillance recorded another on Beechwood Avenue.

Not every case turns on video. In January 2022 Nassau police arrested two people for package thefts on three Mineola streets after a resident described their SUV (Patch), and in October 2023 police said a delivery driver used a food order as cover to take packages in Manhasset and Sands Point (Patch). That second case is the useful lesson. A person carrying a bag up your walk at dinnertime looks exactly like a delivery, so a camera has to show the face, the hands and the car at the curb clearly enough that the difference is visible afterward.

Empty houses on the North Shore

The higher-value end of the county has a different pattern. LongIsland.com reported in February 2025 that three men were charged in residential burglaries in Muttontown and Old Westbury on November 29, December 13 and December 21, 2024, in the evening hours, at houses that were unoccupied; police said the burglars broke rear windows and sliding glass doors and took jewelry, safes, heirlooms and cash. A separate NCPD Major Case Squad investigation into burglaries in Jericho and Manhasset produced four arrests in November 2025 and two more in July 2026 (LongIsland.com).

Read those details as an equipment buyer would. The burglars came to the back of the house, not the front door; they came after dark, around the holidays, when the family was out; and they went in through glass. A camera plan that covers only the front walk and the driveway misses every one of those points. On larger North Shore properties we sell for the rear: cameras on the back corners of the house aimed along the patio and the sliders, a camera on the path from the woods or the side yard, and alerts tied to person detection so the owner hears about a human on the back terrace at 6:30 p.m. rather than every deer in the yard.

Outdoor security cameras for Nassau houses

On a typical Nassau house, outdoor surveillance cameras go in four places: under the front soffit or porch roof looking at the walk and the front door, on the garage or the corner of the house looking down the driveway, on the rear corners looking across the yard, patio and back doors, and on the side of the house covering the gate and the path to the backyard. On a ranch or a Cape the eaves are low, often eight or nine feet, which is good for faces and bad for reach; we choose small turrets for those because they are hard to knock out of aim. On a colonial or a raised ranch the eaves are higher, which favors bullets or turrets with a slightly narrower lens so a person at the door is still large in the frame.

Weather matters more than people expect. Our house-brand outdoor cameras carry IP66 or IP67 ratings, depending on the model, and night vision rated between 60 and 120 feet. Both ratings are dust-tight and handle rain and snow; IP67 adds a rating for temporary immersion. Along the South Shore, from Long Beach and Island Park to Freeport, Merrick and Massapequa, the problem is less the camera and more the salt air and wind on the mount and the cable joint, so outdoor cameras there go out with a sealed junction box and stainless or coated hardware. Weatherproof security cameras for winters are the same cameras on the right mount; what fails in February is usually a connector left exposed, not the camera body.

For a dark backyard or side yard, full color night vision cameras with a built-in spotlight keep color after dark, and the light itself is a deterrent. Call or text for today's price on any outdoor camera.

Indoor security cameras

Indoor surveillance cameras in Nassau houses are fewer than outdoor ones, and they are usually aimed at the doors, not the living space: a camera in the front hall covering the door and the stairs, one in the basement on the walk-out door or the bilco, one in the garage on the interior door to the house. In shops and offices, indoor cameras cover the entrance, the register, the stockroom and the back door, and in medical and dental offices the reception desk and waiting room.

Ceiling-mounted turrets and domes are the default indoors because they are small, out of reach and easy to aim at a door from a corner. How each camera connects: PoE, coax or WiFi

PoE security cameras run on one Cat6 cable each, which carries the video and the power, back to a PoE NVR or a PoE switch. For a new system in a Nassau house this is our default: one cable per camera through the attic, no outlet needed outside, and nothing for a burglar to unplug at the camera. HD coax cameras (sold as HD-TVI, HD-CVI or AHD) run on RG59 or Siamese coax to a DVR, and they are the budget-friendly upgrade in the many Nassau houses and shops that still have coax from a system put in fifteen or twenty years ago. Wireless security cameras talk to your router over WiFi, but they still need power, either from a plug or a battery.

Nassau's frame houses with vinyl siding are kinder to WiFi than city masonry, and broadband is nearly universal here, so we do sell WiFi security cameras more often in Nassau than in the boroughs — usually for a detached garage, a pool house or a shed at the back of the lot where trenching a cable is not worth it. For the main house, wired still wins on reliability. Buy PoE cameras or wired security cameras from us and we size the PoE budget of the recorder or switch for the night-time draw of infrared and spotlights, which is when cameras pull the most power.

Person and vehicle detection, and what "AI" really means

AI security cameras is a marketing umbrella. What a Nassau homeowner actually wants from it is phone alerts that fire for a person on the back patio or a car stopping in the driveway at 2 a.m., and stay quiet for deer, raccoons, swaying branches and headlights sweeping past on the street. Several of our house-brand packages list smart AI human and vehicle detection on their spec sheets, and that is the feature we recommend first. Security cameras with person detection and vehicle detection, smart motion detection cameras and smart search security camera systems turn hours of footage into a short list of clips you can search by "person" or "vehicle."

License plate recognition cameras (also called LPR cameras, ANPR cameras or license plate capture cameras), facial recognition security cameras and people counting cameras are separate products with their own lenses and settings, and we only call a product NDAA compliant, ONVIF compatible, H.265, IK10 or edge-AI capable when the manufacturer's spec sheet says so. We will show you that sheet before you buy, not after.

A network video recorder stores footage from IP cameras. The NVRs we sell are mostly PoE NVRs, with a small switch built into the back so each camera plugs in on its own Cat6 cable and draws power from the recorder. Digital video recorders (DVR)

A digital video recorder stores footage from coax (HD analog) cameras. Today's HD DVRs handle 5MP and 4K analog cameras, so a DVR is the least disruptive upgrade for a Nassau house or shop that already has coax running to the old camera positions: swap the cameras and the box, keep the wire. We sell 4-, 8- and 16-channel DVRs, matched on the 4K kits to the 8MP cameras. Before recommending a DVR to reuse old coax, we test the runs; coax that has been sitting in a hot attic for twenty years, stapled flat, or spliced behind a soffit by a previous owner will not carry a clean 4K signal however good the recorder is.

Surveillance hard drives

CCTV hard drives and surveillance hard drives are built to write video all day, every day, which an ordinary desktop drive is not designed to do. We sell them sized to the retention you want: a 4TB surveillance hard drive for a four- to six-camera house, an 8TB surveillance hard drive for an eight-camera house or shop, and 16TB surveillance storage, usually two drives, for larger recorders. Security cameras with two weeks recording and security cameras with one month recording are a drive-size decision, not a camera decision. The worked example at the end of this section uses real numbers from a recorder screen to show how we estimate it.

PoE switches, injectors and power

A PoE switch for security cameras adds ports and power when the recorder runs out of either, or when a group of cameras is far from the recorder, as on an estate where the pool house and the gatehouse are each a long run from the main house. We size the switch by its total power budget, not just its port count, because cameras with infrared, spotlights or sirens draw the most at night. A security camera PoE injector powers one camera where a switch would be overkill. For coax systems, a fused CCTV power supply feeds several cameras from one box, and a single CCTV power adapter serves one. A small battery backup on the recorder, switch and modem keeps recording through the short outages that come with summer thunderstorms and nor'easters.

Storage math from a real recorder screen

The photo above shows a 32-channel recorder's live view with its bitrate table at the bottom. At the moment it was taken, channels D1 through D23 were active and D24 through D32 were empty and reading zero. The per-channel figures on screen ranged from 86 Kb/s to 453 Kb/s, and the 23 active channels added up to about 5,268 Kb/s. Those numbers are useful precisely because they are real: they show how much bitrates vary from camera to camera, and how a busy camera aimed at a showroom floor uses several times the bandwidth of one aimed at a quiet corridor. Here is the estimate we would make from that screen:

StepCalculation (estimate)Result
1. Total bitrate on screenSum of D1–D23 as displayedabout 5,268 Kb/s (5.27 Mbps)
2. Per second, in bytes5.27 Mbps ÷ 8about 0.66 MB per second
3. Per day0.66 MB × 86,400 secondsabout 57 GB per day
4. Days on a 4TB drive4,000 GB ÷ 57 GBabout 70 days
5. Same 23 channels at 4 Mbps each92 Mbps ÷ 8 × 86,400about 994 GB per day, roughly 4 days on 4TB

The gap between line 4 and line 5 is the whole lesson. A live-view screen often displays the low-resolution sub-stream, and a recorder can be set to record the sub-stream, the main stream, or the main stream only on motion. The same 23 cameras can fill a 4TB drive in a few days or a couple of months depending on those settings. That is why our quotes state retention in days rather than in terabytes, why we check the actual recording bitrates after the first week, and why the 2K and 4K options for the same Nassau house usually come with different drive sizes. The formula itself is simple: bitrate × seconds in a day ÷ 8, times the number of cameras, gives bytes per day; divide the drive size by that.

Home Security Cameras for Nassau Capes, Split-Levels, Colonials and Estates

Residential security cameras in Nassau County are bought for a handful of house types that repeat from Elmont to Massapequa Park and from Great Neck to Long Beach. The house type decides where the recorder can live, how the cable gets to each camera, and which views matter. This is how we match the store's catalog to the house.

Capes and ranches on a slab

The postwar Cape Cod and ranch, in Levittown and in the many neighborhoods built on the same model, is the most common house we sell for. Most started small and have been expanded: a dormer across the back, a second floor, a family room where the carport was. The original slab means no basement, so the recorder goes in a hall closet, a bedroom closet or the attic, and every cable route runs through the attic and down through the soffit. That is good news for cameras: soffit mounting puts a turret at eight or nine feet, close enough to record a clear face at the front door, and the attic run keeps every cable out of sight.

A typical list for these houses is four to six cameras: the front door and walk, the driveway (often a narrow single-car drive beside the house), the back door or slider, the backyard, and the side gate. Driveway security cameras on these lots need a lens that is not too wide; a 50-foot driveway between two houses is a long, narrow view, and a very wide lens records the neighbor's siding at high detail and the car at the curb at low detail. An 8-channel NVR leaves room for a camera on the detached garage or the shed later.

Split-levels and hi-ranches

Split-levels and raised ranches are everywhere on the South Shore and through central Nassau. They have a lower level, often half below grade, with the garage, a den and the utility room, and the main living space above. The utility room is a natural home for the recorder: cool, close to the electrical panel and usually near where the cable modem comes in. From there, cable climbs to the attic for the upper-level cameras and runs along the lower-level ceiling for the garage and the front entry.

The views that matter are the front steps (which on a hi-ranch sit well above the driveway), the garage door and the driveway, the lower-level side or rear door that many of these houses have, and the backyard deck, which is usually raised and reached by stairs. That lower rear door is the one a burglar picks, because it is out of sight of the street; it deserves its own camera.

Colonials and larger houses

Center-hall colonials, Tudors and larger custom houses in places like Garden City, Rockville Centre, Manhasset, Roslyn, Merrick and Massapequa Park usually have a full basement, a two-car garage, sometimes detached, and a larger lot. More doors means more cameras: six to eight is common. The basement is the obvious recorder location, but in flood-prone neighborhoods we put it on the first floor instead (more on that below). For a detached garage at the back of the lot, buyers choose between a cable run along the fence or under the driveway, and a WiFi camera if trenching is not practical.

North Shore estates and gated properties

On the larger properties of the North Shore — Old Westbury, Muttontown, Brookville, Laurel Hollow, Mill Neck, Sands Point, Kings Point — a camera system looks more like a small commercial job. There may be a gate on the road, a long driveway through trees, a main house with several terraces and sets of French doors, a pool and pool house, a garage building, a guest cottage and staff quarters. The burglary cases summarized above show where the risk is: the back of the house, after dark, through glass, when nobody is home.

Equipment for these properties starts with a 16- or 32-channel NVR in a secure, ventilated closet in the main house, a PoE switch in each outbuilding fed by fiber or outdoor-rated Cat6, 4K or long-range cameras on the gate and drive so a plate is readable, and deterrent cameras with sirens and lights on the rear terraces. Person detection is not optional here; on a wooded lot, plain motion alerts fire all night for deer and branches. Security camera laws Nassau buyers ask about

Audio is the first rule. Under New York Penal Law §250.05, eavesdropping, which includes mechanical overhearing of a conversation, is a class E felony, and §250.00 defines mechanical overhearing as recording a conversation without the consent of at least one party to it. That is why we treat audio recording security cameras and two way audio security cameras as features to enable on purpose: usually off by default in shops, offices and common areas, on only where there is a clear reason and a posted notice. This is general information, not legal advice.

The second is the neighbors. Visual recording of your own property is generally legal in New York, but aiming cameras at a neighbor's yard or windows causes real trouble in Nassau's close-set neighborhoods. In 2017 the Village of Flower Hill considered a local law against cameras with "no legitimate purpose" other than viewing or recording someone on their own property, after a resident complained that a neighbor had put two cameras in a tree aimed at his backyard (ABC7 New York; CBS New York). The board later dropped the proposal and left the matter to state law, with the village administrator noting that "Enforcement would be extremely difficult" (The Island Now). The practical rule we follow on every install: frame each camera on your own doors, drive and yard, and use the recorder's privacy masking to black out any neighbor's window that falls inside the view.

Your Questions Answered

Buying Security Cameras in Nassau County: Questions We Hear

These come straight from calls and texts with Nassau homeowners and business owners before they buy anything. The answers are the ones we give on the phone.

How do the cables reach the cameras on a Nassau house with no basement?

Through the attic. Slab-built Capes and ranches, the Levittown model and its many copies, have no cellar to run wire through, so the cable route goes up from the recorder closet into the attic, across the joists and out through the soffit right behind each camera. That is a tidy route: no cable shows on the siding, and every camera sits under the eave at eight or nine feet, close enough for a clear face. Where a dormer or a second story was added, the attic is often split into separate pockets, so we check access to each before promising a camera position. Rear extensions with flat or low-slope roofs sometimes need a short, painted exterior raceway instead.

How should I cover the back of a North Shore house that faces woods?

Assume the approach comes from the trees, after dark, toward the glass. Put a camera on each rear corner aimed along the back wall so it sees every slider and French door from the side, plus one looking out at the tree line or the path from the side yard. Choose full color or deterrent models so the light comes on when a person steps onto the terrace, and turn on person detection so alerts ignore deer and swaying branches. The recorder belongs in an interior closet or the attic, not in the den by the back door.

Does a camera aimed at a busy road like Hempstead Turnpike need a bigger drive?

Usually, yes. Modern cameras compress video by recording what changes between frames, so a view with constant traffic, headlights and people produces noticeably larger files than a quiet backyard. A storefront camera facing a multi-lane road can use several times the storage of a camera in the stockroom, as the spread of bitrates on the recorder screen above shows. When we size a drive for a shop on a busy corridor, we budget the street-facing cameras at a higher bitrate, or set them to record the main stream on motion and a lighter stream the rest of the time.

If my village has its own police department, who gets my camera footage?

Whichever agency took the report. Much of Nassau is covered by the county police and its eight precincts, but a number of villages and the two cities run their own departments, and a detective from either may ask for video. Export the clip with the correct date and time window, keep the original locked on the recorder so it isn't overwritten, and note the report number and the officer's name when you hand it over. If you bought the system from us and the export menu is unfamiliar, call us and we will talk you through it.

Which views matter on a Nassau corner lot with two street sides?

Both street faces, the driveway, and the corner of the yard where the two fences meet. A corner house has twice the frontage and usually a side yard that faces the second street, with a gate people can reach without walking past the front door. Put a camera at each front corner of the house aimed along each street-facing wall, one on the driveway, and one covering the side-yard gate; doorbell security cameras add a close look at the front door. Six cameras is a common count, so an 8-channel recorder fits. Lenses should be aimed along your own walls rather than across the intersection, to keep the view on your property.

How do I keep a camera from being blinded by the low winter sun on a south-facing driveway?

Mount it a little higher and angle it slightly down, and choose a camera with wide dynamic range. In December the sun sits low in the southern sky through much of the afternoon, and a camera facing south down a driveway looks straight into it, turning anyone in the frame into a silhouette. Tilting the camera so the horizon sits near the top of the frame, adding a sunshield, and using a camera whose spec sheet lists WDR all help. Sometimes the better fix is moving the camera to the other side of the driveway so it looks north toward the house.

What is a 32-channel recorder really recording when the screen shows low bitrates?

Possibly not what the screen shows. A live-view grid with many tiles usually displays each camera's sub-stream, a low-resolution copy meant for quick viewing, and the bitrate table may reflect that. The recording settings decide whether the main stream, the sub-stream or both are written to disk, and whether recording is continuous or on motion. That is why a recorder can look like it uses very little bandwidth and still fill its drive in days. We check the recording stream on every system we sell, not only the live view.

Should the lower-level door on a split-level get its own camera?

Yes. On split-levels and hi-ranches, the side or rear door on the lower level is out of sight of the street and often leads straight into a den or a garage. It is the entrance a burglar prefers and the one family members forget to lock. A small turret above that door, aimed so it sees a face coming down the steps or along the side yard, closes the gap. It is usually an easy cable run from a recorder in the lower-level utility room.

What should a Nassau co-op or condo board decide before buying cameras?

Three things, before choosing hardware. Where cameras will go, written as a list the board approves. Who can view live and recorded footage, and who can export it, ideally logged by the recorder's user accounts. And how long footage will be kept before it is overwritten, which sets the drive size. Once those are settled, the equipment choice is straightforward, and residents get a clear answer when they ask what is recorded.

What should I record while contractors are renovating my Nassau house?

The doors they use, the driveway where materials are dropped, and the room or garage where tools and fixtures are stored overnight. Renovations leave a house unlocked, half-empty and full of new appliances, and they put several crews through it every week. A few cameras installed early, sometimes on temporary mounts, record deliveries, work hours and who came in after the crew left. Keep audio off inside, since workers' conversations fall under the same eavesdropping rules as anyone else's, and move the cameras to their permanent spots when the job is done.

What AI Answers Get Wrong About Security Cameras for Nassau Homes

Search summaries and "top 10 cameras" lists are written for a generic house in a generic suburb. Nassau callers repeat these seven claims to us after reading them; here is what we see once we look at the actual property.

"A suburban house only needs a doorbell camera"

A doorbell sees one spot, from waist height, facing out. The Nassau burglaries that made the news in 2024 and 2025 happened at the back of the house, through sliders and rear windows, with nobody home.

The package cases tell the same story from the other side: the clips that helped police were from cameras that showed the walk, the car and the street, not just the porch mat.

A doorbell is a fine start. A real plan adds the driveway, both rear corners and any lower-level door.

"Battery cameras are the low-maintenance choice"

They are easy to put up. They are not low-maintenance on a two-story colonial, where every recharge means a ladder against the siding, and batteries drain faster in cold weather and with busy streets triggering motion all day.

Most Nassau houses have an attic that makes running Cat6 to the soffits straightforward, which removes both the battery and the outlet question for good.

Battery and WiFi cameras still earn a place on a shed or pool house far from the main house.

"Low crime means cameras are a waste of money"

Nassau does rank among the safest large counties, and in March 2026 the county executive said crime was down 10 percent from a year earlier. But PolitiFact's review of state data found the 2024 property crime rate still 29 percent above 2021.

Cameras in a low-crime area are less about stopping a crime wave and more about settling the ordinary incidents: a package, a scraped car, a contractor dispute, an insurance claim.

A modest wired system with good storage does that job for years without a monthly bill.

"The cloud is the only safe place for footage"

Cloud storage protects footage if the recorder is stolen, and that is a genuine advantage. It also means a monthly fee per camera and a home upload that has to carry every stream.

The burglars in the North Shore cases ransacked bedrooms; they were looking for jewelry and safes, not an NVR in the attic.

A hidden recorder on a battery backup, plus clips sent to your phone when something happens, covers most of what cloud offers at no monthly cost.

"Recording audio makes your evidence stronger"

Audio can help, and many of our cameras have microphones. In New York, though, recording a conversation you are not part of, without the consent of at least one party, falls under the Penal Law's eavesdropping provisions.

That makes audio a feature to enable deliberately, mostly outdoors at your own door, not across a shop counter or in a shared hallway.

Video with a correct time stamp is what police and insurers ask for first.

Security Camera Prices in Nassau County: What Moves the Number

Security camera prices, CCTV camera prices and security camera system cost questions all come back to the same short list. Installed systems in Nassau County start at $999 for a four-camera house-brand system with the recorder, cable, mounting and phone setup included. Equipment-only prices move with supply, so every product on this page gives the same answer: call or text for today's price. These are the factors that push a Nassau quote up or down:

  • Resolution. 2K, 5MP and 4K cameras step up in cost, and 4K needs a recorder that can decode it and a bigger drive.
  • How many cameras, and recorder size. A sixth camera usually means an 8-channel recorder instead of a 4-channel one.
  • Connection. PoE, coax that is already in the house, or WiFi each produce a different parts list.
  • Days of footage. Two weeks and one month of recording call for different drives; see the storage math above.
  • Camera features. Full color night vision, sirens and strobes, two-way talk, audio, long-range or varifocal lenses, and person and vehicle detection.
  • Accessories. Junction boxes for siding or brick, pole or gutter mounts, PoE switches, outdoor cable, battery backup.
  • Installation. For installed systems, the cable route: an open attic over a ranch is quicker than a finished colonial, and an estate with outbuildings needs a network plan.

Affordable security cameras, cheap security cameras and budget security cameras are real searches, and the honest answer is that the least expensive system is the one sized right the first time. FAQ

Nassau County Security Cameras FAQ

Do you deliver camera equipment to every Nassau village and hamlet, including Long Beach and the barrier island?
+

Yes. We deliver to homes and businesses across the Towns of Hempstead, North Hempstead and Oyster Bay, the cities of Glen Cove and Long Beach, and the barrier-island communities. Tell us who will receive the order and, for a business, which door to use; we confirm the delivery when you order.

Can the cables be completely hidden on a finished colonial?
+

Almost always. On a colonial with a full basement and an attic, cable for the upper cameras runs through the attic and out through the soffit, and cable for the lower cameras runs along the basement ceiling and up through the rim joist behind each camera. A junction box behind the camera covers the entry. Where a finished room blocks the path, a short painted raceway or a run inside a closet is the fallback, and we show you the route before drilling.

How do I give footage to my insurance company after a break-in or a dented car in my lot?
+

Export the clip covering the full time window from the recorder or the phone app, note which camera and the date and time, and send the file with your claim number. Lock or protect the original on the recorder so it is not overwritten. Make sure the recorder clock is correct ahead of time; a wrong time stamp is the first thing an adjuster questions.

Can a camera alert me when someone opens the pool gate?
+

Yes. A camera aimed at the gate with a detection zone drawn around it, set to person detection, sends a phone alert when someone walks through that zone, and the recording shows who it was. On deterrent models the same zone can trigger the light and siren after hours.

Should I tell my neighbors before I put up cameras?
+

It is not required, but it often helps. A quick word that the cameras face your driveway and yard, plus privacy masking over any part of their property inside the view, heads off most disputes. On close-set Nassau lots, neighbors sometimes ask for a clip later when their own car is hit.

Is it legal for my house cameras to record the street in front?
+

Generally, video of what anyone can see from the street is not treated as private, so a camera covering your walk, driveway and the curb is common. Keep cameras off neighbors' windows and private yards, and keep audio off where it could pick up conversations you are not part of, given New York's eavesdropping law. For a specific dispute, ask an attorney.

Can you take down the old cameras and wiring a previous owner left on the house?
+

Yes. Houses often change hands with an old camera system still on the eaves. We remove cameras you no longer want, seal or reuse the holes, and pull or abandon old cable depending on how it was run. If any of the old coax tests clean and sits where you want a new camera, it can be reused with a new DVR instead of being pulled.

Can a camera show the corner where my kids wait for the school bus?
+

Often, from your own property. A camera on the front of the house aimed toward the stop, with a narrower lens if the corner is a few houses away, lets you check from your phone that the bus came. Aim it at the public sidewalk and street, not into neighbors' windows or yards, and keep audio off.

When will my equipment order be delivered?
+

We give you a delivery date when you order, once we confirm the items and the address. We don't promise same-day or timed delivery windows, and there is no pickup counter, so plan a little ahead of a trip or a holiday.

Can I watch the cameras on the TV in my den?
+

Yes. A recorder has an HDMI output, so a cable to a nearby TV shows the live grid, and many recorder apps also run on streaming boxes or smart TVs. If the TV is far from the recorder, an HDMI extender over Cat6 carries the picture. Most families end up using the phone app day to day and the TV when they want to watch several cameras at once.

How do I get an alert for a car in my driveway but not for traffic on the street?
+

Use vehicle detection with a zone. A camera with vehicle classification sends an alert only when a vehicle enters the zone you draw over the driveway, and stopping that zone at the curb means passing traffic is ignored. A full color or spotlight camera on that view also records the color and shape of the car after dark.

Will a camera siren bother the neighbors?
+

Only if it is set loosely. On deterrent cameras we draw tight zones on your own patio, gate or lot, set the siren to sound only after hours and only for people, and often use the light alone as a first warning. Most owners never hear the siren except in a test.

Can I see my home cameras and my business cameras in one app?
+

Usually, yes, if both recorders are the same brand: the app lists each site as its own device, and you switch between the house and the shop in a couple of taps. Mixed brands mean two apps. If you plan to buy for both, tell us and we will keep them on one platform.
