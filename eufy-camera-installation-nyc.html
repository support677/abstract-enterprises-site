The Neighborhood

Why New York City Properties Need Eufy Camera Installation Done Properly

New York is a city of old buildings, shared doors and long distances between the router and the street, and eufy's consumer cameras were designed with a simpler house in mind. The Citizens Housing and Planning Council counts about 3.7 million housing units in the city and finds that roughly 54 percent of them are in buildings put up before 1947. The Rent Guidelines Board's 2026 Housing Supply Report uses the same 54 percent figure and puts the 2023 total at 3,705,000 units. More than 80 percent of the city's housing is over fifty years old. The building history site Building the Skyline notes that New York added more than 740,000 housing units in the 1920s alone. Those are the walls, doorways and vestibules a eufy doorbell or outdoor camera is going onto: thick masonry, plaster on metal or wood lath, cast-iron gates, wooden doors with glass panels, and entry systems wired decades before anyone thought of a phone app.

The way people share those buildings matters just as much. CHPC reports that about 62 percent of the city's units sit in buildings with six or more apartments, and just over a quarter sit in one- and two-family houses. The mix swings hard by borough: in Manhattan, 97 percent of units are in buildings of six or more apartments, while on Staten Island 83 percent are in one- and two-family homes. Brooklyn has the most even mix of the five. In one listings sample of prewar buildings published by RentReboot, 47.7 percent had fewer than ten apartments and only 41.5 percent listed an elevator. Put plainly, a huge share of New Yorkers live in small walk-ups with a buzzer at the street door, a hallway they do not own, and a landlord, board or managing agent who has a say over anything screwed into it. That is the first fact a eufy installer has to deal with, long before the question of which camera to buy.

Package theft is the reason most of these calls start. Security.org's annual package theft report found that apartment residents were more than three times as likely as single-family residents to have had a package stolen in the previous three months, 10 percent against 3 percent, and it points out that apartment dwellers may not be allowed to put a doorbell or camera outside their units. A roundup of Reddit threads by Delivery Amigo describes older New York apartment buildings that often have no surveillance cameras and no package drop-off area, and cites theft stories from Astoria, Bushwick, Williamsburg and Manhattan. In its 2024 holiday tips, the New York Attorney General's office told New Yorkers to use a doorbell camera or a video surveillance system to deter thieves or help police identify them. Eufy is one of the brands people reach for when they take that advice, because its cameras are sold widely and many of its setups store video locally instead of requiring a monthly plan.

Eufy also carries a New York-specific history that a buyer should know about. In January 2025, as reported by DataBreaches.net from the Attorney General's announcement, Attorney General Letitia James secured a 450,000-dollar settlement from three companies that distribute eufy home security cameras, after an investigation found that in certain situations video sent over the internet from eufy products was not protected by end-to-end encryption and that at least part of the connection had no encryption at all. The settlement requires the distributors to use proper encryption, run regular penetration testing and keep a vulnerability management program. ChannelNews reported a different figure for the same settlement, 640,000 dollars, so we cite the amount the Attorney General's reports carry and flag that sources disagree. Separately, Mozilla's privacy review of the eufy Indoor Cam S350 says that camera offers end-to-end encryption for cloud video but that it is not switched on by default. On March 2, 2026, National Today reported that Representative Elise Stefanik and Senator Rick Scott asked the FCC and the Commerce Department to open a formal investigation into Anker, the company behind eufy. None of this means a eufy camera cannot be used safely. It means setup matters, and the protective settings need to be checked one by one rather than assumed.

The buildings themselves fight wireless gear. Eufy's outdoor battery cameras usually talk to a HomeBase, which sits near the router. A third-party eufy blog, SmartEufy, warns that brick walls, multiple floors and lots of electronics can drop HomeBase range significantly. That is the description of almost every New York rowhouse: a router in a back room or a basement, a parlor floor above it, a brick front wall, and a stoop or areaway where the doorbell and the outdoor camera need to be. In apartment buildings, dozens of neighbors' Wi-Fi networks crowd the same air. Our own NYC wireless camera page, which describes the cost drivers we see across the city, lists mounting on brownstone brick without damaging it, fishing cable through old masonry and plaster, and the gap between a single doorbell in an apartment and a whole-house system. A eufy job in New York is mostly a signal and power puzzle, solved inside a set of building rules.

And then there are the rules. The Landmarks Preservation Commission has designated 38,119 buildings and sites across the five boroughs, including 1,470 individual landmarks and 157 historic districts and extensions, according to the figures it reported for 2026. Its 2018 rule amendments say cameras and similar safety equipment generally should not go on a primary facade unless there is no feasible alternative. Co-op proprietary leases and condo rules frequently treat anything on a hallway wall or door as an alteration. New York State requires anyone in the business of installing CCTV for intrusion or theft monitoring to hold a Department of State license. A eufy camera you mount yourself inside your own apartment does not trigger any of that. A eufy camera a contractor mounts on a landmarked stoop, a co-op corridor or a shop front can trigger all of it.

The brownstone and rowhouse stoop

Rowhouses are where the old wiring question is hardest. Many have a pair of thin wires at the front door that once ran a bell or a buzzer, and many have a combination of both: a button outside, a buzzer and a door-release inside, sometimes an intercom handset on each floor. Eufy's own support article is direct about this. Its wired video doorbell does not work with existing intercom power and signal systems. If the intercom is no longer used, the wiring itself can sometimes be reused, but only with a proper doorbell transformer in the circuit. Eufy's power requirement for its wired doorbell is 16 to 24 volts AC at 30 VA or more. So before a wired eufy doorbell goes onto a stoop, we meter the wires, trace where they go, and find the transformer if there is one. If what we find is an intercom line, we tell you, and the choices become a battery doorbell, a new low-voltage doorbell circuit, or keeping the intercom and adding a camera beside it. The other rowhouse issue is distance. The router is often at the back or in the cellar, and the doorbell is behind a brick front wall up a flight of stone steps. We test the signal at the door before choosing the HomeBase spot, and if the signal is weak, we move the HomeBase forward rather than hoping the camera copes.

The walk-up apartment door

This is the most common eufy situation in the city and the one with the most confusion about what is allowed. A New York legal guide published by Connextivity says no New York State or City law stops a resident from putting a camera on their own apartment door, and none says how it must be aimed. The same guide says a camera on a hallway wall sits in a common area, so co-ops and condos usually require building approval. A JustAnswer exchange with a Brooklyn tenant makes the same distinction: cameras in common areas outside your door belong to the landlord's space and need permission, and a landlord can remove a camera installed without consent. Eufy's own apartment camera page suggests a window camera when the lease does not allow a camera on an outside wall or the door. Our approach is to start inside the unit, where the decision is yours, and only move out to the door or the hall with permission in writing. That keeps a renter's eufy setup on the right side of the lease.

The co-op or condo corridor

Co-ops add a layer that renters sometimes do not expect. A Guzov LLC post on co-op and condo rules says there is no New York City law prohibiting residents from installing doorbell cameras in apartment building hallways, but that a co-op's rules may prohibit a shareholder from putting a video doorbell in a common area. Brick Underground's condo and co-op lawyer column explains that a board can pass a ban on video doorbells as a new house rule without a shareholder vote, and describes a dispute that started because a shareholder aimed a camera straight at a board member's door. A Manhattan renovation firm, KS Renovation Group, writes that most co-op proprietary leases classify door-mounted devices and wiring as alterations needing board approval, even battery doorbells that face shared hallways. So in a co-op we ask for the house rules first. If the board allows a door-mounted eufy, we install it with whatever paperwork the managing agent wants. If not, an indoor camera behind the door or a view through the glass of a window gives you most of the protection with none of the dispute.

The storefront and its gate

A eufy camera in a shop is a different animal. It is usually inside, looking at a register, a door or a stockroom, and it often runs on a plug rather than a battery. The City Council announced on June 30, 2026 that, as of July 1, 2026, security grilles on buildings in the business and mercantile occupancy groups must allow at least 70 percent visibility from the sidewalk. That changes what an inside camera can see through the gate at night, and it is worth planning the camera's aim around the grille that will be there, not the one being replaced. Landmarked storefronts bring LPC into the picture. Guidance for landmarked storefronts from the NoHo BID notes that no LPC permit is required for fixtures that do not physically attach to any part of the building, which is one reason an inside, freestanding or window-mounted camera is often the cleanest answer on a designated block.

The detached house and its driveway

Staten Island, much of eastern Queens and parts of the north Bronx look more like the suburb eufy designs for: a driveway, a side gate, a back yard and a front door with a working doorbell transformer behind it. These are the most straightforward eufy jobs we do. The planning questions are about distance and winter. A HomeBase placed in a front room may reach the doorbell and the driveway but not a camera at the back fence. A battery camera on a north-facing wall will need charging more often than one in the sun, which is where a solar panel can help. Our NYC wireless camera page prices a solar panel per battery camera, sized for New York winter, as an add-on, and we look at where the sun actually lands on the wall in December before suggesting one.

Most of the time lost on a eufy job is lost before the first screw: a doorbell bought for wiring that turns out to be an intercom, a HomeBase set up in a closet the signal cannot escape, a board that wants paperwork nobody asked for, or a lease that forbids the exact mount the box came with. Ten minutes with your phone camera prevents nearly all of it. Please do not open the intercom panel or touch the building's door-release wiring yourself; just look and photograph.

Read the lease before the box

It sounds backwards, but in New York the best eufy decisions are made with the lease or the house rules open, not the product page. If the lease bans drilling, a battery doorbell held by its mounting plate may still count as an alteration if it needs screws. If the co-op has a resolution on video doorbells, the board has already decided for you. If your building is rent-stabilized, keep in mind that Seneca Security notes courts and the state housing agency take seriously any landlord action that could be construed as harassment of a stabilized tenant, which cuts both ways: it is one reason both sides prefer cameras that cover entrances, not individual doors. Send us the clause and we will propose a setup that fits it.

Find the shortest path between the router and the door

In a rowhouse, stand at the router and count what is between it and the front door: walls, floors, a brick party wall, a staircase. In an apartment, check whether the router is in a back bedroom with the door two rooms away. The HomeBase does not have to sit next to the router if it can be connected some other way, but it does have to be close enough to the cameras to keep them connected. A rough sketch with the router and the door marked saves a site visit's worth of guessing. If the router is in a closet in the basement next to the boiler, tell us that too; a HomeBase down there will struggle to reach the stoop, and basements are the wrong place for electronics during heavy rain, as the basement flooding ABC7 reported in Hollis and Flushing during the remnants of Hurricane Ida in September 2021 showed.

Decide who owns the account

Before we pair anything, decide whose phone and email the eufy account will belong to. In a family house, that is usually the owner. In a small business, it should be the owner or a business email the owner controls, not an employee's personal account. In a two-family house where each floor has its own doorbell, each household should usually have its own account. We pair devices to the account you choose and we do not keep the password, which is the single most important thing about who can see your video later.

Eufy Camera Installation for New York City's Architecture

New York packs nearly every kind of building into five boroughs. Each changes three things about a eufy job: what the device is fixed to, how it gets power and signal, and who has to agree before it goes up. These are the types our research and our sources describe across the city, and how we handle each.

Brownstones and Brick Rowhouses

Stone stoops, heavy wooden doors, brick fronts and old bell wiring of uncertain purpose. Many sit in historic districts, and Brooklyn has the largest share of the city's designated buildings. Wiring is metered and the facade question is settled first.

Prewar Walk-Up Tenements

Four- to six-story buildings from before 1929, with a buzzer at the street door and apartment doors on shared corridors. Most eufy work here happens inside the unit or on the unit door with permission.

Prewar Elevator Apartment Houses

Eight- to twelve-story buildings from the 1910s and 1920s and the larger doorman buildings that followed. Thick masonry walls, many neighbors' networks, and managing agents with written contractor rules.

Co-ops and Condos

Proprietary leases, house rules, alteration agreements and boards that can ban video doorbells by resolution. Paperwork first, then a setup the board accepts.

One- and Two-Family Houses

Staten Island's dominant type and common in Queens and the outer Bronx. Driveways, side yards and working doorbell transformers make these the most conventional eufy installs.

Mixed-Use Buildings Over Stores

Apartments above a ground-floor shop on a commercial strip, often with a separate residential door beside the store. Two owners, two doors and sometimes two different eufy setups.

What a business camera has to do that a home camera does not

A home doorbell is watched by the family that owns it. A camera in a shop may be the evidence in an insurance claim, a police report or a dispute with a customer, and it is seen by employees, customers and sometimes the landlord. That changes how we set it up. The account belongs to the owner, not to the manager on shift. Shared access, if any, is given deliberately and removed when someone leaves. The clock in the app is checked against the real time, because a clip with the wrong time is harder to use. Recording storage is checked so that video does not overwrite itself before the owner gets a chance to save an incident. And the camera aim is reviewed against what the law and common sense say about places like restrooms and changing rooms, where cameras do not belong at all.

City and state rules that touch a business eufy setup

  • Licensed installation. The city's business portal says a license from the New York State Department of State is required to install, service or maintain security systems, and a legal summary of 19 NYCRR 195.2(b) explains that a CCTV system used to monitor intrusion, theft or movement falls inside it. We do this work under NYS license #12000287431.
  • Low-voltage wiring. Under the city electrical code as summarized for Local Law 128 of 2024, low-voltage work under 50 volts for signaling, communication, alarm and data circuits generally needs no electrical permit when done by a licensed master electrician, special electrician or qualified person. New line-voltage outlets for a camera are an electrician's job, and our quote says so where one is needed.
  • Grille visibility. The City Council's June 30, 2026 release says security grilles on business and mercantile buildings must have at least 70 percent visibility from the sidewalk as of July 1, 2026. We plan inside cameras that look out with that in mind.
  • Face recognition and signs. New York City's biometric identifier law requires a commercial establishment that collects biometric information, such as through software that identifies faces in video, to post a sign at every customer entrance at least 8.5 by 11 inches, and law firm summaries note individuals can recover 500 dollars for each failure to post. Ordinary video without that analysis appears to be exempt. If any camera's software in your shop is set to identify faces, that feature needs a decision, not a default.
  • Notice under state law. Summaries of New York Penal Law say Section 250.65 exempts security systems from criminal liability where a written notice is posted conspicuously or the cameras are clearly and immediately obvious. Hidden cameras in private spaces can support a felony charge under Section 250.45.
  • Sharing with the NYPD. NYC 311 says any business with a security camera can share live video with the NYPD during emergencies by registering camera locations at NewYorkCityConnect.org. This is voluntary.
  • Help for small retailers. The Council's June 2026 release describes Int. 553-A, which would have the Department of Small Business Services create a program to help small retail businesses pay for security technology, and Int. 623-2026 would pilot cameras and plexiglass in some high-theft ZIP codes. Both were proposals at the time of the reports, not programs you can apply to today.

Your Questions Answered

Eufy Camera Installation in NYC: Questions Answered

Doors, hallways and leases

Am I allowed to put a eufy camera outside my own apartment door in a New York building?

On your own door, generally yes; in the hallway, only with permission. Connextivity's guide to New York apartment camera rules says no state or city law stops a resident from putting a camera on their own apartment door, and none says how it has to be aimed. A camera on a hallway wall is different, because the hallway is a common area that belongs to the landlord, the co-op or the condo. A JustAnswer exchange with a Brooklyn tenant reached the same conclusion: common-area mounts need the landlord's consent, and a landlord can remove one installed without it. Co-ops add their own layer, since Brick Underground reports a board can ban video doorbells by house rule. Our practice is to check your lease or house rules, mount on the door itself only where that is allowed, aim the camera at your doorstep rather than across the hall, and leave audio off unless you have a clear reason to record it.

My landlord wants to put cameras inside the apartment I rent. Is that something they can do?

Inside your home is the one place the law is clearest. Connextivity notes that a camera installed covertly inside a unit can support a charge under Penal Law 250.45, a Class E felony, and the same guide treats living rooms and bedrooms as the resident's call. Cameras in common areas such as the lobby, the stairs and the building entrance are a different matter and are common in New York buildings. If you are worried about a camera inside your apartment that you did not put there, that is a question for a tenant lawyer or the police, not a camera installer. What we do for renters is the reverse: help you put your own eufy cameras inside your own space, pointed at your own door, on your own account.

Can a eufy video doorbell work on an apartment door that only has a building buzzer?

A wired eufy doorbell generally cannot use the buzzer. Eufy's support article says its wired video doorbell does not work with existing intercom power and signal systems, and its apartment buying guide tells renters to check whether the door has doorbell wiring from a low-voltage transformer rather than an intercom or access-control line. The device needs 16 to 24 volts AC at 30 VA or more. Most apartment doors inside a walk-up have no doorbell transformer at all, only the building intercom handset inside the unit. So for apartments we usually look at a battery doorbell on the door, if the lease allows, or a camera inside the apartment looking at the door. The building's intercom stays as it is. If the intercom has been abandoned and the wiring is free, eufy says that wiring can sometimes be reused with a proper transformer, which we test for on site.

Our lease says no drilling at all. How can we still get a eufy camera watching the front of the apartment?

Eufy's own answer for renters is the window camera. Its apartment page says that when a lease does not allow cameras on an outside wall or the door, a window camera is the usual choice, mounted inside and flat against the glass, and its window camera page says it works best on a first- or second-floor window. That suits ground-floor and second-floor units in walk-ups and rowhouse apartments. Higher up, the view of the street gets small and the angle gets steep. The other no-drill option is a plug-in indoor camera such as the S350 on a shelf or table, aimed at the inside of the front door, so you see who comes in rather than who stands outside. We check your view from each window and spot before recommending one, and we set the camera so it does not stare into a neighbor's window across the street or the airshaft.

Buildings, landmarks and permits

Does New York require landlords to install cameras in apartment buildings?

We found no general city or state law requiring cameras in ordinary apartment buildings. Seneca Security, describing NYC building security rules, says plainly that cameras are not required for most residential buildings, and that insurers rather than the law drive much of the demand, since many commercial property insurers set camera coverage as a condition for theft and vandalism claims. The LPC's own permit guidance describes cameras as commonly used safety equipment that is not required by law. Some buildings have them because of public programs: NYCHA told the City Council in 2023 that it had more than 18,500 CCTV cameras across its developments, and a 2025 participatory budgeting proposal for Gowanus Houses asked for cameras in elevators and near apartment doors. For private buildings, adding cameras is the owner's choice, which is why so many residents end up adding their own eufy device at their own door.

Can a eufy doorbell or camera go on a landmarked brownstone or a building in a historic district?

Often yes, but usually not on the front of the building. The Landmarks Preservation Commission's 2018 rule amendments cover security cameras and say many of these objects should not be installed on a primary facade unless there is no feasible alternative or another agency requires the location. Its Permit Guidebook Chapter 11 has a section on installing security cameras that staff use for approvals, and an LPC approval we found for nine ground-floor cameras on another building cited Section 2-20(c)(4). A secondary source claims LPC allows no more than two cameras per 25 feet of street frontage on small landmark buildings; we have not confirmed that against the rule text, so we treat it as unverified and check with LPC on any job where it matters. In practice we look first at mounts inside the vestibule, inside the door or on a window, which avoid the facade altogether, and the NoHo BID's guidance notes fixtures that do not attach to the building need no LPC permit.

Do eufy cameras or the low-voltage wires behind them need a permit from the city?

For a typical eufy job, no electrical permit. As summarized for the city's 2024 electrical code, low-voltage work under 50 volts for signaling, communication, alarm and data circuits generally needs no electrical permit when a licensed master electrician, special electrician or qualified person does it. A doorbell circuit at 16 to 24 volts falls well under that line. What a contractor does need is the state license: New York requires a Department of State license for businesses that install security systems, including CCTV used to watch for intrusion or theft, and our work is done under NYS license #12000287431. Landmark approval, board approval and lease permission are separate from both and often matter more. New 120-volt outlets for plug-in cameras are electrician's work and are quoted that way.

Our Jackson Heights co-op board has to sign off on anything mounted at the building entrance. What do boards usually ask to see before a eufy doorbell or hallway camera goes in?

Fewer than most kits contain, in most apartments, and more than one in most houses. Count the ways in. An apartment in a walk-up usually has one door that matters and perhaps a fire-escape window, so a doorbell or window camera plus one indoor camera often covers it. A brownstone or rowhouse usually needs the stoop, the garden-level door under it and the back yard. A detached house in Queens or Staten Island may add a driveway, a side gate and a garage. Privacy, power and the brand

Didn't eufy have a privacy problem? Is it still safe to put one on our door?

Eufy did, and New York was where it was settled. According to the Attorney General's announcement as reported by DataBreaches.net in January 2025, three companies distributing eufy cameras paid 450,000 dollars after an investigation found that, in certain situations, video sent over the internet was not end-to-end encrypted and part of the connection had no encryption at all. The settlement requires proper encryption, regular penetration testing and a vulnerability management program. Mozilla's review of the eufy S350 says end-to-end encryption for cloud video is available but not on by default. In March 2026, National Today reported a request from Representative Stefanik and Senator Scott for a federal investigation into Anker. Our view is practical: eufy can be used sensibly if the settings are right, so at every install we turn on end-to-end encryption where the model supports it, keep the account in your name with a strong password, review sharing, and leave audio off unless you want it. If those history points rule eufy out for you, we will say what else we would install.

Is a eufy doorbell wired or battery powered, and which one suits a New York door?

Both exist, and the door decides. Eufy sells wired doorbells that need a doorbell transformer supplying 16 to 24 volts AC at 30 VA or more, and battery doorbells such as the E340 that run without wiring and are recharged. A house or rowhouse with a real doorbell circuit is the natural home for a wired model: no charging, and the chime keeps working. An apartment door with only a building buzzer is the natural home for a battery model, if the lease allows it on the door at all. Battery doorbells in exposed New York doorways drain faster in winter cold, so we set motion sensitivity sensibly and tell you what to expect. Not necessarily. Many eufy setups store recordings locally, on a HomeBase or on a card in the device, so you can keep and watch video without a monthly plan. Which storage a given device uses depends on the model, so we check the box with you before the install. If you choose local storage, we set it up and show you how to export clips. What moves the price of a eufy job in New York

  • Number of devices. One doorbell on an apartment door is the smallest job; a house with a doorbell, HomeBase and several outdoor cameras is the largest common one.
  • Doorbell power. A wired doorbell on a working transformer is simpler than one that needs the circuit traced, the transformer checked against eufy's 30 VA minimum, or a new transformer fitted.
  • Mounting surface. Brownstone, brick and stone need slower drilling and masonry fixings; a door, a window or a wood frame is quicker.
  • Signal work. Repositioning a HomeBase, or running and testing a cable to it, adds time where brick or floors block the signal.
  • Solar panels. Panels for battery cameras on walls with good winter sun are priced per camera.
  • Building logistics. Board paperwork, service elevator bookings and walk-up stairs all show up in the borough rate or the quote.

FAQ

New York City Eufy Camera Installation FAQ

Will a wired eufy doorbell still ring the old chime inside our Brooklyn house?
+

Usually yes, if the existing system is a genuine doorbell circuit with a transformer and a chime. Eufy's wiring guide covers connecting its wired doorbell with the transformer and chime, and the transformer must supply 16 to 24 volts AC at 30 VA or more. We meter the transformer, connect the chime and ring the bell from outside before we leave. If the chime is too old to work with the doorbell, we tell you and discuss a replacement chime.

Should our eufy doorbell record sound from the hallway outside our apartment?
+

We usually recommend leaving audio recording off in shared hallways. Connextivity notes that it has never been cleanly settled whether a camera passively picking up hallway conversations counts as unlawful eavesdropping, and summaries of Penal Law 250.00 discuss New York's consent rules for recording conversations. Video alone covers packages and visitors. If you want audio for a specific reason, we explain the setting and you decide.

Do I need to put up a sign for the eufy cameras on my house?
+

For a visible doorbell or outdoor camera on your own home, usually not. Summaries of Penal Law 250.65 say security systems are exempt from criminal liability where a written notice is posted conspicuously or the cameras are clearly and immediately obvious. Many owners add a small sign anyway as a deterrent. Businesses using software that identifies faces have a separate sign rule under city law, which we cover at handover.

Our eufy doorbell keeps going offline every evening. Can you fix that without replacing it?
+

Often, yes. Evening dropouts in New York buildings usually come from a weak signal that gets worse when every neighbor's network is busy, a HomeBase in the wrong place, or a battery that sags in the cold. We test the signal at the door, move the HomeBase or router connection if needed, check the battery and power, and update settings. If the door simply cannot hold a connection, we tell you and suggest a different device or position.

We live on the fourth floor. Will a eufy window camera still show who is at the street door?
+

Probably not well. Eufy says its window camera works best on a first- or second-floor window. From higher floors the street door is small in the picture and seen almost from above, so faces are hard to make out. For upper-floor apartments, an indoor camera aimed at your own apartment door or a battery doorbell on the door, where allowed, is usually more useful.

Could the federal scrutiny of Anker stop our eufy cameras from working?
+

We cannot predict what regulators will do. What the record shows is that on March 2, 2026, Representative Stefanik and Senator Scott asked the FCC and the Commerce Department to open a formal investigation into Anker, according to National Today. A request is not a ruling. If any official action affects eufy devices in future, we will tell customers who ask, and we can quote an alternative system if you would rather not wait to find out.

Is the basement next to the router a good place for our HomeBase?
+

Usually not. A basement HomeBase has to push its signal up through floors and brick to reach a doorbell or outdoor camera, which weakens it. Basements in New York also flood in heavy storms, as the basement deaths reported in Queens during the remnants of Hurricane Ida in 2021 showed. We prefer a spot on the main floor, connected back to the router, where both the signal and the electronics are safer.

Can our shop register its eufy cameras with the NYPD?
+

NYC 311 says any business with a security camera can register its camera locations with the NYPD at NewYorkCityConnect.org, and can share live video during emergencies. It is voluntary. Registration is something the owner does directly, and we can explain what camera locations and details you will need before you sign up.

We are replacing our roll gate this year. Should we install the inside eufy cameras before or after?
+

After, or at least with the new gate's design in hand. The City Council says business and mercantile security grilles must allow at least 70 percent visibility from the sidewalk as of July 1, 2026, so the view through a new gate may be very different from the old one. Aiming cameras around the final gate saves a second visit to re-aim them.

Our managing agent wants a certificate of insurance before any contractor comes up. Do you provide one?
+

Yes. Send us the managing agent's insurance requirements, including the exact names they want listed, and we issue the certificate before the visit. Many Manhattan condo and doorman buildings ask for it on file ahead of time, so tell us the deadline when you book and we will schedule the visit around it and any service elevator reservation.

Our salon wants to try face recognition on a eufy camera at the door. What do we need to know?
+

New York City's biometric identifier law requires a commercial establishment that collects biometric information, such as through software identifying faces, to post a clear sign at every customer entrance at least 8.5 by 11 inches. Law firm summaries say customers can recover 500 dollars for each failure to post. Ordinary video without that analysis appears to be exempt. We recommend deciding deliberately whether you need that feature at all.

Who keeps the password to our eufy system after you finish?
+

You do. We pair every device to the account you choose, set or confirm a strong password with you, and do not keep a copy. If you want family members or a manager to have access, we set up sharing from your account so it can be removed later. The handover sheet lists the account owner so nobody has to guess years from now.

Will our eufy cameras keep recording if Con Edison loses power on our block?
+

Battery cameras and battery doorbells keep running for a while, but the router and HomeBase need power, and without them the cameras may not upload alerts. NY1 reported more than 10,000 customers out across the city during a June 2025 heat wave, and one customer can be a whole building. A small battery backup on the router and HomeBase keeps the system connected through short outages.

We bought a eufy kit online before calling. Will you install equipment we already own?
+

Yes, at labor-only rates from your borough's eufy price page. We check that each device suits your door and building before mounting it, for example that a wired doorbell has a real doorbell circuit, so you can return anything that does not fit while you still can. The equipment stays covered by its own manufacturer's warranty.
