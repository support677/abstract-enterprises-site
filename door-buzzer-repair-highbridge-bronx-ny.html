TERMINOLOGY

The Highbridge Repair Glossary

Names first, so the quote reads honest: entry buzzer, apartment entry system, building entrance system, visitor entry system, door release system, building access system — on the ridge those labels stretch from a walkup’s front vestibule buzzer to a prewar tower’s lobby entrance buzzer, and the same chain sits under every one: a door release button, the electric strike it fires, the buzzer transformer and buzzer power supply behind the circuit, the entry system wiring climbing the building, and on current platforms a remote door release riding a phone.

Long riser. The tall building’s wiring spine, six stories and up. Fails in the middle as often as the ends — and the middle is where lazy diagnoses never look.

Junction. Where the riser branches at each floor. The fourth-floor junction that corroded quietly is the classic ridge failure, taking every line above it.

Multi-panel system. Lobby intercoms with more than one street or vestibule panel. Each panel is tested separately before anything shared takes the blame.

Strike. The electric latch in the frame — lobby-brass or service-steel. The cheapest fix on the sheet and the most-replaced-for innocent on the hill.

Transformer. The shared voltage source, decades in a cellar, then sudden total silence. First test on every all-dead call.

Two-level entrance. Lobby on one street, service door a level below on another — one building, two releases, one security posture that both must serve.

Shared amplifier. The audio brain behind many prewar systems. Hum, crackle or one-way talk in every apartment at once points here first.

Release path. Button to strike, end to end. Visitors heard but never admitted means the fault lives on this path — nowhere else.

El-dust fouling. Steel particulate from the elevated 4 working into avenue-facing contacts and buttons — the Jerome-edge signature, cleaned as a repair and sealed as a cure.

Station. The in-unit wall unit, any vintage. Fails alone, replaced alone — a hundred neighbors never know.

Board-ready finding. Photos, both paths priced, reusable wiring named — the document an HDFC or co-op vote actually needs.

Platform vs. part. The entire repair-or-replace question in four words: parts fail cheap and often, platforms rare and dear. Diagnosis keeps them separate.
