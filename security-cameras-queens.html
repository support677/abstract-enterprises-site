The Neighborhood

What Makes Buying Security Cameras in Queens Different

Queens is the borough of the attached house with a tenant upstairs, the garden apartment built around a courtyard, the detached home with a driveway and a garage out back, and the family-run store under an elevated subway line. A security camera kit designed for a suburban cul-de-sac assumes one owner, one front door and a router in the middle of the house. A Queens two-family has two front doors, a shared side alley, a basement that may hold a third household or may have flooded during Ida, and a landlord who wants to see the gate without seeing into the tenant's kitchen. That gap between a generic box and a real Queens building is the reason we sell security camera equipment the way we do: as a licensed installer who will tell you what fits before you spend anything.

Scale matters too. The 2023 New York City Housing and Vacancy Survey counted 919,800 housing units in Queens, about a quarter of the city's stock, with 863,700 of them occupied and a rental vacancy rate of just 0.88 percent. A borough that tight means most buildings hold more than one household, and almost every camera conversation we have in Queens includes a second party: a tenant, a co-op board, a neighbor sharing a driveway, a commercial landlord or a sibling who co-owns the house. The equipment has to work for all of them.

The other thing that sets Queens apart is who owns the storefronts. The Department of City Planning's The Newest New Yorkers, 2026 counts around 1.1 million foreign-born residents in Queens, close to half the borough. In Flushing and Murray Hill the share is 67 percent, in Elmhurst 66 percent, in Corona 61 percent, and in Jackson Heights nearly 60 percent. Those neighborhoods are lined with independent restaurants, groceries, phone stores, jewelers, travel agencies, money transfer counters and salons run by owners who work long hours, often travel to see family abroad, and want to check the shop from wherever they are. Remote viewing that works reliably from another continent is not a luxury for them; it is the main reason they buy.

For a buyer, the lesson is that the back-window break-in is less likely than it was, while thefts from the curb, the doorstep, the mailbox and the store floor continue. Those incidents happen at predictable spots, which points toward fewer, better-placed cameras with the right lens rather than a box of wide-angle cameras that record shapes.

Mail, packages and the front gate

Mail theft is a Queens problem with a paper trail. The U.S. Postal Inspection Service has described joint operations with the NYPD in Queens that led to 10 arrests: two suspects tied to thefts from postal relay boxes in Ridgewood and Glendale, and eight people arrested in Flushing, where inspectors recovered stolen mail, IDs, credit cards and a postal key. For a building owner, that is the argument for a camera on the mailbox bank and the vestibule, not just on the street door.

Packages are the other half. ABC7 New York reported in April 2024 on a Queens homeowner who said he and his wife had lost packages to porch thieves dozens of times, including a jacket worth $1,500; his own camera recorded the theft that finally pushed him to set a trap. The story ended with an arrest, and also with the station noting that authorities advise against confronting a suspect. We agree with the second point. A camera's job is to record the evidence clearly so the police can act; the person watching the app should stay inside and call it in.

Queens houses make this easier than apartment lobbies do, because so many have a low front gate, a short walk and a stoop of three or four steps. A camera mounted at the top of the steps at roughly head height, aimed across the walk toward the gate, records a face both coming and going. A second camera on the corner of the house or the garage covers the curb, which is where the getaway car or the cargo bike waits.

Basements, floods and where the recorder lives

Many Queens buyers want to put the recorder in the basement because that is where the cable is easiest to run. We ask them to think again. When the remnants of Hurricane Ida hit on September 1, 2021, QNS reported 11 deaths in Queens, most of them in flooded basements in East Flushing, Woodside, Jamaica, Forest Hills and Elmhurst. The human cost is the reason that storm is remembered. For the much smaller question of camera equipment, it is a reminder that a recorder on a basement floor or a low shelf can be underwater in an hour, taking every recording with it. We plan recorders for a closet on the first floor, or at least a high shelf away from the boiler and the floor drain, with a small battery backup beside it.

Industrial Queens

Queens is also a working borough. NYCEDC lists seven Industrial Business Zones in Queens: Jamaica, JFK, Long Island City, Maspeth, Ridgewood, Steinway and Woodside. Those zones are full of distributors, air-cargo forwarders near the airport, bakeries, printers, auto body shops, building-supply yards and food wholesalers. Their camera needs look nothing like a house: dock doors, truck yards, perimeter fences, long unlit stretches between buildings, and a recorder that has to keep weeks of footage because damage and shortage claims arrive late.

Outdoor security cameras for Queens houses and storefronts

Outdoor surveillance cameras in Queens usually hang under a porch roof, on the corner of a brick house above the driveway, under the soffit of a detached garage, on the sign band of a store or on the wall of a warehouse above a dock door. Three things decide whether an outdoor camera is a good buy: its weather rating, what it sees at night, and how wide or narrow the lens is. Our house-brand outdoor models carry IP66 or IP67 ratings, depending on the series, and night vision rated from 60 to 120 feet.

Outdoor winter security cameras are not a separate product so much as the right rating plus the right mounting. Both IP66 and IP67 are dust-tight and cover heavy rain and snow; IP67 adds protection against temporary immersion. In Queens the harder test is often wind-driven rain off Jamaica Bay in the Rockaways and Howard Beach, or salt spray on the bay side of Broad Channel, which attacks cheap brackets and unsealed connectors more than the cameras. We sell outdoor cameras with junction boxes that close over the connection, and we steer buyers near the water toward housings and mounts that won't rust.

Indoor security cameras for Queens shops, halls and basements

Indoor surveillance cameras cover entry halls, stairwells, mailbox areas, store aisles, counters, stockrooms, garages and basements. Indoors the usual goal is a wide, steady view from a high corner or the middle of a ceiling, so turrets, small domes and compact bullets on ceiling mounts are the standard choices. In drop-tile ceilings, common in Queens storefronts and offices, a camera can mount right on a tile or on a short pendant hung from the grid.

Two indoor spots deserve their own camera choice. Cash register security cameras and point of sale surveillance cameras work best above and slightly behind the counter, close enough to read a bill denomination and see hands on the drawer. Basement security cameras in a two-family need good low-light performance, because basement lighting is usually a single bulb, and they should look at the door and the stairs, not into any room someone lives in.

How each camera connects: PoE, WiFi or coax

PoE security cameras run one Cat6 cable each back to a PoE recorder or switch, and that cable carries both the picture and the power. That is our default for new systems in Queens: no outlet needed at the camera, a steady picture, and a camera nobody can unplug from the outside. Wired analog cameras, sold under the HD-TVI, HD-CVI or AHD names, use coax cable and a DVR, and they are the cheapest upgrade when an older house or store already has coax through the walls. Wireless cameras send video over WiFi but still need power from an outlet, a battery or a small solar panel.

Queens houses are a mix: brick attached houses in Ridgewood and Middle Village with thick party walls, frame houses with vinyl siding in Richmond Hill and Woodhaven, and stucco or brick detached homes in Bayside and Douglaston. WiFi does better through siding than through brick, but in all of them the router is usually inside, upstairs or in the middle of the house, while the cameras are on the outside walls. We sell WiFi security cameras for one-off spots, like a detached shed at the end of a yard, and recommend wired for anything that has to record every night without fail. Buy PoE cameras or wired security cameras here and we size the recorder's PoE budget so night-time infrared and spotlights never brown out a camera.

Detection, alerts and analytics

AI security cameras is a marketing umbrella. What most Queens buyers actually want from it is simple: an alert on the phone when a person walks up the driveway at 2 a.m., and no alert for every passing bus, every plane on the approach to LaGuardia or JFK, every cat and every swaying tree. Person and vehicle detection on the camera or recorder is what delivers that, and several of our house-brand packages list human and vehicle detection on the spec sheet. Smart motion detection cameras and smart search security camera systems also make playback faster, because you can jump to the moments a person or vehicle appeared instead of scrubbing through hours of traffic.

License plate recognition cameras, also sold as LPR, ANPR or license plate capture cameras, facial recognition security cameras and people counting cameras are distinct products with their own optics, settings and, in New York City, legal obligations for some businesses. We describe a product as having LPR, facial recognition, NDAA compliance, ONVIF, H.265, IK10 or edge AI only when its manufacturer's spec sheet says so, and we send that sheet with the quote.

Digital video recorders (DVR)

A DVR records analog HD cameras over coax. Current HD DVRs handle 5MP and 4K analog cameras, which makes them the most economical upgrade for a Queens house or store that had a camera system put in years ago: the coax stays, the old cameras and recorder come out, and the picture improves dramatically. We sell 4-, 8- and 16-channel DVRs, and on the 4K kits the DVR is matched to the 8MP cameras. Before a buyer commits to reusing old coax, we test it. Cable that has been stapled flat, run through a damp basement for twenty years or spliced with tape during a renovation often can't carry a 4K signal, and it is better to know that before the order.

CCTV hard drives are designed to write video around the clock, every day, which a desktop drive is not built to do. PoE switches, injectors and power supplies

When the recorder runs out of PoE ports, or a camera sits farther from the recorder than one cable run allows, a PoE switch for security cameras adds ports and power at the right spot. We size switches by total power budget rather than port count, because cameras with infrared, white-light spotlights or sirens draw noticeably more at night. A security camera PoE injector powers a single camera where a switch would be overkill. For coax systems, a CCTV power supply box feeds several cameras from one fused unit, and a CCTV power adapter powers one. A small battery backup on the recorder and switch rides out the brief outages and flickers that come with summer storms and Con Edison work on the block.

A camera junction box, also sold as a CCTV junction box, sits between the camera and the wall, hides the connector and spare cable, and keeps water out of the joint, which is where most outdoor failures start. Cable, connectors and baluns

Security camera laws and privacy rules for Queens businesses

Two sets of rules come up most often when a Queens business buys cameras. The first is New York City's biometric identifier law, Administrative Code §22-1201 and following. Since July 9, 2021, retail stores, food and drink establishments and places of entertainment that collect biometric identifier information from customers, such as face geometry, must post a clear and conspicuous sign near every customer entrance, and they may not sell, lease, trade or share that information for value. A Troutman Pepper summary of the law notes that ordinary video falls outside the disclosure duty when it is not analyzed by software that identifies people and is not shared except with law enforcement. Put simply: a standard camera system does not trigger the sign requirement; facial recognition security cameras can.

The second is audio. Under New York Penal Law §250.05, eavesdropping, which includes mechanical overhearing of a conversation, is a class E felony, and §250.00 defines mechanical overhearing as recording a conversation without the consent of at least one party. Audio recording security cameras and two way audio security cameras are therefore features we configure on purpose: usually off by default in stores and restaurants, on only where there is a clear reason and a posted notice. This is general information and not legal advice; for a specific situation, speak to an attorney.

CCTV regulations and security camera privacy laws for homes come down to common sense more than statute: record your own property and shared areas, never the inside of someone's home, a bathroom or a changing area, and keep cameras from looking into a neighbor's windows. Are security cameras legal at a Queens house or store? Video of your own property and of areas the public can see generally is. The edges are audio, biometrics, tenants' private spaces and employees' private spaces.

"Put the recorder somewhere out of the way, like the basement"

Security Camera Prices in Queens: What Moves the Number

Security camera prices, CCTV camera prices and security camera system cost questions all trace back to the same short list. Installed systems in Queens start at $950 for a four-camera house-brand system with the recorder, cable, mounting and phone setup included. Equipment-only prices shift with supply, so every item on this page carries the same line: call or text for today's price. What raises or lowers the total:

  • Resolution. 2K, 5MP and 4K cameras step up in cost, and 4K also calls for a 4K recorder and more storage.
  • Number of cameras and recorder size. Passing four cameras means an 8-channel recorder; passing eight means a 16-channel one.
  • Connection. PoE, coax that reuses existing cable, or WiFi each change the parts list.
  • Days of storage. Two weeks and a month need very different drives; see the worked example above.
  • Camera features. Full color night vision, audio, sirens and lights, two-way talk, long-range or varifocal lenses, person and vehicle detection.
  • Accessories. Junction boxes, arm or pole brackets, PoE switches for garages, outdoor cable and battery backup.
  • Installation route. For installed systems, an open basement ceiling in a frame house is quicker than finished ceilings in an attached brick house or a landmarked co-op.

FAQ

Queens Security Cameras FAQ

Do you deliver security cameras to every Queens neighborhood, from Astoria to the Rockaways?
+

Yes. Equipment-only orders are delivered anywhere in Queens, from Long Island City and Astoria to Flushing, Jamaica, the Rockaways and Breezy Point. We confirm the address, the entrance to use and who will receive the order when you buy. There is no pickup counter, and we don't promise a same-day or fixed-time delivery window.

Will passing trains, planes and traffic set off constant alerts on Queens cameras?
+

They will with plain motion detection, which reacts to any change in the picture. Cameras and recorders with person and vehicle detection, set up with alert zones around the door, gate or lot, ignore most of that movement. We look for those features on the spec sheet before recommending a camera for a house near the 7 train, Queens Boulevard or the airport approaches.

Can a camera cover the shared driveway between two attached Queens houses?
+

Yes. A camera at each end of the driveway, aimed along its length, covers the cars, the garage and the street without looking into either house. The recorder's privacy masks can black out the neighbor's side windows in the recording. Agreeing the views with the neighbor first avoids most disputes.

Can a landlord's camera cover a basement tenant's entrance in Queens?
+

A landlord can cover the shared path, gate and yard that lead to a basement entrance. The tenant's door is their front door, so we frame the approach rather than the doorway, keep windows out of view and leave audio off. Telling the tenant what is recorded is good practice. For a lease question, ask an attorney.

Does a Queens store need its landlord's permission before drilling for cameras?
+

Usually the lease decides. Many commercial leases require the landlord's approval for work on the facade, the sign band or the roof, while cameras inside the store are the tenant's own business. Check the lease, and if the outside cameras need approval, we can send the landlord a simple camera plan showing where each one mounts and how the cable runs.

Can cameras help catch illegal dumping in front of a Queens property?
+

They can record it. A camera covering the curb with enough resolution to see a vehicle and a plate, plus a light or full color night vision, gives you a clip and a time to report to the city. Person and vehicle alerts on that zone tell you while it is happening. Dumping often happens at night, so the night picture matters more than the day picture.

Do your cameras connect to a Queens building's intercom or video buzzer?
+

They are normally separate systems. A camera system records continuously to its own recorder, while an intercom or video buzzer lets residents see and admit visitors. Some buildings want the entrance camera shown on the super's monitor as well; we can plan that. We also install and repair intercoms if the building is updating both.

Can you deliver camera equipment to a Queens job site for my electrician to install?
+

Yes. Contractors and electricians order from us on a trade quote, and we deliver to the job site or the contractor's shop in Queens. Tell us the site contact and the hours someone will be there to receive it. We can also preconfigure the recorder so the installer only has to mount and connect the cameras.

Are battery cameras a good choice for a detached garage in Queens?
+

For a single camera where no cable can reach, they work, but they record in short clips, need recharging and lose charge faster in cold weather. If the garage needs steady recording or more than one camera, a buried or conduit-run network cable to a small PoE switch in the garage is the more reliable choice.

Do you sell cameras for Queens houses of worship and community centers?
+

Yes. Temples, mosques, gurdwaras, churches and community centers buy cameras for entrances, parking areas, hallways and donation or office areas. Cameras stay out of private spaces such as restrooms and changing rooms. The board or committee usually approves a written list of views before anything is mounted, and we provide one.

Can I take my cameras with me if I sell my Queens house?
+

Usually, yes, if the sale contract doesn't include them. The cameras, recorder and drive come down and the mounting holes are sealed; cable left in the walls normally stays. We can remove the system and reinstall it at your new address, with labor quoted by phone or text.
