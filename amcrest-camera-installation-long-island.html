The Neighborhood

Why Long Island Properties Need Amcrest Camera Installation

Long Island is a place of detached houses with driveways, and that single fact shapes almost every Amcrest job we plan here. The Census Bureau's 2023 five-year American Community Survey counts 477,860 housing units in Nassau County, of which 364,529 are one-unit detached homes, and 579,790 units in Suffolk County, of which 463,095 are detached. The median year built is 1955 in Nassau and 1970 in Suffolk. In plain terms, most of the people who call us about an Amcrest system live in a house with its own yard, its own front door and back door, a driveway that holds one or more cars, and often a garage that may or may not be attached. Each of those is a place a camera is supposed to watch, and each is a separate cable route from wherever the recorder ends up. A one-bedroom apartment in the city might need two cameras; a Long Island ranch on a corner lot can easily need six or eight, simply because there is more outside to see.

The age of those houses matters as much as their number. Kerimurray Architecture's history of Levittown records that 17,447 homes were built there between 1947 and 1951, that they were ranches built on concrete slabs with no basement, and that they were heated with hot-water radiant heating pipes in the slab. That is the extreme version of the postwar Long Island house, but the pattern repeats well beyond Levittown. NeighborhoodScout reports that single-family detached homes make up 69.74 percent of Uniondale's housing units and that 55.45 percent of the housing there was built between the 1940s and the 1960s. Homes.com describes the residential streets south of Hempstead Turnpike as mostly Cape Cods and minimal traditionals, often with detached garages and private driveways, and a Hempstead real-estate guide describes 1950s and 1960s split-levels and hi-ranches near Hofstra and the Coliseum. For an Amcrest PoE system, a slab means the cable cannot drop into a basement and travel underneath the house; it has to go up into the attic instead. A detached garage means a cable that crosses open ground. A split-level means three short floors and a route that has to step between them. We plan for that before we book, not on the day.

The reason people want cameras here is not abstract. In January 2024, ABC7 reported on a multi-jurisdictional task force of Nassau and Suffolk police and district attorneys, the NYPD, State Police and the FBI working on residential burglaries and stolen cars, and quoted police saying the burglars "are attracted by cars in people's driveways." In November 2025, CBS New York reported that the Suffolk District Attorney had charged 14 men in a package-theft ring that worked 31 communities, including Brentwood, Central Islip, Shirley and Deer Park, and allegedly targeted deliveries of phones and tablets. And in May 2026, Daily Voice reported that a Roslyn Heights homeowner saw would-be burglars on his own surveillance system, called police, and arrests followed. None of those stories is about Amcrest in particular, and we do not claim any of those homeowners as customers. They show the two jobs a Long Island camera system is asked to do: watch the driveway, and watch the front step.

Amcrest is a common answer to that job because of how it records. SafeHome.org's review notes that most Amcrest cameras need no subscription and can record to a microSD card, a network video recorder or a NAS drive, and retailer listings for Amcrest PoE cameras describe recording to an FTP server, a local computer, any Amcrest NVR, or Blue Iris software. For a homeowner who does not want a monthly bill for every camera, that is attractive. The trade-off is that the system is only as good as the network it sits on. Amcrest's own support forum explains that the common remote-viewing setup relies on peer-to-peer connection through the internet, so when the internet drops, remote access goes with it. That puts more weight on the installation: a recorder in a cool, dry place, a PoE switch with battery backup, cameras with fixed addresses, and recording that keeps running locally even when you cannot see it from your phone.

There is also a question that did not exist a few years ago. Amcrest says on its own website that it built its brand with Dahua and is moving its line toward NDAA-compliant products, naming its AMLINK and HELIX lines. In 2021, The Intercept reported that an Amcrest representative confirmed its products were not NDAA compliant, while the company described them as designed by Amcrest in Houston, Texas. Amcrest itself is not named on the FCC's Covered List, but Dahua is, for certain security purposes, and iCTally reports that since July 16, 2026, the FCC has barred the continued import and marketing of previously authorized Dahua equipment for those uses. One source, Blinkless, says the rules do not revoke equipment already installed or ban private use. For a Long Island homeowner, that usually means the cameras on the wall today keep working. For a business, a school, or anyone buying replacement parts later, it means the exact model number matters. That is why we ask for the SKU before we quote.

Finally, Long Island is not one jurisdiction. The state's county profile describes Nassau as two cities, three towns, 64 incorporated villages and more than 60 unincorporated hamlets, and Suffolk as 10 towns. The NYC agencies that govern camera work in the five boroughs, such as Housing Preservation and Development, the Department of Buildings and the Landmarks Preservation Commission, do not reach Nassau or Suffolk at all. Here the rules come from the state license law, the county consumer affairs offices, and whichever town or village the house sits in, plus any homeowners' association. We ask for the full address so we know which of those applies.

We also want to be honest about the gaps in what we could find. We looked for Reddit threads, Yelp reviews and hyperlocal news about Amcrest installations on Long Island and found none specific to the brand or the area. We did not find a source describing the walls, attics or siding of Long Island houses as a group, so where this page talks about attic routes on slab ranches or block foundations on Cape Cods, it is drawing on the housing types named in the public record and on what we expect in houses of that age, not on a survey. One restoration contractor in Uniondale warns that postwar Cape Cods and ranches there often contain asbestos materials; we treat that as a reason for care when drilling old siding and ceilings, not as a fact about your house.

Long Island facts that shape an Amcrest installation

  • Mostly detached houses: 364,529 of Nassau's 477,860 housing units and 463,095 of Suffolk's 579,790 are one-unit detached homes, per the 2023 ACS five-year estimates.
  • Different ages by county: the median home was built in 1955 in Nassau and 1970 in Suffolk, which tends to mean slab ranches and Capes on one side of the county line and basement colonials on the other.
  • Slabs with heat in them: Levittown's 17,447 homes, built 1947 to 1951, were ranches on concrete slabs with radiant hot-water heating pipes, so floors are never drilled and cable goes overhead.
  • Many governments: Nassau has 2 cities, 3 towns and 64 villages; Suffolk has 10 towns; permits and rules are checked per address.
  • Storms take the power: during Tropical Storm Isaias in August 2020, ABC7 reported almost half of PSEG Long Island's 1.1 million customers were without power at the peak.
  • Water as well as wind: the NYS Department of Financial Services described the August 18–19, 2024 storm as the first-ever flash flood emergency for Suffolk County, with more than 2,000 residents and business owners reporting flood damage.

What Long Island owners often add once the cameras are up. After the driveway and the doors are covered, the next request is usually a video doorbell at the front step or a screen in the kitchen or office that shows every camera at once. Both can be planned in the same visit if you mention them when booking, so the cable for the doorbell and the HDMI run for the viewing screen go in with the camera cables instead of on a second day.

Nassau: the slab, the attic and the short driveway

Much of Nassau was built in the first two decades after the Second World War, and the median home there dates to 1955. That is the era of the Levittown ranch on a slab, the Cape Cod with a half-story upstairs, and the minimal traditional on a modest lot. For an Amcrest system it creates a very specific puzzle: there is often no basement for the recorder, no crawlspace to route cable under, and a floor you must never drill because it may hold radiant heating pipes. So the cable route goes up. We put the NVR and PoE switch in a closet or utility area with an outlet, take a single bundle up into the attic through a top plate, and fan out across the attic to each eave, where the cameras mount on the soffit or the wall just below it. In a Cape with knee walls upstairs, the space behind those knee walls often becomes the highway between the front and back of the house. The advantage of the Nassau layout is that lots are often compact, so most camera runs are short and well within the distance a PoE camera can be powered over a single cable.

Suffolk: the basement, the long lot and the outbuilding

Suffolk's median home was built in 1970, and the county covers more than five times the land of Nassau. In practice that tends to mean more raised ranches, high ranches and center-hall colonials, many with full or partial basements, on deeper lots with longer driveways. A basement changes everything about the cable plan. The recorder can sit downstairs on a shelf, away from summer attic heat, and cables can run along the basement ceiling to the foot of each outside wall before coming up through the sill. The challenge in Suffolk is distance. A camera at the end of a long driveway, on a shed at the back of an acre lot, or on a detached garage across a yard may sit near or beyond the practical limit of a single Ethernet run, which is about 100 meters including the patch cords. Those are the jobs where we talk about a small outdoor-rated switch in the garage, or a link between buildings, rather than pushing one cable too far.

Where the counties look the same

Some things do not change at the county line. Both counties face salt air on the South Shore and along the Sound, and both lost power widely during Isaias in 2020. Both have office parks and flex buildings where an Amcrest system has to share ceilings and closets with other trades. And in both, the state license law applies in exactly the same way: CCTV used to watch for intrusion, theft or movement is security work that requires a Department of State license. Where the counties differ is in pricing and in the local rules we check, which is why Nassau addresses use our Nassau County Amcrest prices and Suffolk addresses use our Suffolk County Amcrest prices.

On most camera brands, the first question is where the camera goes. On Amcrest in 2026, the first question is which camera it is. Why the SKU matters more than the logo

Two Amcrest cameras that look alike on a store shelf can be very different products underneath. Amcrest's own site says the brand was built with Dahua, and NDAA compliance lists published by integrators such as Tec-Tel place Amcrest under Dahua as a U.S.-distributed OEM. At the same time, Amcrest says it is moving toward lines it describes as NDAA compliant. So the question for your property is not "is Amcrest allowed," but "what is this exact model, where did its hardware come from, and can I still buy a matching camera if one fails in three years." We look up every model number you send us before the visit. If a model is likely to be hard to source later, we tell you before you pay for it to be installed, not afterward.

Homes, businesses and buildings that take public money

For a family in a Massapequa split-level or a Commack colonial, the practical effect of the 2026 rules is small: cameras already bought keep working, and private use is not banned according to the sources we found. The stakes are higher for a business with an insurer that asks about equipment, a contractor working on a building that takes federal funds, a school, a house of worship applying for a security grant, or a property manager planning a large rollout across several sites. The FCC's Dahua entry is limited to particular security and infrastructure purposes, and NDAA Section 889 restrictions turn on federal money. If your site touches either, tell us at booking. We would rather point you to an NDAA-compliant product than install something you may have to replace to keep a grant or a contract.

What we do with a kit you already bought

Many Long Island calls start with an Amcrest NVR kit bought online or at a store. A buyer review on Adorama for one Amcrest NVR kit put it simply: "I am having a problem finding someone to install my system." We install customer-supplied Amcrest equipment, and the model check still comes first. If the kit is sound, we install it and say so. If a camera in it is a model you may struggle to replace, we tell you, so you can decide whether to install it as is, add spares now, or return part of it while you still can. The decision is yours; our job is to make sure you make it with the facts in front of you.

Stand where the camera will stand

It helps more than anything else if you take a photo from roughly where each camera would mount, pointing where you want it to look. A camera under the eave of a ranch sees the driveway at an angle; a camera on a garage corner sees the length of the drive. If the view you photograph includes the street, neighbors' windows or a sidewalk, tell us, because we set motion zones and privacy masks to keep the recording on your property. Long Island lots vary so much that two houses on the same block can need completely different camera positions, and your photos let us plan the right ones.

Count what is plugged in near the recorder

The NVR and the PoE switch both need power, and in a storm they both need a battery behind them if you want recording to continue for a while after the lights go out. Look at the closet, utility room or basement corner where you would like the recorder, and note whether there is an outlet, whether it is on a circuit shared with a freezer or a sump pump, and whether the space gets hot in summer. An attic is a poor home for a recorder for exactly that reason. If there is no good spot, we will suggest one on the walk-through.

Know who else has wired the house

Many Long Island houses already carry cables from earlier jobs: an alarm panel from the 1990s, a cable-TV drop, a previous camera system, a satellite dish. Old cables can sometimes be reused as pull strings and sometimes need to come out. If you know who installed earlier equipment, tell us. If you have an existing recorder of any brand, a photo of its label and of the back with all its cables is the most useful single picture you can send.

Amcrest Camera Installation for Long Island's Architecture

Long Island's houses look similar from the road, but under the roof and behind the siding they split into a handful of types, and each changes where the recorder goes, how the cable travels and what the camera is fixed to. These are the types the public record and local listings describe, and how we approach each.

Postwar Slab Ranches

The Levittown model: one floor on a concrete slab with radiant heat pipes, no basement, an attic above. The floor is never drilled; every cable goes up and across the attic to the eaves.

Cape Cods and Minimal Traditionals

Common south of Hempstead Turnpike in Uniondale and across Nassau, often with detached garages and private driveways. Knee-wall spaces upstairs and block foundations below shape the routes.

Split-Levels and Hi-Ranches

Described near Hofstra and the Coliseum and common in both counties. Three short levels mean a route that steps between floors, usually through a closet or the garage ceiling.

Colonials and Raised Ranches with Basements

More typical in Suffolk, where the median home dates to 1970. The basement holds the recorder and carries the cables to the foot of each wall.

Waterfront and Seasonal Houses

South Shore homes, North Shore houses on the Sound and East End seasonal places. Salt air, wind exposure and long periods empty, which makes local recording and remote alerts matter more.

Condos, Garden Apartments and Rentals

Shared walls, HOA or board rules on exterior hardware, and, in places like Uniondale where NeighborhoodScout counts about 38 percent renters, a landlord who must agree to any drilling.

On slab houses. Kerimurray Architecture records that the original Levittown ranches carried their heat in hot-water pipes in the slab. We assume that any slab house of that era may still have heat or plumbing in the floor, whatever has been renovated since, and we never drill a slab to bring a cable up. The attic route is better anyway: it keeps cables dry, it is short, and it puts every camera at the eave line where it sees the yard from above. The catch is summer heat. Attics on Long Island get very hot, so we keep the recorder and the switch downstairs and only the cables go overhead. Where an attic has old insulation of uncertain type, we look before we crawl, and if anything looks like it could contain asbestos we stop and tell you rather than disturb it.

On detached garages. Homes.com describes detached garages as common in Uniondale's Cape Cod streets, and the same is true across much of Nassau and Suffolk. A detached garage is often the most valuable camera position on the lot, because it sees the length of the driveway and the back yard. It is also the hardest to reach. A cable crossing a yard has to be either buried in suitable conduit or run overhead in a protected way, and it has to enter both buildings cleanly and sealed. Where the garage is far enough away that a single PoE run is marginal, we put a small outdoor-rated switch inside it and feed that switch from the house. We will show you both options and their trade-offs on the walk-through.

On split-levels. Split-levels and hi-ranches are some of the trickiest houses to wire on the island, because there is no single attic or basement that reaches every outside wall. The lower level may sit half underground, the upper bedrooms have their own attic, and the middle level has neither. We usually find a vertical path through a stack of closets or the wall shared with the garage, and use the garage ceiling as a junction where cables split to the front and the side. That planning is the reason we ask for photos of the inside as well as the outside.

What a business camera on Long Island has to do

A homeowner's camera usually needs to show a person on a step or a car in a driveway. For an Amcrest system, that means choosing the lens and resolution for each view instead of using the same camera everywhere, putting the recorder somewhere secure and cool, sizing storage for the retention period the business needs, and running cable in pathways that the landlord and other trades will accept. In an office tower like RXR Plaza with raised floors, those pathways may already exist; in a flex building in Hauppauge, cable may need to run on J-hooks or in conduit along an open deck.

Rules and practical limits for business camera systems

  • State license. New York's General Business Law Article 6-D, administered by the Department of State, requires a security and fire alarm installer license for CCTV used to detect or monitor intrusion, break-in, theft, movement, sound or fire. An industry association states that unlicensed installers face fines of up to ten thousand dollars. Our license number is on every quote.
  • Nassau cabling. A Nassau County permit guide notes that the alarm license applies when cabling integrates with camera systems, so a cabling contractor who is not licensed for security work should not be the one wiring your cameras.
  • Home improvement licensing. Nassau County's Office of Consumer Affairs says it is against the law to run a home improvement business in the county without a license, and its FAQ says work only on commercial buildings does not need that license. Homes do.
  • Federal money. Businesses, nonprofits and institutions that take federal funds or work under federal contracts should ask about NDAA Section 889 before choosing Amcrest models, because some Amcrest products have been described as Dahua-built.
  • Audio. New York's recording law is one-party consent for conversations, but a camera that records sound in a place where no party to a conversation consents can still cross the line under Penal Law 250.00. We set business cameras to video only unless the owner has a clear reason and a policy for audio.
  • Private spaces. Penal Law 250.45 makes recording inside a bathroom, changing room or similar private space without consent a felony. Cameras never point into those spaces.
  • Landlord and insurance papers. Many commercial landlords want a certificate of insurance and scheduled hours before contractors work in a suite. Send their requirements with the booking.

We do not publish stories about a customer's house unless we can document them, so this is not about one address. It is the routine our technicians follow when they arrive at the kind of house Long Island is full of: a one-story ranch on a slab, a driveway down one side, a detached garage at the back, and a box of Amcrest cameras on the kitchen table.

The brand and the rules

Are Amcrest cameras banned on Long Island after the FCC's 2026 changes?

No. Amcrest is not named on the FCC Covered List. Dahua is, limited to certain public safety, government facility, critical infrastructure and national security uses, and since July 16, 2026 the FCC has barred the continued import and marketing of previously authorized Dahua equipment for those uses, according to iCTally. One source says installed equipment is not revoked and private use is not banned. Because Amcrest has historically built on Dahua hardware, we check each model before installing it so you are not caught out on replacements later.

Where are Amcrest cameras designed and made, and should that change what I buy?

Amcrest describes its products as designed in Houston, Texas, and says on its own site that it built its brand with Dahua. Compliance lists published by integrators place Amcrest under Dahua as a U.S.-distributed OEM, and Amcrest says it is moving toward NDAA-compliant lines it calls AMLINK and HELIX. For a private home, the origin mostly matters for future parts and firmware support. For a business with federal money or a federal contract, it can matter a great deal. Tell us which you are and we will advise accordingly.

Does whoever installs my Amcrest cameras need a New York license?

If the cameras are there to watch for intrusion, theft or movement, yes. A New York law firm, Kirschenbaum & Kirschenbaum, explains that the state's security and fire alarm installer license covers CCTV used for those purposes, and the license is issued by the Department of State under General Business Law Article 6-D. One permit guide notes that homeowners installing their own system do not need a state license. A paid installer does. Our license is NYS #12000287431, and it appears on our quotes.

How can I check that a Long Island camera installer is properly licensed?

Ask for the state security and fire alarm installer license number and check it with the New York Department of State. For residential work in Nassau, also ask for the county home improvement license; Nassau's consumer checklist gives the Office of Consumer Affairs as the place to check a contractor, and the county requires licensed home improvement contractors to be fingerprinted. Recording, internet and the app

If our internet goes down during a storm, do the Amcrest cameras stop recording?

They should not, if the system is installed to record locally. Amcrest's support forum explains that remote viewing usually relies on a peer-to-peer connection through the internet, so you lose the ability to watch from your phone when the internet drops. Recording to the NVR inside the house does not need the internet at all. It does need power, which is why we recommend a battery backup for the recorder, the PoE switch and the router. After Isaias, when almost half of PSEG Long Island's customers lost power, that battery is the difference between footage and a gap.

Do the Amcrest PoE cameras in my kit plug straight into my router?

Usually not directly. PoE cameras take power and data over one cable, and most home routers do not supply power on their network ports. Many Amcrest PoE cameras need a separate PoE injector or switch, as product listings note. In an Amcrest NVR kit, the recorder often has built-in PoE ports, and the cameras plug into those. When you add cameras beyond what the recorder can power, or put a camera far from the recorder, we add a PoE switch sized for the load. We check the power budget on paper before we buy anything.

The property and the weather

How do you get a camera cable out to a detached garage across the yard?

There are two honest options. One is a protected run, buried in suitable conduit or carried overhead in a protected way, entering both buildings through sealed holes. The other, when the garage is far enough that a single run is marginal, is to put a small outdoor-rated switch in the garage and feed it from the house. Long Island installer field notes we reviewed say detached garages need their own cable runs, and that matches what we plan for. We never staple an unprotected indoor cable across a lawn or a fence.

Our ranch is on a slab with no basement. Where does the recorder go and how do the cables get out?

The recorder goes in a closet or utility area with an outlet, inside the heated part of the house, and the cables go up, not down. We bring a bundle up through the top of the closet wall into the attic and run each cable across to its eave, where the camera mounts on the soffit or the wall below. The slab is never drilled, because houses of the Levittown era were built with radiant heating pipes in the floor. The attic route is short and dry, and it puts the cameras high enough to see the yard.

Will Amcrest cameras hold up to salt air on the South Shore and along the Sound?

The camera bodies are rated for outdoor use, but the weak points near salt water are usually the fixings, the junction boxes and the cable entries rather than the camera itself. Long Island installer notes we reviewed say waterfront jobs need weather-rated housings for salt air. We use corrosion-resistant screws and mounts, seal every entry, leave drip loops so water runs off before it reaches the connector, and avoid putting the recorder in a garage on a flood-prone lot. Sandy's damage on the South Shore is the reason for that last rule.

Privacy, police and the law

Can my Amcrest cameras legally record sound around my Long Island house?

Carefully. New York is a one-party consent state for recording conversations, according to the Digital Media Law Project, which means a camera recording a conversation that no one present has agreed to record can be a problem under Penal Law 250.00. A doorbell where you are speaking to a visitor is different from a backyard camera picking up the neighbors over the fence. Video in a bathroom, bedroom or changing room without consent is a felony under Penal Law 250.45. We set outdoor cameras to video only unless you ask otherwise and understand the rule.

Should I register my cameras with the Suffolk police, and do they get to watch them?

Registration is your choice, and it does not give police live access. The Suffolk County Police SHIELD program lets residents and businesses register the locations of their systems so the Department can ask participants to check video for a particular date and time, with retrieval at the owner's convenience. A separate Emergency Remote Camera Access option is opt-in. In Nassau, we found village programs such as Garden City's, which says registration does not give police access to privately owned systems, but no county-wide registry.

Can my Uniondale landlord stop me from putting up cameras on the house I rent?

The landlord controls the building, so drilling into the outside of a rented house needs the owner's agreement, and a lease may say so in writing. We found vendor claims that New York law now requires landlords to allow tenant cameras, but we could not verify any such statute, so we do not rely on it. What a renter can usually do without drilling is a camera inside the unit looking at their own door, or a battery doorbell that replaces an existing one with permission. We ask for the owner's written approval before any exterior work on a rental.

FAQ

Long Island Amcrest Camera Installation FAQ

Will I have to pay a monthly fee for my Amcrest cameras after the installation?
+

Not for recording. Most Amcrest cameras need no subscription and record to a microSD card, an NVR or a NAS, according to SafeHome.org. On Long Island we set recording to an Amcrest NVR in your house, so footage stays with you and keeps saving even when the internet is down. Some models offer optional cloud features, and we tell you before the visit which ones your models have.

Do I need a paid plan to see snapshot pictures in my Amcrest View alerts?
+

It depends on the camera and the app version, which is why an owner asked exactly this on Amcrest's own forum. Basic motion alerts usually arrive without a plan, but image previews and some smart features vary by model. We check your models before the install and set up alerts during the visit, so you see on your own phone what arrives with and without any optional plan.

Can our Amcrest system run completely offline, with no app and no internet connection?
+

Yes, for recording and viewing inside the house. The cameras record to the NVR on your local network, and you can watch on a screen connected to the recorder or on a computer at home. Amcrest cameras also work with Blue Iris software. What you give up offline is remote viewing from your phone, because Amcrest's usual remote setup relies on the internet.

Amcrest cameras are inexpensive, so is it worth paying someone to install them?
+

Review sites describe Amcrest as budget-friendly, and that is part of its appeal. The cost of a camera system on a Long Island house is mostly in the cable routes, the mounting and the setup, not the cameras. A cheap camera mounted in the wrong spot, on a weak surface, or on a cable that fails in the first winter costs more in the end. If you are handy, you can install it yourself; if not, the labor is where the value is.

Besides Amcrest, which camera brands do you install on Long Island?
+

We install a range of brands, including Lorex, Dahua, Hikvision, Uniview, Reolink and others, as well as video intercom and access control lines. If you have not bought equipment yet, we will tell you honestly whether Amcrest is the right choice for your property or whether another brand suits it better, especially for a business that needs NDAA-compliant equipment.

Do I need a town or village permit for cameras on my Long Island house?
+

One Long Island installer states that standard residential camera installation usually needs no permit, but that is an installer's claim, not an official ruling. Nassau has 2 cities, 3 towns and 64 villages, and Suffolk has 10 towns, each with its own code, and many neighborhoods also have HOA rules. We check the rules for your address and your association before the visit.

I read that an Amcrest camera talked to an IP address outside the owner's network. Can you lock ours down?
+

That complaint comes from a buyer review on a retailer's site, and it usually reflects the camera's cloud or peer-to-peer features reaching out to the internet. During setup we change default passwords, update firmware where an update is available, turn off cloud and remote features you do not want, and can place the cameras where only the recorder talks to them. You keep control of what leaves the house.

Our cameras keep changing addresses and dropping out of Blue Iris. Can that be fixed?
+

Usually, yes. A Blue Iris user reported exactly this, and the common cause is that the cameras get new addresses from the router after a restart, so the software loses them. We assign each camera a fixed address outside the router's automatic range, update Blue Iris or the NVR to match, and test a full restart of the router to confirm every camera comes back.

Can Amcrest SmartHome cameras and our Amcrest NVR cameras live in one app?
+

Not always. A retailer listing for one SmartHome-series camera says it works only with the Amcrest SmartHome app and has only view-only access through Amcrest View Pro. Mixing product lines can leave you with two apps. We check every model before buying or installing, and if a mixed system is unavoidable we set up both apps and show you which camera lives where.

Will you take over an Amcrest system that another company or a previous owner installed?
+

Yes. Houses on Long Island often change hands with cameras already on them, and installers move on. We trace the existing cables, reset the recorder with you as the owner, check the camera models, replace anything failing and re-label the system. Because Amcrest shares Dahua hardware lineage, most Dahua repair procedures apply, which helps with older recorders.

How do I hand my Amcrest footage to Nassau or Suffolk police if they ask for it?
+

Export only the clip they ask for, by date and time, to a USB drive or a file, and keep the original on the recorder. We show you how to do this during the handover. Programs such as Suffolk's SHIELD camera registry and Garden City's private camera registration ask owners to check video for a time window; they do not give police access to your system.

Our house on the East End sits empty all winter. How should the Amcrest system be set up for that?
+

Plan for the times no one is checking. That means recording to the NVR inside the house, battery backup for the recorder, switch and router, alerts set to your phone for the doors and driveway, and storage sized for the weeks between visits. It also helps to confirm that the internet service stays on over the winter, because remote viewing depends on it even though local recording does not.
